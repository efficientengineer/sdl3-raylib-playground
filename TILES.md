# TILES — the field, Sega style (D18)

Owner's decision (2026-09-19), final after a day of 2.5D, painted block-outs, angled painted screens and
painted top-down maps: **"Sega style, top down, using tile sheets, with walking based on a grid. No more
screen navmesh."** This file is the contract between the engine, the art pipeline and the map authors.
`FIELD.md` (3D) and the painted-screen work are parked: the code stays, nothing new is built on them.

## The look and the numbers

- Phantasy Star IV field: top-down oblique tiles, ground from straight above, objects showing roof and
  south face, hard tile edges, no perspective.
- **The world is 32 logical px to the tile.** The view is **640x360 logical** (20 x 11.25 tiles; PS4
  showed 20 x 14), the camera is centred on the leader and snapped to whole logical pixels, and a walker
  is **32x48 logical** — one tile wide, one and a half tall, feet on the tile, head over the tile above.
  Nothing about the world, collision or the maps is in any other unit.
- **The art is 4x that.** An atlas cell is **128 px**, a walker frame **128x192**, a portrait **288 px
  tall**, and the tile field renders straight into an FBO the size of the drawable — 2400x1080 on the
  phone — so a tile comes out as a 96-px quad sampled from its 128-px cell. Those numbers are measured,
  not guessed: `tools/sizes/REPORT.md` put every class beside the size it is actually displayed at, and
  the rule that came out of it is **store at the largest target display size, never below it and not
  far above it**. Walker frames and portraits were each about twice the needed size in each axis; the
  atlas cell was already right, and 64 is visibly mushy. Nothing is snapped to an art-pixel grid —
  ChatGPT's fat pixels are not on a clean lattice, and snapping breaks eyes, mouths and thin ties. Filtering is **linear** both ways (the art is
  minified about 0.75x; nearest would alias it) with no mipmaps, every quad's device-pixel edges are
  rounded so neighbouring tiles share an edge exactly, and every UV is inset half a texel so linear
  never reaches into the next atlas cell.
- **Grid walking.** Four directions. A step is exactly one tile, animated smoothly (~0.16 s a tile walking,
  ~0.10 s running); input is read at the end of each step, so holding a direction glides tile to tile.
  Diagonal input resolves to the most recent axis. Facing can change without moving (tap). Blocked tile =
  turn to face it, no step. Party members follow in a snake, one tile behind each other, PS4 style.
  Interact acts on the tile faced. Step counts drive random encounters later.

## Tilesets — `story/field/tilesets/<set>/`

- `atlas.png` — a grid of tiles, **16 columns**, generated by the tool from cut sheets. Index 0 is
  empty/transparent. `atlas.json` carries `"cell": 128`, the atlas pixels per tile; with no `atlas.json`
  the engine assumes 32 and the placeholder-only path works exactly as before. Alpha is soft and cell
  edges are extruded, because the art is filtered.
- **Walkers** — `story/field/walkers/<id>.png`, **9 frames in a 3x3 grid: rows S, side, N; columns
  stand, step-A, step-B**, each frame **128x192**, so a sheet is **384x576**. The engine **mirrors the
  side row** for the other direction and plays the columns **1, 2, 1, 3**. Beside every sheet is
  `story/field/walkers/<id>.json` — `{rows, cols, frame, sheet, mirror_side}` — and the engine reads
  the layout from there rather than assuming a grid, because a character marked `- asymmetric: yes` in
  `characters.md` keeps both side rows: **12 frames, rows S W E N, 384x768**, `mirror_side` false.
  `./story_prompt.py walker <Name> [<Name> ...]` draws up to three characters on one sheet (the frames
  would fit six; the cap is three because each one needs its reference sheet attached and past three
  attachments ChatGPT starts blending the designs). `./story_prompt.py walker compact <id>` converts an
  old 16-frame sheet, archives it under `story/sheets/walker16-<id>.png`, and reports the Oklab dE
  between the drawn E row and a mirror of the W row so an asymmetric design is caught, not flipped.
  The party's ids are the story's names: `falke.png` is the leader, `ottilie.png` the follower.
- `tiles.md` — one `## <name>` per tile or stamp: `- index: n` (or `- index: n WxH` for a multi-tile
  **stamp**, row-major from its top-left tile), `- layer: ground|object|over`, `- solid: yes|no|<rows>`
  (for a stamp: which of its tiles block, as rows of `#`/`.`), `- desc:` the one-line artist description the
  art pipeline uses. Houses, trees, wells, carts, fences are **stamps** built from tiles; the top rows of a
  tall stamp (roof ridge, tree crown) are `layer: over` so a walker passes behind them.
- **What looks solid is solid, and nothing solid is small** (owner, 2026-09-19). A building is **at
  least 2x2 tiles** in nearly every case (the rare exception is a deliberate hut or kiosk), and a
  building's art **fills its footprint edge to edge** — walls to the left and right edges of its tiles,
  the front wall down to the bottom edge — so there is no strip of grass inside a solid tile that looks
  walkable and is not. The same goes for any solid stamp (well, cart, woodpile, stall): draw it to fill
  its tiles, or make it smaller in tiles. Roof rows that are `over` are the only part of a building the
  walker can overlap. The tileset prompt states this per slot, and `tmap check` warns when a solid
  stamp's art leaves more than ~15% of a solid tile transparent.
- **Edges are computed, not drawn.** See "Terrains, masks and decals" below: the three fringe tiles a
  terrain used to need are gone, and so is every problem they had.

## Maps — `story/field/tmaps/<map>.tmap`

Plain text, agents and humans edit it by hand.

```
## meta
name: halm
tileset: valley
size: 48 36
spawn: 24 20 S
music: hope
## legend            # one char → tile or stamp name from tiles.md
. grass
, dirt
# paving
~ water
H house_a           # a stamp: the char marks its TOP-LEFT tile; the rest of its footprint is written as '+'
## ground            # size rows, one char per tile (ground layer only)
## objects           # size rows, '.' = nothing; stamps by top-left char, '+' for the rest of the footprint
## triggers          # x y w h kind arg — kinds: exit <map> <x> <y> <facing> | door <map> <x> <y> <facing>
                     #   | message <text-id> | npc <walker> <facing> <text-id> [wander r] | zone <table> | trap <id>
```

Collision comes from the tiles (`solid`), plus NPCs. Doors are triggers on the tile in front of a door
tile. Examine text and NPC lines stay in `story/field/text.md` by id (interact press, "!" above the player
when the faced tile has something). The map design rules from `FIELD.md` still hold inside the grid: towns
grow round a reason, lanes bend and pinch (in tile steps), nothing in rows, ragged edges, paths 3 tiles
wide, one landmark; grids and symmetry are for the ancients.

## Art — ChatGPT tile sheets through the template pipeline

`./story_prompt.py tileset <set>` reads `tiles.md`, lays every tile and stamp out on template sheets (flat
magenta, white-bordered numbered slots, each slot an exact multiple of the tile: **4x, so a tile is a
128-px slot and a 4x3 house is 512x384**), writes the prompt with the locked map style blocks, and `ingest`
cuts the return **at that size — no downsampling** — keys magenta on `object`/`over` tiles, extrudes each
cell's edge, and packs `atlas.png` with its `atlas.json`. Ground tiles are asked for seamless. A set is a
few sheets: ground+fringes, buildings, nature, furniture. Until a sheet exists the engine draws a
procedural placeholder for that tile, so maps are walkable before art.

## Terrains, masks and decals (TILES2, D20)

Owner approved 2026-09-19 from `TILES2_PROPOSAL.md`, which carries the research and the sources.
**Drawn transition tiles are gone.** A terrain is one seamless swatch sampled in world space, and the
boundary where two terrains meet is a 1-bit mask the tool *computes*. ChatGPT went from four images a
terrain that had to match each other pixel for pixel, to **one**, and the per-terrain art risk to zero.

### The dual grid

Terrain ids stay on the **world cells** — one per cell, exactly as `## ground` writes them — which is
what keeps collision and grid walking untouched. This is a rendering change and nothing else. The
**display grid is offset half a tile** in both axes: display cell `(i, j)` covers world space
`[i-0.5, j-0.5]..[i+0.5, j+0.5]`, its four corners are the world cells `(i-1,j-1) NW`, `(i,j-1) NE`,
`(i,j) SE`, `(i-1,j) SW`, and a `W x H` map draws `(W+1) x (H+1)` display cells, half-tile-clipped at
the border.

For each terrain in **priority order, low first**, the four corners give four bits — *is this corner
my terrain* — and the 16 patterns are **five shapes and a rotation**. Bits: 1 NW, 2 NE, 4 SE, 8 SW;
rotation is clockwise 90-degree steps.

| bits | corners | shape | rot | | bits | corners | shape | rot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | — | *nothing drawn* | — | | 5 | NW+SE | `diag` | 0 |
| 1 | NW | `corner` | 0 | | 10 | NE+SW | `diag` | 1 |
| 2 | NE | `corner` | 1 | | 11 | NW+NE+SW | `inv` | 0 |
| 4 | SE | `corner` | 2 | | 7 | NW+NE+SE | `inv` | 1 |
| 8 | SW | `corner` | 3 | | 14 | NE+SE+SW | `inv` | 2 |
| 3 | NW+NE | `edge` | 0 | | 13 | SE+SW+NW | `inv` | 3 |
| 6 | NE+SE | `edge` | 1 | | 15 | all | `full` | 0 |
| 12 | SE+SW | `edge` | 2 | | | | | |
| 9 | SW+NW | `edge` | 3 | | | | | |

The 16 fall into exactly **six classes under rotation** — empty (1), corner (4), edge (4), diag (2),
inv (4), full (1) — and **every class is already closed under reflection**, which is the finding that
answers the owner's "flip and mirror" directly: for *transitions*, rotation alone is sufficient and
mirroring adds nothing. Mirroring earns its keep on decals and on nature stamps instead, and it is
taken there. `masks.json` carries this table, so the engine reads it rather than retyping it.

### The masks are generated, never art

`./story_prompt.py tileset <set>` writes **`story/field/tilesets/<set>/masks.png`** and `masks.json`:
five shapes x three variants x one row per `edge_style` in use, each **128x128** (the atlas cell), as
an indexed PNG on the master palette — index 2 inside the terrain, index 1 outside. Rotation is a UV
swizzle in the shader, so **no mask is ever stored twice**. The variant is `hash(i, j, terrain) % 3`,
which is what stops a long straight bank repeating its own wobble.

Each shape is a signed distance field over the unit display tile:

```
full    d = +1
edge    d = 0.5 - y                                  (terrain in the north half)
corner  d = 0.5 - |(x, y)|                           (quarter disc at NW, radius 0.5)
diag    d = max(0.5 - |(x,y)|, 0.5 - |(x-1,y-1)|)    (two quarter discs, TOUCHING at the centre)
inv     d = |(x-1, y-1)| - 0.5                       (everything but a quarter-disc bite at SE)
```

**Radius 0.5 is not a taste decision**, and this is the one line in this file that must not change:

> **The boundary of a mask crosses each tile edge at the midpoint of that edge, perpendicular, and
> nowhere else.**

That is the invariant that lets any shape meet any other shape, in any rotation, in any variant, with
no break. The raggedness is a displacement of `d` by two octaves of value noise, **windowed to zero
within 0.16 of the tile border**, so the pinned midpoints survive whatever the noise does. The
amplitudes come from the terrain's `- edge_style:`:

| `edge_style` | octave 1 amp/freq | octave 2 | for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ragged` | 0.105 / 5.5 | 0.045 / 13 | grass, dirt, crop, mud |
| `smooth` | 0.022 / 3 | 0.010 / 7 | paving, plank, any laid floor |
| `bank` | 0.060 / 3.5 | 0.022 / 9 | water — a **hard bank**, the owner's Phantasy Star call |

The **saddle** (bits 5 and 10) is genuinely ambiguous and the convention is fixed here: `diag`'s two
quarter discs **touch at the centre**, so diagonal terrain reads as connected. A pinch reads as a
path; a gap reads as an accident. It is also the case today's fringe system cannot express at all.

A terrain may declare **`- border: sand 0.085`** — a band drawn in the *outside* of the mask,
`0 > d > -0.085`, in its own pass before the terrain. It is **off by default**: the owner chose a hard
bank for water. No extra art either way.

### Swatches

One per terrain, **`- swatch: WxH` tiles** (2 to 4 a side) at 128 px a tile, in
`story/field/tilesets/<set>/swatches/<id>.png`, sampled in **world space** (`uv = world_pixel mod
swatch_size`) so the drawing is not cut at cell boundaries and there is nothing to match between
neighbouring cells. The cut does three things in this order and every one of them matters:

1. **Flatten** the drawing's own low-frequency lighting, with a **wrapped** box blur. A swatch with a
   bright side or a grassy rim bands wherever it repeats, and the model draws one whatever the prompt
   says. The blur must wrap: a clamped one leaves a bright rim, and the seam roll then puts that rim
   through the middle of the tile, where it reads as a pale cross at every repeat.
2. **Make it seamless** by rolling it half a tile — so its outer edges were adjacent lines of the
   drawing and the wrap is continuous by construction — and hiding the resulting cross with a
   **minimum-error cut**, never a cross-fade. A cross-fade reads as a pale ribbon at every repeat.
3. **Palettise** (PALETTE.md): exact nearest in Oklab, no dithering, hard alpha.

The cut also **measures the swatch's contrast** and says so: a busy swatch fights the decals and
shows its own repeat, so `calm` is checked rather than hoped for. The prompt asks for "a piece cut
from the middle of an endless field": flat, no border, no lit side, no vignette, no object, 3 or 4
flat tones close together.

### Decals

Small cut-outs scattered by `hash(cell_x, cell_y, id)` — sub-tile jitter, one of three sizes, a
mirror flag, and a **low-frequency density field** (period `- cluster:` tiles) so they arrive in
drifts and patches rather than at an even rate. `- on:` lists the terrains one may sit on,
`- edge_bias: water 2.0` puts more of them where a cell touches that terrain (reeds at the water,
pebbles at a path). They straddle cell boundaries freely, never collide, and draw under the walkers.
A decal is **an object on nothing**: no ground patch, no shadow, no base plate. One sheet a biome,
about twenty slots, is the entire variety budget, and it is what `grass_tuft` and `grass_flower` used
to be — without a tile-shaped repeat.

**A decal lives in the atlas**, like every other sprite: one cell each, its art in the cell's
**top-left corner**, its real size in pixels under `px` in `atlas.json` along with its `on`,
`density`, `cluster`, `edge_bias`, `sizes` and `flip`. It gets an `- index:` from the tool exactly as
a tile does. One texture, one batch — a second texture of loose files would be a second draw call for
the smallest thing on screen. `story/field/tilesets/<set>/decals/<id>.png` is still written beside
the atlas, but that copy is for the preview and for looking at one; the game reads the atlas.
A **terrain**, by contrast, has no atlas cell at all: it is a swatch, and `atlas.json` lists it under
`terrains` with its priority, edge style, swatch size, border, drift and cycle.

### `tiles.md` and `.tmap`

`tiles.md` gains `- kind: terrain|decal` (an entry with no `- kind:` is a tile or stamp, as before)
and the keys listed at the top of `story/field/tilesets/valley/tiles.md`. Two more, optional, on any
entry, and both are carried into `atlas.json`: **`- pass: NESW`** — which sides of a tile may be walked *through*, as the letters that are
open (a counter you talk across, a one-way ledge); and **`- tag: <word>`** — what the entry is to the
game (counter, bush, damage). Animated tiles stay `- frames: n`; water now uses `- cycle: water`
instead, which hands it to the palette cycle in `story/palette/cycles.md` and costs no atlas cells.

`.tmap` gains two **optional line-list sections**, chosen over a parallel grid because neither is
dense enough to be worth keeping aligned:

```
## decals            # x y <id> [flip] — a decal placed by hand where the scatter cannot be trusted
## flips             # x y            — the top-left cell of a stamp placement drawn MIRRORED
```

`## ground`, `## objects`, `## legend`, `## meta` and `## triggers` are unchanged, and a map with
neither new section reads exactly as it did. **`## flips` is for nature only**: an entry has to carry
`- flip: h` to be mirrored at all, and no building has it — every roof and wall face in the set is
lit from the upper left, so a mirrored building is lit from the wrong side. `tmap check` enforces it.

`./story_prompt.py tmap preview <map>` renders a map the v2 way on the Mac — masks, swatches, the
same hash scatter — so a map and the masks can be judged before the engine lands. A terrain with no
swatch yet draws as a flat colour, which is still the right *shape*.
