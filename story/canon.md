## 1. The Tally — the fifteen questions (frozen)

The Order of the Late Hour's liturgy, and the strand-integrity check the builders of the Seam wrote.
Frozen by DECISIONS D8.1 using Act V's text. **Quote these verbatim and in order. Do not paraphrase,
reorder, renumber, or invent a sixteenth.** Also now in `story/bible.md` section 5.

1. Do you know where you are?
2. Do you know who is holding your hand?
3. What is your name?
4. What name did your mother use?
5. What did you eat last?
6. What was the weather?
7. What happened the day before this one?
8. What did you do that you would do again?
9. Who is owed something?
10. Who owes you?
11. What is unfinished?
12. What should be told, and who to?
13. Is there anything you would rather nobody knew?
14. Is there anything you want said out loud now, while there are people?
15. What do you want done?

**Why it is shaped this way.** It runs locate → identify → verify continuity → verify completeness →
verify dependencies → declare disposition. **Question 7 is the edges test** of hard rule 5, which is
why the archivists' trick works — they inherited it without knowing. **Question 13 the sister never
follows up**: she asks whether there is such a thing, never what it is. **Question 15 is the only one
that cannot be answered from a record**, which is why the maintenance manual leaves it blank with the
instruction *ask the holder*, why Lyra could not finish it on herself, and why Bron asking it of Maren
is the turn of Act V rather than a trick.

## 2. Rulings

### From DECISIONS D8, restated as plain facts you must obey

1. **The fifteen questions above are the only ones.** They are heard whole in ch.1, seen on a wall in
   ch.5, read off a manual in ch.9, left unfinished in ch.11, and used to hold Maren together in ch.15.
2. **Maren holds nothing of Bron's own past.** She holds four hundred and ten other lives and her own —
   four hundred and eleven records. The warm winter in Bron's head is **her** ninth childhood winter,
   drawn out of her in 1093 and set into him to give stock template 4 some specific detail. He was in
   the radius but standing over the open shaft, not at the bell. **There is no copy of his childhood
   anywhere and there never was.** He is the one victim who can never be restored.
3. **Anneke Brae is Kell.** Four foot eleven, the same height as her son, and neither of them knows.
   Iron-grey hair that will not lie flat, amber eyes, bare arms in a hot room, complains about steam.
   Bron was born to her in Tellwater in 1084. Neve Ardo, who was *posted to play* Hesta Sallow, is
   human — which is why the childhood Bron was issued has a Kell boy raised by a human widow, and why
   he believes his mother was human.
4. **Scene ids are `CCSS` with any two-digit order number.** Steps of five are a convention, not a
   rule; a chapter may hold more than twenty scenes. Ids must stay inside their own chapter block.
5. **Ket-Ossun's lights have one ledger** and it is section 3 of this file. No act spends one that is
   not listed there. No writer ever lights one back up.
6. **Side contracts** invented for chapters 1, 3, 6, 11 and 13 are accepted. **"Bad water at Kettle"
   is posted in chapter 2 at `0240` and can only be cleared in chapter 6 at `0607`.**
7. **At most one optional PANEL scene per chapter.** Currently satisfied: `0487_domma_sath` (ch.4),
   `0555_company_mortar` (ch.5), `0840_the_door` (ch.8). Any further optional panel becomes a TALK
   scene.
8. **Canon on the mechanism.** A stored pattern has to be **rehearsed** to stay coherent — that is why
   the Seam sings, why Maren is crowded, and why giving a record to a Vess chant-line empties her:
   once the line rehearses it she no longer has to, and nothing is copied. **"Taking the hour" is not
   a device**: it is being present, asking the Tally in order, and carrying it afterwards. No frame,
   no strand, no glass, no light, no transfer.
9. **Act I's existing scenes:** `p01` revised (the party is never split — the fourth Line is *bring
   them back*), `001` replaced under the same file name, `002` revised (**"old magic" is cut**; the
   word is banned and the guards are a procedure in armour), `003` and `003b` kept unchanged. Zeph and
   Pip are twenty feet up the corridor during `003`; the camera is simply not on them.

### Distances and travel times the outlines rely on

- The Basin is **four hundred miles across**. The Sallow Basin is the floor of a dried inland sea.
- **Sallowgate ↔ Windrow ↔ Tellwater**, Act I, on foot on the Rim road: a day or two between each.
- **The terrace stair**: four hundred feet of cut steps from the Rim Hills to the seabed, then **three
  days walking west beside the laid rail** to meet a sledge (`0405`).
- **Braid ↔ Saltmouth**: ninety miles. **Ferry-rail** crossings are measured in days, with the sledges
  stopping overnight at sidings.
- **Ninefold Terrace**: eleven hundred feet of cut steps up from the terrace landing. Nine door-mouths
  in one cliff face.
- **Saltmouth crossing from the hill paths**: two days across the crust, passing Kettle.
- **Kettle** is four days across the salt from Windrow, which is why the ch.2 bill cannot be cleared
  before ch.6.
- **Emberrow** is two days south of Vantage down the ravine road.
- **The Pale Flats**: Ostry Bar to the Ilder stump is forty miles by crawler; the ch.9 cordon is eleven
  miles wide.
- **The company road up to Vantage**: three days from the Flats. Six tiers in a quarry face.
- **The maintenance rail** (laid 1071) links every hall-mouth under the Rim Hills, eleven stations
  named after doors. It is the fast travel and it opens the whole map at once in ch.13.
- **The Deep Stair**: a mile of cut stair from the Ninth Door down to Thurn. Three hundred and forty
  people are walked up it by hand, one at a time, over seven months.
- **Bore One at Tellwater**: 340 feet deep, radius of inversion 1,100 feet. The relay hut is two miles
  out. Teach carried Bron four hundred yards.
- **Vintry** is a three-hundred-foot tower, dark since year 944 — a hundred and sixty-three years.
