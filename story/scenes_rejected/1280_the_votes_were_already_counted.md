# Scene 1280: The Votes Were Already Counted

- location: a long panelled room with a single oval table and twelve chairs, a brass water carafe and glasses, a fireplace with a low fire at the left, tall shuttered windows on the right, evening lamplight
- characters: Kerrow, Crewe
- mood: confront, then dread
- staging: Crewe stands at the head of the table on the left of frame and faces right down it. Kerrow stands at her place beside him, further right, facing right down the table. The board sits along both sides; the party stands at the far right end

## Beat
Continuous. Crewe is three names into the list when Kerrow stands, puts one hand flat on the table,
and relieves him of the chair in the middle of the sentence, citing the instrument, the notice
period and the count, which she has had since the morning and which is not close. It is legal. It
is quick. Two directors sign something and pass it along. Crewe stops, agrees that the ruling is
correct, sits down, and the board moves to the next item. Kerrow is not triumphant. She has the
face of a woman who has been holding a door shut with her shoulder for nine years and has just been
allowed to let go, and the party can see it, and nobody in the party gets to object usefully.

## Dialogue
- Kerrow [1] {confront}: Chairman. Stop there.
- Kerrow [1]: Article nine. Notice was served on the sixth. I have the count and it is eight to three.
- Crewe [2]: ...Yes. I think that's right.
- Director [2]: At the foot, and again at the margin. Both copies, chairman.
- Kerrow [3]: The chair is vacated at the end of this item. Everything said under it stands in the minute.
- Crewe [4]: I hadn't finished the list.
- Kerrow [4]: It goes in whole. There is nothing further to be done with it today.
- Director [5] {dread}: Next item. Tier One ventilation, and then the returns for Dust.
