# Scene 1285: Every Large Bore Inverts

- type: talk
- location: a wide unheated corridor of pale cut stone outside the board floor, a tall shuttered window at the far end with the cold coming off it, a bench against the wall, hanging lamps in a line, night
- characters: Kerrow, Bron, Zeph, Hesk, Ket
- mood: confront

## Beat
The new chairman has ten minutes and spends them in the coldest corridor on the tier. She does not
lie; she declines to answer, and here she declines nothing. She tells them herself, unpressed: what
she took at six in the morning on the tenth of Fallow 1093, how old she was, what she completed in
1099 and what she did with it, and what the arithmetic actually says. Then she tells them to publish
it, offers to have it copied for them, and tells them in numbers what happens to the Basin in the
winter after they do. Bron asks a factual question. Hesk objects about the hills and is answered
with a number. Ket confirms one figure, which makes it worse. Zeph says nothing at all. Nobody
refutes her, because there is nothing to refute her with. She stands with her back to the window
the whole time and she is shaking very slightly, and it is not nerves, and nobody says so.

## Dialogue
- Kerrow {confront}: Ten minutes, and you can have them out here in the cold. Ask me, or I'll simply tell you.
- Kerrow: I took the message at six on the morning of the tenth of Fallow, ninety-three. I was twenty-one. I woke the Director.
- Kerrow: In ninety-nine I finished a study. Extraction, ninety-nine, twenty-six. I put it in a drawer and left it there.
- Kerrow: Drawing thins a branch. A thin branch inverts. Every large bore inverts in the end. There's no engineering in it.
- Kerrow: The safe way is small bores. Several hundred of them. It costs about four times what this company is worth.
- Bron: How many people have read it.
- Kerrow: Two. I wrote it; a draughtsman set the tables and he died in eleven oh two. I'll have it copied for you.
- Kerrow: Publish it if you like. Then count. Forty-one thousand roofs under Ossun; nine wells standing; two running.
- Ket: Two running. Correct.
- Hesk: Then take your bores out of my hills.
- Kerrow: The branch is under the hills. That's the whole of the geography. There is nowhere else to take them.
- Kerrow: Fifty-one heads under the Rim. Take them out and the Ossun head is cold inside nineteen months. I costed that too.
- Kerrow: You may still be right to publish. I'm not telling you you're wrong. I'm giving you the number.
- Bron: ...
