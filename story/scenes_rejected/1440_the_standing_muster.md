# Scene 1440: The Standing Muster

- location: a broad stone landing before double stone doors at the back right, stone weapon racks along the left wall, a shallow step worn hollow, lamp columns on both sides lighting from above, warm even light, no daylight
- characters: Sefa, Hesk, Zeph, Pip
- mood: dread, then tense
- staging: The party stands at the near left edge of the landing and faces right. The thirty ranked guards and the council doors fill the right and back of every panel, facing left. Nobody on either side moves

## Beat
Thirty suits of old segmented armour stand in three correct ranks across the landing, halberds at the
proper angle, two small steady blue points in each helm, and they have been doing it correctly for
one thousand one hundred and seven years. They do not advance and they must never be drawn lunging:
they are a procedure wearing a suit, doing its job exactly, and that is the horror. No skulls, no
bones, no faces — the armour is empty. Sefa opens her case and produces the Registry transcript with
the air of a woman who has waited a long time to be useful, and reads it out low to the party at the
near edge, flat, the way a Registrar reads a document, and nothing happens, because a handover is not
a reading. Hesk hears the whole of it for the first time and says out loud that what her family
passed down is a mangled quarter. Zeph takes a breath.

## Dialogue
- Hesk [1] {dread}: Three ranks. Ten to a rank. They've stood like that since the year the doors shut.
- Pip [2]: He's worn a hollow in the flagstone. Just standing on it.
- Pip [3]: ...There's nothing in the helmet. I've looked. There's nothing in there at all.
- Sefa [4]: Fourteen years a Registrar, and at last a door that wants a document.
- Sefa [4]: The next watch is at the post. The word is carried. The count is kept.
- Sefa [4]: The post is relieved. The watch is spoken for. Stand aside and count them by.
- Hesk [5]: ...My grandmother had the last of that. A quarter of it, and two words wrong.
- Zeph [6] {tense}: It is not a paper. It is a part. Somebody has to take it and sing it at them.
