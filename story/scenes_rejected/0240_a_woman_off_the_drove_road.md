# Scene 0240: A Woman Off the Drove Road

- type: talk
- location: a gravel yard of laid rail and turnouts, a squat brick engine shed with open doors, a water crane and a coal bunker, cinders underfoot, grey afternoon light from the right, wet ground
- characters: Nessa, Bron, Pip
- mood: brisk, furious, fair

## Beat
An old woman has walked three days off the drove road and then paid tram fare she cannot afford,
because there is no Even Hand hall nearer than Sallowgate and nobody has ever come out to Kettle.
Her cistern is fouled. Her bill is properly sealed and she says so before she says anything else,
and she will pay on completion out of her own purse. Then she explains, accurately and at length,
what the metered rate does to eleven people on one cistern, and there is nothing either the party or
the player can do about it. And they cannot reach Kettle: it is four days across the salt and there
is no crossing out of Windrow. She knows. She says it herself. She will leave the bill up.

## Dialogue
- Nessa {tense}: Sealed. Look at it. My seal, my bill, and I pay on completion out of my own purse.
- Nessa: I've said that first because I'm told hunters like it said first.
- Bron: What's the work?
- Nessa: Cistern's fouled. Kettle. Three buildings, eleven people, one cistern.
- Pip: Where's Kettle?
- Nessa: Four days across the salt, and there's no crossing out of this town. I know. I walked round.
- Nessa: Three days on the drove road, then tram fare I can't afford, because nobody comes out to us.
- Pip: We can't get there. Not from here. There's no road that goes.
- Nessa: I know that as well.
- Nessa: And since I've paid to be stood here, you'll hear what the rate does to eleven people.
- Nessa: Fourpence the gallon at the box. The box is theirs. The water fell out of the sky onto my roof.
- Bron: We'll take it.
- Nessa: How long does a bill stay good for?
- Bron: Till it's cleared.
- Nessa {hope}: Then I'll leave it up. I've nowhere else to put it.
