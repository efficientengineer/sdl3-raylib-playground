# Scene 0880: The Doorway

- location: a narrow stone housefront on a steep street, a scrubbed doorstep, a chair set out beside the door, a tanner's leather apron on a hook in the jamb, warm lamplight from the open door, night
- characters: Dalla, Ross
- mood: sorrow
- staging: The Kettle doorway is at the right of frame, lit from inside. Ross is out in the street at the left with his back to it, facing left and away down the hill. The party stands further left again and stays off panel

## Beat
Minutes later. Dalla Kettle comes to her own door with a folded cloth in her hands because of the
noise in the street, and her husband of nine years turns, looks straight at her, and goes on talking
about a woman with another name. He is not cruel and he is not raving. He simply does not know she
is there. She says one ordinary thing to him about his tea, which he does not hear, and then one
ordinary thing to the party, which is the worst thing anybody says in Act III and is said politely,
and nobody answers it. Then she goes back in and leaves the door standing open, because it is cold
out and he will come in eventually, and she knows that about him if she knows nothing else tonight.
Maren is not in this scene. She is still down the steps and the camera does not go and look at her.
