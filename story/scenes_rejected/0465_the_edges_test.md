# Scene 0465: The Edges Test

- type: talk
- location: a row of open-fronted sheds of stone and plank on the seabed floor, rail sledges on laid track inside them, furled sails on spars, coils of hand-crank chain, hard flat afternoon light, salt glare beyond
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip
- mood: hope -> dread

## Beat
A Vess ferry-rail hand is sitting on a crate outside the shed eating, and Zeph bets Pip a coin he can tell her something true
about him in three questions. He asks about the afternoon at the autumn fair the man has been telling
everybody about, and the man tells it happily, at length, with the dog and the almonds and his
brother's eleven marks. Then Zeph asks what he did the day before. The man is cheerful, unbothered,
and has nothing at all — not distressed, not troubled, nothing — and he says so and laughs. Then he
volunteers where the afternoon came from, because it is not a secret and he is rather proud of it.
Zeph explains the edges test the way a man explains a card trick, and collects his coin. Lyra does
not laugh. She asks the man his name, and he gives it instantly, and walks off whistling.

## Dialogue
- Zeph {hope}: A coin says I can tell you a true thing about that man in three questions.
- Pip: He's eating. Easiest coin I ever made.
- Zeph: The fair. You had a fine afternoon at the autumn fair and this whole shed has heard about it. Tell me.
- Rail hand: Best afternoon of my life. Sun on the salt, the pole race, hot almonds, my brother losing eleven marks on a dog.
- Zeph: Good. And the day before that one?
- Rail hand: The day before. ...Nothing. I have nothing at all. Isn't that funny.
- Rail hand: Year-end, that one was. They give us one each instead of the rise. All different. Mine was the fair.
- Pip: That's it? That's the whole trick?
- Zeph: That's the whole trick. Nobody builds the day before. Why would you.
- Zeph: The archivists called it the edges. Coin.
- Bron: He isn't lying.
- Zeph {dread}: No. He is not.
- Lyra: What's your name?
- Rail hand: Oren Ander. Why?
