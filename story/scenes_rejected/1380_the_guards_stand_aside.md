# Scene 1380: The Guards Stand Aside

- location: a long dressed-stone gallery with a coffered stone ceiling, shallow pilasters at regular intervals, a dry stone channel running along the floor, an empty lamp niche at head height, one carried lamp from the left, no daylight
- characters: Maren
- mood: dread, then wonder
- staging: The two great doors and the two hall-guards are at the far right of frame, facing left down the gallery. Maren comes up the gallery from the left and faces right. Her lamp is the only light and it travels with her

## Beat
No party member is present. The player watches. A tired woman in a grey travelling coat, with a
satchel and bare ungloved hands, walks up a thousand-year-old gallery toward a pair of great doors
held by two suits of armour with nothing at all inside them. They do not advance and they do not
raise a weapon: they lower their halberds across the gap and give her the warning, which is all
their oath allows them to say. She stops. She waits, politely, the way you wait at somebody's door. Then she
speaks — thirty words, in a voice that is not hers, because she is carrying the hour of a man who
said them four times a day for years, and her accent moves twice inside them. The halberds lift.
The doors part a hand's width. She says one thing in her own voice, which is an apology, and goes
through, and the armour closes and re-forms behind her. Nothing is broken. Nothing is forced. No
music sting when the halberds come up.
