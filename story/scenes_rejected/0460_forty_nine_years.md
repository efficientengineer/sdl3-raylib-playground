# Scene 0460: Forty-Nine Years

- type: talk
- location: worn stone steps up to a chant-hall side door, an iron handrail bolted into the wall, a stove pipe venting above, the seabed and the rails going out below, cold light from the left, evening
- characters: Tovin, Ket, Bron
- mood: sorrow

## Beat
Tovin-Caleth is coming down the hall steps. Ket-Ossun is coming up them. They have not spoken since
the year the Caleth well died and one of them took a bill instead. Neither of them stops walking for
longer than this takes. Nothing is embraced, explained, raised, or forgiven; one gives a figure, the
other corrects it by a year, the correction is accepted, and then one of them says the only thing
either of them actually came to say, which is not about the service. Then Tovin goes on down the
steps. Ket stays facing the empty steps for one beat longer than she needs to, and then turns round
and asks Bron something practical, and Bron does not answer.

## Dialogue
- Ket {sorrow}: Forty-nine years.
- Tovin: Forty-eight. You are counting the year I was still on the hill.
- Ket: Forty-eight.
- Tovin: You are at five. Do not spend one on this.
- Ket: How much do the four of you weigh?
- Ket: Not now. Have it by the sixth. I will want it in pounds.
