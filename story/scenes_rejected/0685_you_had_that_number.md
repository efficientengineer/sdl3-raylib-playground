# Scene 0685: You Had That Number

- type: talk
- location: a long canvas relief tent open along one side on white salt, trestle tables in two rows under it with enamel bowls stacked, a tea urn on a crate, hard flat afternoon light from overhead, salt glare
- characters: Lyra, Kerrow, Bron
- backdrop: 0680_the_count:1
- mood: tense, then dread

## Beat
Lyra has sat two hundred deathbeds and is extremely hard to move along, and she does not move along.
She asks how far it went. Kerrow gives her the radius and the number without hesitating and without
consulting the board in her hand, which is the whole scene. Lyra says the thing out loud. Kerrow says
yes. She does not defend it, soften it, explain how she knew, or pretend she did not say it — and
when Lyra asks the second question she declines to answer rather than lying, and says that is what she
is doing. Bron says nothing until the end. The last thing Kerrow offers is not a trap; it is an
invitation, courteously made, to go and look at the forty-one, and it is much worse than a trap.

## Dialogue
- Lyra {tense}: How far did it go?
- Kerrow: Eleven hundred feet. Forty-one people.
- Lyra: You had that number before you counted.
- Kerrow: Yes.
- Lyra: ...
- Lyra: How did you have it?
- Kerrow: I'm not going to answer that. I'm not saying I don't know. I'm declining.
- Lyra: You could have told me you didn't know.
- Kerrow: I could have. I don't do that.
- Lyra: No. I can see that you don't.
- Kerrow {dread}: The care-house has the forty-one. They're clean, they're warm, and they've been fed.
- Kerrow: You're welcome to look in on them. Most people won't. I would rather somebody did.
- Bron: Why?
- Kerrow: Because they're pleasant company and nobody is going to visit them twice. Next.
