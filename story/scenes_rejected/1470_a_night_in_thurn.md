# Scene 1470: A Night in Thurn

- type: talk
- location: a wide swept street of fitted stone flags between stone housefronts with square doorways, tall lamp columns at intervals, a stone water trough running with clear water, warm steady light from the columns above head height
- characters: Bron, Ollo
- mood: sorrow, then hope
- backdrop: 1415_the_lit_street:1

## Beat
Two men who cannot sleep because the lamps do not go out. Bron finally asks the question he has been
carrying since a payroll page in chapter six: what Neve Ardo was actually like — not as his mother, as
somebody Ollo worked beside. What Ollo has is small, specific, useless, and therefore worth more than
anything in a file: she was bad at bread and knew it, she was cold all the time, she kept a notebook
of things a nine-year-old had said so she would have them right the next time, and in the last week
of the posting she cried in the flour store and told everybody it was the dust. The man who was
pretended at and the man who was pretending, and neither of them blames the other. Ollo does not
apologise on her behalf. Bron does not say what any of it means to him; he asks whether the notebook
still exists, and it does not.

## Dialogue
- Bron {sorrow}: You were there a year with her. Before the posting closed.
- Ollo: Fourteen months. She came in at the end of ninety-five.
- Bron: What was she like. Not — not as that. As somebody you worked next to.
- Ollo: Bad at bread.
- Ollo: Genuinely. Terrible. And she knew she was, which is rarer than being good at it.
- Ollo: Cold all the time. Sat on the oven bricks with her boots off in the middle of High.
- Bron: ...
- Ollo: She kept a notebook. Little brown thing, went in the apron.
- Ollo: Things a boy had said to her. So she'd have them right the next time.
- Bron: Right for what.
- Ollo: So she'd say them back the same way round. She said children notice.
- Ollo: Last week of the posting she was crying in the flour store and told everyone it was the dust.
- Ollo: There's no dust in a flour store. There's flour.
- Bron: ...Have you still got it. The notebook.
- Ollo: No. I'm sorry. No.
- Ollo {hope}: I signed a paper. That's all I did. Everybody keeps looking at me like I signed a different one.
