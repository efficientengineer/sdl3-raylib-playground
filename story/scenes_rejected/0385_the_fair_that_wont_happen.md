# Scene 0385: The Fair That Won't Happen

- type: talk
- location: a flat grassless field at the town edge, four bare timber fair frames standing in a row, trodden circles of ground, a coil of bunting rope on a post, low evening sun from the right, long shadows
- characters: Ollo, Bron, Pip, Lyra
- mood: hope, then sorrow

## Beat
Side contract, scene three of three. Payment on completion, in bread, in an absurd quantity,
already wrapped before they got there. Ollo has heard they are leaving and is sorry about it and
does not ask why, because it is not his business and he would not know what to do with the answer
if he had it. He tells Bron the fair is in nine days and that he would be welcome, and says it the
way you say it to a neighbour. And he says one thing that sits very badly and he has no idea that
it does, because he is complaining cheerfully about being bad at his own job. Last image: a man in
a green apron standing under bunting in a field with nobody in it, waving.

## Dialogue
- Ollo {hope}: Here. Take it. It's already wrapped, so there's no arguing with it.
- Bron: That's nine loaves.
- Ollo: It's eleven. Pay on completion. That's the completion.
- Pip: Eleven loaves. For four people.
- Ollo: You'll be glad of the fourth and embarrassed by the eleventh. That's how bread works.
- Ollo: I hear you're off. I'll not ask.
- Ollo: It isn't my business and I'd not know what to do with the answer if you gave me one.
- Lyra: ...
- Ollo: Fair's in nine days. You'd be welcome. Walk in like a neighbour, that's all it is.
- Ollo: I've got the frames wrong, mind. Denner never got the frames wrong.
- Ollo {sorrow}: Eleven years, and I still put them up like a man who arrived on Tuesday.
- Ollo: You don't act for eleven years, though, do you. After about four you're just there.
- Bron: ...
- Pip: Save us the burnt ones.
- Ollo: There aren't any burnt ones.
- Pip: Then burn some.
