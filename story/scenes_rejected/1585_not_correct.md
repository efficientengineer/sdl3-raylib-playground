# Scene 1585: Not Correct

- type: talk
- location: the sunken central disc of a stone chamber, a ring of brass sockets set flush in the floor, a broken gimbal frame canted over one of them, dust motionless in the air, faint light from the wall behind
- characters: Ket, Hesk, Bron, Ollo, Sefa
- mood: tense, then sorrow, then hope
- backdrop: 1505_the_seam_face:2

## Beat
Ket squats down beside a thousand-year-old draw head with the recovered specification open on her
knee, because the head is a machine and a machine has a shutdown sheet. The sheet is fifteen lines
long and she reads it out where everyone can hear it, which takes about forty seconds. Thirteen
lines come back correct. One does not, and cannot be made to, and has not been able to for
centuries, and she says so once and does not dress it up and nobody asks her again. The fifteenth
line on the sheet is blank, with an instruction beside it to ask the holder, and there is nobody
left in the world to ask, so she answers it herself and says out loud what she is answering. The
cost is stated flatly and it is not small. Nobody in Thurn gets anything back — not a past, not a
name, not a generation. They get tomorrow. Then Hesk does the arithmetic on what that means in
labour and answers her own question.

## Dialogue
- Ket {tense}: The head has a shutdown sheet. Fifteen lines. I will read it aloud so that you all hear it.
- Ket: Correct. Correct. Correct. Correct. Correct. Correct.
- Ket: Not correct.
- Hesk: Which one?
- Ket: The seventh. One length of the record is gone. It has been gone a long time. Nobody gets it back.
- Ket: Correct. Correct. Correct. Correct. Correct. Correct. Correct.
- Ket: Fifteen is blank. The sheet says: ask the holder. There is nobody to ask, so I will answer it.
- Ket: It takes a week out of every living thing in this city, every day, and has done for eleven hundred years.
- Ket: Stopped, it takes nothing. Stopped, the lamps go out, the ovens go cold and the water stops.
- Ollo: What happens to Nine?
- Ket: Somebody holds her arm and walks her up a mile of stair. Then somebody does it again, three hundred and thirty-nine times.
- Ket {sorrow}: Months of it. They get nothing back. They get tomorrow, and then the one after that.
- Ket: It is stopped.
- Hesk {hope}: That's a job. Who's doing it?
- Hesk: ...We are.
