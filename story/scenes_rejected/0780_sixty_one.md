# Scene 0780: Sixty-One

- location: a wide table under a hanging lamp with a large plain map unrolled and weighted at the corners, coloured pins in clusters across it, a box of pins and a rule, flat light from directly above, night
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip, Ket, Hesk
- mood: tense, then dread
- staging: The map table stands square under the lamp. Bron stands at the right of the table in every panel; the others read from the left of it. All eyes go down to the map, and then right, to Bron

## Beat
A clerk's table in the middle of the stacks, the roll unwound and weighted at the corners with
whatever was on the shelf. It is the best piece of work anybody in this story has ever done: eleven
towns, four hundred and seven names, a trade against every one, a date, a cost per head, and a
cross-index at the back listing what was issued and to whom. Zeph reads it the way a reader reads,
out loud and far too fast and honestly admiring, and then he hits the cross-index, and he reads four
short clauses off it, and he stops in the middle of a line and does not start again. Nobody
explains anything. Bron does not ask anybody what it means. He puts his finger at the top of the
column and counts it himself, twice, and says the number out loud, and then asks the only other
question he has, which is what she charged them, because that is a fact and a fact is a thing he can
hold.

## Dialogue
- Zeph [1] {tense}: Eleven towns. Four hundred and seven names, a trade against each, a date, and a cost per head.
- Zeph [2]: Lomm forty-one. Emberrow sixty-three. Windrow a hundred and nine. It is beautiful work and I want that said out loud.
- Zeph [2]: And a cross-index at the back. Issued templates, numbered one to nine, and what is put into each of them.
- Zeph [3]: Four. Widow. One son. Name used — Hesta. A kitchen. A well on a square. An autumn fair.
- Zeph [3]: ...
- Bron [4] {dread}: Sixty-one.
- Bron [4]: I've counted it twice. Sixty-one.
- Hesk [5]: Mm.
- Bron [5]: What did she charge us for this.
- Lyra [5]: Twelve marks and four.
