# Scene 0895: A Day and a Half

- type: talk
- location: a small stone kitchen behind a tannery, a black range with a kettle on it, a scrubbed table and four chairs, a cupboard with a man sitting on the floor against it, a lamp on the sill, night
- characters: Dalla, Maren, Bron, Lyra, Pip, Hesk
- mood: sorrow, then hope
- backdrop: 0880_the_doorway:4

## Beat
The following evening. Maren has not left. She has been sitting on the kitchen floor beside Ross
with her back to the same cupboard for a day and a half, which is longer than anybody was ever asked
to sit with her, and she has fetched nothing, fixed nothing, and promised nothing. Dalla Kettle
makes tea for six people she has every reason to hate and hands it round and tells them not to be
polite about it, and it is the most ordinary act in the chapter and nobody in the party knows what
to do with it. Maren tells Bron where she is going without being asked, states plainly that she is
slow and the company is not, and asks him whether they mean to stop her. Bron does not answer. Hesk
answers, and says the hard true thing, and Maren agrees with her and produces the figures, because
she counts. Nobody arrests anybody. Nobody decides anything. That is Bron's failure and the chapter
lets it stand.

## Dialogue
- Dalla {sorrow}: Sit down. Six of you and four chairs, so two of you are on the step.
- Dalla: It's stewed and I'm not making a fresh pot at this hour, so don't any of you be polite about it.
- Dalla: Take it. My hands are full and it's hot.
- Maren: He slept four hours. That's the first. He asked me the name of the street twice and had it right the second time.
- Dalla: He's taken four sugars in that cup. He never took sugar.
- Maren: He will for a while. Then he won't.
- Bron: Where do you go after here?
- Maren: North. Ostry Bar, then out on the salt. There are more of them out there than anywhere else and nobody has ever counted them but me.
- Maren: I walk. They've crawlers and a telegraph. So — do you mean to stop me?
- Bron: ...
- Hesk: You've no idea how many of them come apart. You hand a man fourteen years and you're gone north by morning.
- Maren: Eighty-one, in two years. Twenty-seven of those are in a care house or worse. Two of them I sat with until they died.
- Maren {hope}: I count all of it. It's the only sum I've ever been any good at.
- Dalla: Anybody want more?
