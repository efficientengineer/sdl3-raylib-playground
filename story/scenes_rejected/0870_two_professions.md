# Scene 0870: Two Professions

- type: talk
- location: a low vaulted stone cellar at the foot of a worn stone stair, a lamp hung on a hook at the turn, a drain grate in the floor, warm lamplight from the left, no daylight
- characters: Maren, Lyra
- mood: tense, then sorrow
- backdrop: 0860_the_woman_on_the_crate:8

## Beat
Four lines in the corner of a cellar while a man comes down a stair, and each of them works out what
the other one does for a living. Neither offers the other any comfort. Neither is impressed. They do
not compare their work, they do not mention what it costs, and nothing is said in the abstract at
all; the recognition is entirely in what each of them thinks to ask. The scene ends when Ross
reaches the bottom step and neither of them turns round until he does.

## Dialogue
- Lyra {tense}: Do you sleep?
- Maren: Not in the way you mean it. I lie down. It's crowded.
- Maren: You've sat with people. You've got the face for it. How many?
- Lyra: Two hundred and six. I stay the hour, I ask what has to be asked, and I carry it out with me.
- Maren: And where do you put it down?
- Lyra {sorrow}: I don't. That's the arrangement.
