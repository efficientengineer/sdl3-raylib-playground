# Scene 1210: Two People Who Lost the Same Job

- type: talk
- location: a steep open iron stair bolted to a cut-stone face, a landing with a bench and a lamp post, laundry strung under the flight above, the drop falling away on the left, warm light coming up off the street below
- characters: Hesk, Ollo
- mood: hope
- backdrop: 1205_the_market_shelf:1

## Beat
A doorward whose nine doors were opened without her noticing, and a baker who was a posting,
sitting on a cold step above a warm street. Neither of them is comforting the other. Hesk takes a
person's measure by asking them technical questions and seeing whether the numbers come back, and
his do, and she revises her opinion of him upward and says so in one flat sentence. Then they
compare trades, which is the closest either of them will get to the subject. He offers her food.
It is too hot. Everything is too hot for her, and somebody has finally noticed.

## Dialogue
- Hesk {hope}: What's the flue on a bread oven. Height and bore.
- Ollo: Fourteen foot, nine-inch bore, if it's drawing right. Mine drew badly on the left for eleven years.
- Hesk: That's not your flue. That's your arch, bedded out of square. Somebody built it wrong and never said.
- Ollo: ...I worked that out around year four and never once had it looked at.
- Hesk: Mm. You're not a fool, then. I had you down as one.
- Hesk: Nine doors at Ninefold. Three opened behind me and re-mortared, and I was living over the top of them.
- Ollo: Eleven years of another man's bread, and I never met him.
- Hesk: I was good at mine.
- Ollo: So was I.
- Ollo: There's the heel of yesterday's in my coat. It's better cold than it sounds.
- Hesk: Too hot to eat.
- Ollo: Everything's too hot for you.
- Hesk: Yes. Finally. Somebody's listening.
