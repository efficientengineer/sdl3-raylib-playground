# Scene 1015: Everyone Works For One Of Two Things

- type: talk
- location: a boarding-house front parlour of grey brick and plaster, four mismatched armchairs round a small black stove, a plank table with a tin tray of cups, a net-curtained window, flat overcast daylight from the right, midday
- characters: Orla, Bron, Zeph, Lyra
- mood: warm, then not
- backdrop: 1010_the_wall:1

## Beat
Orla Winch has let them the same three beds she let them in the spring and is charging for the
chair again. Asking about the sanatorium in Windrow turns out to be like asking about the weather:
everybody in the room has a firm opinion, none of the opinions agree, and not one of them is about
the people inside. Nobody here is lying and nobody is hiding anything. They have simply never had
a reason to find out, and they are pleasant, and the place pays on the first. A tram fitter in the
other chair is equally certain and equally wrong. Zeph counts the answers out loud and the room
does not notice him do it.

## Dialogue
- Orla {hope}: Eightpence the bed. The chair by the stove is a penny on top of that. Same as in the spring.
- Zeph: The wall at the top of the town. Who is behind it?
- Orla: The sanatorium. It buys these four laundries their whole winter and it pays on the first.
- Fitter: Nerves. Company men gone over on the nerves. My cousin's wife had a fortnight of it and came back right as rain.
- Orla: It's not nerves, it's the old ones. Families that can't keep them any more. Ask anybody.
- Fitter: Down the yard they'll tell you it's the fever wards. They're wrong as well.
- Zeph: That is three answers and no two of them agree.
- Orla: My sister was nine years in the linen room there. Nine years.
- Zeph: Nine years. And what did she tell you about it?
- Orla: Tell me? It's a linen room. Sheets in, sheets out. There was never anything in it to tell.
- Bron: Does anybody come out?
- Fitter: All the time. They get better and off they go. That's what it's for.
- Orla {dread}: You'll want that chair. Penny.
