# Scene 0215: Three Beds

- type: talk
- location: a narrow upstairs lodging room with three plank beds in a row, a washing line of damp shirts strung across it, a sash window propped open on a broken catch, grey morning light from the left
- characters: Orla, Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip
- mood: domestic, warm, no plot at all

## Beat
Three beds, four hunters, and a landlady who is not running a charity. Pip conducts the whole
negotiation at a speed nobody else can follow and invents a cot that turns out to exist. Zeph does
not fit anything in the room. Lyra offers to take the floor in a way that makes it impossible for
anyone else to take the floor, and knows she is doing it. Bron settles it by falling asleep in a
chair in his coat with the window open, and Orla charges them for three beds and a chair.

## Dialogue
- Orla {hope}: Three beds. There's four of you. I can count and so can you.
- Pip: Three beds, four of us, and one of us is a stick. He'll go anywhere.
- Zeph: Stick. I am six foot four. Not one of those is six foot four.
- Pip: So go diagonal.
- Zeph: Diagonal is a direction. It is not a length.
- Orla: I charge by the bed, not by the body in it. What you do after that is yours.
- Pip: Four and two for the room, and we'll have the cot off the landing.
- Orla: There's no cot on the landing.
- Pip: There's a cot on the landing.
- Orla: ...There's a cot on the landing, and it's four and six.
- Lyra: I'll take the floor. I've slept on worse and I'd rather nobody made an evening of it.
- Pip: And now nobody can take the floor. You know exactly what you just did.
- Pip: Shut that window. Shut it. — He opened it. Of course he opened it.
- Orla: He's asleep in the chair.
- Pip: In his coat. Every single time.
- Orla {hope}: Three beds and a chair, then. Wash comes in at six. Touch it and I'll know.
