# Scene 0230: One Question Too Many

- type: talk
- location: a narrow upstairs lodging room with three plank beds in a row, a washing line of damp shirts strung across it, a sash window propped open on a broken catch, grey morning light from the left
- characters: Lyra, Zeph
- mood: light, then honest, then light again

## Beat
Evening, the landing outside the room, the window that will not shut, wash on the line. Zeph has
worked out that Lyra always asks one question more than the conversation wanted, and has decided to
tell her so, with a count and examples, because that is his idea of a pleasant evening. She does not
argue with the count. Then she tells him why, which is the first honest thing she has said about her
Order to anybody: she has never been sure the hour she sits is worth anything, and asking is the
only test she has got. Zeph, who is never quiet, is quiet, and then gives her the only comfort a
Vess has, which is a technical statement and both of them know it.

## Dialogue
- Zeph {hope}: Eleven. In four days. I have been keeping it.
- Lyra: Keeping what?
- Zeph: That. That is one. — The question you ask after the conversation has finished with you.
- Zeph: The clerk behind the grille had said no three times, and you asked him what the season was.
- Zeph: The man on the bench had said good morning twice, and you asked him about last week.
- Lyra: Eleven sounds about right.
- Zeph: You are not going to fight me on the count?
- Lyra: No. It's a good count.
- Lyra {sorrow}: I sit an hour with people at the end. I ask a list, in order. The same list, every time.
- Lyra: I have never once known whether the hour was worth anything to them. Asking is the only test I have.
- Zeph: ...
- Zeph {hope}: I have it as worth something.
- Lyra: That's a technical statement.
- Zeph: Yes.
- Lyra: Why did you count?
- Zeph: So that I could say it to you and be right.
