# Scene 0850: The Gate

- type: talk
- location: a tall clean stone building at the head of a street with glazed windows and a scrubbed step, a painted company board with no words above the door, window boxes, cold morning light from the left
- characters: Pip, Bron
- mood: sorrow, then hope
- backdrop: 0810_smoke_in_the_cut:7

## Beat
Two in the morning, at the top of the street, outside a gate Pip has no intention of going through.
Bron came up looking for her because she was not in the hall and he did not ask anybody where she
was. She is standing in the cold in front of the best-kept building in Emberrow, and what she gives
him is three facts about it in the flat voice of somebody reading a notice board, and every one of
them is a good thing, which is the point and neither of them says so. He asks her one question. She
answers it straight and then tells him about a pair of boots, and does not explain why she is
telling him, and does not know herself. He does not comfort her, does not work anything out, and
does not ask again. Then they go and steal a lamp off a rope-walk, and they do it badly, and it is
the last easy thing in the chapter.

## Dialogue
- Pip {sorrow}: Forty beds. Twenty and twenty, two rooms, boys one side.
- Pip: Stove in the middle of each and it's lit from Fallow to Thaw. Not metered, either. Company pays it.
- Pip: Eggs twice a week. Real ones, whole, one each. Nobody in this town gets that.
- Bron: ...
- Bron: Were you all right in there?
- Pip: Yeah.
- Pip: That's the thing. I was.
- Pip: They gave me these at that door. I was six. A man measured my foot with a stick and wrote it in a book.
- Pip: They're the oldest thing I own. By a long way.
- Pip {hope}: Right. Come on. Let's go and steal somebody's lamp.
