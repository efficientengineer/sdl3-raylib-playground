# Scene 1265: The Warm Gardens

- location: a walled roof garden on the topmost shelf, clipped hedges and gravel walks, warm pipes running under iron grilles in the paths, a stone bench facing the drop, lamps on posts, cold clear night sky
- characters: Hesk, Crewe
- mood: wonder, then dread
- staging: Crewe stands at the stone parapet on the right of frame with the black Basin beyond it; the party comes up the walk from the left and faces right. The house guard stand off behind him at the garden gate on the right

## Beat
A walled garden at the top of the world in the middle of Dust: green, growing, fruiting, and warm
at night, with the cold black Basin beyond the parapet and forty-one thousand metered roofs below
it. The party stands in it and nobody says anything about it. Hesk, who has watched terraced barley
die in steps for twenty years, looks up at a fruit tree in leaf and says nothing at all, which is
worse. Crewe is at the parapet with his cane, thinner than his coat. He apologises for the hour,
cannot ask after anyone's family because he has not been introduced, and says he has been expecting
somebody for about fourteen years and is glad it turned out to be people who walked up. The garden
is beautiful and well kept and nobody in it is playing a game.

## Dialogue

- Crewe [3]: You walked up. Everyone I expected would have come in a car, with a warrant and a clerk.
- Crewe [4]: I've been expecting somebody about fourteen years. I'm glad it was people who walked up.
