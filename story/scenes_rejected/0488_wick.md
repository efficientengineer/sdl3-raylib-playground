# Scene 0488: Wick

- type: talk
- location: a plank walkway on floats between raft houses of weathered timber, rope handlines on both sides, a hanging net and a bucket on a pole, still water below, flat overcast noon light, no wind
- characters: Bron, Ket
- mood: sorrow -> wonder

## Beat
Six hundred people whose entire town is on skids, because the water has gone down eight times in
forty years and every time it goes down the town follows it. The raft-keeper describes the previous
eight moves the way a farmer describes weather — distances, directions, how long it takes if the
weather is kind — and then mentions, in the same voice, that there is nowhere for the ninth. Ket
gives him the year it started and the rate, in two figures, because he asked and because she is the
only person alive who was standing here at the time. He thanks her for the number, which nobody has
ever given him. Then he asks the other question, and gets an answer, and takes it as one.

## Dialogue
- Raft-keeper {sorrow}: We've moved eight times. Last one was four years back. Two hundred and ten yards, east.
- Raft-keeper: Before that nine hundred yards. Before that a mile and a half. You get quick at it.
- Raft-keeper: Walkways first, then the houses, then the poles. Three days, if the weather's kind.
- Bron: And the ninth?
- Raft-keeper: There isn't a ninth. There's rock under the next bit. Nothing to float on and nothing to stand on.
- Raft-keeper: When did it start going? Properly. Does anybody actually know?
- Ket {wonder}: Year three hundred and ten. Six inches a year for eight hundred years.
- Ket: In the last forty it has been four feet a year. It is not a steady figure. It has never been steady.
- Raft-keeper: ...Four feet. Right. Thank you for the number. Nobody's ever given me the number.
- Raft-keeper: Will it stop?
- Ket: Not correct.
