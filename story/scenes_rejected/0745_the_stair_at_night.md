# Scene 0745: The Stair at Night

- type: talk
- location: a steep open iron stair bolted to a cut-stone face, a landing with a bench and a lamp post, laundry strung under the flight above, the drop falling away on the left, lamplight from the post, night
- characters: Bron, Ket
- mood: sorrow, then hope

## Beat
Two in the morning on the public stair between Tier One and Tier Two. Bron cannot sleep in a warm
room and has come down until the air went bad again, which is where he can sleep. Ket is on the
landing because a keeper sits up anyway and always has. He asks her factual questions, one after
another, the way another man would drink, and she answers every one of them with a real number and
no softening and no follow-up, and it is the most restful hour he has had in six chapters. She tells
him what the hum under the bunk rows is, flatly, and gives him the depth, and when he asks whether
it stops she tells him it does not stop, it thins. Neither of them makes anything of that. She
announces her own state once, because to her that is manners. He asks her for one more question and
she gives him the one she has been waiting to ask since Braid.

## Dialogue
- Bron {sorrow}: How deep is it.
- Ket: Eleven hundred and forty feet to the face they are cutting. Eight hundred when they began it.
- Bron: How wide.
- Ket: Nineteen feet across the bell. The largest that has ever been built, and they are proud of it in writing.
- Bron: How old's the door it's standing on.
- Ket: Older than me. I do not have the year. Nobody has the year.
- Bron: The hum under the bunk rows. What is that.
- Ket: The branch, being drawn. You slept last night above a thing coming out of the ground.
- Bron: Does it stop?
- Ket: Not correct. It does not stop. It thins.
- Ket: I have not slept in nine days. I will sleep on the eleventh. It is ordinary. You need not plan around it.
- Bron {hope}: ...Ask me something.
- Ket: How much do the six of you weigh now? I will want it in pounds.
