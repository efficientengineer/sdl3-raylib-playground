# Scene 0335: The Keeper of the Dark Well

- type: talk
- location: a stone platform at the foot of a tall dark ring tower, swept clean, a plain broom leaning against the stonework, a stack of stone chips squared off, flat grey daylight from the left, morning
- characters: Sem, Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip
- mood: wonder, then tense

## Beat
The only resident of Tellwater who is not on a posting, standing on a platform under a tower that
has been dark for sixty-eight years. She is exact, unhurried and entirely without agenda, and she
answers precisely the question she is asked and then stops. They ask about the company parties and
she gives them counts and months. They ask when the flood was and she gives them the day. She
corrects Zeph's arithmetic without any interest in having corrected it. Lyra asks whether she was
here when it flooded, and she says yes, and the conversation moves on, because nobody thinks to
ask the next question. She would answer it. She has been ready to answer it for fourteen years.
Nobody in the party gets a feeling about her. She goes back to sweeping.

## Dialogue
- Zeph {wonder}: Keeper. Company parties on this ground. How many, and when?
- Sem: Three this year. Six men in Thaw. Four in Turn. Nine, eleven days ago, with a cutting rig they did not unload.
- Bron: What did they do?
- Sem: They walked the edge of the flooded ground and wrote. They did not go down. They asked me for the meter readings.
- Lyra: And the flood. When was the flood?
- Sem: The low ground was flooded on the twelfth of Fallow, ten ninety-three.
- Zeph: About fourteen years, then.
- Sem: Thirteen. Fourteen is in Fallow.
- Lyra: Were you up here when it flooded?
- Sem: Correct. I was on this platform.
- Lyra: ...
- Pip {tense}: Does the town bother you? Sixty people and all this.
- Sem: No. The bread is good. I am given two loaves a week and I put them on the wall for the birds.
- Sem: I am at four lights. Two a side. You should plan around that.
- Bron: Plan around it how?
- Sem: However you plan. I am going back to the north face. It spalls a hand's width a decade and I would rather see it.
