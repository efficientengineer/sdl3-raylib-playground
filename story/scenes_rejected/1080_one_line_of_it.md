# Scene 1080: One Line Of It

- type: talk
- location: a gravel yard of laid rail and turnouts, a squat brick engine shed with open doors, a water crane and a coal bunker, cinders underfoot, a lamp set on the floor of the shed, before dawn
- characters: Zeph, Bron, Lyra, Hesk, Pip, Ket
- mood: sorrow, then dread

## Beat
An empty engine shed an hour before the town wakes up, a lamp on the floor, Maren's box open and
going into stacks. Halfway down it Zeph finds forty-odd pages that are not sanatorium paper at all:
one man's handwriting, front to back, never filed, listing where every one of Tellwater's four
hundred was sent — town by town, name by name. Somebody wrote it out by hand and sent it to this
building years ago so that one person in the Basin would have it. Zeph cross-references the house
on the intake form against the Registry roster and reads a line out loud before he has worked out
what he is saying. Nobody says whose hand it is. Nobody offers to go with Bron and nobody tries to
stop him. He takes the page, folds it, and asks about opening hours.

## Dialogue
- Zeph {sorrow}: This isn't sanatorium paper. Wrong weight, wrong rule, wrong everything. It has never been in a file.
- Zeph: Forty-three pages. One hand the whole way, and the hand does not get tired, which means it was done in sittings.
- Hesk: A list of what?
- Zeph: Where they went. All four hundred. Town, trade, household. Somebody sat down and wrote out four hundred people by hand.
- Hesk: And never filed it.
- Zeph: And then sent it here, years ago. Somebody wanted one person in this Basin to be holding it.
- Zeph: House thirty-one, Tellwater. The roster for that house is one adult and one child, and the child is not named.
- Zeph: And the adult —
- Zeph: Brae, A. — Windrow — laundry.
- Zeph: ...
- Bron {dread}: Hesk. What time do the laundries open?
- Hesk: Six.
