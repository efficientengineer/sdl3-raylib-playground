# Scene 0830: Ross and Dalla

- type: talk
- location: a narrow stone housefront on a steep street, a scrubbed doorstep, a chair set out beside the door, a tanner's leather apron on a hook in the jamb, hazy low sun from the right, late afternoon
- characters: Ross, Dalla, Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Hesk
- mood: hope

## Beat
A good small tannery with a kitchen behind it and a pot on. Ross Kettle is fifty-eight, glad of the
company, and gets the bad news about the trade out of the way in his second sentence so that the
rest of the hour can be pleasant. He feeds them. He is funny about his knees and prouder of his
apprentice than he will ever tell the boy. Dalla married him nine years ago and they bicker like two
people who like each other and have practised. He mentions, with no weight on it at all, that he
came down in '93 with the flood lot from some town up north and cannot remember much from before
that and never could, and his wife says he has always been like that, and they both find it
restful. Nobody in the party reacts to the year. This is about ten minutes of the player's life and
it is meant to be good ones.

## Dialogue
- Ross {hope}: Sit down, sit down. Not that stool. That stool's a liar.
- Dalla: Mind the beam, love, it's lower than it looks. He never warns anybody. He thinks it's funny.
- Ross: Trade's finished, by the way. I'll say it now, before you ask me, and then we can have a nice time.
- Ross: Nine yards a week out of this shop when I took it on. Two now. I've one lad and I can't pay two.
- Dalla: He could pay two. He won't ask the bank.
- Ross: The bank wants my knees for security. My knees aren't worth a bark sack.
- Bron: What's the lad called?
- Ross: Teb. Sixteen, and he's better at it than I was at thirty. I've never told him that and I'm not going to.
- Hesk: How long have you had the shop?
- Ross: Fourteen years. Came down in ninety-three with the rest of the flood lot. Some town up north.
- Ross: Couldn't tell you much about before that. Never could. Ask me about ninety-two and you'll get a long quiet.
- Dalla: He's always been like that. Suits me. I've grudges enough in me for the pair of us.
- Lyra: Do you miss it? The town up north.
- Ross: Miss it? I'd have to have had it first. No, this one's mine. Forty-one steps up from the yard and I know every one.
- Dalla: He counts them out loud coming home. Every night. I can hear him from the range.
- Ross {hope}: Come back tomorrow, there'll be better bread. That's yesterday's and she knows it.
