# Scene 0325: The Schedule

- type: talk
- location: a flat grassless field at the town edge, four bare timber fair frames standing in a row, trodden circles of ground, a coil of bunting rope on a post, low evening sun from the right, long shadows
- characters: Zeph, Pip, Ista, Ollo
- mood: hope, then flat

## Beat
Side contract, scene two of three. The frames go up against a printed company form that comes with
the postings in the same packet: which frame stands where, the order the games are played in, how
many spans of bunting and which way they run. Zeph reads it because Zeph reads everything, and
what offends him is professional rather than moral — a record that never changes is not being
kept, it is being re-issued. Ista is delighted with it and says so with complete sincerity, which
is worse. Ollo says that Denner always put the long frames on the east side and that the sheet
says east side, and he says both halves of that sentence in exactly the same voice. Pip stops
joking somewhere in the middle and does not start again.

## Dialogue
- Zeph {hope}: There is a form. There is a printed form for a fair.
- Ista: There is. It comes up in the packet with the postings, same as everything.
- Zeph: Frame one, north-east. Frame two, south-east. Bunting, four spans, east to west over the cleared ground.
- Zeph: Games in order. The weight, the rope, the children's run, the dancing.
- Pip: Somebody wrote down when to dance.
- Ista: Somebody wrote down when to dance, and it works beautifully.
- Zeph: I have it as identical to last year's sheet.
- Ista: It is.
- Zeph: And to the year before that one.
- Ista: That's what's nice about it. You always know where you are.
- Zeph: Nobody is keeping this. They are re-issuing it. Those are not the same thing.
- Ollo: Denner always put the long ones on the east side.
- Ollo: And the sheet says east side. So that's that settled.
- Pip: ...
- Ista {dread}: It's a lovely fair. Every single year, exactly the same.
