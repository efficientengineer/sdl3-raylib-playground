# Scene 1025: A Hundred And Sixty-Three Years

- type: talk
- location: a tall stone ring tower standing whole and dark over a tram town, birds circling the upper rings, a swept stone apron at its foot, a broom and a stacked pile of squared stone chips, grey overcast morning light
- characters: Arro, Ket, Bron, Pip
- mood: dry, then sad, then not sad

## Beat
The apron of a dead machine, swept to the edges. Arro-Vintry is at one light and has kept a skywell
that has not run since the year 944, and his inventory is current to this month. He and Ket greet
each other by counting each other's lights, which neither of them finds rude, and then talk shop
for six lines that nobody else in the party can follow and that is plainly warm. He would like ring
seat six cleared of two hundred years of fallen stone. He will not call it a job, will not put it
on a board, and will not pay for it, because there is no bill and no money and he is nobody's
employer. Pip asks him the rude question and he answers it literally, at length, and without one
ounce of self-pity. Ket does not offer him anything and does not pity him either.

## Dialogue
- Arro {sorrow}: Ket-Ossun. You are at four.
- Ket: Four. You are at one.
- Arro: One. Apron swept. Inventory current to the ninth of Dust. Ring seat six is under two hundred years of fall.
- Ket: Six carries the north quarter.
- Arro: It does. I turned the idler by hand in Green. Eleven pounds on the bar.
- Ket: It took nine when I was here.
- Arro: It took nine when you were here. That is in the book as well.
- Pip: I understood not one word of that and I have never been happier.
- Pip: Rude question. Why do you bother? It hasn't turned since before anybody's grandmother.
- Arro: A hundred and sixty-three years next Deep. I am aware of the joke. Here is the answer, properly.
- Arro: Swept apron. Clear seats. Current book. Then the day somebody wants it, it is a day's work. Not a hundred years.
- Arro {hope}: I will not see that day. Clear seat six if you wish. There is no bill, no money, and I am nobody's employer.
