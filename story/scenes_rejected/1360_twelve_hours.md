# Scene 1360: Twelve Hours

- type: talk
- location: a rail head on the dry seabed below a terrace town, four loaded sledges strung out on laid rail, a chalked board on a post beside the first, flat overcast daylight from the left, dust raised low
- characters: Kerrow, Bron, Ket, Sefa
- mood: confront
- backdrop: 1355_the_warrant:1

## Beat
Beside the carriage, in the wind, before it pulls away. She has twelve hours of paperwork whichever
way the warrant goes and she spends four minutes of them telling the party exactly where the Long
Bore stands, what it is rated at, what she has ordered and why, in numbers, under five boxes. She
does not lie and she does not gloat and nobody wins. Ket does the arithmetic out loud and agrees
with the physics, and says so, and that is the worst moment of the chapter for everyone standing
there. Sefa asks one procedural question, gets a straight answer, and is briefly and visibly
admiring. Then Kerrow tells them where the service entrance is, unprompted, because she would
rather they went down and looked at it than stood about guessing. She does not know what is behind
the inner doors and does not pretend to. She keeps her back to the wind the whole time and her
hands in her coat, and nobody says anything about it.

## Dialogue
- Kerrow {confront}: Four minutes. I would rather you had it from me than off a gate notice, so ask or I will simply say it.
- Bron: What does full mean.
- Kerrow: Nineteen feet across the bell, rated at eleven degrees on the gimbal, running at sixteen. That is full.
- Kerrow: At sixteen it takes the branch under the Rim to a level inside fourteen months. I have costed the fourteen months.
- Kerrow: Then every other head under the Rim is safe to shut down. Fifty-one of them, inside a year, and I will shut them.
- Kerrow: Then I cap it, and capping it ends extraction, and ending extraction ends inversions. That is the whole of the plan.
- Ket: ...
- Ket: Drawing thins the branch. A thin branch is what inverts. The arithmetic is correct and I would like it not to be.
- Kerrow: Thank you.
- Ket: That was not agreement with you. It was agreement with the figure.
- Kerrow: I know the difference. It is the only kind I get.
- Sefa: Who reviews the fourteen months, and when?
- Kerrow: Nobody. There is no review clause and I did not put one in, because I would have had to obey it.
- Sefa: ...That is very good drafting.
- Kerrow: The service entrance is at the foot of Tier One, north of the bunk rows, and the gate is not manned at night.
- Kerrow: Go and look at it. I would rather you looked than guessed. Guessing has cost this company a great deal of money.
