# Scene 1625: The Ninth of Fallow

- type: talk
- location: a narrow boarding-house kitchen, a black range with a kettle on it, a long scrubbed table with eight chairs, cups hung on hooks, one small window over the sink, warm range light, late night
- characters: Ivo, Mira
- mood: sorrow

## Beat
Deep, the winter after. Dismissed, charged, acquitted on the evidence, and finished. He keeps a
boarding house in Windrow for the widows of men who died on his jobs and has paid for it himself for
twenty years and has never mentioned it to anybody. Tonight he sits down in his own kitchen, puts
the kettle on because his hands want something to do, and tells his wife about the 9th of Fallow,
1093 — the rider, the eleven constables, the street at twenty past one, four hundred people standing
up and pleased to see him, the children who did not know what the words were for, and the boy he
carried four hundred yards and put in a cart. He has never said it to anyone, including her,
including the boy, and he will not say it to the boy. He does not cry and does not ask to be
forgiven and does not explain himself once. She asks him two questions and both of them are
practical, and she does not tell him it was all right.

## Dialogue
- Ivo {sorrow}: Sit down a minute. I'll do the kettle. My hands want something to be doing.
- Mira: Is this the hearing, or is it older than the hearing?
- Ivo: Older.
- Ivo: The ninth of Fallow, ninety-three. The Tellwater call. I've never told you about it.
- Ivo: A rider came into Windrow at five to midnight and said there was a fire. There was no fire.
- Ivo: I took eleven constables out. Three hours on the road, and nobody spoke for the last of it.
- Ivo: Twenty past one, and there were four hundred people standing in the street.
- Ivo: Warm. Breathing. Looking at us. Pleased to see us, most of them.
- Ivo: The children wouldn't walk when you told them. They didn't know what the words were for.
- Ivo: So you carry them. You pick one up, you carry it to the carts, and you go back for the next.
- Ivo: I carried a boy four hundred yards. Nine years old. He had his hand in my collar the whole way.
- Ivo: I've never said that to anybody. Not to the office. Not at the hearing. Not to him.
- Mira: Is the boy all right now?
- Ivo: I don't know.
- Ivo: I've never asked him.
