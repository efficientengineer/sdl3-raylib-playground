# Scene 1610: The Hearing

- location: a long plain stone hall in a freight town, a single table across one end with chairs behind it, a plain witness chair facing it, benches filling the floor, flat grey daylight from high windows on the left, midday
- characters: Crewe, Emmet, Sefa, Zeph, Kerrow, Arden
- mood: tense, then sorrow
- staging: The inquiry table runs across the far right of the hall with its chairs behind it. The packed public benches fill the left and the foreground. Witnesses stand in the middle ground facing right toward the table. Everyone's attention goes right

## Beat
The fourth day, and everything that is ever going to be done about the 9th of Fallow, 1093, is being
done here, and it is paperwork. Ansel Crewe has testified for two days, courteously, completely, and
has handed over forty-three pages in his own hand, and he will not be charged with anything, because
nothing he did was against the law. A duty clerk is called for eleven minutes to identify a relay
log, states its class and where it was filed, asks to be excused, and goes. A woman who logged an
objection at 21:40 and cannot remember doing it hands in eleven years of a notebook of questions she
has asked strangers, and it is taken as evidence and she is thanked and she writes that down too.
Sefa Quill is struck off in one sentence and does not react. Nobody wins anything. The room is
cold, full, and patient, and at the end of it a nineteen-year-old stands up to answer and cannot
finish a sentence, and not one person in the hall helps him.
