# Scene 0675: Eleven Hours

- type: narration
- mood: everything done correctly

## Beat
The night in flat administrative order. A count is opened, a board is held, people are walked
somewhere with beds, a shaft is stopped, a shift is stood down and a shift is paid. Every sentence in
it is a procedure being carried out correctly by somebody competent. Nothing in the wording grieves
and nothing in it apologises, and the last line looks at the town in daylight and finds it exactly
the way it was the day before.

## Dialogue
- Narrator {sorrow}: The count opens at four fifty-two, in the dark, on a trestle carried out of the counting house.
- Narrator: A director in a slate coat takes the board herself and does not hand it to anybody for eleven hours.
- Narrator: The forty-one are walked to the care-house in pairs. It is the only building in Saltmouth with beds standing empty.
- Narrator: They walk well. They are warm. They are civil to the constables, and two of them say thank you.
- Narrator: The bore is stopped at six. The shift is stood down at six. The shift is paid at six.
- Narrator: Nine marks a fortnight, less the bunk, into the same hands as last month, and the hands are steady.
- Narrator: The sun comes up at seven forty on a town that looks exactly the way it looked yesterday.
