# Scene 1375: It Will Be Running by Morning

- type: talk
- location: a plain stone house interior, a desk with a shaded lamp, a wall of shelved ledgers, a black cane leaning in the corner, a shuttered window behind, low firelight from the right, night
- characters: Crewe, Bron, Sefa, Ket
- mood: sorrow, then tense

## Beat
The draw gear is down. It took forty minutes on eleven wheels against the governor and it cost them
both palms each; Ollo's right hand is wrapped to the wrist and Bron cannot close his. They are
walked up to the Cap under an escort that is not quite an escort and put in a small cold room with
a deposed chairman in it, and the first thing he does is send for a basin and apologise for the
temperature. Then he explains, without any pleasure at all, why they have not won: the order is not
his any more, there are four relief crews on a rota and a maintenance contract that pays whether
anybody in the building is awake, and the place runs itself. Then he gives Bron the original —
forty-three pages in his own hand, never filed — and folded in at the back of it the constables'
roster for the night of the 9th of Fallow 1093, eleven names, because he kept everything. Bron
reads down the eleven, stops at one, says nothing whatever about it, and puts the pages inside his
coat. Sefa looks at the handwriting and says the cruellest true thing anybody says in the chapter,
and he agrees with her.

## Dialogue
- Crewe {sorrow}: Put the hands in the water first. I'm sorry about the cold; I am no longer able to have that changed.
- Crewe: You will want to know whether it worked. It worked. You have stopped it for about four hours.
- Crewe: The order is not mine. It was not mine when it was written. Four relief crews on a rota, and a maintenance contract.
- Crewe: It will be turning again by morning. Not because anybody decides to. Because nobody has to.
- Bron: Who do we stop, then.
- Crewe: ...That is the right question and I have never had an answer for it. Take these instead.
- Crewe: Forty-three pages. Where all four hundred went, town by town, in my hand, filed nowhere.
- Crewe: And the constables' roster for that night, folded in at the back. Eleven names. I kept everything.
- Bron: ...
- Sefa: You wrote it beautifully and you filed it nowhere, which is burning it and takes less nerve.
- Sefa: A record nobody can find is a record nobody has to answer for. You knew that when you chose the drawer.
- Crewe: Yes.
- Crewe: Is Anneke Brae keeping well?
- Bron: She's at the laundry. She's well. She doesn't know me.
- Crewe: ...Thank you. I had hoped that would be easier to hear than it is.
- Ket: It will be at full by six. From six the branch under the hills thins. There is nothing else to plan around tonight.
