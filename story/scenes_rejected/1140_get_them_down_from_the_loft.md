# Scene 1140: Get Them Down from the Loft

- type: talk
- location: an open-sided timber pole store at a fair ground, bare fair frames stacked in racks along one wall, a plank loft above with coiled bunting rope on it, trodden bare ground outside, hard afternoon sun from the right
- characters: Pip, Ollo, Hesk, Ket, Zeph, Jory
- mood: hope

## Beat
Sixty trestles, eleven years of bunting and a fiddle case, in a loft the temperature of an oven.
Hesk says the weight of each trestle before she lifts it and is right every time. Ket will not
round the count and mentions her lights the way another person mentions the weather. Zeph is
useless, knows it, and provides commentary. Then a salvage foreman arrives with a contract to
strip the loft for timber this afternoon, and he is entirely within his rights, and the party
brawl him about it anyway and then beat him with his own paperwork. Pip, who is frightened of
everything, is having the best day of her life.

## Dialogue
- Hesk {hope}: Twenty-six pound. ...Twenty-six pound.
- Pip: You said that about the last one.
- Hesk: The last one was twenty-six pound too. They're the same trestle, that's the point of trestles.
- Ket: Fifty-eight.
- Ollo: There's sixty.
- Ket: There are fifty-eight. Two were broken up for the frost in eleven hundred and four. I am at four lights, by the way. Plan around it.
- Zeph: I am holding a rope. I would like that noted. I am holding it extremely well.
- Pip: You're holding the end that isn't tied to anything.
- Zeph: I have it as a rope.
- Jory: Who's in my loft? I've a contract for this timber this afternoon. Signed, sealed and paid for.
- Jory: Fair frames, loft boards, and the roof off it after. It's paper. Read it. It's paper.
- Hesk: It is. It's good paper. It says loft boards. Trestles aren't boards, they're plant.
- Hesk: Plant's inventory. Inventory goes on the clerk's tally board. Go and argue with her; she's got tea.
- Ollo: Is anybody hurt? ...Nobody's hurt. Good. Is anybody hungry?
