# BESTIARY — chapter one

Rewritten 2026-09-21 for the hunt on the high pasture. **The west road is gone and its creatures with
it** — `{{ROAD_CREATURE}}`, `{{THIEF_CREATURE}}`, `{{AMBUSH_CREATURE}}`, `{{STALKER_CREATURE}}`,
`{{RUIN_CREATURE}}`, `{{CAVE_CREATURE}}` and `{{SHRINE_CREATURE}}` are out of chapter one. One
survivor: `{{GRAIN_CREATURE}}`, because the player needs one harmless thing to hit inside Halm.

Five things to fight, and all but the boss are one idea each: **three field creatures, the machines
(three of them, named not numbered), and the boss.** Every proper name is a token (`NAMES.md`); values below are the designer's placeholders
until the writer sets them.

Every creature has **one tell** and the parry rules in `COMBAT.md` apply to all of them without
exception. No creature explains itself in dialogue. Villagers know what a thing does, not what it is.

---

## In town

### {{GRAIN_CREATURE_PL}} *(lids)* — the only ones in Halm
**Silhouette.** A flat grey disc the width of a dinner plate, walking on its rim on dozens of short
legs, a hot pink vent in the middle of its back. Slate grey and hot pink.
**Where and why.** The grain yard, three of them, day one only. They are why the yard's door is
propped and why nobody is standing in it — which is how the player gets in to find the teaching find.
**Behaviour.** Tips onto its edge and rolls at you in a straight line. It cannot turn while it rolls.
**Tell.** It stands up on its rim. Parry it and the roll goes past you and it lies there OPEN.
**Group.** Three, one behind another. Two or three cycles and it is over.
**Drops.** Grain. One coin, the first coin the player ever picks up.
**Its job in the chapter.** To be easy. The first parry the player lands that is not a machine.

## The hill path, dusk

One creature, because the hill path is a ten-minute climb and its job is *the first real fight*, not
variety.

### {{HILL_CREATURE_PL}} *(burrs — the writer's name and concept)*
**Silhouette.** Knee-high, wide and low, a mouth along the whole front edge and no visible eyes,
covered in hooked seed-burrs the colour of the dry grass it lives in, so a standing one is a tussock
until it moves. Straw and dull green, with a wet dark mouth line.
**Where and why.** All over the hill path in ones and twos, thicker near the top. They are the
grazers' pest of this whole country — the {{HERDER_PEOPLE_PL}} keep them off their flocks and nobody
keeps them off Halm's. **Why so many tonight:** they have come down off the high ground ahead of the
{{GUARD_BEAST}}'s sweeps. The player is walking up through what is running down.
**Behaviour.** Barges in a short straight charge and **clings to the leg**: while one is on you, you
are **slowed** — in battle your speed drops and you act last; in the field you walk at half pace
until you shake it. Shaking it is an action, and at effort 1 it takes two goes and at effort 3 it
takes one, which is the player's first real use of the slider.
**Tell.** The mouth line opens and the whole body sinks an inch. Parry it and the charge runs into
nothing; it lies there stunned and OPEN and it is not on your leg.
**Group.** One or two on the lower path; three near the top. About one encounter every twenty paces.
**Drops.** Grass-matted wool, worth a coin or two to {{SHEPHERD}}.
**Its job in the chapter.** To be the first real thing coming at you with no Hart, no wall, no retry,
and to make the case for parrying rather than swinging in one sentence the player feels: parry it and
it never touches you; swing at it and you spend the next two rounds getting it off your leg.

## The high pasture, night

Two, and the area's one strong idea is **darkness**, so both of them are about what you can and
cannot see. Neither is a puzzle; both are fights.

### {{NIGHT_CREATURE_PL}} *(lanterns — the writer's name and concept)*
**Silhouette.** No body worth drawing: a pale soft light the size of a held lamp, hanging a little
above the grass, with something dark and thin inside it that you only make out when it is close.
**Where and why.** Out on the open pasture, always, and nobody from Halm has ever been up here at
night to meet one. From a distance it looks exactly like somebody else's lantern, which is the whole
trick and needs no explanation from anyone.
**Behaviour.** **It pulls you a step closer.** Look at it and the party drifts toward it a pace at a
time — in the field the input is briefly pulled that way; in battle whoever it works on loses their
guard that round. When it has you close it flares and **blinds one character for two rounds**, and a
blinded character cannot see tells at all, so parrying becomes a guess.
**Tell.** The light **dims for a beat before it flares.** In the dark that dip is the only warning,
and without {{HERDER}} it comes a beat too late to react to reliably; with her it is called early and
the fight is fair. **This is the creature that makes night sight a mechanic rather than a line.**
**Group.** Two or three, spread out, each in its own patch of dark. One of them is the set piece in
`chapter01.md` P7 segment 3 — the one the player walks toward believing it is a person.
**Drops.** Nothing you can hold. Killing one leaves a dark patch of grass, which is how a player
learns to tell the pasture's lights from the real lantern ahead.
**Its job.** To make the dark hostile, to make the party's own lantern ambiguous, and to make Distel
joining feel like a gift.

### {{PASTURE_CREATURE_PL}} *(fleeces — the writer's name and concept)*
**Silhouette.** A sheep from behind. From the front there is no face and the wool goes all the way
round. It stands among the sleeping animals with its back to you.
**Where and why.** Only where the sleepers are. They follow the {{GUARD_BEAST}}'s sweeps and bed down
in what it leaves — so **they are a clue before they are a fight**: a knot of sleeping animals is
also where one of these is standing, and the player learns to read a group before walking into it.
**Behaviour.** Does nothing at all until you are beside it, then turns. One hard hit, then slow.
**Tell.** It **turns its head the wrong way** — a beat of movement in a field where nothing else is
moving, read by motion and not by light, so it is the one thing on the pasture the lantern does not
help with. Parry it and it is off balance and OPEN and looks, briefly, absurd.
**Group.** One or two in a knot of sleepers, never more.
**Drops.** Wool. It is real wool, which is quietly horrible and nobody comments on it.
**Its job.** To put a price on the chapter's best activity — walking up to sleeping animals and
examining them — without ever making the player stop doing it.

## The machines — {{MENTOR}}'s yard

Not creatures. Wood, iron, rope and counterweights, built by a man whose knee ended his hunting, each
one teaching exactly one thing, in this order. They are the whole of the game's combat teaching.

**There are three, and they are named, never numbered.** *(Revised 2026-09-21: the barrel on a rope
is cut. Its lesson was "a straight line cannot turn", which the grain-yard creatures teach in play an
hour later, and its real job was to introduce the word* tell *in a sentence — and no sentence
introduces anything in this chapter any more. Cutting it takes two minutes and a tutorial out of P1.)*

| The machine | It teaches | Window | Beaten by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the post** — a split post on a spring that leans away when struck | *hit a thing that is moving* | none | three clean hits |
| **the arm that holds** — a sprung jaw that closes on the wrist | *things telegraph, and waiting can beat swinging* | 0.6 s | parry it, or spend two rounds getting out |
| **the swing** — a counterweighted arm on a pivot | **wait, turn the swing aside, strike the opening** | 0.45 s | the parry, and only the parry |

**The swing in full, and it is the effort system's tutorial** (`COMBAT.md` §2, §5, and §4a for how it
teaches without anybody speaking). Every attack that is not into an opening **winds the
counterweight** — the arm comes back faster and harder — and the counter it deals **drains the
attacker's stamina**. So a player swinging at effort 5, which is what {{HERO}} does and what every
new player does, is spending 24 a turn to make the machine worse, and inside a session they are at
zero and **cannot afford to swing at all.** Attack and Hard swing grey out with their costs showing;
**Guard, which costs no effort ever, stays lit.** That is the answer, and the machine walks them into
it without one word of coaching from anybody.

**The tell is big and it is a performance**: the **counterweight drops a hand's width**, the arm
**winds back** with a ratchet sound, and about a second later it comes — **always from the same
side**, every single time. After three failures in a session it plays about a fifth slower; after
five, the Guard entry pulses once as it plays. Both reset on a parry, and neither is ever spoken
aloud. Parry it and the machine stands wide open for one round, and now spend.

Falke has beaten the post and the arm for a year. He has never beaten the swing and does not know
why, because he is trying harder each time, which is exactly the wrong thing.

**Failure is fast and free.** Flat on your back, up in two seconds, no menu.

## The boss — {{BEAST_NAME}}, a {{GUARD_BEAST}}

*The kind is a {{GUARD_BEAST}}; this one is {{BEAST_NAME}}, raised from a pup by {{HERDER}}. The
player hears the kind first, in the field, and the name only in C5 — which is the whole reason the
fight hurts.*

**Silhouette.** Bigger than a cart and built like a cattle dog that has been drawn by somebody who
has only heard one described: long low body, a heavy head carried down, six legs, and a mane of pale
fibres that stand up and move on their own when it works. Slate blue and bone white; the mane is the
only bright thing in the fight and it is what you read.

**What it is.** The {{HERDER_PEOPLE_PL}}' guard beast. It gathers animals at night, puts them to sleep with
its own light work, and moves them somewhere safe. It has done that job perfectly for years. It is
still doing it. There is nobody in charge of it any more, so it gathers everything it finds, sleeps
it, and moves it on — which is every clue in the job sheet, and the player should be able to assemble
that themselves before anybody says it.

**Why here, why now.** Its handler ran ahead of their family to fix it. Guard beasts do not go rogue.
Something was done to it, and the chapter ends on the visible evidence of that and no explanation.

### Phase one — hold it open *(cannot be lost)*
Goal line: *Hold it open so {{HERDER}} can calm it.*
The mane lifts — the tell, enormous and slow, a full beat and a half. {{HERO}} parries (**Guard costs
no effort**), it is OPEN, and {{HERDER}} spends **Settle at effort 5, 24 stamina**, into the opening.
**Attacking it does nothing**: the message says it is not hurt, every time, which is the game stating
its own rule out loud. So the phase is entirely **restraint** — cheap parries, everything the party
has poured into her — and the player watches her bar fall and not come back.
Three cycles. It goes quiet, and its head comes down. Then the mane lifts again, and it looks at
{{HERDER}}, and it does not know her.

### Phase two — a real fight *(no run; losing restarts phase two)*
The same tell, **faster, and the opening is one round but the window to choose is tighter.** Damage
counts now. After round three it **stops attacking the party and turns on {{HERDER}}**, who cannot
parry and cannot survive it; from then on every round the player does not open it is a round it is
standing over her. {{HEALER}}'s pool of 24 and {{HERDER}}'s drained one are what the player has left,
so a careless phase one costs something without ever having been a failure — **that, not reflex, is
the difficulty.** This phase is the release: parry cheap, then spend everything into the opening.

It dies to a parry and the effort after it. {{HERO}}'s first kill, and it is a mercy.

**Afterwards.** Something visibly wrong is on it. The design needs the object to exist, be found by
the player rather than shown to them, and not be explained. What it is, and what it means, is the
writer's and `THREADS.md`'s.
