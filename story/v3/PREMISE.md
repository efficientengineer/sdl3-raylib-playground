# PREMISE

## The world

The planet was dying once. The ancients built engines to hold it together — engines that made rain,
moved rivers, lit whole valleys — and then worked out that the engines were the thing killing it.
So they shut everything down and walked away, and thousands died in the quiet that followed, and the
planet got better. That was a long time ago. Now the world is small towns, long roads, hunters, and
a guild with a job board. The ancients' ruins are everywhere, half-buried, warm to the touch, still
humming under the grass. Most of them are still carrying out the last order they were given.
**Wants:** to be left alone again.

## Sevran — the young lord in the west

His city was dying of drought and his father died apologising about it. He found an old machine
under the hills, turned it on, and it rained. Now his city is green, his people love him, and he has
started listing the other things that are broken. He is charming with everyone and genuinely good
with children, and he will drown a coast to keep a promise he made at a funeral.
**Wants:** to save his city, and then everything, and to be told those are the same thing.

## Bron — hunter's apprentice, seventeen

Short, broad, red-haired, carrying his father's axe on a back it does not fit yet. His father was
the best hunter in town and died on a job no one will describe to him. Bron is earnest, a little
dumb, and the bravest person in any room he's in. He can hear the ancients' machines — a hum he's
had since he was small, from the well and the hill and the ground. He assumed everyone had it.
**Wants:** to be a real hunter, so that people stop saying his father's name before his.

## Lyra — healer, twenty-three

Tall, pale-gold hair, a gold sun medallion, a mace she is better with than she admits. The old
hunter's other student, three years ahead of Bron, and the first person who ever said out loud that
he'd be good. Kind and sharp, and completely willing to tell you the frightening thing over
breakfast.
**Wants:** to keep every one of them alive, and to be asked along instead of followed.

## Zeph — scholar of the ancients, age unclear

Owns forty books and one shirt. Has read everything about the ancients and believed all of it.
Talks through fights. Gets loud about rubble. Corrects people mid-scream. He is the only one who can
read their marks, which is unbearable for everyone including him.
**Wants:** to touch one thing the ancients touched, and to be thanked for it in public.

## Pip — thief, small, loud

Steals from the party in the first week and stays for the food. Funniest person in the game and the
only one sending money home; her little sister is kept breathing by an ancient lamp in a coast
clinic, and she says so herself in chapter six.
**Wants:** one more month of her sister's lamp, paid for in advance.

## Dorn — the old hunter, the mentor

Trained Bron's father, then Lyra, then Bron. Bad knee, worse temper, a house with one chair. Says
"you're not ready" the way other men say good morning. He has been ready to die usefully for about
eleven years and is quietly annoyed that it keeps failing to happen.
**Wants:** Bron to come home; failing that, to be standing in the doorway when it goes wrong.

## Cray — the rival

Nineteen, taller, better gear, actually good. Took the jobs Bron wanted for two years and was right
to. He sneers, he preens, and he hates Bron for the crime of being liked. He takes Sevran's coin in
chapter one and spends nine chapters learning what he bought.
**Wants:** to be first at something, once, in front of people who'll remember.

## The game in ten beats

1. **Take your first job and clear the old halls.** Inside, the building is still trying to evacuate
   itself — lights herd you out, doors unseal upward — and a panel counts the people left inside.
   It says four while five of you stand there. Bron is the one it hears and the one it won't count.
   You walk out into rain this valley has never had. (Chapter one.)
2. **Follow the rain west.** Reach the green city, get fed, get cheered, get paid — and meet the lord
   who made it, who likes Bron immediately and offers him a job.
3. **Get Cray out.** A contract goes bad on purpose, Cray's crew is spent like coin, and he joins you
   swearing at everyone that he was fine.
4. **Climb the tower you've seen from three towns.** Race soldiers up the inside of it. At the top,
   Zeph reads the ancients' shutdown order aloud and tells the party it's a forgery.
5. **Hold the stair.** Dorn stands in the door so the five of you get down it, and spends his last
   minute telling Bron he was ready two years ago and should stop asking.
6. **Steal the old barge and learn to fly it badly.** The map opens. Pip is at the wheel. Every coast
   town has work and the good jobs are optional.
7. **Shut down your first engine and find out what that costs.** The town that dries up is the one
   that cheered you in chapter two, and they come out to the road with rocks.
8. **Beat him to the deep hall.** The ancients left a message: they turned it all off knowing it
   would kill thousands, they did it anyway, and the message is an apology.
9. **Follow the hum to where it starts.** The first engine has one worker logged as still inside,
   and it has been asking him to come out every day for eleven years. That's the sound Bron has
   heard since he was small. That's why the machines file him as staff and skip him in the count:
   they are asking for the tool, and the tool is on his back.
10. **Turn off the last one.** It stops from inside the housing only, and Sevran takes that, still
    saying his city and the world are the same thing. Outside, the lever that kills the grid kills
    every ancient lamp on the coast, and Pip pulls it herself with her sister's name in her mouth.
    The world goes quiet and small and hard again. Three years later, it rains on its own.
