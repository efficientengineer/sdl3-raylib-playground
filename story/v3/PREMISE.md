# PREMISE

## The world

The planet was dying once. The ancients built engines to hold it together — engines that made rain,
moved rivers, lit whole valleys — and then worked out that the engines were the thing killing it.
So they shut everything down and walked away, and thousands died in the quiet that followed, and the
planet got better. That was a long time ago. Now the world is small towns, long roads, hunters, and
a guild with a job board. The ancients' ruins are everywhere, half-buried, warm to the touch, still
humming to themselves under the grass.
**Wants:** to be left alone for another thousand years.

## Sevran — the young lord in the west

His city was dying of drought and his father died apologising about it. He found an old machine under
the hills, turned it on, and it rained. Now his city is green, his people love him, and he has started
listing all the other things that are broken. He is charming, funny, genuinely kind to children, and
he will drown a coast to keep a promise he made at a funeral.
**Wants:** to save his city, and then everything, and to be told those are the same thing.

## Bron — hunter's apprentice, seventeen

Short, broad, red-haired, wearing his father's axe on a back it does not fit yet. His father was the
best hunter in town and died on a job no one will describe to him. Bron is earnest, a little dumb,
and the bravest person in any room he's in. Ancient doors open for him and he has no idea why.
**Wants:** to be a real hunter, so that people stop saying his father's name before his.

## Lyra — healer, twenty-three

Tall, pale-gold hair, a gold sun medallion, a mace she is better with than she admits. The old hunter's
other student, three years ahead of Bron, and the first person who ever said out loud that he'd be
good. Kind, sharp, and completely willing to tell you the frightening thing over breakfast.
**Wants:** to keep every single one of them alive, and to be asked along instead of followed.

## Zeph — scholar of the ancients, age unclear

Owns forty books and one shirt. Has read everything about the ancients and believed all of it. Talks
through fights. Delighted by rubble. Corrects people mid-scream. He is the only one who knows what any
of the machines do, which is unbearable for everyone.
**Wants:** to touch one thing the ancients touched, and to be thanked for it in public.

## Pip — thief, small, loud

Steals from the party in the first week and stays for the food. Funniest person in the game and the
only one sending money home; her little sister is kept breathing by an ancient lamp in a coast clinic,
and Pip has never told anyone that and never will need to, because she says it in chapter six.
**Wants:** one more month of her sister's lamp, paid for in advance.

## Dorn — the old hunter, the mentor

Trained Bron's father, then Lyra, then Bron. Bad knee, worse temper, and a house with one chair.
Says "you're not ready" the way other men say good morning. He has been ready to die usefully for
about eleven years and is quietly annoyed that it keeps failing to happen.
**Wants:** Bron to come home; failing that, to be standing in the doorway when it goes wrong.

## Cray — the rival

Nineteen, taller, better gear, actually good. Took the jobs Bron wanted for two years and was right to.
Sneers, preens, and hates Bron for the crime of being liked. He'll take Sevran's coin in chapter one
and spend nine chapters learning what he bought.
**Wants:** to be first at something, once, in front of people who'll remember.

---

## The game in ten beats

1. **Take your first job and clear the old halls.** A door opens for Bron alone, an ancient guardian
   asks you to please turn things off on your way out, and you walk out into rain that this valley
   has never had. (Chapter one.)
2. **Follow the rain west.** Reach the green city, get fed, get cheered, get paid — and meet the lord
   who made it, who likes Bron immediately and offers him a job.
3. **Get Cray out.** A contract goes bad on purpose, Cray's crew is spent like coin, and he joins you
   swearing at everyone that he was fine.
4. **Climb the tower you've seen from three towns.** Race soldiers up the inside of it. At the top,
   Zeph reads the ancients' shutdown order aloud and tells the party it's a forgery.
5. **Hold the stair.** Dorn stands in the door so the five of you get down it, and spends his last
   minute telling Bron he was ready two years ago and should stop asking.
6. **Steal the old barge and learn to fly it badly.** The map opens. Pip is at the wheel. Every coast
   town has work, and the good ones are optional.
7. **Shut down your first engine and find out what that costs.** The town that dries up is the one
   that cheered you in chapter two, and they come out to the road with rocks.
8. **Beat him to the deep hall.** The ancients left a message: they turned it all off knowing it would
   kill thousands, and they did it anyway, and the message is an apology.
9. **The doorkeeper opens for Bron and finally says why.** It opened for his father too, who held it
   shut for a night so a town could get up the valley, and who asked it not to tell the boy.
   Then the old thing walks outside for the first time in a thousand years, sees weather, and stops.
10. **Turn off the last one.** It shuts from inside only, and Sevran takes that, still saying his city
    and the world are the same thing. Outside, the lever that kills the whole grid kills every ancient
    lamp on the coast — and Pip pulls it herself, out loud, with her sister's name in her mouth.
    The world goes quiet and small and hard again. Three years later, it rains on its own.
