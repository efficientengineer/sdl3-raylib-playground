# PREMISE

All names are tokens. See `NAMES.md`.

## The world

The planet was dying once. The ancients built engines to hold it together — engines that made rain,
moved rivers, lit whole valleys — and then worked out that the engines were the thing killing it.
So they shut everything down and walked away, and thousands died in the quiet that followed, and the
planet got better. That was a long time ago. Now the world is small towns, long roads, hunters, and
a guild with a job board and a ledger. The ancients' ruins are everywhere, half-buried, warm to the
touch, still humming under the grass, most of them still working through a task list from the day
they closed.
**Wants:** to be left alone again.

## {{VILLAIN}} — the young lord in the west

His city was dying of drought and his father died apologising about it. He found an old machine under
the hills, turned it on, and it rained. Now his city is green, his people love him, and he has started
listing the other things that are broken. He is charming with everyone and genuinely good with
children, and he will drown a coast to keep a promise he made at a funeral.
**Wants:** to save his city, and then everything, and to be told those are the same thing.

## {{HERO}} — hunter's apprentice, seventeen

Short, broad, red-haired, carrying his father's axe on a back it does not fit yet. His father was the
best hunter in town and died on a job no one will describe to him. {{HERO}} is earnest, a little
dumb, and the bravest person in any room he's in. He can hear the ancients' machines — a hum he's had
since he was small, from the well and the kitchen floor and the ground. He assumed everyone had it.
**Wants:** to be a real hunter, so that people stop saying his father's name before his.

## {{HEALER}} — healer, twenty-three

Tall, pale-gold hair, a gold sun medallion, a mace she is better with than she admits. The old
hunter's other student, three years ahead of {{HERO}}, and the first person who ever told him to
his face that he'd be good. Kind and sharp, and completely willing to tell you the frightening thing over
breakfast.
**Wants:** to be asked along instead of followed, and to be thanked for saying yes.

## {{SCHOLAR}} — scholar of the ancients, age unclear

Owns forty books and one shirt. Has read everything about the ancients and believed all of it. Talks
through fights. Gets loud about rubble. Corrects people mid-scream. He is the only one who can read
their marks, which is unbearable for everyone including him.
**Wants:** to touch one thing the ancients touched, and to be thanked for it in front of people.

## {{THIEF}} — thief, small, loud

Steals from the party in the first week and stays for the food. Funniest person in the game and the
only one sending money home; her little sister is kept breathing by an ancient lamp in a coast
clinic, and she says so herself in chapter six.
**Wants:** one more month of her sister's lamp, paid for in advance.

## {{MENTOR}} — the old hunter, the mentor

Trained {{HERO}}'s father, then {{HEALER}}, then {{HERO}}. Bad knee, worse temper, a house with one
chair and a roof he keeps climbing. Keeps a private ledger of what everyone in town owes him. Says
"you're not ready" so often it has stopped being an insult and become a greeting neither of them
means.
**Wants:** the house to have {{HERO}} in it; will call that his safety for as long as it works.

## {{RIVAL}} — the rival

Nineteen, taller, better gear, actually good. Took the jobs {{HERO}} wanted for two years and was
right to. He is generous in public, which is far worse than cruelty, and he knows how the guild's
paperwork works when {{HERO}} does not. He takes {{VILLAIN}}'s coin in chapter one and spends nine
chapters learning what he bought.
**Wants:** to be first at something, once, in front of people who'll remember.

## The game in ten beats

Beats marked **[non-default]** are the ones the genre reaches for first. The note says how the
writing avoids the default version.

1. **Take your first job and clear the old halls.** Inside, the lights keep running ahead into empty
   rooms, and a panel counts people still in the building. It reads four with five of them standing
   there, and under the number is the date the ancients walked out. It has been looking for four
   people since that day. You come out into rain this valley has never had. (Chapter one.)
2. **Follow the rain west.** Reach the green city, get fed, get cheered, get paid, and meet the lord
   who made it.
   **[non-default]** He never asks {{HERO}} to join him and never argues for his cause. He offers a
   contract on better terms than the guild's, with his own name on the guarantor line — the exact
   line the guild left blank in chapter one. The temptation is paperwork, and {{HERO}} nearly signs
   it before anyone says a word about right and wrong.
3. **Get {{RIVAL}} out.** A contract goes bad on purpose, his crew is spent like coin, and he joins
   you swearing at everyone that he was fine.
4. **Climb the {{TOWER}} you've seen from three towns.** Race soldiers up the inside of it. At the
   top, {{SCHOLAR}} reads the ancients' shutdown order aloud and tells the party it's a forgery.
5. **Hold the stair.** {{MENTOR}} dies on it.
   **[non-default]** His death proves nothing about {{HERO}}. He is on that stair because he took a
   wrong turning, he knows it, and he spends his last minute furious about the cart fare and about
   the roof he never finished. There is no benediction and no permission: the party is already two
   floors down and only hears it. What {{HERO}} inherits is the ledger, with his own name in it.
6. **Steal the old barge and learn to fly it badly.** The map opens. {{THIEF}} is at the wheel. Every
   coast town has work and the good jobs are optional.
7. **Shut down your first engine and find out what that costs.** The town that dries up is the one
   that cheered you in chapter two, and they come out to the road with rocks.
8. **Beat him to the deep hall.** What the ancients left behind is a procedure. How to shut the rest
   of them down, in order, with the number of people each shutdown killed written beside it as a line
   item, because they expected somebody to have to do this again. The party walks out with a to-do
   list that has a body count printed down the side of it.
9. **Follow the hum to where it starts.** The first engine has one name outstanding on its list and
   has been calling it every day for eleven years. It is {{HERO}}'s father, who heard the hum too,
   who told his wife he was hunting, and who is still in there. The hum is the machine saying a name
   slowly, and it is a name {{HERO}} knows.
10. **Turn off the last one.** It stops from inside the housing only, and {{VILLAIN}} takes that,
    still saying his city and the world are the same thing. Outside, the lever that kills the grid
    kills every ancient lamp on the coast, and {{THIEF}} pulls it herself with her sister's name in
    her mouth.
    **[non-default]** No closing callback and no weather. The lamps go out in a long line across the
    dark while the four of them are mid-argument about who owes whom money, and the argument carries
    on over the top of it, and the game ends inside the sentence.
