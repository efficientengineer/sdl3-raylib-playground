## What each clip contains, and what it must not

### C1 — `0110_the_yard` (panels, day one, midday)
Falke loses to machine four for the last time today; Ottilie calls the swing from the wall before it
lands and laughs; she comes down and patches his wrist with light magic and has to sit down
afterwards; Hart says *not quite ready yet* and will not explain the machine he built.
- **Shows, for the first time:** magic exists, it is ordinary, and it is small — the whole of what it
  does here is close a wrist, and it costs her the rest of the afternoon. That is the chapter's one
  statement about magic, and it is shown, not said.
- **Must not contain:** the job, the board, the sword, the herders, Falke's father.
- **Ends on:** Falke, not Hart. (`STYLE.md` structural default 12.)

### C2 — `0130_the_counter` (talk, day one, afternoon)
Falke puts the repaired part on the counter. The clerk recites the board's two rules because Falke
asks when he can take a job; then, because Falke says he will sign tomorrow, the third: **no sword,
no signature** — an apprentice signs when his teacher says he is ready, and a hunter's first sword
comes from his teacher, so the clerk looks for the sword. Stolz is at the counter with a new coat and
lends him four coin for the bread, which is the second time today somebody is kind to him about
losing.
- **Must not contain:** any job, any sheet, the herders, the beast. The board is empty and the player
  can walk over and read it (field line).
- **The player's question is the line:** "So what do I do, go and get a sword?"

### C3 — `0140_supper` (talk, day one, sundown)
Falke says he will sign tomorrow. Hart says he is not ready, and when Falke shouts that he hit it
eleven times, Hart says the one sentence the whole chapter turns on: **it isn't about hitting it.**
Ottilie is at the table because she is at the table most nights; the fact the player gets is that she
walked over from the dark house next door and that she is an apprentice too, with a teacher who
decides when she is finished.
- **Must not contain:** her parents' deaths as a speech from her. Hart says the plain fact in four
  words and she changes the subject by teasing Falke. (Brief: shown in behaviour, never stated by her.)
- **Ends on:** Falke saying he will be out at the machines before light.

### C4 — `0150_the_sword` (panels, day two, evening)
The player has just parried machine four themself. Ottilie goes quiet for one beat and then ruins it.
Hart, surprised, brings out the sword he made long ago and says in plain words what it means: show it
to the clerk and the clerk will write your name. The evening bell starts inside this clip.
- **The understanding the owner asked for** is carried by three lines, not one: Hart says he made it;
  Hart says a first sword comes from your teacher; Hart says *take it to the clerk and sign.*
- **Must not contain:** Falke's father, Hart's knee, Hart's own hunting, any job.

### C5 — `0160_the_herder` (panels, night, the pasture edge)
Falke nearly swings at a small figure crouched over a sleeping ewe. Ottilie stops him. Distel is of
the night herders — a people the player has heard named twice by now in the field and never seen —
and she is tracking her own guard beast, which she raised, which has gone wrong, and which she does
not want killed.
- **The player's question is the line, twice:** "What are you?" and "What is a drover?"
- **Must not contain:** why the beast went wrong, why her family came down, what the holes are. None
  of that is known to anyone on screen.

### C6 — `0180_what_was_on_it` (panels, night into first light)
Falke has struck the beast down to save Distel. Behind its left ear there is a ring of nine small
holes, all the same size, healed shut. Distel has known this animal since it was a pup and has never
seen them. The sheep wake and walk. The shepherd pays Falke his first coin. Distel says a guard beast
does not forget its handler, so something was done to this one; if this one, then others; she is
going to find out. Ottilie takes her along without asking anybody, including Distel.
- **Must not contain:** an answer. The holes are a question, and `THREADS.md` carries it.
- **Ends on:** Falke.

## What the day carries instead of boxes (field lines — writer's text, designer's placement)

Every one of these is a `## <map>.<id>` heading in `story/field/text.md`. This list is the writer's
offer; the designer's ids in `designer_to_writer.md` win where they differ, and anything they ask for
that is not here gets written.

**Day one, Halm.** The errand said in the yard (bread for three, the repaired part). The baker. The
four coin. The well and Hart's winch on it — *he made that* — which is how the player learns what an
engineer is before anyone says the word. The empty job board. The bell tower, so the bell that rings
in C4 is a thing the player has already stood under. **Two mentions of the night herders**, both
idle: a carter's saying about them, and the shepherd Garbe at the market complaining that nobody will
take her job sheet tomorrow. Neither says anything a player has to hold.

**Day one, night.** Falke's room: the window, the empty hook on the wall where a sword is not.

**Day two, the run.** The board, read as an examine: three job sheets struck through and one left.
The last job sheet is the only place the job is ever written down, and it is written as a shepherd
wrote it: animals go from the high pasture at night and turn up days later, miles off, asleep and not
wakeable; the tracks stop in open ground; pays four coin. Stolz at the door, two lines. Ottilie asked
at her door, two lines, while the bell is still going.

**The hill path and the pasture.** The sleeping animals themselves, examinable one by one. The tracks
that stop. One saying about the night herders from Ottilie, in field, so that when Distel appears the
player has the word.

## The three voices, kept apart

- **Falke** — blurts and asks. Repeats the fact he just heard back as a question, then reacts.
- **Ottilie** — *loosened this draft.* The older cool neighbour: contractions always, teases warmly,
  never formal, never says what she wants. Counts things at Falke. Her worry arrives as a joke.
- **Hart** — four words where most people use twelve, never asks a question, shows care by handing a
  thing over.
- **Distel** (new) — blunt and literal. Answers the question that was actually asked. States her own
  feelings as facts and keeps walking. No softening, no politeness, no idiom, because she has never
  spoken to a human before today.
