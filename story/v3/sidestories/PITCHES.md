# SIDE STORIES — three interlude pitches

Each one is a **cutaway**: the game leaves {{HERO}}'s party, puts a title card up, and hands the
player a different person in a place the moved {{SECOND_MOON}} has hurt. Self-contained, one to two
hours. At the end that person walks out of their own region — and later joins the main party as a
full, permanent member, carrying their level, their gear and anything the player found.
The party never gets told what happened here. The player was there.

All names are tokens. New tokens proposed in `story/notes/writer-sidestory.md`.

---

## 1. The Early Field — a new fifth member

**Where it sits.** Between chapter one and chapter two. {{HERO}} and {{HEALER}} turn west at the end
of chapter one; the screen goes to a title card and gives the player {{SOWER}}, two days up the road
and three weeks earlier.
**How it opens.** {{SOWER}} at the ridge marker post at first light, measuring the notch for the
eleventh time, being brought her breakfast by a boy who will not look at her.

**The hurt place.** This village plants on the day the {{SECOND_MOON}} clears the notch on the ridge.
It cleared three weeks early. They planted, the seed came up, the frost had not gone. The player sees
it and walks in it: stepped banks of wheat a hand high and black, a barn being emptied of next year's
seed grain, one bank at the top still green because an old man planted late out of spite. No one knows
why the day moved. {{SOWER}}'s guess is that she read the notch off the wrong post.

**The protagonist — the case for a fifth.** {{SOWER}}, about sixty, has called the planting day for
four villages for thirty years. She fills the slot the four do not: **the heavy**. {{HERO}} is fast
and fragile, {{HEALER}} mends, {{SCHOLAR}} and {{THIEF}} are light. Nobody in the party can stand in
front. She wants somebody to be angry at her out loud, because everyone has agreed to be kind to her
and it is killing her.
**Weapon and kit, permanent.** A long-handled hoe: slow, wide, hits the whole front rank, and she can
plant it and hold ground — she takes a hit aimed at anyone beside her. Signature: **Scatter** — a
double handful of seed grain thrown on the ground; every enemy that eats drops its target and goes for
the grain for two rounds. Solo, that is her whole defence: she cannot outrun anything, so she makes
the fight happen where she wants it.
**Second body.** The boy, about twelve, who carries her measuring rod and will not fight; he counts
rounds aloud, and when he shouts a tell she gets a free strike. He stays in the village.
**How she meets the party.** Beat 5. The party is walking out of the green city on a job and she is in
the line of people walking into it, and she stops {{HERO}} in the road to ask him how it looks further
west — the same question she asked strangers in her first scene. She does not explain herself. The
player already knows what she is asking and why.

> {{SOWER}}: You're the fourth today. Go on, say it — I'm the woman who killed a hill.
> {{SOWER}}: Don't help. Stand there and be furious with me for a minute, it's all I've asked anyone.

**The play.** *The low banks* — stepped fields, each bank a two-block drop: down fast, back up slow,
and the dead crop hides what is standing in it. *The seed barn* — one tall indoor space, loft,
ladders, and a hole in the floor you can drop through into the grain. *The top bank and the marker
post* — narrow walking along wall tops, with gaps to jump.
**Creatures.** **Earlies** — finger-long white grubs that come up when the ground warms, up three
weeks early with nothing above them to eat; they burrow and surface under whoever struck last.
**Candles** — pale moths that steer by the {{SECOND_MOON}} and now steer a hand's width off; they
pile onto one target in a cluster that grows each round until it is cut off. **Sacks** — soft grey
things the size of a grain sack, moved into the store and swelling as they feed; every round they are
tougher, so they are the ones you kill first.
**Hazard.** The wet banks: jump down onto planted ground and it collapses, dropping you two more
levels into the flooded ditch, with the climb out on the far side.
**Optional find.** Cut the old man's late bank with him and he fills your food bag for a week, free.
**Hidden loot — the notch glass.** Smoked glass left on top of the marker post, reached by jumping
from the highest bank wall across a gap the route never asks you to cross. Whoever carries it always
acts first in the opening round of any fight the party starts. It is in her pack when she joins.
**Set piece, not a fight.** The village burns the dead crop in one pass at dusk, and {{SOWER}} walks
the fire line with a wet sack keeping it off the barn.

**The ending.** The seed grain is gone; next year is borrowed either way. She gets her fight — the old
man tells her to her face that she was wrong and that he still wants her calling the day. Nothing
about the sky is explained. The image the player carries into the green city two hours later:
a hillside of black stripes, and then fountains.
**Plants.** The refugees of beat 5 are these people, and the player farmed with them.
**Cost.** One hill map (banks, barn interior, ridge), one portrait, one walker, three creatures, one
non-fighting child follower. No new engine work.

## 2. The Wrong Water — {{THIEF}}

**Where it sits.** Mid chapter two, before the party reaches the green city and before she has ever
seen them. Title card, and the player is {{THIEF}} on a coast four days north.
**How it opens.** She is counting out what she has on a clinic step, and it is not enough.

**The hurt place.** The tides here have always answered the {{SECOND_MOON}} and no longer do. The
player sees it before a word is said: the fleet standing upright in dry mud a quarter mile out, stone
fish pens full of dead water, a town whose whole floor is walkable now and smells. The low water comes
at the wrong hour and goes out further than anyone has seen — and the last working thing the ancients
left on this shore, which keeps her sister alive, stands where it always stood, with the sea gone from
around it. The town's guess is that the city upstream dumped something in the bay.

**The protagonist.** {{THIEF}} — small, loud, fast, jokes by understatement, never says thank you. Her
want is the one PREMISE gives her: the caretaker keeps coming twice a year, and the clinic's fee is
paid every quarter or her sister is moved. Two months without a catch is two months without anyone in
this town having anything worth taking.
**Weapon and kit, permanent.** Two short knives and the vault — she does not block, she is somewhere
else. Signature: **Over the Line**, she goes over the front rank and hits the back one, so the mender
at the rear is dead before it heals anything. Solo, the kit is hit-and-move: she steals from enemies
mid-fight and sells what she takes, and a fight she cannot win she can always leave.
**Second body.** A clinic orderly of about fifty who carries the stretcher, will not hit anyone, and
patches her between fights for a fee she keeps promising to pay. He stays with the sister.
**How she meets the party.** Beat 3, on the pilgrim road, exactly as PREMISE says: she steals from the
party and stays for the food. The player watches her pick {{HERO}}'s pocket knowing precisely what the
money is for, and she says nothing about it for a chapter.

> {{THIEF}}: Twelve boats, four families, one of me. Nobody's got anything! Worst town I've ever worked.
> {{THIEF}}: The water's wrong, the fish are gone, and the fee's due Thursday. Fine. Thursday's a day.

**The play.** *The flats* — the drained bay, a mud bowl with the fleet standing in it; you drop in off
the quay and cross hull to hull, keel to keel, because the mud between is soft going. *The stone pens*
— a sunk grid of walled channels, walkable while empty, climbable out in some places and not others.
*The bird ledges* — the cliff at the bay's end, one-block steps and short jumps with the whole bowl
below you.
**Creatures.** **Combs** — flat pale filter-feeders that lived at the old low-water line, stranded
high and hungry; they latch on and drain until cut off. **Netters** — long-armed things out of the
deep channel that the wrong water carried in; each drags a target forward into the front rank.
**Beacons** — birds that fed on the flats by moonlight, feeding at the wrong hour now and starving;
they mob whoever is hurt worst.
**Hazard.** The water comes back into the pens at an hour no one predicted and fills the channel you
are standing in. You climb out where you can, not where you came in.
**Optional find.** The pen keeper's tally box, which pays out the town's old debt in salt fish — worth
real money four days inland, and worth nothing here.
**Hidden loot — the plumb.** A lead plumb-line hanging inside a boat lying on its side far out on the
flats, off the route, found by dropping into the bowl and jumping three keels the wrong way. Whoever
holds it sees every tell a beat early: guard-break windows stay open twice as long, for the rest of
the game. It is on her when she joins.
**Boss.** **The Anchor** — a comb the size of a boat grown fast across the pen gate, which is why the
gate will not open and why the pens are dead water.

**The ending.** She gets the quarter's fee and not the next one, so she leaves, which is the cost: the
sister stays and she does not. The caretaker is still coming. The tides are still wrong. Last image:
twelve boats upright in dry mud with their sails still bent on, seen from the road out.
**Plants.** Beat 8 — a thousand years of measuring how far the planet has healed — lands on a player
who has stood in a bay that is the wrong depth. And every time the party is paid after this, the
player knows where her share goes.
**Cost.** One coast map (flats, pens, cliff), one portrait, one walker, three creatures, one boss,
one non-fighting follower. Engine: soft mud as slow ground, and water that rises once, scripted.

## 3. The Ferry — {{SCHOLAR}}

**Where it sits.** Mid chapter two, a week before the pilgrim road. Title card: {{SCHOLAR}}, forty
books and one shirt, arriving in a ferry town to catch a boat he is going to be told does not run.
**How it opens.** He is on the quay, at the top of the stairs, asking for the crossing, and everybody
he asks points down.

**The hurt place.** This river fills in spring when the melt comes off the high ground. It did not,
because the cold came at the wrong time up there. The player walks in the riverbed: the channel is a
road now, walled, two blocks deep, with the town standing over it on posts and stairs that end in air.
What is left is a chain of pools, and everything in the valley that needs a drink is standing at them.
The town's guess is that the city upstream took the water.

**The protagonist.** {{SCHOLAR}} — exclaims at everything, asks three questions in a row, tells you
who first wrote a thing down. His want is PREMISE's: to stand in a room no living person has stood in.
The dry channel has opened a way under the old road that has been underwater since before the town,
and he is the only person here who wants to go in rather than around.
**Weapon and kit, permanent.** A surveyor's rod — long, two-handed, keeps things at arm's length —
and the marks he can half-read. Signature: **Read It Out** — he says one of the ancients' words aloud
at a creature that came out of their works, and it stops for a round; he is the worst of the eight
readers alive, so it fails about a third of the time and he says so. Solo, that is the design: the
best support technique in the game, on a man who cannot take a hit.
**Second body.** {{FERRYMAN}}, forty-five, twenty years of crossings and still being paid by a council
that will not say the word. He wants to sell the boat and wants one person to agree with him out loud.
He does the fighting {{SCHOLAR}} cannot, he will not leave the valley, and he is not a party member.
**How he meets the party.** Beat 3, on the pilgrim road, as PREMISE says: he attaches himself to
anyone who has seen the ancients' language. {{HERO}} and {{HEALER}} have just come from the {{STAIR}}.
He does not mention the ferry town at all — and the player can see he is walking west because there
was nothing left where he was.

> {{SCHOLAR}}: Who measured this channel? When it was full, or now? Did anyone write the number down?
> {{FERRYMAN}}: Six crossings booked for Thursday. Six! I'm going to stand there and take their money.

**The play.** *The channel* — the dry bed walked end to end, pools you cannot enter, sandbars to jump,
ferry ropes overhead as shade and as landmarks. *Under the town* — posts, jetty stairs and platforms
standing over dry ground; the way on is up and the way back is a drop. *The gravel fan upstream* —
open and bright, the mouth of the feeder that never came, and the only horizon in the region.
**Creatures.** **Ropes** — eels stranded in the pools, striking out of water you cannot follow them
into, so you fight them from the bank or not at all. **Cobbles** — river mussels the bed is paved
with, which close on a foot and hold whoever stepped until the shell is broken. **Kettles** —
pot-bellied things that came down with what melt there was and carry water inside them; they burst
when killed and the splash hits everything close, so they are killed at reach.
**Hazard.** The pools are the only water for a day, so every fight in the channel drifts toward one,
and the ground at a pool's edge gives under a jump.
**Optional find.** The ferry's lost coin box in the second pool, fished out with the grapple.
**Hidden loot — the crossing horn.** Under the far jetty, reached by dropping off the last ferry post
instead of climbing up into the town. Whoever carries it takes the first round every time an encounter
catches the party from behind, for the rest of the game. He has it when he joins.
**Set piece, not a fight.** The town hauls the boat up out of the channel and into the square, and the
player is on the rope with them. The room under the old road stays shut: the way in is full of gravel
and it is the one thing he came for.

**The ending.** The crossing stops and eleven households lose their living. {{FERRYMAN}} gets his one
honest witness and walks upstream to look at the high ground himself. {{SCHOLAR}} walks the other way
with his forty books and the door he could not open still shut. The river is not coming back.
**Plants.** The cold arriving on the wrong day is beat 12 two regions early, with no word for it yet;
{{FERRYMAN}} walking into the high ground is a face the party can meet up there.
**Cost.** One river map (channel, under-town, gravel fan), one portrait, one walker, three creatures,
one fighting follower. Engine: pools as impassable water inside a walkable channel, one group haul.

## Recommendation

1. **The Wrong Water** first. {{THIEF}} is already written into beat 5's coast and into the premise,
   so the interlude costs the story nothing and buys the most: the player knows what her share of
   every purse is for, a chapter before the party does.
2. **The Early Field** second, and it is the one that has to exist somewhere — the bill must be in the
   player's hands before the green city, and it is the cheapest build of the three.
3. {{SOWER}} is a real addition and not a convenience: the party has no one who can stand in front.
   She is also the only protagonist here who is at fault, which no one else in the game is.
4. **The Ferry** last. It is the best fit for {{SCHOLAR}}'s want, but his interlude ends on a door he
   does not open, which is a harder first impression to sell than a sister or a burnt field.
5. Two interludes, not three. Three cutaways before beat 5 is more time away from {{HERO}} than the
   chapter can carry.
