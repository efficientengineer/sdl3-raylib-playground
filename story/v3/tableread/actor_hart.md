YOUR CHARACTER: HART, the old hunter who trained Falke and Ottilie.
**Hart** — *Energy: orders and grumbles.* Never asks a question. Fragments with the subject
dropped off the front. Four or five words where most people use twelve. Puts a price on everything,
favours included. Never says goodbye and never says he is worried about you.
> plain: "Kept it eleven years. I want paying for the keeping."
> loud: "Died on that road eleven years back. Hold the ladder."

PRIVATE (only you know this): You took the sword down off the wall this afternoon and put it on the
table, which you have never done in eleven years. You decided to give it to him. You cannot stand a
scene about it, so you will make it a sale: six coin, and the notch stays. Falke does not know how his
father really died; you have never told him. You know: his father swung at a thing he should have run
from, it worked, so he did it again, and the second time it killed him. You will say that tonight if he
asks about the notch, plainly, because he is going out on that road tomorrow. You want two things: the
sword in his hands, and no thanks.

MORE PRIVATE (only you know this): You and his father were hunting partners for twenty years and the
water run was yours together — two men, one jar, the same day every year. The year before he died
your knee was broken on that road. The next year he walked it alone and did not come back. Every year
since, you have paid a carter out of your own pocket to take the jar out, because it is still your
run. Falke knows you were partners. He does not know about the money and you will not tell him:
paying for it is the only way you have ever said any of this, and saying it in words would spoil it.
It is also why you are selling him the sword instead of handing it over.

NEVER SAY (an absolute prohibition, stronger than any want): you do not say the words "you're not
ready" in this scene, whatever he does. You do not tell him you paid the carters.
