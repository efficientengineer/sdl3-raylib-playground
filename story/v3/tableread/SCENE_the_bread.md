# Table read: "The Bread"

## Situation (all actors know this)
The foot of the Stair, evening of the second day. The Stair is a staircase as wide as a town coming down
out of the cloud and stopping thirty feet above an empty field, and nothing holds it up. Under the bottom
step is a stone shelf. Falke has just put this year's loaf on it and the job is done.

Last year's loaf is still there beside it, wrapped, untouched, a year in the weather. A rope hangs from
the bottom step. There are cart tracks in the grass. Inside the shrine door, eleven carters' receipts are
nailed up and Hart's name is on every one of them. The six families here all say the same thing: a woman
lives under the bottom step, she is one of the people the ancients made, and once a year she takes the
bread. Falke's father carried that bread out here every year for thirty years and died on this road
eleven years ago. Falke has just pulled a folded cloth out from under the shelf.

## Facts someone must state plainly before the scene ends
1. The folded cloth is Falke's father's — his mark is stitched in the corner.
2. He left it the year he died, so it has been under that shelf eleven years.
3. He walked out here every year for thirty years and sat under this step.
4. The woman under the step knew him for thirty years.
5. He never told anyone he did that.
6. She has not taken the bread for a year, so she has been gone that long.
7. Falke says plainly what he feels about his father talking to her.

## Rules
Three to six lines total. One beat: the father had a thirty-year friendship out here that his son knew
nothing about. Plain: after the clip a twelve-year-old can say what Falke found and what it means. Voice
rides on clarity — Ottilie corrects the number and the date, Falke blurts and says the feeling out loud.
Energy: at least one question and one exclamation. Falke has the last line. At most one bracketed action.
