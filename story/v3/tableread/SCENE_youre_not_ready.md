# Table read: "You're Not Ready"

## Situation (all actors know this)
Hart's yard in Halm, an hour after the guild hall. Hart is up on his roof nailing down tiles with a bad
knee and he will not come down. Ottilie is at the foot of the ladder holding it. Falke has just walked
in from the guild hall, where the clerk put his name on the bread run.

The run goes out to the Stair on the same day every year and that day is tomorrow, so it leaves at dawn:
two days there, two days back, thirty coin. Hart posted the sheet himself this morning and told Falke
nothing. Whoever posts a job can withdraw it until dawn, and Hart has said around town this afternoon
that he means to have it back off the board before dark. Every carter in the valley has walked west for
eighty coin a week, so there is no one else to carry it. Hart trained Falke's father, then Ottilie, then
Falke. Falke's father walked that bread out for thirty years and died on that road eleven years ago.
Hart has said "you're not ready" to Falke so many times it has stopped being an insult, and he has not
said it once today.

NOTE FOR THIS TAKE: the last read got as far as "eleven years tomorrow" and stopped. Somebody in this
scene has to say the whole thing in plain words: Falke's father walked that bread out for thirty years
and he died on that road eleven years ago. Not the date on its own — the death, said out loud.

## Facts someone must state plainly before the scene ends
1. Hart put the bread run on the board this morning and never told Falke.
2. Hart means to take it back off the board before dark; whoever posts a job can pull it until dawn.
3. It goes out tomorrow at dawn or it does not go at all.
4. There is no one else left to carry it, and Hart's knee will not walk him there.
5. Falke's father walked that bread out for thirty years and died on that road eleven years ago.
6. Hart is not putting Falke on it.
7. Hart has not said "you're not ready" today, and he is saving it.

## Rules
Three to six lines total. One beat: Hart refuses, and admits out loud that he is saving the refusal.
Plain: after the clip a twelve-year-old can say what happened and why. Voice rides on clarity — Hart
never asks a question and drops the front of his sentences; Falke blurts. Energy: at least one question
and one exclamation; people interrupt with a dash. At most one bracketed action.
