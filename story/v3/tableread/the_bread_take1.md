[The stone shelf under the bottom step of the Stair, evening. This year's loaf is on it beside last year's, which is still wrapped. Falke is crouched at the shelf with a folded cloth in his hand.]
FALKE: [unfolds the cloth] That's my father's mark in the corner — he stitched it into everything he owned. Why's his cloth under here?
OTTILIE: Look at last year's loaf — still wrapped, a full year of rain on it. She has been gone a year, Falke.
FALKE: [sets the cloth down] A year? Then he left this here the year he died — it's been under this step eleven years. Why?
OTTILIE: Thirty years, Falke — he sat under this step every one of them and told no one. She knew him longer than you did.
FALKE: Thirty years and he never once said her name to me? He talked to her every year and gave me the weather!
OTTILIE: He gave the whole town the weather. Sit down and eat — I am not carrying you back to Halm tomorrow.
FALKE: [sits down on the grass] Fine — I'll eat. But you knew him too, so tell me one thing about my father that isn't weather!
