## 2. Singletons (the menu)

★ = judged strongest.

- **Copper economy** (D1) — the town survives by stripping copper from ruin walls that are still live.

- ★ **Twelve keys, one per region, and the last door needs all twelve** (D3) — a collection spine that is also a diplomacy problem, since every key is in someone's floor.

### 2.2 Party

- ★ **The order's last student, with a ruined office still standing in {{HOME}}** (D3) — an institution that has shrunk to one person and one address.
- **Wants to stand where no living person has stood** (D4) — appetite rather than scholarship as the reason to go into ruins.
- **Keeps the good ruins off the map** (D4) — a surveyor who sells maps and lies on them.
- **Carries her father's cracked shield** (D5).
- **Explains things while doing them, never after** (D4) — speech tic.
- **Gives orders first and apologizes a day later** (D5) — speech tic.

## 3. A proposed assembly

Built from singletons only. Gaps are marked **INVENTED**. Checked against §1 after drafting; the
terraced home valley, the sixteen-year-old, the hatch, the crawler tutorial, the salt flats, the sky
ring, the boat, the dive bell and the "wants to be trusted" want were all removed on that pass.

### Premise (three lines)

The old builders sank engines to hold the ground still, found the engines were doing the grinding, shut
them all off and left.
{{ANTAGONIST}} relit one tower under the valley he once declared safe, and that valley has not moved
since — so he wrote down exactly how he did it and gives the method to any town that asks. **INVENTED**
{{HERO}} keeps {{HOME}}'s burial list, four hundred and twelve names of people from that valley, and is
carrying the last of the twelve shutdown keys when the towns start lighting their own towers.

### The party

- **{{HERO}}** — nineteen, writes and reads for a town that mostly cannot, and cuts the names into the
  burial stone; wants to finish a year without adding one; reads other people's words aloud better than
  he says his own. **INVENTED**
- **{{FRIEND1}}** — quit {{ANTAGONIST}}'s crew at the third tower and wants forgiveness from people who
  do not know yet what he did; explains things while doing them, never after. *(D4)*
- **{{FRIEND2}}** — the last student of the order that kept the keys, still holding its one ruined office
  in {{HOME}}; wants to stand in a room no living person has stood in; says everything twice, once for
  the room and once for the record. *(D3 + D4; tic **INVENTED**)*
- **{{FRIEND3}}** — kept one small engine quiet by himself for six years before it was taken from him;
  wants the humming stopped everywhere; talks to machines, not to people, and the party listens in.
  *(D5; tic **INVENTED**)*

### The antagonist

{{ANTAGONIST}} was the surveyor who stood in a valley's square and told four hundred people the ground
under their houses would hold; it did not, and he walked out of the inquiry into the ruins to find a
tower. *(D3)*
He has not switched on an engine in two years — he relit one, wrote the method out in nine pages, and
hands a copy to every town that asks, so the party is chasing paper and arriving after the work is
done. **INVENTED**

**Want:** "I will not stand over another buried town."

### Twelve regions

1. **{{REGION1}}** — {{HOME}}: a stone town on the road above the fallen valley, half its houses full of
   that valley's survivors, the order's burnt office on the square; the party takes the last key out of
   the office floor. **INVENTED**
2. **{{REGION2}}** — the still country: cracked highways, houses on jacks, and no movement in the ground
   since spring; the party watches a town unbolt its braces. *(D3)* — **on foot**
3. **{{REGION3}}** — the lake town over the drowned library, where the order's map of all twelve towers
   is under four metres of water; {{FRIEND2}} goes down for it. *(D3)* — **cable ferry**, repaired here *(D3)*

## 4. What the defaults tell us

The model-default story for this genre is **the Warm Hatch story**: a sixteen-year-old from a terraced
farming valley opens a warm hatch in the floor of the family mill, walks down blue-lit stairs, kills two
crawler drones, learns the whole map in one room, is not believed by the elders, and chases a polite,
honest, never-lying former warden — a man whose town dried up — one region ahead along a ring or chain of
twelve engines, through salt flats, a stilt town, a drowned forest, a red canyon, a glass desert, a
volcano, an ice shelf and a cold plateau, acquiring a river boat, a diving bell and a flying machine, in
the company of a two-fingered pump mechanic, an archivist who reads plaques aloud, and a terse guard who
wants everyone home, before throwing the last switch by hand in a ring above the clouds and becoming the
custodian who walks the world teaching people what not to touch.
The tells are not the plot but the texture: *terrace*, *hum*, *hatch*, *crawler*, *"wants to be handed
something that matters"*, *"asks one question too many"*, *"they were dead for centuries" / "they were"*,
and the antagonist's dated promise of rain by the fourth month.
Future briefs should forbid these by name: the terraced valley home, the floor hatch, the blue stair, the
crawler tutorial, the drought-motivated polite official, the crew of four one step ahead, the salt flat,
the stilt town, the seafloor station, the boat/bell/flyer ladder, and the sky ring finale.
