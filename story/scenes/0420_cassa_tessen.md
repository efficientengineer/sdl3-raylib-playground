# Scene 0420: Cassa Tessen

- type: talk
- location: a plank sledge deck with a hand-crank drum at the stern, a bench of lashed crates, the mast foot and sheet cleats, a water barrel roped down, hard flat daylight from above, salt glare
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip, Cassa
- mood: hope
- backdrop: 0415_the_rails_to_braid:7

## Beat
Two hours of open salt, and Cassa talks for all of it. She counts sleepers, gulls and questions
without stopping the crank, adores Pip within four minutes, and explains what a chant-line is by
describing her own. Pip tries a small lie on her for the fun of it and is caught for entirely
ordinary reasons, which annoys her, so Cassa cheerfully shows her the party trick instead. Nobody
makes anything of it. Zeph has not said a word since the sheds and is sitting where the sail shadow
is; Pip notices and files it. Cassa asks for their names last, on purpose, because once she has them
she will not be putting them down again.

## Dialogue
- Cassa {hope}: Two hundred and forty sleepers since the siding. No gulls. That is four crossings now with no gulls.
- Pip: How fast does this go?
- Cassa: Fast. That is the sixth time you have asked and I have enjoyed all six.
- Pip: Who's Ilven? You've said Ilven four times.
- Cassa: Ilven. My bond-pair. Seventy years old, first chanter, and somebody in this world has to do her washing.
- Cassa: Seventeen of us hold the same eleven hours between us. Every evening we say them back. Nothing on paper.
- Pip: I've worked rail since I was six, me. Family trade.
- Cassa: Rail. You have never worked rail. Nobody who has ever worked rail sits on the windward crate.
- Cassa: All guesswork, that. With one of mine there is no guessing. There is a second tone under this voice, a fifth below.
- Cassa: It goes flat when I lie and I cannot stop it. Yours has nothing under it at all. Human witnesses are exhausting.
- Pip: ...Zeph. You've not said a word since the sheds.
- Cassa: Zeph. Names before we get in, all four, and I will still have them in eleven years.
