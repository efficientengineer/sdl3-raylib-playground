# Scene 1270: What Is Your Name

- type: talk
- location: a walled roof garden on the topmost shelf, clipped hedges and gravel walks, warm pipes running under iron grilles in the paths, a stone bench facing the drop, lamps on posts, cold clear night sky
- characters: Crewe, Bron
- mood: sorrow
- backdrop: 1265_the_warm_gardens:1

## Beat
Chairs are being carried up the walk. The old man asks the young man his name and gets it, and says
it back in a vowel nobody has used in fourteen years, and then asks after Anneke — not Hesta,
Anneke — and whether she is keeping well, because on the list in his own hand Bron is filed under
his mother, and Crewe is the only person alive who thinks of him that way. He assumes Bron has
always known. Bron has known eight days. He does not correct him. Crewe works it out himself,
apologises for the wrong thing, and it is nobody's fault, and that is the whole scene. Nobody
explains what just happened.

## Dialogue
- Crewe {sorrow}: While they bring the chairs. What is your name?
- Bron: Bron. Sallow.
- Crewe: Bron Sallow. I've had that off a page for fourteen years. It's a different thing in a mouth.
- Crewe: And is Anneke keeping well? Not Hesta. Anneke.
- Bron: ...She's at the laundry.
- Crewe: You'll have known that a long while. — ...You haven't. Have you.
- Crewe: I'm sorry. That was a thing to do carefully and I've done it on a garden path.
- Bron: How long have you had her on a page.
