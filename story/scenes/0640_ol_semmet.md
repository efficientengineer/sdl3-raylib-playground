# Scene 0640: Ol-Semmet

- type: talk
- location: a single stone room built into a ring tower's foot, a stone sleeping ledge with a folded blanket, a swept floor, a plain broom leaning, one shuttered opening letting a bar of daylight in from the left, morning
- characters: Bron, Ket, Hesk
- mood: two colleagues, one dead machine

## Beat
The keeper of a well that died forty-six years ago is still at the well, at one light, sweeping a
platform on a schedule nobody has asked him to keep. He and Ket exchange status the way two people
in the same trade exchange weather: lights, hours of rest, stores, the state of a spindle. He tells
her the company's shaft goes through his foundation and that he has written about it eleven times,
and he brings out the eleven acknowledgments in a flat tin case, filed in order, none of them an
answer. He is not angry and does not want anything. He offers tea he does not drink. Hesk, who has
not said a word since the door, says four words to him on the way out that are as close to an apology
as a doorward gets, and he takes them as a statement of fact and goes back to the platform.

## Dialogue
- Ol: Ket-Ossun. You are at five.
- Ket: Five. You are at one.
- Ol: One. It has been one since Rime, ten ninety-four. I have four hours in eleven days and I am taking them at night now.
- Ket: The governor spindle at Meddra. Eleven years asked, eleven acknowledgments, no spindle.
- Ol: Eleven. I have the same number and a different form.
- Ol: The shaft goes through my foundation. Two hundred feet of it is my floor. I have written about that eleven times.
- Ol: Here they are. In order. None of them is an answer. They are all correct.
- Ket: They are correct.
- Ol: There is tea. I do not take it. It is kept for people who do.
- Bron: We'll take it.
- Hesk: We shut the door on you.
- Ol: Correct. The platform wants sweeping before the shift.
