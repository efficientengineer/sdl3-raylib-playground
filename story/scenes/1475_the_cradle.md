# Scene 1475: The Cradle

- location: a long vaulted stone room lined with empty glass-fronted tanks on brass frames, their pipework cut through and capped, a floor drain channel, one tank shattered inward, dim lamp panels overhead, no daylight
- characters: Ket, Bron, Hesk
- mood: wonder, then sorrow
- staging: The party comes in from the left through a single doorway and the room runs away to the right. Ket walks right, alone, down the middle of it; Bron and Hesk stay at the left in the doorway and do not follow

## Beat
The room the Onn have been asking politely to be let into for a thousand years. It is not a tomb and
there are no bodies in it: the Onn are grown, not born, and the bays are empty because the last
muster walked out of them in year minus one hundred and forty-one. The room is whole, dry, and swept
by the city's own machinery. In the centre the drive gear has been struck apart with sledges —
deliberately, from the inside, by people on their way out — and the two hammers are still lying where
they were dropped, because nobody tidied up afterwards. Ket walks the whole length of it slowly,
alone, looks at the break for a long time, and names it. She does not weep, shout or collapse and
nobody comforts her. Two of her eight temple lights are lit; six are dark. Hesk stays in the doorway
because her people did this.

## Dialogue
- Ket [1] {wonder}: A hundred and four bays. I was grown in one of them. I have never known which.
- Ket [2]: The hammers are still down. They did not tidy up after. They did it and they walked out.
- Ket [3]: ...
- Ket [4] {sorrow}: The drive gear is sheared in four places. From this side. With those.
- Bron [5]: You can come in.
- Hesk [5]: No.
- Ket [6]: It is not damage. Damage is an accident with a date on it. This is a decision.
