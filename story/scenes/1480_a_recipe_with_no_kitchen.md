# Scene 1480: A Recipe With No Kitchen

- type: talk
- location: a long vaulted stone room lined with empty glass-fronted tanks on brass frames, their pipework cut through and capped, a floor drain channel, one tank shattered inward, dim lamp panels overhead, no daylight
- characters: Ket, Bron, Zeph, Sefa
- mood: sorrow, then hope
- backdrop: 1475_the_cradle:1

## Beat
In the same hour Ket-Ossun gets the two things her people have wanted for a thousand years and loses
the one that mattered. The skywell maintenance specification is here, complete, in a cabinet nobody
has ever opened, and it is good work: it tells her exactly what is wrong with Vintry and with Dree,
and how long each repair takes, to the day. And the cradle is a decision that was taken with a
hammer, so the specification survives and the facility does not. She states it without self-pity.
Zeph offers, once, to look for a way round it, and is told no, once, and that is the end of it
forever: there is no workaround, no second cradle, and nobody may ever propose one. Then she turns,
in a single line and without underlining it, from the worst news of her life to eleven days of work
she would like to start.

## Dialogue
- Ket {sorrow}: The cabinet at the end is the specification. All nine wells. Nobody has ever opened it.
- Ket: Every joint, every ring, and the hours against each. It is a very good document.
- Ket: Vintry is eleven days. Dree is nineteen, and nine of those are one shaft.
- Zeph: And the cradle.
- Ket: The cradle is a decision that was taken with a hammer. There is no work in it.
- Zeph: If the drive gear is the only part they—
- Ket: No.
- Zeph: I have not finished the sentence.
- Ket: I have had a thousand years to finish it. There is no way round, and I will not be asked twice.
- Bron: How many of you are left.
- Ket: Six hundred and eleven. And no more, ever. It is a recipe. There is no longer a kitchen.
- Sefa: ...I have nothing to file that under.
- Ket {hope}: Bron. Vintry is eleven days of work. I would like to start.
