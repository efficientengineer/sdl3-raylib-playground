# Scene 0245: Birds in the Rings

- type: talk
- location: a walled drying yard of wet brick with rows of washing lines strung across it, a mangle and stacked baskets against the wall, three hundred feet of dark stacked stone rings standing directly above, overcast morning light from the left
- characters: Orla, Pip, Zeph, Bron
- mood: brisk, comic, small

## Beat
Orla has a sealed bill, a small fee and a grievance. The birds in the dead tower's rings are ruining
a week of other people's washing and she has been to the tram company, the town and the Works, and
all three have told her it is one of the others' problem. She wants somebody to go up and do
something, and she is not fussy what. Zeph estimates the height and is wrong, and is corrected by a
woman who paid a man to measure it. And on her way back to the mangle she mentions, as an
afterthought, that there is a fellow living up there, and that he is no trouble, and that she has
never learned his name.

## Dialogue
- Orla {hope}: Sealed bill, small fee, and a week of other people's washing ruined.
- Orla: Birds. Up in the rings. They sit there and they let go over my yard.
- Orla: I've been to the tram company, the town, and the Works. All three say it's one of the others.
- Pip: How high is it?
- Zeph: Two hundred feet. A little over, perhaps.
- Orla: Two hundred and eighty to the apron. I paid a man to tell me and he had a line to do it with.
- Pip: I'll go up. I like a stair.
- Bron: What's the fee?
- Orla: Six and two, on completion. I'm not made of it.
- Orla {hope}: There's a fellow lives up there, by the way. He's no trouble. I've never learned his name.
