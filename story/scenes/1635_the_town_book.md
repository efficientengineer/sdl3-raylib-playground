# Scene 1635: The Town Book

- type: talk
- location: a neat sandstone town square with a raised stone well at its centre, swept flagstones, whitewashed housefronts with green shutters, a painted board over the bakery with no words, bright clean midmorning sun from the left
- characters: Ollo, Bron, Sem
- mood: hope

## Beat
Green 1108, in a square that is still too clean. Sixty people whose postings closed on the eleventh
of Dust stayed anyway, because eleven years is eleven years and there was nowhere else to be, and
they have voted. Ollo Marrick wanted the bakery. What they have voted him is keeper of the town
book, which is a ledger of who lives in Tellwater, started fresh at page one, sixty names in it and
no dates in it before this year, because there are none. He is genuinely outraged by the amount of
paperwork and cannot say out loud how proud he is, and he still has the brass posting badge on. Sem-
Dree, who stood on her platform for fourteen years and was asked one question in all of it, is now
asked something roughly every forty minutes, has the exact figure, and delivers the only joke in the
scene with no expression whatever. Nothing here has been restored. It is a beginning with the loss
written into the first page.

## Dialogue
- Ollo {hope}: They voted. Sixty of them, in this square, over a thing I never stood for.
- Bron: The bakery?
- Ollo: The bakery's got two on it now and they're both better at it than me. No. The book.
- Ollo: Keeper of the town book. Which is a ledger. Of who lives in Tellwater. Which is sixty people.
- Ollo: Page one. I've had to start it at page one, because there isn't a page before it.
- Ollo: Sixty names and no dates earlier than this year, because there aren't any earlier than this year.
- Ollo: Do you know how much paper a town is? Nobody tells you. Nobody tells you anything, ever.
- Ollo: I've a drawer of it. A drawer. In a house they've also voted me, which I also did not want.
- Bron: You're pleased.
- Ollo: I am nothing of the sort.
- Bron: You've still got the badge on.
- Ollo: It's mine. I was here eleven years. Nobody is having it back.
- Bron: Sem-Dree. How many people have asked you anything this year?
- Sem: Four hundred and eighty-one. That is one about every forty minutes that the sun is up.
- Sem: I preferred the previous arrangement.
