# Scene 0785: That's Not an Argument

- type: talk
- location: a wide table under a hanging lamp with a large plain map unrolled and weighted at the corners, coloured pins in clusters across it, a box of pins and a rule, flat light from directly above, night
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip, Hesk
- mood: confront, then tense

## Beat
Bron takes it apart, and he is right. He is not shouting, he is not stupid, and every single
objection he raises is a good one: Hesta is a common name; four hundred houses will hold sixty
widows; people move, and the whole sheet is a record of people being moved; and a list in a drawer
is a thing somebody was paid to write. Zeph concedes a point because it is a fair point. Hesk says
one flat sentence, which is that she has already said her piece about that name once and will not
say it twice, and then she stops and does not help either side. Lyra asks him the archivists'
question, gently, once, and drops it the instant it is answered — and the answer is warm and
specific and lovely and comes out of him without a pause, and it happens exactly the same way every
year of his life. Then Bron rolls the map up and puts it inside his jacket, because it is paper, and
he paid for it, and he can put his hand on it. Somewhere below them, lamps are coming up the stair.

## Dialogue
- Bron {confront}: Sixty-one people had a mother called Hesta. That is the whole of what that says.
- Bron: It's a common name in a company town. Four hundred houses. Sixty of them will have had a widow in.
- Zeph: The sixty-one are not in Tellwater. They are in eleven towns and not one of the eleven is Tellwater.
- Bron: Then they were moved. The whole sheet is people being moved. You read it out yourself.
- Zeph: ...I did read it out myself.
- Bron: A list in a drawer isn't a proof. Somebody wrote it and somebody was paid by the day to write it.
- Bron: Bring me the thing underneath it. I'll sit up with the thing underneath it all night and not complain once.
- Hesk: I said my piece about that name once, on a terrace, in the rain. I'll not say it twice.
- Bron: Good.
- Lyra: Bron. The autumn fair — the one you went to every year. What happened the day before it?
- Bron: Day before? Shutters down and the stove out. She'd not leave a fire in an empty house.
- Bron: And I'd carry the stool down to the square the night before so we'd somewhere to sit. Every year of it.
- Lyra {tense}: ...Thank you.
- Bron: I'm keeping this. It's paper, I paid for it, and I can put my hand on it.
