# Scene 0610: Saltmouth

- location: a labour town of plank bunk rows on white salt crust at the foot of a broken stone ring stump, a bore head derrick above the roofs, a crawler depot fence, hard flat noon light, no shadows
- characters: Bron, Pip, Ket, Hesk
- mood: busy, then something older than the town
- staging: The broken ring stump and the bore derrick stand on the right of every panel and rise out of the top of the frame. The open salt and the way in are on the left. The party stands on the left facing right; the shift crowd walks from right to left across them

## Beat
A town laid out by an accounts department. Bunk rows in numbered lines, one square, one counting
house, a care-house at the end of the street with the cleanest windows in Saltmouth, and standing
over the whole arrangement a dead three-hundred-foot ring stump with birds going in and out of it
and a company derrick driven through its floor. The shift is changing and two thousand people are
walking the same way at the same speed, and none of it is grim: it is busy, organised, and working.
Pip reads the town off its fittings. Hesk watches the crowd rather than the tower. Ket stops under
the stump, looks up for a long beat, and gives a name and a year and does not explain either of them.

## Dialogue
- Pip [1] {tense}: They've numbered the rows. Who numbers the row a person sleeps in?
- Hesk [2]: An accounts man. That's a town with one employer. You don't argue with it, you queue at it.
- Pip [3]: A meter. On the standpipe. On water, out here, where there isn't any.
- Hesk [3]: That's what a meter's for.
- Pip [4] {sorrow}: How long's that thing been dead?
- Ket [5]: Semmet went out in ten sixty-one. Forty-six years in Deep.
- Hesk [6]: You knew it.
- Ket [7]: I knew the keeper. Ol-Semmet. He is still at the foot of it. He will have swept this morning.
