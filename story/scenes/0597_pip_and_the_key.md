# Scene 0597: Pip and the Key

- type: talk
- location: a squared sandstone door-mouth standing open on black darkness, its cut-stone fill stacked aside in numbered rows, a mortar tool and a coil of rope on the steps, low evening sun from the left
- characters: Pip, Hesk
- mood: hope, then quiet

## Beat
Two people sitting on the step of an open door in the dark. Pip has been looking at Hesk's door-key
for two days — a bar of iron the length of a forearm with no wards and no teeth — and finally asks
what it opens. Nothing. It is not a key, it is a tally stick, and every notch on it is a doorward
of the Fifth, back further than the village can read. Pip asks to hold it and Hesk lets her, which
is enormous, and neither of them says so. Then Pip, who cannot leave a personal subject alone
without joking, makes the joke, and Hesk does not laugh and does not fill the silence either.

## Dialogue
- Pip {hope}: Right. Two days. What does it open?
- Hesk: Nothing.
- Pip: It's a foot and a half of iron with no wards and no teeth and you sleep with it on your shoulder.
- Hesk: It opens nothing. It isn't a key. It's a tally stick.
- Hesk: Every notch is a doorward of the Fifth. You start at the thin end, where the iron's worn.
- Hesk: More than the village can read. The first forty are gone off it. Mine is the fourth from that end.
- Pip: Fourth from the end. So there's room past you for three more.
- Hesk: If the iron's kind.
- Pip: Can I hold it?
- Hesk: ...
- Hesk: Both hands. It's heavier than it looks.
- Pip: I've never owned anything older than my boots. Care-house issue, resoled four times.
- Pip {sorrow}: Sixteen years and one pair of boots. They were very good boots.
