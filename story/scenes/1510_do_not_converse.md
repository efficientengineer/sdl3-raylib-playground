# Scene 1510: Do Not Converse

- type: talk
- location: the gallery rim of a great oval chamber of fitted stone, stone standing posts along it, stepped tiers dropping away below, a seam face filling the back wall, faint even light from that face
- characters: Bron, Sefa, Hesk, Zeph, Pip, Ollo, Ket
- mood: tense
- backdrop: 1505_the_seam_face:1

## Beat
The last ordinary conversation any of them will have, held in whispers at a stone post with the
chord coming up out of the floor. Sefa quotes a standing company notice she countersigned herself,
hears the sentence leave her mouth in a room where she can see the woman it was written about, and
stops. Nobody helps her with it. Hesk wants all seven to go down together and is overruled by a bad
reason she cannot argue with, because the real reason is one Bron will not say out loud. Pip says
she is going anyway, is told no, says "right", and comes down the steps behind him about forty
seconds later, and Bron does not send her back. Hesk's last piece of advice is the most practical
thing anybody says in the chapter, and she has no idea what she has just handed him.

## Dialogue
- Sefa {tense}: The Fair Copy. Do not converse.
- Sefa: ...That is a standing notice. I countersigned it in ninety-four.
- Sefa: I have just quoted my own filing at a woman I can see from here.
- Hesk: We go down together. Seven of us, one stair, nobody out in front.
- Bron: One voice.
- Hesk: Whose.
- Bron: Mine. There's four hundred of her. She'll not hear seven.
- Hesk: That's not an argument. That's a thing you've already decided.
- Bron: Yes.
- Pip: I'm coming down with you.
- Bron: No.
- Pip: Right.
- Hesk: Say your name first when you get to her. A stranger with a name is a person. One without is a noise.
