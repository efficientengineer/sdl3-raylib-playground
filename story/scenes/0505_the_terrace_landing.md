# Scene 0505: The Terrace Landing

- location: a sheer sandstone cliff face with nine squared door-mouths in a row along its foot, a small flat-roofed stone house on the ledge above each one, stepped barley fields below, early sun from the left
- characters: Bron, Pip, Ket, Cassa
- mood: wonder, then something colder
- staging: The cliff and its nine door-mouths fill the right of the frame and rise above everything. The party stands on the left of the landing facing right and up. The sledge and the rails are behind them at the far left

## Beat
The sledge sets six people down at a rail stop at the foot of a cliff with nine squared black
notches cut along its base, a village on the ledges above, and eleven hundred feet of cut steps
between. The scale takes the top off the conversation. Cassa Tessen will not climb and is not
embarrassed about it; her line's record does not go up there, so neither does she. Bron pays her
and she arranges to come back in six days. Ket counts the doors out loud, gets nine, and says that
is correct, and something in the way she says it makes Bron stop looking at the cliff and start
looking at her. She does not explain and nobody asks.

## Dialogue
- Cassa [1] {wonder}: Nine. I have them as nine, and I have never been nearer to them than this rail.
- Cassa [2]: This is where I stop. My line holds nothing above the landing, so I hold nothing above the landing.
- Pip [2]: You're not even a bit curious?
- Cassa [3]: Curious, yes. Climbing, no. Six days, first light, and I will count you aboard.
- Ket [4] {tense}: Nine doors. Nine households sitting on top of them. Eleven hundred feet of steps.
- Ket [5]: Nine. That is correct.
- Ket [6]: I have been told nine for eight hundred years. I am now at nine myself. That is better.
- Ket [7]: We should start. The light comes off that face at four.
