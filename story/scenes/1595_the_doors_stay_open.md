# Scene 1595: The Doors Stay Open

- location: the ninth squared door-mouth in a sandstone cliff foot standing wide open on darkness, its cut-stone fill stacked aside, worn steps in front, a line of people coming up out of it, grey first light from the left
- characters: Hesk, Bron, Nona
- mood: sorrow, then hope, then wonder
- staging: The open door-mouth is at the right of frame with the dark of the passage behind it. The rail cut and the grey daylight come from the left. Hesk and Bron stand at the threshold on the right facing left; the people coming up out of the dark also face left, toward the light

## Beat
First light, sideways down a rail cut, into a doorway that has been shut for one thousand one
hundred and seven years. The first person out of Thurn is a woman who bakes, and she is delighted by
the light and has no idea she has never seen it before, and she will not have this morning next
week. It is a grey cold dawn and there is no glory in it: a long job is starting and it will take
months. Hesk stands at the threshold with a pocket notebook and a carpenter's pencil and asks every
one of them for a name and a trade and writes down whatever they say this week, because a line in a
book is a line in a book. The door is not closed behind them and will not be. The last doorward of
the Ninefold is standing in an open door, and she is watching it, and those are two different jobs
that she is now doing at once.

## Dialogue
- Hesk [1] {sorrow}: Mind the step going out. It's worn hollow and it'll have you.
- Nine [2] {wonder}: What's that at the end? That colour. What is that?
- Bron [2]: It's the morning. That's outside.
- Nine [3]: Is it that colour every time?
- Bron [3]: ...No.
- Hesk [4]: Name, and what you do.
- Nine [5]: Nine. I bake. It's Nine this week. It'll be something else next week.
- Hesk [6] {hope}: We live over the door. We keep it open. We write down who comes out, and we teach the next one.
