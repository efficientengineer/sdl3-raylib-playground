# Scene 0486: One Paper Sleeve

- type: talk
- location: a shop counter with shallow trays of paper sleeves in seasonal colours under a glass top, a brass till, a tally frame on the wall behind, a lamp on a bracket at the left, evening
- characters: Pip, Zeph
- mood: hope -> dread

## Beat
The unsealed bill asks for one paper sleeve, by receipt number, paid for in full and never collected.
The shop clerk finds it in four minutes, because the filing here is excellent and he is proud of it,
and he explains the retail line on the way — the season's colour, what a Comfort costs, and how long
a special order takes. He is pleasant and he is not hiding anything. Then Pip, holding it up to the
lamp, notices that it does not do what the ones in the tray do. He looks. He is sorry, and he means
it, and he is also correct about the returns policy, and both of those things are true at the same
time. He countersigns the form.

## Dialogue
- Pip {hope}: Receipt number. Four, four, one, eight, nought, six.
- Clerk: Four four one eight nought six. One moment. Our filing is very good.
- Clerk: There. Four minutes. Paid in full on the eleventh of Rime, never collected.
- Pip: Two years it's been sat there?
- Clerk: Special order, that one. Two years to fill. Most of what's on the counter is Comfort — your evening's wage.
- Clerk: Season colour's the mulberry this year. Next year it'll be the blue and everyone will say the blue is better.
- Pip: The ones in your tray move. There's a sort of crawl in them. This one's flat.
- Clerk: ...Ah. May I? — Yes. That's spent.
- Clerk: It does happen. I'm sorry. Spent stock isn't returnable, I'm afraid; it's on the back of the receipt.
- Clerk: I am sorry, though. Properly.
- Pip: Do you know whose it was?
- Clerk {dread}: No. We never do. Sign there and I'll countersign.
