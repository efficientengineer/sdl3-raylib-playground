# Scene 1435: The Boy Who Got Cold

- type: talk
- location: a wide swept street of fitted stone flags between stone housefronts with square doorways, tall lamp columns at intervals, a stone water trough running with clear water, warm steady light from the columns above head height
- characters: Pip, Bron, Hesk, Ollo
- mood: sorrow, then hope
- backdrop: 1415_the_lit_street:1

## Beat
The party beds down against a warm wall in a city with no night, and everybody is too hot. Hesk
complains about the heat continuously and with real feeling, because that is what Kell do. Pip, who
has been carrying the same joke since chapter two, says it, and then hears herself say it, and stops
dead. She does not apologise; she has no way to. Bron says the thing he has been working up to for
four chapters: he still has the winter, he knows exactly whose it is, and he intends to keep telling
it out loud with the name of the woman it belongs to attached to it. He does not say he feels hollow
or false, because he does not think in those words; he states a decision about what he is going to do
with a story. Hesk, who has never once softened anything for anybody, approves. Ollo hands out food
nobody asked for, which is how he says the thing he cannot say.

## Dialogue
- Hesk {sorrow}: The stone's warm. I've had my hand flat on this wall an hour and it's still warm.
- Pip: That's the fourth time you've said it.
- Hesk: It'll be the fifth in a minute.
- Pip: Bron doesn't mind it. Bron likes it. — You got cold once, didn't you? You lot sleep in snowdrifts for fun.
- Pip: ...
- Pip: ...I've had that one in my mouth for a year and I never heard it once.
- Bron: Say it. I've no quarrel with it.
- Bron: I got lost above the fold at nine. Two nights. Bored, then frightened, then carried down.
- Bron: It's hers. Maren Ostry's. She was nine in it, and I'm going to keep saying so out loud.
- Bron: I'm not giving it back and I'm not putting it down. I'll just say whose it is when I tell it.
- Hesk {hope}: That's the first sensible thing anybody's said since the stair.
- Ollo: Right. Sit down, all of you. There's bread, and there's the end of the cheese.
- Ollo: Pip, you're having the cheese.
- Pip: I don't want the—
- Ollo: You're having the cheese.
