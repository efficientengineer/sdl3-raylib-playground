# Scene 0491: Night on the Salt

- type: talk
- location: a laid-rail siding on white crust at dusk, a sledge with its sail furled on the spar, a small fire in a salt-brick ring, bedrolls laid out on the deck, firelight from the centre, cold sky
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Ket
- mood: hope -> wonder

## Beat
Watch, on the last night before they leave Braid, and nothing happens. Pip and Zeph are asleep on the
deck. Lyra asks Bron the sort of question she asks, which is always one more than the conversation
needs, and he answers it easily and warmly and at some length, because he likes the answer and he
always tells it well. Lyra says it is a good story, and means it. Ket, who does not sleep and has not
been asked anything, gives a figure about the weather that year. Nobody picks it up. Nobody frowns.
Bron rolls over and goes to sleep happy, which is the point of the scene.

## Dialogue
- Lyra {hope}: Is it your watch or mine?
- Bron: Mine. You've been up since the stacks.
- Lyra: What's the first thing you remember?
- Bron: Standing on a stool in our kitchen at Tellwater. Five, about. Small enough that the stool mattered.
- Bron: Ice on the inside of the window, and the pot going, and the whole room white with it.
- Bron: She put my hands flat on the table so I'd stop reaching. Told me to count to a hundred. I got to eleven.
- Bron: Then she gave me the crust off the top and told me off for eating it standing up.
- Bron: That's the first thing there is. I've had it all my life.
- Lyra: That's a good story.
- Bron {wonder}: It was a good morning.
- Ket: Ten eighty-nine was the softest winter in sixty years.
- Ket: Four above at Tellwater, on the average. Nothing froze that year.
- Bron: Mm. Night.
