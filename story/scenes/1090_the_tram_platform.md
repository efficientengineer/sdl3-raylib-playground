# Scene 1090: The Tram Platform

- type: talk
- location: a short brick platform beside laid rail, an iron bench bolted to the boards, a lamp post with an unlit lantern, a stack of crated freight under canvas, cold grey morning, flat light from the right
- characters: Bron, Pip
- mood: sorrow, then hope

## Beat
An hour later, waiting for the Vantage tram. Pip saw the whole of it from the corner and does not
ask him how he is, because she would not want to be asked. She sits down next to him and talks
rubbish about the crawler and the timetable and Zeph until there is somewhere to put things. Then
Bron asks her, out of nowhere, the personal question he has never asked her, and for the first time
in ten chapters she answers one straight, with no joke in front of it and none behind it. It is
three lines long and it is about boots and days and whose they are, and she does not cry and does
not ask him for anything. Then she asks him one back, and it is small and practical, and his answer
is about tomorrow. Neither of them says one word about the laundry.

## Dialogue
- Pip {sorrow}: Crawler's throwing a plate again. Hesk says it's the cold. Hesk says everything's the cold.
- Pip: Eight-ten to Vantage. Calls at Dunnow and nowhere else, about which Zeph has strong feelings.
- Pip: He read me the whole timetable last night. In a voice. There was a voice.
- Bron: Pip.
- Bron: What was in the drawer.
- Pip: ...
- Pip: Eleven years of afternoons. Sixty-two of them kept. The rest went for boots and a bed and the winter coal.
- Pip: If I took them back they'd be hers. Some kid with my name who never got out of Emberrow.
- Pip: I got out. She didn't. I'm not taking her days off her to make up the difference.
- Pip: Right. My turn. Are you eating today, or are we doing yesterday again?
- Bron: I'll eat on the tram.
- Bron {hope}: And the crawler plate gets done before Vantage. Ask Hesk what she wants off me.
