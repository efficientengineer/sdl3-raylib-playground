## Beat
The first minute of the game, at the end of a training session the player has just played and lost. {{MENTOR}} built these four machines out of wood, iron, rope and counterweights after his knee ended his hunting, and each one teaches a hunter one thing. {{HERO}} has beaten three of them and has spent all afternoon attacking the fourth, which is a counterweighted arm on a pivot: every hit he lands winds it up, and it swings back. He has just walked into that arm and it has caught his wrist. {{HEALER}} watches this from the wall between the two houses most days, calls the swing before it lands and finds it funny, and comes down and closes the wrist anyway with a small warm light in her hands, quickly and easily, teasing him the whole time — magic here is ordinary and it does light work, and this is light work. {{MENTOR}} is at his bench and will not explain the machine he built.

## Dialogue
- {{HEALER}} [1] {wonder}: There he goes. He's at the last one again.
- {{HEALER}} [2]: Watch the arm, {{HERO}}! It comes back on the left. It always comes back on the left —
- {{HERO}} [3]: I had it! I hit that thing eleven times!
- {{HEALER}} [4]: You hit it eleven times. It hit you once. That's the whole afternoon.
- {{HERO}} [5]: My wrist's fine.
- {{HEALER}}: Put it down, then. I can't patch a wrist you're holding on to.
- {{HERO}}: How is anyone supposed to beat that thing?
- {{HERO}}: It hits back harder every single time I hit it!
- {{HEALER}}: I know. I've watched you find that out four times this week.
- {{HERO}}: Four?
- {{HEALER}} [6]: Four. Hold still, this takes a moment.
- {{HEALER}}: There. Closed. You'll have the bruise for a week, which serves you right.
- {{HERO}}: That's it? That's all of it?
- {{HEALER}}: That's all of it. It's a wrist, {{HERO}}, not a war.
- {{HERO}}: You make it look like nothing.
- {{HEALER}}: It is nothing. Bring me something interesting one day.
- {{MENTOR}} [7]: Again. From the gate.
- {{HERO}}: Again? I can't hold a stick!
- {{MENTOR}}: Tomorrow, then.
- {{HERO}}: {{MENTOR}} — say it straight. Am I ready or am I not?
- {{MENTOR}} {sorrow}: Not quite ready yet.
- {{HERO}}: That's what you said in the spring!
