# Scene 1120: Whatever Is in the Till

- type: talk
- location: a neat sandstone town square with a raised stone well at its centre, swept flagstones, whitewashed housefronts with green shutters, a painted board over the bakery with no words, bright clean midmorning sun from the left
- characters: Ollo, Pip, Bron, Lyra
- mood: hope

## Beat
A bill has gone up on the board by the well, pinned crooked, stamped in wax with a company posting
badge because the man who wrote it has no seal of his own and is embarrassed about it. The work is
sixty trestles out of the fair-ground loft and up in the square by dusk. The fee is whatever is in
the bakery till, which is company money he is not entitled to keep and intends to spend anyway.
Lyra asks one question too many and gets an answer. Pip takes the bill down before Bron can open
his mouth.

## Dialogue
- Ollo {hope}: That's not — I know it isn't a proper seal. I haven't got a seal. I pressed the badge into the wax.
- Pip: It's stamped, it's numbered, and it's yours. That's a seal. Third Line satisfied.
- Bron: What's the work?
- Ollo: Sixty trestles in the pole-store loft. And the bunting, and a fiddle case. Up in the square by dusk.
- Ollo: The fee's whatever's in the till. I'll tell you now it's company money and I'm not entitled to a penny of it.
- Pip: That's our favourite sort.
- Lyra: The fair's five weeks early.
- Ollo: It moves earlier every year. This year it moves a great deal earlier. There'll be no town in Fallow.
- Lyra: Who is it for, then? If they're all leaving.
- Ollo: For the ones who are leaving.
- Pip: Taken. Don't argue, Bron, you were going to say yes slowly.
