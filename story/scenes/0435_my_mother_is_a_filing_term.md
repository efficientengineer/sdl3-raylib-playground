# Scene 0435: My Mother Is a Filing Term

- type: talk
- location: a row of open-fronted sheds of stone and plank on the seabed floor, rail sledges on laid track inside them, furled sails on spars, coils of hand-crank chain, hard flat afternoon light, salt glare beyond
- characters: Zeph, Pip
- mood: tense -> sorrow

## Beat
Pip finds him out of the wind at the back of a shed, sitting on a coil of chain. She does not ask the
obvious question, because she never does; she asks a stupid one, which is how she gets at things.
Zeph answers it fast and brilliantly and at length, because facts are where he goes when he is
cornered, and he is very good company for about ninety seconds. Then she asks who signed it, and he
gives her the office and not the person, and she asks again, and he gives her the office again. The
third time he says the thing he has clearly said to himself a great many times, and changes the
subject so hard it snaps. Pip lets him have it.

## Dialogue
- Pip {tense}: How long would it take me to get in there? If I queued.
- Zeph: You would not get in. No card. A card wants a line name and a Braid address.
- Zeph: Two till four, twelve at a time. Twenty-nine years to put this town through its own door once.
- Pip: And the rent?
- Zeph: One mark a year, to eleven eighty-seven. My great-grandmother cut the stone of the third hall.
- Pip: Who signs a thing like that?
- Zeph: The Registrar signs the schedule.
- Pip: The name, Zeph.
- Zeph: The Registrar. Of the Clement Works.
- Zeph {sorrow}: My mother is a filing term. Next question. The stacks flood at the third floor, chest deep, and we go in tonight.
- Pip: ...Tonight, then.
