# Scene 1230: Ross Kettle Outside His Own House

- type: talk
- location: a narrow stone housefront on a steep street, a scrubbed doorstep, a chair set out beside the door, a tanner's leather apron on a hook in the jamb, hazy low sun from the right, late afternoon
- characters: Ross, Dalla, Bron
- mood: sorrow

## Beat
Four chapters after Maren gave him fifty-eight years back in one piece in front of the party, Ross
Kettle is sitting outside his own house in a chair, calm, thin, and mostly here. He remembers the
party and he is not angry with them. He says one clear thing about what it is like and then loses
the thread of it, and he puts his first wife and his second wife in the same sentence and does not
notice, and Dalla, in the doorway with a cloth in her hands, notices every time. Nobody offers
anything. Nobody proposes anything. Dalla walks them to the corner, asks a question they cannot
answer, asks for nothing, and goes back in.

## Dialogue
- Ross {sorrow}: You're the ones from the yard. I know the faces. Sit on the step, there's room on it.
- Ross: Dalla keeps that chair out for me. Enna used to keep it out. Same chair, I think.
- Ross: I'll tell you what it's like and then I'll lose it, so have it now.
- Ross: It all came back at once and it's all today. Fourteen years, and every one of them is this afternoon.
- Ross: Enna died on a Tuesday and I've buried her twice. One of them was last month.
- Ross: Dalla'll tell you. Enna was there, she'll tell you. ...Who was I saying?
- Dalla: ...
- Bron: Do you want us to go?
- Ross: Stay. There was a thing I meant to tell you and it was important.
- Dalla: He sleeps in the chair. He won't come in after dark. I've stopped asking him to.
- Dalla: The woman who did it to him. Does she know what it's like after?
- Dalla: No. Mind the step going down, it's worn through the middle.
