# Scene 0150: The Door-Mouth

- type: talk
- location: a squared door-mouth in a sandstone cliff foot with its cut-stone fill stacked aside, a spoil barrow and a coil of rope, cold grey dawn light from the left
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip
- mood: wonder, then wrong

## Beat
Morning, the hill path above the town, the roofs and the cisterns of Sallowgate small below them.
A thousand-year-old sealed door that is not sealed: the fill is out of it, every block, and stacked
tidily to one side by somebody who was not in a hurry. Zeph reads the doorward marks off the jamb
and gets most of them right and is insufferable about it. Bron looks at the stack for a beat too
long and says nothing. Pip is inside before anyone has agreed to go in.

## Dialogue
- Zeph {wonder}: Doorward marks. Cut, not painted. Nobody has recut those in a thousand years.
- Zeph: That is a hold name. That is a count. That one is a date and it is older than this town.
- Pip: What do they mean?
- Zeph: They mean exactly what they say. What they are for is a different question and I do not know it.
- Lyra: And this?
- Pip: Someone's chipped the whole fill out. Every block.
- Bron: ...
- Bron: Stacked it in rows.
- Pip: I'm going in.
- Lyra {tense}: Who tidies up after breaking into somewhere?
