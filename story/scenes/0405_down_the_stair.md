# Scene 0405: Down the Stair

- type: narration
- mood: three days west

## Beat
The morning after the camp on the salt. Three days of walking west beside two lines of laid rail,
with the stair and the Rim road behind them and no town ahead that will sell to four names that went
round on a slate. The narration takes an inventory and then a distance, and neither is encouraging.
The last thing it gives is the one fact that makes the walk bearable, and nobody in the party says
it out loud: the rails are in use.

## Dialogue
- Narrator {sorrow}: Three days west of the stair, beside two lines of laid rail that do not turn and do not stop.
- Narrator: Behind them, six towns on the Rim road that will not sell bread to four names off a slate.
- Narrator: In hand: a bill cleared and a poster who never paid. One axe. One mace. One staff. Two knives.
- Narrator: Ahead: no shade at any hour, no water they know of, and no turning shorter than the one behind.
- Narrator {wonder}: And the rails run west and do not stop. Somebody laid them. Somebody is still using them.
