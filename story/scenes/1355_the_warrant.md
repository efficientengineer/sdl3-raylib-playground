# Scene 1355: The Warrant

- location: a rail head on the dry seabed below a terrace town, four loaded sledges strung out on laid rail, a chalked board on a post beside the first, flat overcast daylight from the left, dust raised low
- characters: Ivo, Kerrow, Hanna
- mood: confront, then tense
- staging: Kerrow stands at the right of frame beside the company carriage with her clerks, facing left. Ivo comes up the platform from the left and faces right. The last sledges are running out to the left behind them. The party watches from the far left and does not move

## Beat
Wind off the crust, the last sledges going west, and a very large man walking the whole length of a
platform to arrest the chairman of the Clement Works. He does it quietly and correctly and it takes
a long time to cross the ground, and it is obviously not going to hold. He is not triumphant and he
has nothing to say for himself, then or ever. Kerrow takes the warrant, reads it properly — all of
it, at her own pace, in the wind — finds one clause she genuinely thinks is well drafted, says so,
hands it back, and gets into the carriage, because being arrested and being obeyed are two
different things and she has twelve hours of paperwork whichever way it goes. Kesk says one word in
a particular tone. Nobody raises a voice. The party is forty yards off and does not speak, and the
player, who watched a post room at Windrow, knows exactly what this is.
