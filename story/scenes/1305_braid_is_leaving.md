# Scene 1305: Braid Is Leaving

- location: a Vess town on a terrace lip stepping down onto white dry seabed, narrow tall stone houses with shuttered openings, laid rail running to a flat horizon, a patched stone ring tower behind, morning sun from the left
- characters: Bron, Zeph
- mood: orderly, beautiful, wrong
- staging: The party stands on the terrace road at the right of frame and looks down and left. The town, the terraces and the ring tower are on the right and above; the seabed and the rails run out to the left. Company fittings are always at the right of frame

## Beat
Day thirteen of fourteen. A town of seven thousand is going out onto a white salt plain on rails,
sledge by sledge, in good order, and singing, because a chant-line rehearses every day and a line
that is moving rehearses moving. It is the most beautiful thing in the act and it is a company
order being obeyed correctly. Nobody is panicking; nobody is looting; nobody resents the party.
Zeph, who was put out of this town's annex and has not come home since, stands on the lip and
cannot make a joke, which from him is an alarm, and nobody explains it for him. Ket stands a little
apart, looking at her own well, working out what a fifth of a well is worth with nobody left to
turn it by hand.

## Dialogue
- Bron [1] {sorrow}: How long have they been at this?
- Zeph [1]: Thirteen days. Two hundred and six sledges, and they have run every one of them full.
- Zeph [2]: They are singing. A line says its passage every day. A line that is moving says it moving.
- Bron [3]: Nobody's shouting.
- Zeph [4]: ...
- Bron [4]: You haven't said a word since the terrace road.
- Zeph [5]: I have it as forty-one paces from that doorway to the reading desks. I have never once walked it.
- Ket [6] {wonder}: Meddra runs at a fifth. Four of us and nine Vess hand-work it. The nine are on those sledges.
- Ket [6]: It wants eight hundred and eighty hours of hand-work in a year. Four of us cannot find that. It will go dark.
