# Scene 0725: The Founding Hall

- type: talk
- location: an old stone hall with a high beamed roof, a burned-edged job board on the back wall, long benches, a cold stove with a pile of unswept ash, a company notice board with no words, grey daylight from the left
- characters: Bron, Pip, Zeph, Hesk, Idda
- mood: sorrow, then tense

## Beat
The room the Even Hand started in, on Tier Two, older than the two tiers above it. Half of it is in
crates. Six years of lease notices, each one legal, each one correct, and the keeper has stopped
reading them properly and started stacking plates in straw. The stove is out and has been out long
enough for the ash to go grey, and Pip is the one who notices, and Bron takes the whole thing harder
than anything else in the chapter, which is how you know what he is. Into the middle of it walks a
schoolmistress from two tiers up with a sheet of paper she has clearly rewritten several times. She
has walked down because the boards up there belong to the company, and she has used them properly
for six years, and nothing has ever come of it. She is exhausted and embarrassed and entirely
specific. The fee is out of her own wages. The keeper chalks it and takes no tenth off it, and does
not say why, and nobody asks.

## Dialogue
- Stobb {sorrow}: Fifty-four years the hall's been in this room. Board's come off that wall twice, and once was to paint behind it.
- Bron: Who signed the notices?
- Stobb: All of them, in order, and every one of them is correct. That's what's the matter with them.
- Bron: Then don't pack.
- Stobb: I'm not packing because they're right. I'm packing because the crates came and I'd nowhere to put them.
- Pip: Your stove's out.
- Stobb: It is.
- Idda {tense}: I'm sorry. Is this where one — I've written it out. It's all on the one sheet, you needn't take my word for any of it.
- Idda: Tier Four. The school. Something wakes the children at night, and I have kept the nights. Six years of them.
- Idda: The boards up there are company boards. I have used them properly and correctly and nothing has ever come of it.
- Idda: I am not asking anyone to believe me. I am asking for one person to come up and stand in a corridor and listen.
- Idda: It's out of my own wages. It isn't much. I've put down exactly what it is.
- Stobb: No tenth off that one. Chalk it as it stands.
