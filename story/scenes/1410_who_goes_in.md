# Scene 1410: Who Goes In

- type: talk
- location: a short plank platform on a cut-stone ledge beside laid rail, an iron lamp stanchion, a stacked pile of sleepers and spikes, a squared ancient doorway in the wall behind, lamplight from above, no daylight
- characters: Hesk, Pip, Sefa, Ket, Bron, Zeph, Ollo
- mood: tense, then hope
- backdrop: 1405_the_ninth_door:5

## Beat
Hesk plants herself in the gap and will not move until every one of them has said a name and a trade
out loud, slowly, so she can get it down. She never says why, and nobody asks her, and the scene only
works because she never explains. Sefa enjoys it more than she has enjoyed anything in a month and
spells her line name. Ket volunteers her post, her muster year and her remaining lights, unprompted,
because that is what an entry ought to contain. Pip gives a false name to be funny and Hesk simply
waits, and Pip gives the real one and then cannot look at anybody. Bron says his last: a surname a
clerk took off a survey map, given to a woman who is writing it in a book. Nobody comments on it and
nobody comforts him.

## Dialogue
- Hesk {tense}: Nobody goes past me until I've had it. Name, and your trade. Slow enough to write.
- Pip: Or what? You'll shut the door?
- Hesk: Yes.
- Sefa: Sefa Quill. Q, U, I, double L. Registrar of the Clement Works. Fourteen years of it.
- Sefa: Put the years on. Nobody has ever once asked me for them and I find I want them down.
- Ket: Ket-Ossun. Keeper, Meddra. Last muster, minus one hundred and forty-one. Two lights.
- Hesk: I didn't ask what you'd got left.
- Ket: An entry should say what stood at the door. In a year you will want the number.
- Pip: Sorrel. Pip Sorrel. Doors, windows, and other people's pockets.
- Hesk: ...
- Pip: ...
- Pip {sorrow}: Rime. Pip Rime. Locks and blades. Don't any of you look at me.
- Hesk: I had Marrick and the reader on the stair coming down. Baker and reader, both in.
- Bron {hope}: Bron Sallow. Hunter. Out of Sallowgate.
