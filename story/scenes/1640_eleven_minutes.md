# Scene 1640: Eleven Minutes

- type: talk
- location: one laundry doorway close, wet brick jamb, steam pouring past a hanging sheet, a copper tub and a scrubbing board inside, a chipped enamel cup on the step, low warm sun from the left, evening
- characters: Bron, Anneke
- mood: sorrow, then hope

## Beat
High 1108, a cold street in Windrow with steam rolling out of a doorway at ankle height. A laundry
presser who was resettled in 1093 with a stock template and filed as having no dependents has read
the list in the papers like everybody else and has worked out that she is on it. She is not
distraught and she is not asking to be comforted; she has a copper on and a fixed amount of time,
and she wants the young Kell hunter who stood in her doorway a year ago and would not take a cup of
tea to say what he knows. He says it: her name, the town, the house, the year, the fact of a son,
and that there is nothing to give back to either of them and there never was. It takes eleven
minutes. It is not enough, and both of them know it while he is still talking. They do not
recognise each other. Nothing is restored. At the end of it she arranges a next meeting, because
that is what a practical woman does with a thing she cannot mend.

## Dialogue
- Anneke {sorrow}: You're the one that wouldn't take the tea.
- Anneke: I've read the list. Four weeks in the paper, and half this street says somebody got it up.
- Anneke: Brae, A. Windrow. Laundry. And a column saying where I was before, and I wasn't there.
- Anneke: I'd know if I'd been at Tellwater. That's what none of them understand. I don't, and I would.
- Anneke: You knew something a year ago. You stood in that doorway and you didn't say it.
- Bron: Yes.
- Anneke: Then say it. I've a copper on and about eleven minutes before it wants me.
- Bron: Anneke Brae. Born at Tellwater. You lived in house thirty-one, and you were in it in ninety-three.
- Bron: The roster for that house is one adult and one child. You had a son. He was nine.
- Anneke: And him?
- Bron: Alive. Grown. Working.
- Bron: There's nothing to give back. Not to you and not to him. There never was and there never will be.
- Anneke: ...
- Anneke: That's the eleven minutes, near enough.
- Anneke {hope}: I put the kettle on at six, the first of the month. Would that be all right?
- Bron: Yes.
