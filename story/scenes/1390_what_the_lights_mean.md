# Scene 1390: What the Lights Mean

- type: talk
- location: an open flat iron rail car with a hand-crank pump handle at its centre, a lamp on a bracket at the front, tool boxes lashed at the corners, cut-stone walls close on both sides, lamplight forward, no daylight
- characters: Ket, Ollo
- mood: sorrow, then hope

## Beat
Riding the maintenance rail out, an hour of tunnel wall going past, the others asleep along the
deck. Ollo asks the question everybody has been too polite to ask for nine chapters and asks it the
way he asks about an oven: what are the lights, what do they do, how many have you had. She tells
him exactly, with figures, and she is not upset by any of it, because it is a specification and she
has had twelve hundred years to know it. Partway through the answer the third one goes out. She
corrects her own figure without a flicker and carries on with the sentence. Ollo puts food in front
of her, which she can do very little with and takes anyway out of courtesy, and then corrects him
about the bread at length and accurately. He says something kind and clumsy about how long twelve
hundred years is. She says the thing about the joke. No comfort is offered and none is wanted.

## Dialogue
- Ollo {sorrow}: Can I ask about the lights. I've been not asking for a while and it's started being rude.
- Ket: Four a side. Eight in all. They are a reading of what is left in me and they do not come back on.
- Ket: Eight at muster. Six by the year six hundred. Five in ten eighty-two, when they metered my well.
- Ket: Four on the Flats. Three, thirty-four days ago, on a gallery above the Ossun head. So I am at three—
- Ket: Two. Correct. Two, as of now. One a side, which is tidy, and I have never had that before.
- Ollo: ...
- Ket: At none I stop. I do not fall down and there is no noise. I stop, and whatever I was doing stays undone.
- Ket: Six hundred and eleven of us are left. There will not be a six hundred and twelfth.
- Ollo {hope}: Here. Eat that, it's the heel and it's the best of it.
- Ket: Thank you. I will have about a quarter of it and it will last me nine days. — Your crumb is tight on the left.
- Ket: Your oven pulls about forty degrees hotter at the back, and you have been turning the loaf to hide it for eleven years.
- Ollo: ...Twelve hundred years. I've had forty-seven and I couldn't tell you where I put them.
- Ket: There is a joke in it. I am aware of the joke. So is Arro-Vintry, and his is a better one.
