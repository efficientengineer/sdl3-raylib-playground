# Scene 0275: Four Days' Wage

- type: talk
- location: a plank working floor at the bottom of a sandstone cut, a dead cutting rig of iron frame and brass valve gear standing over a shaft collar, coiled hose and a barrow, naphtha lamps on poles still burning, the hours before dawn
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip, Marek
- mood: nobody dead, nobody grateful
- backdrop: 0270_the_cutting_rig:1

## Beat
The rig is stopped, nobody is dead, and nobody is grateful. The crew are sitting down on the spoil
heap with their lamps still burning. Marek does the arithmetic out loud, without heat, because the
arithmetic is his entire argument. Pip makes a joke and it does not land, and she hears it not
landing, and it is the first time in the game her one defence fails her. Lyra does not apologise and
does not defend it either; she asks him what is at the bottom of the cut, and he tells her, because
it is not a secret. Then he tells them the field superintendent will be up at first light, and he
says it the way you say a thing you are looking forward to.

## Dialogue
- Marek {sorrow}: Eleven people. Four days. That's forty-four days of wage off this hill.
- Marek: The drive housing is a shop repair. That comes off the crew's bonus. Not off mine and not off yours.
- Pip: We could... help push it?
- Pip: ...
- Pip: Right. That's me finished. That's me not talking.
- Lyra: I'm not going to tell you we were right.
- Marek: Good. I'd have had to stand up.
- Lyra: What's at the bottom of the cut?
- Marek: A door. Cut stone, filled solid, older than the town. There's nine of them along this face.
- Marek: We don't break into them. We buy them. Somebody owns every one of those doors and gets paid.
- Zeph: Owns.
- Marek: Owns. Hill families. Leases and schedules, and the schedules are correct.
- Bron: Who signs them?
- Marek: Not me. I cut what I'm told to cut, where I'm told to cut it.
- Marek {tense}: Superintendent'll be up at first light. You can explain yourselves to him.
