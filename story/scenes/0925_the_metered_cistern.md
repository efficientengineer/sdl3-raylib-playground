# Scene 0925: The Metered Cistern

- type: talk
- location: three low plank buildings alone on white salt beside a rutted drove road, a raised stone cistern on timber legs between them, a brass meter box at the tap, a hitching rail, hard flat noon light
- characters: Bron, Zeph, Pip, Hesk, Nessa
- mood: funny, then a small cold thing

## Beat
Eleven people, three buildings, one metered cistern, and Nessa Tull, who sells water at a rate she
despises and tells you so at length while taking your money, because that is the arrangement she has
come to with herself. About a third of it is funny on purpose. Behind the station is the wreck of a
survey sledge she rents out for scrap, and while Zeph is poking at it he pulls a brass tag off the
frame with a study reference stamped on it and a year. Nobody in the party can do anything with it.
Nessa mentions, without knowing what she is saying, that a company man came out two years ago and
bought every other tag in the yard and paid over the odds for them.

## Dialogue
- Nessa {hope}: Two marks the barrel. I'll tell you now that's robbery, and I'll tell you again while you're paying.
- Bron: Two marks.
- Nessa: Rate's set in Vantage by a man who has never once been thirsty. I take his rate and I say what I like.
- Hesk: Whose meter is it?
- Nessa: Mine. Theirs. It's bolted to my wall and the key comes out of their office. You work it out, I never have.
- Zeph: Keeper. The sledge behind the shed. May I look at it?
- Nessa: You may have it, if you can lift it. There's nothing on it worth the barrel you just bought.
- Zeph: ...There is a tag wired to the frame. Brass, stamped, filed. The year on it is ninety-nine.
- Nessa: Tags. A company man came out two years back and bought every tag in that yard. Paid over the odds, too.
- Bron: Every tag but that one.
- Nessa: That one was under the runner. He'd not get down on his knees for it. They don't.
- Pip: What did he want them for?
- Nessa: Didn't say. Men like that never do, and I never ask, because I'd only get told.
