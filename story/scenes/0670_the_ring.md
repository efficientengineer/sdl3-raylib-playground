# Scene 0670: The Ring

- location: a timber derrick over a shaft collar on white salt, a winch house of corrugated iron beside it, a bank of valve gear on a plank stand, coiled cable, a row of tallow lamps on poles, cold clear night
- characters: Tibb, Sela
- mood: tense, then dread
- staging: The headframe and the shaft collar stand on the right of every panel. The open salt is on the left. The stillness travels from right to left across every panel it appears in; the party stands at the far left, outside it

## Beat
Twenty to five in the morning. Everybody is out and standing about in the cold being relieved about
it, drinking tea out of tin, complaining about the hour. Sela Marrin is thirty feet nearer the bore
than Tibb is, mid-sentence, holding a cup. Then the stillness goes out from the headframe across the
dust and the salt at a walking pace, and everything it crosses stops where it is: a man bends to his
boot and stays bent, a woman takes four more steps and then does not take a fifth, a thrown match
keeps falling. The ring reaches the party, stops forty feet short of them, and goes out. Nobody
screams. Nobody falls. There is no violence anywhere in this and there are no bodies. Sela is still
standing with the cup in her hand, and she is warm, and she is breathing, and she looks at Tibb with
mild friendly interest.
