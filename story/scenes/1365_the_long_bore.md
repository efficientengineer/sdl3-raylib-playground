# Scene 1365: The Long Bore

- location: a great cut-stone chamber with an ancient squared hall-mouth in its back wall, a brass bore head the size of a house bolted across the opening, catwalks and valve banks around it, lamps on stanchions, dust hanging still
- characters: Ket, Hesk, Ollo
- mood: dread
- staging: The party stands on the gallery at the left of frame and looks down and right. The shaft, the hall-mouth and the head are at the back and right; the stair out is behind them at the left. Everyone's eyes go down into the shaft

## Beat
Down through the service entrance, along two hundred yards of catwalk, and out onto a gallery rail
with the whole thing under them. The biggest draw head in the world, running at full, two hundred
feet below: a brass bell on a gimbal at the bottom of a lit shaft, throwing no light at all. The
only thing the machine does that anyone can see is that the air around it has stopped — a lamp
flame on the gallery stands dead straight, and the dust that came off their own boots is hanging
where they left it. Ket gives the rating, the actual figure and the difference, and then says what
shutting it down by hand will take, in wheels and in minutes and in hands. Hesk has spent
forty-four years keeping a door shut over exactly this and says the smallest sentence available.
Ollo has never seen anything industrial larger than an oven, asks what it is for, gets an honest
answer, does not understand it, and understands it perfectly.

## Dialogue
- Ket [1] {dread}: Nineteen feet across the bell. Eleven hundred and forty feet of shaft to the working face.
- Ket [2]: The gimbal is rated at eleven degrees. It is at sixteen. The difference is five and the difference is all of it.
- Ket [2]: Look at the flame. That is the whole of what it does. Everything near it stops moving.
- Ket [3]: To shut it: eleven wheels on the pedestal, in order, against the governor. Two of you on each of the last four.
- Ket [3]: Forty minutes. It will burn hands. I have no better way and I have thought about it since the stair.
- Hesk [4]: Nine doors.
- Hesk [5]: ...
- Ollo [6]: What is it for?
- Ket [6]: It draws the branch. What comes up the shaft goes into paper sleeves, and the sleeves go to the shops.
- Ollo [6]: ...Right. So that's the windows on Tier Three. The trays with the coloured papers laid out in them.
