# Scene 0160: The Fire

- map: ridge_camp
- type: talk
- location: a dip under the ridge out of the wind, one fire between two bedrolls, the grass they crossed all day black below them and the {{STAIR}} standing against the night sky behind the camp
- characters: {{HERO}}, {{HEALER}}
- mood: worn out, easy, then not
- dialogue_box: no

## Beat
First watch, night one, after the worst day of fighting either of them has had. {{HEALER}} has the
first watch because {{HERO}} is asleep on his feet, and she keeps the count of the day the way she
keeps everything, exactly. The thing he wants to say first is that the parry held: he waited for a
{{ROAD_CREATURE}} the way he waited for the machine, and it worked. Then he asks her about his father,
because she is the only person out here, and she gives him the one thing she remembers — the man came
home a day late off this run, every year. {{HERO}} was six when he died and has never been told that.
She knows why she remembers it, says so in one line, and sends him to sleep.

## Dialogue
- {{HERO}} {hope}: That was the worst day I have ever had. How many was that?
- {{HEALER}}: Nineteen. And I patched you four times, twice for the same arm. Some things do not change.
- {{HERO}}: You saw the first {{ROAD_CREATURE}}, though. I waited for it and I turned it!
- {{HEALER}}: You did. Two days ago you would be lying in that grass wrapped up like a parcel.
- {{HERO}} {wonder}: Did you know my father? Properly, I mean.
- {{HEALER}}: I was twelve, and he was the big one who came and went.
- {{HEALER}}: I remember one thing about this run. He came home a day late off it. Every year.
- {{HERO}} {sorrow}: A day late, every year? I was six when he died and no one ever told me that!
- {{HEALER}}: {{MENTOR}} used to stand at the gate and count the days.
- {{HERO}}: How do you even remember that? You were twelve.
- {{HEALER}}: I had lost mine three years before. You notice whose father still comes home.
- {{HERO}}: {{HEALER}} —
- {{HEALER}}: Sleep. I wake you at the turn.
- {{HERO}}: Wake me early. I want to see it in the light.
