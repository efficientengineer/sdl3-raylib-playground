# Scene 0160: The Fire

- map: ridge_camp
- type: talk
- location: a dip under the ridge out of the wind, one fire between two bedrolls, the grass they crossed all day black below them and {{STAIR}} standing against the night sky behind the camp
- characters: {{HERO}}, {{HEALER}}
- mood: worn out, easy, then not
- dialogue_box: no

## Beat
First watch, night one, after the worst day of fighting either of them has had. {{HEALER}} has the
first watch because {{HERO}} is asleep on his feet, and she keeps the count of the day the way she
keeps everything, exactly. He asks her about his father because she is the only person out here and
because he has been carrying the road all day, and she gives him the one thing she remembers: the man
came home a day late off this run, every year. {{HERO}} was six when he died and has never been told
that. She sends him to sleep before he can do anything about it.

## Dialogue
- {{HERO}} {hope}: My arms have gone. How many was that today?
- {{HEALER}}: Nineteen, and I patched you four times, twice for the same arm. Sleep — I wake you at the turn.
- {{HERO}} {wonder}: Did you know my father? Properly, I mean. You were in {{MENTOR}}'s yard.
- {{HEALER}}: I was twelve and he was the big one who came and went. I remember one thing about this run: he came home a day late off it. Every year.
- {{HERO}} {sorrow}: A day late, every year? I was six when he died and no one has ever told me that!
- {{HEALER}}: {{MENTOR}} used to stand at the gate and count. Sleep.
