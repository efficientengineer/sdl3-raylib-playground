# Scene 0689: Tibb and Sela After

- type: talk
- location: a clean plank-walled dormitory with twelve small beds and folded grey blankets, a warm stove at the centre, a shelf of enamel cups, a barred high window, even lamplight, early evening
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Pip, Tibb, Sela
- mood: sorrow

## Beat
The care-house day room, clean and warm and well lit, with forty-one calm people in it who are all
perfectly well. Sela Marrin is sitting up with a cup of tea, pleasant, interested and entirely
healthy, and she answers every question Tibb asks her warmly and wrongly. She does not know the
ledger joke. She does not know whose round it is. She is glad to hear about the bill. Twice in eleven
lines she asks him what his name is, and apologises nicely each time for forgetting it, and he tells
her, and she thanks him. He keeps going. He tells her the whole story of the bill that nearly killed
him, every word of it, and she laughs in all the right places, and at the end she says it sounds like
he had a good friend with him. Pip does not speak at all. Nobody consoles anybody. Tibb does not
name one single thing that is happening to him.

## Dialogue
- Tibb {sorrow}: There she is. Tea. They've given her the good chair as well.
- Sela: It is a good chair. Are you one of the ones from the yard?
- Tibb: I'm Tibb.
- Sela: Tibb. Thank you. I'm sorry, there's been a lot of people through.
- Tibb: There has. Listen — whose round is it? Second of Rime, eleven oh-three. You know this one.
- Sela: I don't, I'm afraid. Is it a good story?
- Tibb: It's a terrible story. It's the best thing I own.
- Sela: Then I'd like to hear it.
- Tibb: Right. So. The culvert above Windrow, nine goats, in the dark, and a ladder with one foot in a drain —
- Tibb: — and the rung goes, and I'm up to here in it, and somebody puts a hook through my collar and hauls.
- Sela: Nine goats.
- Tibb: Nine.
- Sela: ...I'm sorry, I've lost it again. What was your name?
- Tibb: Tibb.
- Sela {sorrow}: Tibb. That's a good story. It sounds like you had a good friend with you.
- Tibb: I did. I do.
- Tibb: Would you like to hear it again?
