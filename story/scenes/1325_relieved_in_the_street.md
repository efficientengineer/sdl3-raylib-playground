# Scene 1325: Relieved in the Street

- location: a row of open-fronted sheds of stone and plank on the seabed floor, rail sledges on laid track inside them, furled sails on spars, coils of hand-crank chain, hard flat afternoon light, salt glare beyond
- characters: Orrin, Hesk
- mood: sorrow, then confront
- staging: Orrin stands at the left of frame facing right. The Pale officer and the company sheds are on the right, facing left. The crews and handcarts fill the ground between them; the rails and the open salt run out behind the officer to the right

## Beat
Midday, with the last third of Braid loading around them. Orrin Cadder pulled his own crews off
three bore heads at first light on the first, without authority, and then spent the morning of the
second emptying streets the schedule had put on tomorrow, against a direct order, because he read
the same numbers Ket read and did not wait for anybody. A Pale officer reads him off his post in a
street in front of two hundred people who would follow him into a fire and have. He hands the
survey rule over handle first, and makes a joke, and the joke does not land, and he tries a second
one and that does not land either. Nobody in the crowd says a word. The party watches and does not
intervene and nobody thanks him. Hesk, who spat on this man's boots in her own council room, has
something on her face that is not sympathy and is not satisfaction, and she does not speak.

## Dialogue
- Officer [1] {sorrow}: Field Superintendent Orrin Cadder. You are relieved of your post from this hour, by direction of the chair.
- Orrin [1]: Marl. You've come ninety miles to read me a page I helped write.

- Officer [2]: Streets nine to fourteen cleared on the second, ahead of schedule and against direction.
- Orrin [3]: All correct. Put fourteen, though — it was fourteen. You'll want the rule. Mind it, it cost more than the coat.
