# Scene 1135: The Woman Who Came Back

- type: talk
- location: a flat grassless field at the town edge, four bare timber fair frames standing in a row, trodden circles of ground, a coil of bunting rope on a post, low evening sun from the right, long shadows
- characters: Neve, Bron
- mood: sorrow

## Beat
A fifty-three-year-old shop clerk out of Lomm has paid her own fare up and taken leave she did not
ask for, to stand in a square for an afternoon. She played Hesta Sallow, widow, one son, from 1093
to 1097. She does not ask him for anything and she does not apologise, because she has had ten
years to arrive somewhere with it and has not arrived. Bron asks factual questions, because that
is how anyone can tell what he is feeling. Lyra stands a little way off and says nothing at all.
Nobody gives a verdict and nobody is owed one.

## Dialogue
- Neve {sorrow}: You won't know me. I'm off the company shop at Lomm. I had four years here. Ninety-three to ninety-seven.
- Bron: I know who you are. There's a page of payroll with your name on it.
- Neve: Ardo. Yes. That's the name on the page.
- Neve: I paid my own fare up. I didn't ask anyone for the days, I just didn't go in.
- Bron: Which house?
- Neve: The one on the corner with the green door. Four years in the one house.
- Bron: What did you do all day?
- Neve: Shopped. Swept. Sat out in the square. It's a posting. You do the town.
- Neve: I mended a coat for you once. Grey, at the elbow. I made a poor job of it, I'm no hand with a needle.
- Neve: Have you still got it?
- Bron: No.
- Neve: No. You'd have grown out of it inside the year anyway.
- Neve: I'm not asking you for anything. I've had ten years to think what I'd say and I never got anywhere with it.
- Neve: I wasn't pretending, by the end. You don't pretend for four years. After about two you're just there.
