# Scene 0730: The Fair

- location: the market shelf dressed for the autumn fair, timber stalls with striped awnings, strung lamps overhead, a plank stage with bunting and a pair of company flags with no markings, warm crowded evening light
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip, Hesk, Crewe
- mood: hope, then dread
- staging: The stage stands at the right of every panel, up-tier, with the drop and the stalls falling away left. The crowd faces right toward it. The party stands at the left edge, at the back of the crowd, looking right

## Beat
The autumn fair on Tier Three opens a fortnight earlier than last year, and the chairman of the
Clement Works opens it himself. He is very tall and very thin and walks up onto the boards on his
own legs, taps the cane once, says two sentences about the cold and the date, and is done. Then a
small girl loses her mother in the crush at the foot of the stage, and he comes down off the boards
and crouches on the planks and holds her hand and stays there, much longer than he needs to, until
the mother gets through. He is tired and he is kind and none of it is performed; he does not know
the party is in the crowd and never sees them. The party watches from the back with nothing useful
to say. Hesk says the unwelcome thing because she always does, and it does not help anybody. Bron
says the only flat true sentence available and it settles nothing at all.

## Dialogue
- Crewe [1] {hope}: Cold tonight, and the fair a fortnight earlier than last year. I'm told that is progress.
- Crewe [2]: The stalls have paid for their pitch. Do them the courtesy of spending something. That's all.
- Lyra [3]: He came up those steps on his own legs. Nobody put a chair out for him.
- Crewe [4] {sorrow}: We'll wait here by the lamp. She'll come past the lamp. They always come past the light.
- Hesk [5]: Four of you are on a notice with that man's seal at the bottom of it.
- Hesk [5]: Nobody has to answer me.
- Bron [6] {dread}: Nobody told him to do that.
- Lyra [6]: No.
