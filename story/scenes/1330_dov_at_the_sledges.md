# Scene 1330: Dov at the Sledges

- type: talk
- location: a rail head on the dry seabed below a terrace town, four loaded sledges strung out on laid rail, a chalked board on a post beside the first, flat overcast daylight from the left, dust raised low
- characters: Dov, Hesk, Pip
- mood: hope
- backdrop: 1305_braid_is_leaving:1

## Beat
Afternoon, crew kit stacked on the salt, nothing to do but wait for a sledge. Hesk's nephew is
twenty-six and cheerful and on a company bore crew against his whole family's wishes, and he has
just been pulled off a bore head by a man his village will not look at, and he is out of a job
along with everybody else on the rail head, and he is not going to concede a single point to his
aunt on principle, while eating her food. They argue about the terraces, the wage, the clinic at
Cadder Step and whose turn it is to write to his mother, and neither of them wins any of it,
because neither of them is arguing about barley. Pip watches two people who love each other find it
easier to shout about a grain yield, and enjoys it enormously, and says almost nothing.

## Dialogue
- Dov {hope}: Four days' wage out of it and the kit's ours. That's better than the terrace gave me in a year.
- Hesk: The terrace gave you a house, a name and a door. You were never on it for the wage.
- Dov: Eleven bushels an acre off the third step last harvest. Eleven. It was nineteen when my father worked it.
- Hesk: It was nineteen when Caleth was lit. You know that and you say the eleven anyway.
- Dov: I say the eleven because the eleven is what's in the sack.
- Hesk: And Cadder Step's got glass in the school windows. Say the rest of it.
- Dov: They have. And a clinic with a doctor in it four days a week, and my mother's chest would like both.
- Hesk: Your mother's chest would like her son eleven miles up a hill and not nine hundred feet down one.
- Dov: ...That's not an argument, that's a worry with numbers in it.
- Pip: He's got you.
- Hesk: He has not.
- Dov: Who's writing to her, then. Because I wrote at Green and you have not written since Turn.
- Hesk: I've been busy.
- Dov: So've I. I still wrote.
- Hesk: ...Mm. Give me the pencil, then, and eat something, you're grey.
