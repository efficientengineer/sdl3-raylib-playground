# Scene 0840: The Door

- optional: yes
- location: a narrow outside stair of dark stone climbing a tenement front, iron rails, three plank doors opening on a shared landing, a rope line of drying hanks above it, hazy low sun from the right, late afternoon
- characters: Bron, Hesk
- mood: tense, then sorrow
- staging: The collector and the party are on the landing at the left, facing right. The mother stands in her own doorway at the right, facing left, with the girl behind her. The stair down is at the bottom left of frame

## Beat
Five doors of ordinary miserable collection work — a bark account, two shop books, a man who pays in
front of his children — and then the sixth. On this one the ledger's asset is not a debt against a
household. It is the child. The fee was never paid, the interest ran for nine years, the mother
signed the schedule and has known what it said the whole time. The collector is not enjoying any of
this and does not pretend the girl is a number; he reads the clause out because he is obliged to,
and then he lays the three ways out flat, because he is obliged to do that as well, and he takes
care not to lean on any of them. Lyra is at the back of the landing. Pip stopped four steps down and
has not come up. The mother does not beg. Hesk does not move at all.

## Dialogue
- Collector [1] {tense}: Sixth door. This is the one I'd walk past, if walking past was a thing I was able to do.
- Collector [2]: You'll know why I'm here. The schedule hasn't changed since the letter.
- Mother [3]: I know what it says. I signed it.
- Collector [4]: Fifty-two marks and four. Or the clause under it.
- Bron [4]: Say the rest of it.
- Collector [5] {sorrow}: The debt may be discharged by intake. Discharged. That's the word on the paper and that's what it means.
- Collector [6]: Three ways, and I'll give you all three because I have to. You settle it yourselves, out of your own money.
