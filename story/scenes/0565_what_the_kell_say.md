# Scene 0565: What the Kell Say

- type: narration
- mood: flat, dated, and very old

## Beat
The story as the Kell tell it, in Hesk's telling, over black. No adjectives, no wonder, and a date
wherever there is one. The terraces were here first. Somebody heard a sound under the hill and dug
down to it and did well down there, and then did something to each other with what they found that
the telling does not describe. The last council voted, the doors were sealed from the inside, and
the households above stayed where they were. The last line is the doorwards' formula, which is a job
description and not a prayer.

## Dialogue
- Narrator {dread}: The terraces were here first. Barley, beans, and nine households on one cliff.
- Narrator: Somebody heard a sound under the hill. They dug down to it. That was a hundred and eighty years before the count starts.
- Narrator: They built a city down there and it did well. Better than up here. Everyone says that part.
- Narrator: They found they could do something with what was under them, and the telling does not say what it was.
- Narrator: They did it to each other. Then they did it to settle arguments.
- Narrator: Inside two generations nobody down there could say which of their own days they had lived.
- Narrator: The last council voted. Every door was sealed in one season, from the inside, and the stone was fitted from the inside.
- Narrator: The guard corps stayed below and took the standing oath. Nobody came out.
- Narrator: The households above stayed where they were. They have been here since.
- Narrator: We live over the door. We keep it shut. We count it four times a year, and we teach the next one.
