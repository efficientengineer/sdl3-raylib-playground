# Scene 1075: The Holding Frame

- location: a tiled treatment room built around a brass yoke the size of a doorway on a floor gantry, padded straps hanging loose, a winding drum and a bank of valve wheels, harsh lamplight from above, night
- characters: Ovel, Lyra, Pip, Bron
- mood: tense, then confront
- staging: Ovel and his staff are on the left of frame, between the party and the hall door; the party are on the right facing left. The brass yoke stands on its gantry in the middle, between them

## Beat
The alarm has gone and Doctor Marsh has come down in his dressing gown and slippers, and he is not
angry — he is upset, and frightened for them. They have been in his file room, they are carrying a
resident's papers, and to him that is not burglary, it is illness; and there is a procedure for
people who are unwell that takes four minutes, after which everybody is calm and pleased with
everybody. He offers it the way a doctor offers a sedative. Behind him six ward staff walk to
positions they have walked to a thousand times without being told, and not one of them thinks they
are doing anything but their job. Nobody in this building is a sadist. The boss is the room: the
fight is not to end up in the frame.

## Dialogue
- Ovel [1] {tense}: Four minutes. That is the whole of it. Four minutes, and everybody in this hall is calm and pleased.
- Ovel [2]: You've been in my file room. You're carrying a resident's papers. You are not well. None of you is well.
- Lyra [3]: Doctor. Stand away from the door.
- Ovel [3]: I am not angry. I want that understood. I have never once been angry in this building.
- Lyra [4]: Six of them went to their places and nobody told them to.
- Ovel [5]: Please don't — the gantry isn't built to be struck —
- Bron [6]: Nobody goes in it.
- Ovel [7] {confront}: You will be pleased with me by Thursday! All of you! You will be pleased with me!
- Bron [7]: East end. Go.
