# Scene 1215: Corrie at the Counter

- type: talk
- location: a bright shop counter of polished wood, shallow trays of paper sleeves in seasonal colours, a brass tally frame and a till, a stool behind, a lamp on a bracket above from the left, evening
- characters: Corrie, Pip, Zeph
- mood: tense
- backdrop: 1205_the_market_shelf:2

## Beat
A seventeen-year-old out of a care-house works the strand counter, and she is genuinely cheerful
and very good at her job. She explains the season's sleeves the way you would explain jam. Zeph
asks her one archivist's question and she answers it wrongly and happily, quoting the counter's
patter about donors, and believes every word of it, and nobody corrects her. Pip leaves in the
middle of a sentence and waits outside. Zeph, who talks constantly, does not ask her about it when
he comes out, and she tells him the price of her boots instead.

## Dialogue
- Corrie {tense}: An afternoon or an evening, and the winter colour's in this week. That's the grey sleeve with the band.
- Corrie: An afternoon is somebody's good afternoon. Out in the sun, nothing happening in it. They're lovely.
- Corrie: The evening's dearer. There's more of it awake in there. Don't ask me why, I only know the price.
- Zeph: Dearer. You said dearer. Where does an afternoon come from, before it comes here?
- Corrie: Donors. Old people, mostly. One good afternoon, and the grandchildren eat for a month off it.
- Corrie: They come in with the form, they go out with the money, and they're pleased as anything. It's nice work.
- Zeph: ...Pleased as anything.
- Pip: ...
- Corrie: Oh — is she all right? She went out very quick. Did I say a wrong thing?
- Zeph: No. She does that. Thank you for your time. You explain it very well.
- Pip: These cost four marks in eleven oh four. They've been resoled four times.
- Zeph: ...
