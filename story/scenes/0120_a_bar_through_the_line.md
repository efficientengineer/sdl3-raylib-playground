# Scene 0120: A Bar Through the Line

- type: talk
- location: a sandstone wall covered floor to ceiling in narrow ruled columns of small handwriting, some lines struck through with a single ruled bar, a lamp on a bracket lighting it from the right, night
- characters: Tovin, Bron, Lyra
- mood: flat, careful

## Beat
Downstairs, before dawn. Tovin goes to the wall, finds the line without looking for it, and rules
one bar through it with a straight edge and too much care — she wipes the chalk twice and checks
the bar is level. She says the count and the date and will not soften either. Bron wants to know
what happens to the line now. Nothing happens to it. That is what a line is for.

## Dialogue
- Tovin {sorrow}: Merrit Tack. Two hundred and twelve bills cleared. Not two hundred and ten.
- Bron: You didn't look for it.
- Tovin: I wrote it. Ninth of Green, ten eighty-one. I do not have to look.
- Bron: What happens to the line now?
- Tovin: Nothing happens to it. It stays. That is what a line is for.
- Tovin: Sister. Did he say anything worth writing down?
- Lyra: No. I've sat two hundred of these. Nobody has ever had anything clever to say.
- Tovin {hope}: The board still eats at six.
