# Scene 1155: The Fair

- location: a flat grassless field at the town edge, four bare timber fair frames standing in a row, trodden circles of ground, a coil of bunting rope on a post, lanterns strung between the frames, cold green sky after sundown
- characters: Ollo, Pip, Ket, Lyra
- mood: hope
- staging: The fair ground fills the frame with the town roofs and the dark ring tower behind it on the right. The oven and the bread tables are on the left; the dancing is centre and right. The party come in from the left in every panel

## Beat
Sixty people who have four days left hold the autumn fair five weeks early. One fiddle, far too
much bread, and a dance nobody is sure of the steps to. Ket counts the steps out loud and is
wrong, and finds being wrong extremely interesting. The clerk who is closing the town dances with
the baker she is putting out of work and neither of them finds that odd, because it is a small
town and she has lived here four years. Ollo runs the oven all night and will not sit down. Bron,
who has had a fortnight of finding out that everything warm in his head was issued to him, has a
genuinely good evening.

## Dialogue
- Ollo [1] {hope}: Five weeks early and nobody is to remark on it!
- Ollo [1]: Bread's free. If you sit down I'll find you something to carry.
- Pip [2]: That's my fourth. I'd like it on the record that nobody stopped me.
- Ollo [3]: Oven's been going since four. She'll be warm till Rime.
- Ket [4]: One. Two. Three. — That was not three.
- Pip [4]: You don't count a dance. You go where you're hauled.
- Ket [4]: I am counting it wrongly. I would like that noted. I do not intend to stop.
- Lyra [5]: ...
- Bron [6]: Leave the lanterns up. They can come down in the morning.
