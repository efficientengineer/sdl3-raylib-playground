# Scene 0355: The Plate and What Was Under It

- location: a round flooded shaft bottom churning slowly, brass valve gear half submerged around the walls, dull grey glass threads turning in the water, a broken gimbal ring canted over, one lamp above at the left
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip
- mood: dread, then confrontation
- staging: The party wades in from the left with the one lamp carried on their left. The great winch housing and the stone alcove are on the right; the open water and the flood gear are centre and right. Everything they look at is to the right

## Beat
The bottom of the shaft is full of soft paper sleeves with a dull grey thread out of every one,
turning slowly in black water, in a quantity that makes no sense at all. Pip knows what they are
because she has stood in front of the counter they are sold over, and saying so does not help
anybody: nobody here can make it mean a thing, and nobody guesses. What Bron finds is the only
part of this room that any of them understand. In an alcove above the waterline there is a cast
brass plate, and when the silt comes off it under his thumb there is a year on it, and the year is
the same year as the flood the flood was supposed to explain. Then the water moves, and the sunk
flood gear comes up out of it.

## Dialogue
- Pip [1] {dread}: Water's at my knee and coming up. Noting that.
- Zeph [2]: Paper. Hundreds of them, gone soft, and a grey thread out of every one.
- Pip [3]: Those are sleeves. Company shop, under the glass, next to the tobacco.
- Pip [3]: Grey means spent. They're rubbish. A hall full of rubbish.
- Lyra [4]: ...
- Bron [5] {tense}: Alcove above the water. There's a plate screwed to the stone.
- Bron [6]: Cast brass. A maker's mark and a year. Ten ninety-three.
- Lyra [7]: The flood was ten ninety-three.
- Bron [7] {confront}: I've seen the plate. It's dated. That's not an argument, that's a date.
