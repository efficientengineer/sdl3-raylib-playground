## Beat
The first minute of the game, at the end of a training session the player has just played. {{MENTOR}} built these four machines out of wood, iron, rope and counterweights after his knee ended his hunting, and each one teaches a hunter one thing. {{HERO}} has beaten three of them and has spent all afternoon attacking the fourth, which is a counterweighted arm on a pivot: every hit he lands winds it up, and it swings back. He has just walked into that arm and it has caught his wrist. {{HEALER}} has watched this from the wall between the two houses a hundred times, calls the swing before it lands, finds it funny, and comes down and patches the wrist anyway — and it costs her more than she lets on. {{MENTOR}} is at his bench, unbothered, and will not explain the machine he built.

## Dialogue
- {{HEALER}} [1] {wonder}: Here he goes. He is at the last one again.
- {{HEALER}} [2]: Watch its arm, {{HERO}}. Left. It always comes back on the left —
- {{HERO}} [3]: I had it! I hit that thing eleven times!
- {{HEALER}} [4]: You hit it eleven times. It hit you once. That is the whole afternoon.
- {{HERO}} [5]: My wrist is fine.
- {{HEALER}} [6]: Let go of it, then. I cannot look at a wrist you are holding.
- {{HERO}}: How is anyone meant to beat that thing? It hits back harder every time I hit it!
- {{HEALER}}: I know. I have watched you find that out four times this week.
- {{HERO}}: Four?
- {{HEALER}}: Four. Hold still. That is me finished until the evening.
- {{MENTOR}} [7]: Again. From the gate.
- {{HERO}}: Again? I can't hold a stick!
- {{MENTOR}}: Tomorrow, then.
- {{HERO}}: {{MENTOR}} — say it straight. Am I ready or am I not?
- {{MENTOR}} {sorrow}: Not quite ready yet.
- {{HERO}}: That is what you said in the spring!
