# Scene 0915: Four Hundred Miles

- type: narration
- mood: wide, then narrow

## Beat
Time passing, text over black. What the Flats are, and what it is like to live on a machine crossing
them. No threat and no irony: the place is simply enormous and indifferent, and the last line closes
the distance down to one woman on foot who is not being caught.

## Dialogue
- Narrator {wonder}: The Flats are a sea floor with the sea taken off it. Four hundred miles across, two feet of crust over brine.
- Narrator: Crawler tracks print white on white and stay a year. No shade. No water that is not sold by the cup.
- Narrator: Drovers do not stop to talk. Stopping costs a day, and a day costs more than talking is worth.
- Narrator {tense}: You live inside the noise. The engine gives its heat back all night, and the horizon does not change.
- Narrator: Three days. They are following a woman who is walking, and they are not gaining on her.
