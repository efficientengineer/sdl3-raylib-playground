# Scene 1465: Nine Hundred Tallies

- type: talk
- location: a sandstone wall covered floor to ceiling in narrow ruled columns of small handwriting, some lines struck through with a single ruled bar, a lamp on a bracket lighting it from the right, night
- characters: Tovin, Pip, Bron, Tibb
- mood: hope
- optional: yes

## Beat
One rail ride up from Thurn, the Sallowgate hall, the stove that is never let out. The bill on the
board is in Tovin's own hand with her own seal on it and the fee is nothing. She is copying
fifty-four years of the ledger wall into bound books, four thousand-odd entries, by hand, because the
Works has moved to lease the building out from under her and she intends the names to outlast it. She
is at two lights and she has done the arithmetic and knows she will not finish. She never rounds a
number and cannot be lied to about a date. Somewhere on that wall, without a word said about it, is
a line with a single ruled bar through it and a date in Dust; Bron finds it, stands in front of it,
and says nothing, and nobody remarks on it. Pip sits down, picks up a pen, and starts copying without
being asked and without making a joke, which is how the player knows. Tovin's last remark is
delivered without any change of expression whatsoever and drives Tibb out of the room.

## Dialogue
- Tovin {hope}: Board's thin. Nineteen out. You'll want the one on the left, it's the only one on there.
- Bron: That's your seal on it.
- Tovin: My hand, my seal, no fee. Three of the four Lines. I have written to myself about the fourth.
- Bron: What's the work.
- Tovin: Four thousand and six lines on that wall. Fifty-four years of them. I am putting them into books.
- Tovin: The Works has moved to lease this building. The building will go. The names are not going with it.
- Tibb: She's been at it since Dust. I've told her she wants six people and a fortnight.
- Tovin: I want six people and a fortnight. Nobody has brought me six people.
- Pip: ...
- Tovin: Nine hundred and six done. Three thousand one hundred to go.
- Tovin: Two lights. At that rate I do not finish it. I have had that figure a while now.
- Tovin: Orsk, M. A hundred and forty bills cleared. Your mother was on this wall before you could walk.
- Tovin: She never once slept alone in this hall in nine years, and that is in the book as well, because she signed for the blankets.
- Tibb: ...I'm going to go and stand in the street.
