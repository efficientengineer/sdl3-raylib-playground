# Scene 0235: The Under-Impression

- type: talk
- location: a closed office door of dark varnished timber in a wet brick street front, a brass night bell beside the jamb, a grille at head height with a lamp behind it, washing lines strung overhead, flat grey light from the left, afternoon
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip
- mood: polite, immovable

## Beat
The office the under-impression names is a locked door with a night bell and a clerk behind a
grille who is extremely sorry and cannot do anything at all without a reference number. He is not
hiding anything and he is not being difficult; the paperwork is simply correct, which is the second
time this week the party has met that and the first time it has annoyed them. Pip offers to acquire
a reference number and is stopped twice. What they get instead, freely and at length, is procedure:
seals come down from the field, the field office this season is the cut up the hill road, and no,
there is no bill for walking up a hill. Four people end the scene looking up a hill road in bad
light.

## Dialogue
- Clerk {tense}: I'm very sorry. I can't help you without a reference number.
- Zeph: I have a number. It is pressed into this paper. You may have the paper.
- Clerk: That's a seal number, sir. I'd want the reference number.
- Pip: What's the difference?
- Clerk: The reference number is the one I can look up.
- Pip: ...And where do you keep those?
- Bron: Pip.
- Pip: I'm asking. Asking's free.
- Bron: Pip.
- Clerk: Seals come down from the field. I don't make them, I file them.
- Clerk: The field office this season is the cut, up the hill road. Two hours if the weather's kind.
- Lyra: Is there anything you can give us that says we may go up there?
- Clerk: You can walk up a hill without me. Nobody's paying you for it either. Good day.
- Pip {tense}: ...I hate that he was nice about it.
