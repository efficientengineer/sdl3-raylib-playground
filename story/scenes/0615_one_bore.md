# Scene 0615: One Bore

- type: talk
- location: a long shed of double plank bunks with hanging blankets between them, salt dust drifted along the duckboard gangway, a row of tin basins, a stove at the far end, low sun entering from the right, evening
- characters: Bron, Lyra
- backdrop: 0610_saltmouth:1
- mood: patient, and very good at sums

## Beat
A woman off the night shift, boots off, sitting on her own step with her feet in the salt. She gives
them the whole town in six lines without being asked twice and without a word of complaint in any of
it: what the face pays, what the surface pays, what the bunk takes out before you see it, what the
water costs, what the care-house takes and what it hands back, and why nobody leaves. She is not
bitter. She is extremely good at arithmetic and slightly proud of it. Then she asks Lyra to look at
her hands, which are perfectly all right, and says why. The last thing she says is the thing the
company told the town in Green, and she says it the way people say a thing they have decided to
believe.

## Dialogue
- Woman {tense}: Sit if you're sitting. Boots off if you're on my step, I sweep it.
- Woman: Nine marks a fortnight on the face, six on the surface. The bunk comes out of it before it reaches your hand.
- Woman: Bunk's a mark and a half. Water's off the standpipe at the meter. Half a mark a week if you're careful, and nobody's careful.
- Woman: Care-house is at the end there. They'll take a child at six if you can't feed one, and they feed them better than I do.
- Woman: They come out at sixteen with boots and a place on the books. Four in this row came out of one.
- Lyra: And nobody leaves.
- Woman: Leaves for where? Everything I can count on is down that hole. Find me a town where the wage comes out of two places and I'll walk to it.
- Woman: Here. Look at my hands.
- Lyra: There's nothing wrong with your hands.
- Woman: No. I know there isn't. I like being told by somebody who'd say if there was.
- Bron: How long's the bore got?
- Woman {hope}: Nine years. They came out and told us in Green. Nine years in it yet, and that's from the men who'd know.
