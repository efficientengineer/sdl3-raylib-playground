# Scene 0360: Wet and Cold

- type: talk
- location: a shallow flooded hollow east of town, still brown water over drowned fence lines, the broken roof of a shed above the surface, reeds at the edges, cold flat daylight from the right, no wind
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip
- mood: sorrow, then tense
- backdrop: 0345_the_water_over_the_pasture:1

## Beat
Four people sitting in mud with a piece of brass between them. Zeph does the arithmetic out loud
and cannot find an order of events that is innocent. Pip is shaking and says it is the cold, and
it is not the cold, and nobody challenges her. Lyra says the thing none of them want said: if the
flood was made, it was made for a reason, and the reason is at the top of the hill in the town
that fed them this morning. Bron will not take the last step and says so plainly, and then puts
the plate in his pack, which is the actual answer. The plate is brass, it weighs something, it
fits in a pack, and it is company property, and all four of them know that. Bron ends it by
proposing the most dangerous thing anyone does in the chapter, sincerely.

## Dialogue
- Zeph {sorrow}: Flood: twelfth of Fallow, ten ninety-three. Plate: cast ten ninety-three.
- Zeph: The gear that drowned this ground was made the same year the ground drowned.
- Zeph: I have tried it in every order. There is no order where that is innocent.
- Pip: I'm shaking. It's the cold. That water was cold.
- Lyra: ...
- Lyra: If the flood was made, somebody made it for a reason.
- Lyra: And the reason is up that hill, in the town that fed us this morning.
- Bron {tense}: A date isn't a crime. Ista Fenn isn't a crime.
- Bron: Ollo put his hand on my shoulder and gave us bread for nothing.
- Zeph: Nobody said Ollo.
- Bron: You will.
- Pip: ...
- Lyra: You're keeping it.
- Bron: It's four pounds of company brass and I'm on nobody's bill. Yes. I'm keeping it.
- Bron {tense}: We'll go up and ask somebody. Somebody up there will just tell us.
