# Scene 1085: The Tea

- location: a row of open laundry doorways in a wet brick wall, steam rolling out at ankle height, copper tubs and mangles just inside, washing lines overhead, cold low light striking down the street from the left, seven in the morning
- characters: Anneke, Bron, Pip
- mood: ordinary
- staging: The propped laundry door and Anneke are at the left of frame; Bron stands in the street at the right, facing left. Pip is much further right, at the corner, and never crosses. Nobody moves from these positions for the whole scene

## Beat
Seven in the morning. The coppers have been lit an hour and the whole street is standing in steam.
Bron has been on the far side of it long enough that his collar is soaked through. Inside, a
fifty-four-year-old presser with her sleeves rolled is working the tubs, and she notices a young
man standing still in the cold outside her door and does what anybody in Windrow does about that:
brings out a chipped cup, holds it out, tells him the steam gets everybody, and suggests he come in
out of it or go home. She is cheerful and a bit brisk and complains about the heat in the shop. She
talks to him for about forty seconds about nothing. She does not know him. There is nothing in her
face at all. She is four foot eleven and so is he. He does not take the cup and he does not tell
her. She says suit yourself, pleasantly, and goes back in, and the door stays propped because of
the steam. Pip is at the corner and does not cross and says nothing.

## Dialogue
- Anneke: You've been stood out there since before I lit the second copper.
- Bron: I'm all right.
- Anneke {hope}: You're not. That steam soaks you worse than rain does. It gets everybody.
- Anneke: Here. It's only tea and it's stewed, but it's hot and it's free.
- Anneke: Take it or come in out of it. Though it's worse in than out, I'll tell you that for nothing.
- Bron {sorrow}: No. Thank you.
- Anneke: Suit yourself.
- Anneke: Mind the ruts going down. They fill up and you can't see them for the steam.
