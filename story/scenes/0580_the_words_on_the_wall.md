# Scene 0580: The Words on the Wall

- type: talk
- location: a straight stair of square-cut stone treads between close dressed walls, a shallow rope groove worn into the right-hand wall, a broken stone lamp shelf at head height, one carried lamp from below, dark
- characters: Lyra, Zeph
- mood: tense, then something quieter
- backdrop: 0575_the_sealed_stair:4

## Beat
A rest point partway down. Zeph cannot leave a wrong count alone: the list on the wall has fifteen
entries and two of them are the same question asked twice, one straight after the other, and he
wants to know what kind of procedure repeats itself. Lyra, who sits the last hour of the dying for a
living, knows exactly why you ask a frightened person the same thing twice. She does not lie to him
and she does not explain. She asks him something else instead, with complete composure, and he lets
her have it because he likes her. Then she takes the lamp and goes down first.

## Dialogue
- Zeph {tense}: Fifteen. I have counted it four times and it is fifteen every time.
- Zeph: And two of them are the same question. Not similar. The same, asked twice, one under the other.
- Lyra: Which two?
- Zeph: The thirteenth and the fourteenth. To a reader they are one question written out twice.
- Lyra: To a reader. Yes.
- Zeph: Have you ever seen a working procedure repeat itself?
- Lyra: I have.
- Zeph: ...And?
- Lyra {sorrow}: And I have.
- Lyra: Are you warm enough? It gets colder further down. Give me the lamp, I'll take the front.
