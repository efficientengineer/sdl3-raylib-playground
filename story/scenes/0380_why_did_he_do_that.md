# Scene 0380: Why Did He Do That

- type: talk
- location: a straight dirt road leaving town between two low sandstone walls, wheel ruts, a leaning stone mile post, dry grass on the verges, flat overcast morning light, the town roofs low behind
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip
- mood: dread, then tense

## Beat
An argument about a kindness, which is the hardest kind to have. Pip thinks they were let go and
would like one good thing today. Zeph thinks they were released, which is a different word, and
will not be talked out of the difference. Lyra says the version that frightens everybody: he was
not deciding whether to take them in, he had decided that before he came down the road, and she
does not know what the other thing was. None of them gets near the answer. Bron is angrier about
being let go than he would have been about being arrested and cannot explain why, and Pip lets it
alone, which is the kindest thing anybody does in the chapter. Then the practical news: both roads
are posted, and there is one way off this terrace, and Zeph throws it away as he says it.

## Dialogue
- Pip {dread}: We got let go. That's a good thing. I would like to have one good thing today.
- Zeph: Let go. You said let go. We were released. Those are different words.
- Pip: They are exactly the same word with a hat on.
- Zeph: One of them is something that happens to you. The other is something a person does.
- Zeph: He did it. On purpose. In front of thirty of his own.
- Lyra: He wasn't deciding whether to take us in.
- Bron: What, then.
- Lyra: I don't know. He'd decided that before he came down the road.
- Lyra: He came down to look at something else. He looked at it, and then he went back up.
- Bron {confront}: I'd rather he'd taken us in.
- Pip: ...Would you?
- Bron: ...
- Zeph {tense}: The rim road west is posted. Two coats and a pole across it.
- Pip: So's the spur. I walked up and looked and a man said good afternoon to me.
- Zeph: There is a stair. Four hundred feet of it, cut, down the terrace face to the salt.
- Zeph: And there are people on the rail down there who owe my line a favour.
