# Scene 1630: The Well Comes Up

- location: a tall stone ring tower standing whole and dark over a tram town, birds circling the upper rings, a swept stone apron at its foot, a broom and a stacked pile of squared stone chips, grey overcast morning light
- characters: Ket, Arro, Bron, Pip
- mood: wonder, then hope, then sorrow
- staging: The Vintry tower fills the right of frame and rises out of the top of it. The town's roofs and the laundry street lie below and to the left. Ket and Arro stand on the apron at the tower's foot on the right, facing up and right. Everyone in the scene looks up and right at the tower

## Beat
Green of 1108. Eleven days of work in the specification turned into seven months, because the sheet
was written for people with a supply chain and what Ket-Ossun had was a keeper at one light and a
town with a tram yard. This morning the third ring takes the load, the air starts to tremble at the
joints, and the hum arrives in everybody's teeth at once. The laundries of Windrow come out into the
street to look at a tower that has been scenery for a hundred and sixty-three years. Arro-Vintry,
who has swept it every day of that time, says almost nothing; he puts his broom into a cupboard he
has never had a use for. Ket gives the number that matters and then the number that does not, in the
same voice. This is the only unambiguously good thing that happens in the epilogue, and the joy in
it belongs to the town. The two Onn are simply at work, and finished for the day.

## Dialogue
- Ket [1] {wonder}: Arro-Vintry. Keeper of this well since the year nine hundred and forty-four.
- Ket [1]: A hundred and sixty-three years sweeping a tower that does not run. Stand where you can see the joints.
- Ket [2]: Eleven days of work in the sheet. It has taken seven months. The sheet assumes a supply chain.
- Ket [3]: The third ring is taking load. It starts at the seam. — There.
- Arro [4]: ...
- Ket [5] {hope}: Four of nine standing. Three of nine running, as of this morning.
- Ket [6]: And a flat file. Second cut, eight inches. You will want it at the third ring joint.
- Arro [6]: I have put the broom away. There is a cupboard. I have never once used the cupboard.
- Ket [7] {sorrow}: Six hundred and eleven of us. I am at two lights. There will not be another one of us.
