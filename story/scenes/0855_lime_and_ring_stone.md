# Scene 0855: Lime and Ring Stone

- type: talk
- location: a stone cellar of sunken vats in rows with plank lids, drip channels cut in the floor, a stack of bark sacks, a lamp set on a vat lid lighting upward, no daylight
- characters: Zeph, Ket, Hesk, Bron, Pip
- mood: tense, then wonder

## Beat
Getting under the tannery is a tannery problem and not a dungeon problem: pits of lime that will
take the skin off a hand, a sluice that has to be dropped in the right order, and a foreman upstairs
who wants it all shut by six and has said so twice. Below the vats the cellars change. The walls
stop being courses of rubble and start being pieces of machine, dressed on the wrong face and bedded
flat in lime by masons a hundred years dead who did not care what they were handling. Ket walks two
hundred feet of it with one hand on the wall and does not say why. Zeph reads the stone correctly
three times and then miscounts it spectacularly, and is corrected with a number and takes it well.
Hesk hates being under a town that is not hers and says so once. Nothing down here wakes up. It is
dead material, quarried and reused, and that is the whole of what it is.

## Dialogue
- Hesk {tense}: Sluice shut by six, he said. It's half five and he meant it.
- Hesk: And I don't like being under a town. Under mine, fine. This one isn't mine.
- Hesk: That's the once. I'll not say it again.
- Zeph {wonder}: ...Stop. Bring the lamp back. That is not masonry. That's a face.
- Zeph: Dressed, curved, machined groove along the top edge, and laid flat and bedded in lime by somebody who did not care what it was.
- Zeph: Nine courses. The groove runs out of true by a finger every course, which means nine rings, which means they took nine of them down —
- Ket: Four.
- Zeph: Four.
- Ket: A ring is eleven feet through. They cut it in eights. You are counting cuts and calling them rings.
- Zeph: ...I am counting cuts and calling them rings. Yes. I am.
- Ket: The dressed face is turned inward. That side was never meant to be looked at by anybody.
- Ket: It is a wall now. That is the whole of what it is.
