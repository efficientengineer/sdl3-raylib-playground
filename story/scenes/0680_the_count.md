# Scene 0680: The Count

- location: a long canvas relief tent open along one side on white salt, trestle tables in two rows under it with enamel bowls stacked, a tea urn on a crate, hard flat afternoon light from overhead, salt glare
- characters: Lyra, Kerrow, Orrin
- mood: sorrow, then tense
- staging: The bore gate and the north gallery mouth are on the right of every panel; the open salt and the care-house are on the left. Kerrow stands at the trestle facing left toward the queue; the queue comes from the left

## Beat
A small, very upright woman in a plain slate coat with no braid on it is standing where she has been
standing since before dawn, with a tally board under her hand, taking names from anyone who will give
her one. There is dust to her knee. She has not delegated it, has not sat down, and has not asked
anybody to fetch her anything. Behind her Orrin Cadder comes up out of the north gallery mouth on his
own feet with a man over his shoulder, having been down there since it started, sets him on a trestle
and turns round and goes back. Nobody is performing for anybody. Kerrow asks Lyra for her name and
writes it in, and does not look up.
