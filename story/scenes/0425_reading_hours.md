# Scene 0425: Reading Hours

- location: a heavy Vess-built stone façade with a single tall doorway, an iron company grille fitted across it, a queue of tall figures along the wall, a lamp bracket above the grille, cold grey morning light from the right
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip
- mood: patient, cold
- staging: the annex façade and its grilled doorway stand on the right of every panel. The queue runs away to the left along the wall. Bron, Lyra, Zeph and Pip stand at the near end of the queue on the left, facing right. The company doorkeeper stands in the doorway on the right, facing left

## Beat
A line of Vess three hundred long stands in the wind outside a building their great-grandmothers
cut. The doorkeeper is polite, bored, and correct about every rule he quotes; none of this is his
idea and he has explained it four thousand times. An old woman near the front has a folding stool
and a place in the queue she has plainly held for years. Pip asks who owns the building as a joke
and gets a straight answer with a date on it, and the joke stops. Lyra asks one more question than
anyone wants answered. Zeph does not join the queue, does not explain himself, and walks away, and
the camera stays with the queue.

## Dialogue
- Pip [1] {wonder}: That queue goes round the corner.
- Pip [2]: ...And round the one after that.
- Doorkeeper [3]: Reading hours are two till four. Twelve readers at a time. Bring your card.
- Pip [4]: Whose building is it, then? Asking for a friend.
- Doorkeeper [4]: The Clement Works. Since the twelfth of Green, ten sixty-seven.
- Lyra [5] {sorrow}: And the woman on the stool. How long has she held that place?
- Doorkeeper [6]: Couldn't tell you. She was here when I started.
- Doorkeeper [7]: Same again tomorrow. Two till four.
