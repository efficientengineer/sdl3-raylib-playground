# Scene 0735: The Strand Counter

- type: talk
- location: a bright shop counter of polished wood, shallow trays of paper sleeves in seasonal colours, a brass tally frame and a till, a stool behind, a lamp on a bracket above from the left, evening
- characters: Pip, Zeph, Lyra, Corrie
- mood: hope, then tense

## Beat
A company shop off the fair ground, ten minutes before closing. The girl on the counter is
seventeen, quick, and genuinely delighted to explain the season's range to anybody who stands still
long enough. She is not sad and nothing about her is to be pitied. She recognises Pip's boots the
way one leaver recognises another and asks the friendly question, twice, in the friendliest possible
way, and gets a joke and then a better joke and then Pip walks out of the shop in the middle of her
own sentence and stands in the street with her back to the window. Corrie is left holding the tray
and honestly does not know what she did. Zeph, who has worked out nothing whatsoever, buys a sleeve
to smooth it over, pays the middle price, thanks her twice, and comes out into the street unable to
understand why Pip will not look at him.

## Dialogue
- Corrie {hope}: Mulberry this season. It goes green after Rime and everybody hates the green, but they buy it.
- Corrie: Comfort's an evening's wage. A stranger's good afternoon, nothing in it, very quiet. Night shifts take two at a time.
- Corrie: Occasions are dearer. A wedding, a fair, a first snow. We had a first snow in and it went in a day.
- Zeph: And the people it comes off. They are paid for it?
- Corrie: Older folk, mostly. They sell the company an afternoon and their grandchildren eat. It's lovely, when you sit down with it.
- Pip: Lovely.
- Corrie: Those are house boots. I had those boots. Where'd you come up?
- Pip: Big place. Lot of windows. Terrible soup.
- Corrie: That's all of them!
- Pip: Then you've got me. Ask me another one and I'll — no. No. I'm going to go and stand in the street.
- Corrie {tense}: ...Did I say a wrong thing?
- Zeph: One of the mulberry. The middle price, please. Thank you. That is — thank you.
