# Scene 1485: The Door We Shut

- type: talk
- location: a long vaulted stone room lined with empty glass-fronted tanks on brass frames, their pipework cut through and capped, a floor drain channel, one tank shattered inward, dim lamp panels overhead, no daylight
- characters: Hesk, Ket
- mood: sorrow, then hope
- backdrop: 1475_the_cradle:5

## Beat
Nine chapters of civility at visible cost, ended in a doorway by the only two people who were never
going to discuss it. Hesk says the thing she has not said since chapter five: her people shut the
door, the hammers were Kell hammers, and that is why there are six hundred and eleven Onn. Ket does
not absolve her, does not say it is all right, and does not pretend it was somebody else. She says it
is correct. Then she says the second thing, which is the only thing that was ever going to be any use
to either of them. Hesk cannot say anything back, so she offers to carry something instead, which is
how this game does grief. No apology is offered and none is accepted, and it is over in eight lines.

## Dialogue
- Hesk {sorrow}: My lot shut that door.
- Hesk: And the hammers were ours. Kell hammers, Kell arms. That's why there's six hundred and eleven of you.
- Ket: Correct.
- Hesk: ...
- Ket: You were not there.
- Ket {hope}: And the work is still the work.
- Hesk: ...
- Hesk: What wants carrying.
