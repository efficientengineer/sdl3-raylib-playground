# Scene 0104: The Table

- map: hart_yard
- type: talk
- location: {{MENTOR}}'s one room at sundown: the door propped open on the yard, three plates, a lamp, and a thick canvas-bound workshop book open at the far end of the table with a pencil laid across it
- characters: {{HERO}}, {{HEALER}}, {{MENTOR}}
- mood: supper, three people who do this every week
- backdrop: 0101_the_dummies:1

## Beat
Supper at {{MENTOR}}'s, which is where {{HEALER}} eats most nights. {{HERO}} cannot talk about
anything but the job sheets going up at dawn; {{MENTOR}} tells him plainly he is not ready and says
the one true thing about the last machine, which {{HERO}} does not hear at all. {{HEALER}} is right
about what is actually wrong with him and both of them talk straight past her. Then {{HERO}} asks
what is in the workshop book — every machine, every day, and how it went — and {{HEALER}} says
{{TOWN_HEALER}} keeps one on her too, which is how the player learns what she is and what happened to
her, in plain sentences, from {{MENTOR}} and not from her.

## Dialogue
- {{HERO}} {hope}: Job sheets go up at dawn. The clerk told me himself.
- {{MENTOR}}: Heard you the first time.
- {{HERO}}: And the board is open all day! I could sign and be gone by noon.
- {{MENTOR}}: You could not. You are not ready.
- {{HERO}}: I beat three of your machines! Three out of four!
- {{MENTOR}}: The fourth one is the one that is out on the road.
- {{HEALER}}: He is right about the fourth one. You never wait, {{HERO}}.
- {{HERO}}: I hit it eleven times! What is there to wait for?
- {{MENTOR}}: It isn't about hitting it.
- {{HERO}}: Pass the bread.
- {{HEALER}}: Both of you. He does not know what you meant, and you have not said it.
- {{MENTOR}}: Said it.
- {{HERO}}: What is that book you write in every night?
- {{MENTOR}}: Every machine, every day, and how it went. Eat.
- {{HERO}}: Four years of that. It's a book of me losing!
- {{HEALER}} {wonder}: {{TOWN_HEALER}} keeps one on me. Mine is longer than yours.
- {{HERO}}: Why would she keep a book on you? You've healed this town since I was small.
- {{HEALER}}: I am an apprentice. She writes down what I can close and what I cannot.
- {{HEALER}}: I am twenty-three. You are seventeen. We are both somebody's apprentice.
- {{HERO}}: What can't you close?
- {{HEALER}}: Anything big. I run out of strength part way, and then I have to sit down.
- {{MENTOR}} {sorrow}: She was nine when {{TOWN_HEALER}} took her on. A fever took her mother and her father that winter.
- {{HERO}}: Nine?
- {{MENTOR}}: She tried to close it herself. Was not strong enough. Next door on her own ever since.
- {{HEALER}}: {{MENTOR}}. He asked me what I cannot close.
- {{MENTOR}}: He did.
- {{HERO}} {hope}: I'm going out to the machines before it's light.
