# Scene 1030: Bad Supper

- type: talk
- location: a narrow boarding-house kitchen let with the rooms, a black range with somebody else's pans on it, a long scrubbed table, a bare bulb window black with night, warm range light
- characters: Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip, Hesk, Ket
- mood: good company

## Beat
The last quiet hour before the file room, and nobody at the table mentions it. Hesk has taken over
the range and is cooking something with total confidence and will not be advised, corrected or
helped. Zeph has bought a newspaper for the tram timetable and is reading the withdrawn services
aloud in a voice usually kept for funerals. Ket asks a question about human sleep that derails
everything for five minutes and is not being funny. Bron says almost nothing and eats all of it.
In the middle of it Lyra asks him, in front of everybody, whether he still wants her to tell him if
he is wrong about himself, and he says yes, and she says all right, and Hesk asks for the plates
and the table moves on and nobody ever goes back to it.

## Dialogue
- Hesk {hope}: Sit. It's done when I say it's done.
- Pip: What is it?
- Hesk: Food.
- Zeph: The seven-forty to Vantage called at Dunnow, Cadder Halt and the quarry road. The seven-forty is withdrawn.
- Zeph: The eight-ten calls at Dunnow. Only at Dunnow. Consider, if you can bear to, what that means for Cadder Halt.
- Pip: If you read one more line of that I will put it in the pan.
- Ket: A question. You are unconscious for seven hours in every eight and you do this every day.
- Ket: Is that a fault, or is it the specification?
- Pip: It's the — Zeph. What is it?
- Zeph: Nobody has ever asked me that and I find I have nothing at all to say.
- Lyra: Bron. Do you still want me to tell you if you're wrong about yourself?
- Bron: Yes.
- Lyra: All right.
- Hesk {tense}: Plates. Now, or it goes back in the pan and I start again.
- Pip: Is that a threat or an offer?
