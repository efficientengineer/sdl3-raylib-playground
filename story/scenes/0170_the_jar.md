# Scene 0170: The Jar

- map: stair_shrine
- location: the field under {{STAIR}}: a stone staircase wider than a town coming down out of the cloud and stopping thirty feet above empty grass, a long cut shelf of pale stone under its bottom step, six low houses and a well off to one side
- characters: {{HERO}}, {{HEALER}}, Shrinewoman
- mood: a holy place that turns out to be a village
- staging: the bottom step and the stone shelf under it fill the right of the screen. The shrine houses, their plank door and the well are on the left. {{HERO}}, {{HEALER}} and Shrinewoman stand on the grass between, facing right toward the shelf
- dialogue_box: no

## Beat
The job is done: the new jar is on the shelf, sealed, next to last year's. Then the shrine's oldest
woman starts talking, the way she talks to anybody who stands still, and what she says is that the
hunter who used to bring the jar stayed a whole day with the woman under the step, every time, for
twenty years. {{HERO}} works out in front of her what the extra day was that his father spent every
year. {{HEALER}} has gone to the shrine door and is reading the receipts nailed inside it: eleven of
them, one a year, every one paid by {{MENTOR}}, who has kept this going for eleven years and told
neither of them. Last year's jar has not been opened, so the woman under the step has been gone a year
and the carter who came instead never noticed.

## Dialogue
- Shrine Woman [1] {wonder}: The hunter who used to bring it stayed a whole day with her, every time. Twenty years of that, and then one year a carter came instead.
- {{HERO}} [2]: A whole day? That's where the extra day went — he was never late, he was here!
- {{HEALER}} [3] {dread}: {{HERO}}. Eleven receipts, one a year, and {{MENTOR}} paid every one of them. Last year's jar is still sealed, so she has been gone a year and the carter never noticed.
- {{HERO}} [4] {sorrow}: Twenty years he sat with her and he never once said her name to me!
- {{HEALER}} [5]: She knew him longer than you did. And {{MENTOR}} has paid to keep this going for eleven years without telling either of us.
