# Scene 0170: The Jar

- map: stair_shrine
- location: the field under the {{STAIR}}: a stone staircase wider than a town coming down out of the cloud and stopping thirty feet above empty grass, a long cut shelf of pale stone under its bottom step, six low houses and a well off to one side
- characters: {{HERO}}, {{HEALER}}, Shrinewoman
- mood: a holy place that turns out to be a village, and then the sky moves
- staging: the bottom step and the stone shelf under it fill the right of the screen. The shrine houses, their plank door and the well are on the left. {{HERO}}, {{HEALER}} and Shrinewoman stand on the grass between, facing right toward the shelf. On the last page both of them face right and up
- dialogue_box: no

## Beat
The job is done: the new jar is on the shelf, sealed, next to last year's. The shrine's oldest woman
talks to them the way she talks to anybody who stands still, and she names the person no one has
named all chapter — the woman who lives under the bottom step, who has no name of her own to give and
has been here longer than these houses. She says the hunter who brought the jar sat a whole day with
her, every year, for twenty years, and {{HERO}} works out in front of her where his father's extra day
went. Then {{HEALER}} calls him to the shrine door and makes him count the papers nailed inside it
himself: eleven carters' receipts, one a year, every one paid by {{MENTOR}}, who has kept this going
since the year the father died and told neither of them. Last year's jar has not been opened, so the
woman under the step has been gone a year and the carter who came instead never noticed. That night
they sleep under the stone, look up at last, and the second moon — which an old
man in {{HOME_TOWN}} told {{HERO}} has never moved — moves west while they watch, and every bell in
the village starts.

## Dialogue
- Shrine Woman [1] {wonder}: Set it down there, beside last year's. She keeps the shelf for them.
- {{HERO}}: Who does? Who takes the water?
- Shrine Woman [2]: The woman who lives under the bottom step. She was here before these houses were.
- {{HERO}}: What is her name?
- Shrine Woman: She has never given one. I have asked her for sixty years.
- Shrine Woman: The hunter who used to bring the jar sat with her a whole day. Every year, twenty years.
- {{HERO}}: A whole day.
- {{HERO}}: That's the day! He was never late coming home — he was here!
- {{HEALER}} [3] {dread}: {{HERO}}. There are papers nailed inside this door. Come and count them.
- {{HERO}}: They're carters' receipts. One for carrying the jar.
- {{HERO}} [4]: Eleven of them. One for every year since he died.
- {{HEALER}}: Read whose name is at the bottom of each one.
- {{HERO}} {sorrow}: {{MENTOR}}. It's {{MENTOR}} on all eleven. He paid a carter every single year.
- {{HEALER}}: He never told you, and he never told me.
- {{HERO}} [5]: Twenty years my father sat here and he never once said her name to me!
- {{HEALER}}: He may not have had one to say. Look at last year's jar.
- {{HERO}}: The seal is still on it.
- {{HEALER}}: Then she did not come for it. She has been gone a year and no one noticed.
- {{HERO}} [6] {wonder}: It goes up and up. Where does the top of it come out?
- {{HEALER}}: In the cloud. I have been looking at it an hour and I cannot find an end.
- {{HERO}} [7]: That old man in the square told me the {{SECOND_MOON}} has never moved.
- {{HEALER}}: It has not. It has sat over the same roof his whole life.
- {{HERO}} [8] {dread}: {{HEALER}} — it is moving. It is moving west, right now!
- {{HEALER}}: I see it. Listen — every bell in the village has started.
- {{HERO}} {confront}: Pack the fire. We're going west before it stops.
