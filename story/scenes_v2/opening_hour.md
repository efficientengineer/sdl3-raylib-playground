# THE FIRST HOUR

What the player sees, does and hears, in order, from the first frame to the hook. Sixteen moments.
No names except the four leads, **Tellwater**, and **Cadder** — and Cadder is only read off a piece
of paper at the very end.

---

### 1. Rain (0:00)

No title, no logo, no crawl. Black, then rain coming sideways across a bare hillside at night, and
you are already running. You have control before you know who you are. Three people run with you: a
tall woman in white, a slim grey-skinned man with a glowing staff, a girl in goggles. Lightning
shows a stone door in the hillside, twelve feet tall, standing wide open.

> PIP: That's open. Bron, that's *open*.
> BRON: I know.
> PIP: We were hired to open it!
> BRON: I know.

### 2. Something comes out (0:02)

Low, fast, pale as something that has never been outside — and there are three of them. The tutorial
fight is a real fight in the rain: Bron swings, Pip gets behind them, Zeph lights the hillside up,
Lyra pulls a wound shut with her hands. A line each, and that's all it takes to know them.

> ZEPH: Oh, that is *wonderful*. Look at the legs. Nobody made that.
> PIP: Nobody panic. I've already panicked, there's none left for you.
> BRON: Lyra, get behind me.
> LYRA: No. You're on my left and you're not breathing out when you swing.
> BRON: Then something came out. Fine. Job's the same.

### 3. The wrong way (0:05)

Quiet after. Rain dripping off the lintel. Bron crouches in the doorway with a lamp: the dust on the
threshold has handprints in it, and every one of them faces out.

> BRON: These go outward. Somebody opened this from the inside.
> LYRA: Or something did. I'd rather it was somebody.
> ZEPH: Three days of walking to open a door that's open. I'd like my fee anyway, please.

### 4. Three days ago (0:07)

Hard cut, warm light, the smell of a stove: the hunters' guild hall, three days back, sixty seconds
long. The guild master — one of the old made people, seven feet of narrow bone-white patience,
lensed eyes that click when they refocus, four little lights down each temple — holds out a bill
with the bottom corner burned black.

> GUILD MASTER: The seal has been removed with a lamp. Removed, not damaged. Someone wanted this
>   unreadable and had time.
> BRON: What's it pay?
> GUILD MASTER: Triple. That is the other thing wrong with it.
> BRON: Hall takes its tenth. That's a roof through winter.
> GUILD MASTER: Correct. I am telling you it stinks. I am not telling you no.
> BRON: Noted.
> GUILD MASTER: Then bring them back. That is the only line of ours I have ever cared about.

### 5. Somebody's been mopping (0:10)

Back inside, walking. The corridor is cut stone, dead straight, running *downward* — the whole place
was built to go down. There are lamps burning in wall brackets, and they are not theirs, and they
are not old, and the floor has been swept.

> PIP: Who sweeps a tomb?
> ZEPH: Old. You said old. This is nine hundred years past old, and that lamp was filled this week.
> LYRA: Then we're not the first people in here. We're the second.
> BRON: We're the ones getting paid. Keep walking.

### 6. The floor falls (0:12)

The corridor opens into a hall the size of a market square, and the floor of it is the top of a lift.
It takes their weight, thinks about it, and drops. The set piece: a mile of black shaft, lamps
streaking upward, a counterweight the size of a barn coming the other way, and an old maintenance arm
sweeping the shaft on a schedule nobody cancelled — swinging out of the dark at them, again, and
again. Bron chops its cable; Pip lies flat with her arm through the grating to reach the brake.

> PIP: Hold my belt! HOLD MY BELT—
> BRON: I've got you.
> ZEPH: Four hundred feet. Five hundred. I'm counting because it's *interesting*—
> PIP: Zeph, I will end you.

### 7. The bottom (0:18)

It sets down with a sigh, and the dust settles, and it is warm. The halls down here are lit and clean
and dry, and a cold wind comes along the corridor from somewhere much further down, and under the
wind, very faintly, something like a lot of people humming one note.

> ZEPH: Do you hear that?
> LYRA: I hear it.
> ZEPH: I want to know what that is more than I want the money.
> BRON: Nobody's paying you for that either.

### 8. The stove (0:22)

A guard room, a fire made of somebody's broken furniture. The first real rest: a save point and eight
minutes of four people who like each other. Pip steals half Bron's rations in front of him. Lyra sits
with her boots off. Zeph draws the corridor from memory and gets it right. And Bron gets got at.

> PIP: Tell the winter one.
> BRON: No.
> PIP: Tell the winter one or I'm telling him about the goat.
> BRON: I was nine. Above Tellwater. I went up past the fold on my own, and the drifts came in, and I
>   sat down under a rock because I was bored, and then I was frightened, and then somebody carried
>   me down. I remember the wool on his coat. That's it. That's the story.
> PIP: You got *cold*? Your lot sleep in snowdrifts for fun.
> BRON: I got cold.
> LYRA: (quietly, to no one) That's a nice thing to have.
> ZEPH: I have it as the third time you've told me and the first time it had a rock in it.
> BRON: It always had a rock in it.

### 9. The sitting man (0:28)

Further down, a dead man in ancient armour sits against the wall with his hands in his lap. He does
not move when the lamp reaches him. He does not move when Pip pokes him. The party edges past him
one at a time and nobody says anything for twenty feet.

> PIP: He's not going to get up.
> LYRA: No.
> PIP: Say it like you mean it.
> LYRA: He's not going to get up, Pip.
> PIP: ...Better.

### 10. The warning (0:32)

A great stone door stands open onto black. In the gap, in ancient armour, a guard **is** standing —
and it lowers a halberd across the opening, raises one gauntlet, and points, unmistakably, back up
the corridor. It does not attack. It does not move again. (This is the approved panel scene; the art
exists and stays.)

> BRON: It's blocking the way.
> LYRA: No. Look at it. It's pointing us back the way we came.
> BRON: A dead man doesn't give me orders.
> BRON: Then it can point while I walk through it!
> LYRA: Bron, wait!
> LYRA: It hasn't moved. It could have cut us down the moment that door opened.
> LYRA: It isn't guarding the door from us. It's still guarding us from the door.

### 11. Deciding, out loud (0:36)

Zeph is halfway up the wall with his lamp, reading the carving around the door frame and going quiet,
which he never does. The party decides to go on, and the game makes each of them say it out loud,
because it is the last easy choice any of them get.

> ZEPH: The bar was on the inside face. They locked it behind themselves.
> PIP: People do that when something's chasing them.
> ZEPH: People do that when they're the thing.
> LYRA: I'd like to go in, and I'd like it to be because we decided, not because we were paid.
> BRON: Then decide. I'm not carrying anybody through a door they didn't pick.
> PIP: In.
> ZEPH: In. Obviously in.
> LYRA: In.
> BRON: (to the guard, quietly) Sorry. You were clear.

### 12. The broken post (0:40)

The corridor beyond ends in rubble, and two more of them are standing in the middle of it holding a
line across a hallway that collapsed six hundred years ago. There is no hallway. There is no gap to
hold. They come at anything that moves, and this is the hour's boss, and it is not satisfying and is
not meant to be.

> LYRA: Their post isn't there anymore. They're still holding it.
> BRON: Then we make them stop.
> PIP: (after, not joking) I hated that.
> BRON: Yeah.

### 13. The last door (0:48)

A seal of grey stone across the passage, exactly as the bill described it: *clear the entrance.* Pip
on the lock ring, Zeph on the counterweights, Bron on the hinges with the flat of his axe. Music
builds. Everyone braces. The door goes over with a noise like a mountain clearing its throat.

> BRON: Formation. Whatever's in there has been in there a thousand years.
> ZEPH: Say that again, I want to remember exactly how wrong you sounded.

### 14. The tent (0:52)

Behind the door is a canvas tent. A folding table, a tin kettle on a spirit lamp, forms weighted down
with a rock, and a clerk in a company coat who looks up with the mild irritation of a man interrupted
twice in one shift. Behind him, company lamps on poles run a hundred yards down a widened corridor,
and eight people in overalls sit on crates eating lunch.

> CLERK: Entry chit?
> BRON: ...What?
> CLERK: Entry chit. Blue card, corner stamped. You can't be down here without one. Honestly, nobody
>   reads the postings.
> PIP: Is he real? Bron, is he real?
> CLERK: I'm going to need four names.

### 15. The fuse (0:56)

He isn't frightened of them, which is the worst part. He explains while the kettle boils, in the tone
of a man explaining a return policy. The old defences had to be tripped by somebody, and company
people can't trip them, because company people are insured.

> CLERK: The hazard's contractual. Outside contractors clear the entrance, the site opens, the crew
>   comes in behind. That's the whole reason your bill existed.
> LYRA: You hired us to be hurt instead of you.
> CLERK: I didn't hire anybody. I'm the duty clerk. But — yes, that's the arrangement, put like that.
> BRON: We're the fuse.
> CLERK: (writing) You're the entrance clearance. There's a form for it.
> ZEPH: (delighted, appalled) There's a *form* for it.

### 16. Your name is already on it (1:00)

Bron leans over to sign, and the clipboard has four lines on it, and the top line is already filled
in. It is in print, not handwriting. Name, people, age — and a town. The player reads it before Bron
does.

> BRON: That's my name.
> CLERK: Then you're in the right place.
> BRON: It's printed. It was printed before we got here.
> CLERK: (looking, mildly) So it is. Requisition came down with the names on. Saves me writing.
> BRON: Who signed the bill?
> CLERK: (turning the sheet) Superintendent Cadder, field office. He'll be up top by now. He'll want
>   to buy you dinner — he's like that.
> LYRA: Bron.
> BRON: What?
> LYRA: The line under your name. It says where you're from.
> BRON: Tellwater. Everyone knows where I'm from.
> LYRA: Then why does the company?

Cut to black on the kettle starting to whistle.

---

**Where the player is at the end of the hour:** they have fought in the rain, fallen a mile, been
warned by a dead man, killed two more who could not be warned, and found paperwork behind the last
door. They like these four people. And the last line of the hour is a question the next six hours
answer wrong.
