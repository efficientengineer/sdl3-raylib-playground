Three interlude pitches: the player leaves the main party, plays one person in a place the moved
{{SECOND_MOON}} has hurt, and that person later joins as a full member.

the three place tokens stay descriptive ("the hill village", "the coast town", "the ferry town") —
I would prefer that, see the conflict below.

## Conflicts and questions

1. **`NAMES.md` rule: "Sixteen named things exist in the whole game. Adding a fifteenth is a decision,
   not a convenience."** A fifth party member ({{SOWER}}) plus three place names is four new capital
   letters. My recommendation: name {{SOWER}} only, leave the three places described, and give
   {{FERRYMAN}} a role-only label (FERRYMAN) since he is one interlude's follower.
2. **A fifth party member is a premise change.** PREMISE has four. The case for her is a battle slot
   the party lacks (someone who can stand in front) and a protagonist who is at fault, which no one
   else in the game is. If the owner will not take a fifth, pitch 1 should be re-cast with
   {{SCHOLAR}} and pitch 3 dropped.
3. **PREMISE beat 3 says both {{SCHOLAR}} and {{THIEF}} join on the pilgrim road, and {{THIEF}}
   "steals from the party and stays for the food."** Both pitches keep that meeting exactly as
   written; the interlude just happens earlier in the running order, so the player knows her and the
   party does not. Nothing in PREMISE needs changing. What does need a ruling: whether her interlude
   plays before beat 3 (my design) or is held back as a flashback later.
4. **PREMISE beat 5 already has "a coast whose tides no longer match the moon" as a main-party job.**
   Pitch 2 uses the same coast. Either the beat-5 coast job becomes a return to a place the player
   already walked — which I think is the stronger version — or one of the two has to move.
5. **Level and gear carry-over.** Each interlude's hidden loot arrives with its protagonist. That
   means a player who never explored the interlude permanently loses that item, which is what LOOT.md
   wants, but it also means the main party can receive an item mid-chapter-two; the loot table will
   need a row per interlude before any of it is written.
6. **The lord's city upstream** is the wrong guess in pitch 3, and the villagers' wrong guess in
   pitch 2 is also the city. Deliberate — it is what people reach for — but if both ship, change one.

## Hardest rules to satisfy

- **STYLE bans the word "terrace"** (model-default list), and the brief's own example play list
  suggested terraces. I wrote "stepped banks" throughout. Same for **"engine"** — the orchestrator's
  framing uses it and the style file bans it, so nothing in the pitches names a machine at all.
- **SMELLS bans "salt flats" and "the stilt town".** Pitch 2 is a coast of dry mud and stone pens with
  a stranded fleet, and pitch 3's town stands on posts and stairs — I kept both off those two images
  on purpose, but they are adjacent and an owner read should check them.
- **STYLE 12, "one hazard and at most one optional per area, two or three enemy kinds per area, no
  chained penalties."** The hard part was not adding a fourth idea. Each pitch is three areas, three
  creatures, one hazard, one optional, one hidden item, one set piece, and that is all it holds.
- **The reversal-as-revelation ban.** The natural shape here is "the sky fixed one place by breaking
  another" — a mirror. I kept every region's harm concrete and local instead: a date that moved, an
  hour that moved, a season that did not come. None of them is the inverse of a first guess.
- **Rule 5, "find, then see, then hear."** Hardest in pitch 1, where the harm is a calendar. The
  answer was to make the player walk the dead banks and the emptying barn before anyone speaks.
- **No lecture, no one knows the cause.** Every region gets exactly one wrong guess and no correction.
- Only 3-4 sample lines per pitch, per the brief, so voices are indicative. {{THIEF}}'s and
  {{SCHOLAR}}'s lines are written against their `NAMES.md` voice entries; {{SOWER}}'s voice is new and
  unfixed — she would need a row in the Voices section before any clip is written.
