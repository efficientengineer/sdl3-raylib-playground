## Canon wording established in chapter 10

Later writers: copy these character for character.

### The intake form — `1065_four_marks`

Spoken by **`Narrator`**, in four boxes, over the object insert of the page, **before any character
speaks** — so nobody in the room reads it aloud and the player has it first. Precedent: `0575` in
chapter 5 prints the two Tally questions the same way.

> Narrator [1] {dread}: Windrow intake, ninety-three. Tellwater, house thirty-one. Subject aged nine.
> Narrator [1]: Condition: blank. Disposition: set and returned. Template 4. Two sleeves, source O.
> Narrator [1]: Local placement arranged. Surname issued from survey map.
> Narrator [1]: Recorded cost: four marks, two sleeves. Signed A. Fessel.

The four elements `canon.md` section 5 promises to `1435` and `1650` — **template 4, two sleeves,
source O, four marks** — are in boxes 2 and 4 and may be quoted in any combination. The form is
**silent about the given name** (outline-act3 item 6), and no character remarks on the surname line.
**Source O is never expanded**, here or anywhere in the chapter.

**One addition to the outline's text, flagged for the orchestrator:** *Tellwater, house thirty-one.*
The outline requires `1080` to cross-reference "the address on the intake form" against the Registry
household roster, and the form as the bible writes it carries no address. House thirty-one is that
address and it is the only invented field. It is deliberately not sixty-one, which would rhyme with
chapter 7's sixty-one adults. Change the number freely; `1080` is the only scene that uses it.

Bron's three lines in the scene, all factual, none about how he feels:

> Bron: Move the lamp over.
> Bron: ...
> Bron: Where's the rest of it.

and Hesk's answer, which `1070` takes its title from: **"That's the whole of it. One page."**

### Crewe's list — `1080_one_line_of_it`

Forty-three pages, one hand, never filed, sent to the Windrow sanatorium years ago. **Nobody says
whose hand it is** — that is chapter 12's. Zeph's reading, in order:

> Zeph: Forty-three pages. One hand the whole way, and the hand does not get tired, which means it was done in sittings.
> Zeph: Where they went. All four hundred. Town, trade, household.
> Zeph: House thirty-one, Tellwater. The roster for that house is one adult and one child, and the child is not named.
> Zeph: And the adult —
> **Zeph: Brae, A. — Windrow — laundry.**

The entry is exactly as `canon.md` section 5 fixes it. The list stays in the party's possession for
the rest of the game. Bron's last line of the scene is about opening hours and nothing else:

> Bron: Hesk. What time do the laundries open?
> Hesk: Six.

### The laundry — `1085_the_tea`

Eight lines. **Anneke six, Bron two.** Nobody else speaks; Pip is at the corner and never crosses.
Anneke does not know him, learns nothing, and is completely fine (hard rule 6). Nothing is explained
and nobody cries on panel.

> Anneke: You've been stood out there since before I lit the second copper.
> Bron: I'm all right.
> Anneke {hope}: You're not. That steam soaks you worse than rain does. It gets everybody.
> Anneke: Here. It's only tea and it's stewed, but it's hot and it's free.
> Anneke: Take it or come in out of it. Though it's worse in than out, I'll tell you that for nothing.
> Bron {sorrow}: No. Thank you.
> Anneke: Suit yourself.
> Anneke: Mind the ruts going down. They fill up and you can't see them for the steam.

The two-shot (panel 5) is the shot `characters.md` calls the whole of the scene: the two of them a
yard apart, **exactly the same height**, and neither of them knows. The chipped cup goes back in
through a propped door that stays propped because of the steam. `{hope}` is on the doorway line and
`{sorrow}` appears only on page 3. In the epilogue she asks him to tell her; nothing here is a
goodbye and it must never be replayed as one.

  Bron's and is about tomorrow: **"And the crawler plate gets done before Vantage. Ask Hesk what she
  wants off me."**

## Departures from the outline, and why

- **`1010` is 6 panels, not 5.** The outline's page 1 put `establishing_wide` next to
  `establishing_tall` (two wide-scale panels, R3) and its page 2 was two squares (R4). Page 1 is now
  wide / Ket's portrait / the tower / Bron's profile, and page 2 is the gate insert and the wall.
  The party stand at the **left** foreground, not the right, to obey `locations.md`'s Windrow staging
  default (arrivals come from the left). **Ket speaks**, which the outline's beat requires and its
  speaker list omits.
- **`1035` is 7 panels and 10 lines, not 6 and 9.** Two squares on a three-panel page break R4 every
  way it is cut. Page 1 gained the bed-tag insert alongside Marsh's warm profile; page 2 is a tall
  day-room window with the shelf of identical enamel cups, Pip's portrait, and the outline's stated
  last image. The residents face **left**, toward the doorway the party are standing in, so every
  eyeline in the scene points the same way.
- **`1060` ends on the file insert, not on Zeph's eyes.** Same panels as the outline, reordered so
  page 2 alternates scale; the outline's stated last image survives and Zeph's eyes are the beat
  before it. 9 lines, not 8, because panel 5 needed a reveal that was not Bron's.
- **`1065` is 6 panels, not 5.** The outline's page 1 was insert / wide / square, two distinct shapes.
  The added panel is `establishing_tall` — the shelving going up into the dark over the one lit desk
  — and it is where the silence between reading it twice and asking the question sits.
- **`1075` is 7 panels, not 6.** Page 1's `establishing_tall` became `establishing_wide` (two talls
  beside each other break R4) and page 2 gained the yoke stopped low over an empty station, which is
  the outline's stated last image; `impact` stays.
- **`1085` is 8 panels and 8 lines, not 7 and 7.** Every shape arrangement that keeps the street, the
  door, her face, the two-shot at the same height, the cup and the empty street breaks R3 or R4 at
  seven. The eighth line is Anneke's, not Bron's — he still speaks twice.
- **`1040` and `1030` run one and zero lines over.** `1040` is 15: Marsh answering "did anybody ever
  ask her what she wanted" needs its own box and so does the question.
- **`1070` gives Lyra no line.** The outline lists her as a speaker and its own beat says she does not
  say anything. The beat governs. She is in the room and silent, which is also the last useful thing
  chapter 11 can do with her.
- **The forty notebooks stay on Maren's own shelf.** The outline puts them on a shelf in Marsh's
  office; `locations.md`'s `sanatorium_marens_room` puts them in her room. Marsh points at them from
  the office in `1040` ("still on her shelf, where she left them") and the player sees them in
  `1050`. This keeps both files true and links the two scenes.

- **`1075`, Marsh's last line.** Three exclamations in a row is the loudest anybody gets in the
  chapter, and he is the kindest person in it. It is meant to be the most frightening line in the
  act. It may simply read as a man shouting.
