## Canon wording established in chapter 12

Later writers — chapter 15's hearing above all — copy these character for character.

### Crewe's confession — `1275_the_confession`

Eleven Crewe boxes with five reactions between them (Bron twice, a director twice, Kerrow once).
He is tired, courteous, relieved to be doing it, and never asks for anything. The eleven, in order:

> Crewe: Before the first item, and not on the agenda. Extraction Bore One, Tellwater, the ninth of Fallow, ninety-three.
> Crewe: I signed the season order that spring. It authorised the night run. That is my hand on the copy.
> Crewe: The fourth of Thaw. The branch density logs had been falling two years. They came to my desk quarterly.
> Crewe: At twenty-one forty the shift engineer entered a written objection and asked for the run to be stopped.
> Crewe: At twenty-two oh two Vantage answered her. Continue, per season order. A duty clerk quoted my signature at her.
> Crewe: Nobody decided anything at twenty-two oh two. I should like that in the minute in those words.
> Crewe: Twenty-two thirty-one. Nine seconds. Four hundred and seven residents, and four of the five crew.
> **Crewe: I was four years old in the last Hungry Winter. I did the arithmetic. I could live with the answer, and I still can.**
> Crewe: The ninth day. A flood and a subsidence. Sefa Quill drafted the instrument in two days, at nine marks and two a head.
> **Crewe: One more item. There is a study. Extraction, ninety-nine, twenty-six. It was completed and it was never circulated.**
> **Crewe: These are the pages. My hand, never filed. Kettle, R. — Emberrow — tannery. Brae, A. — Windrow — laundry.**

Key facts fixed here and quotable in ch.15:

- **The reason, stated once**: the Hungry Winters box above. It is the whole justification he offers
  and nobody in the room answers it. **His sin is that he can live with it and he says so.**
- **"Nobody decided anything at twenty-two oh two"** is his own phrase, entered in the minute at his
  own request. Ch.15's `1575` message log proves the sentence true; do not have anyone quote it back
  to him as an accusation, because he asked for it.
- **Crewe's season order is dated the 4th of Thaw 1093.** New; nothing else in the tree fixed it.
- **The resettlement's recorded cost is nine marks and two a head.** New. Deliberately not the
  four marks on Bron's intake form (`1065`), which is a separate line for a separate disposal.
- **The buried study's number is `Extraction, ninety-nine, twenty-six`** (ladder G's ch.12 rung).
  Kerrow repeats it in `1285` in exactly that form. Chapter 9's `0925` landed while I was writing
  and its survey tag carries **the year only** — *"Brass, stamped, filed. The year on it is
  ninety-nine."* — so the two agree: the party has had the year since ch.9 and gets the reference
  here.
- **The names are read in the `1080` format**, and the first two the player hears are people they
  know: Ross Kettle and Anneke Brae. `Brae, A. — Windrow — laundry` is verbatim from `canon.md`
  section 5 and from ch.10's `1080`.
- Bron's two lines are both about dates and nothing else: **"What day."** and **"What day was it
  filed."** He says nothing else in the scene.
- Kerrow's one line is **"Clerk. Take all of it."** — she makes sure it is minuted, which is what
  makes the next scene possible.

### Kerrow takes the chair — `1280_the_votes_were_already_counted`

She interrupts him three names into the list. Legal, quick, and correct:

> Kerrow: Chairman. Stop there.
> **Kerrow: Article nine. Notice was served on the sixth. I have the count and it is eight to three.**
> Crewe: ...Yes. I think that's right.
> Kerrow: The chair is vacated at the end of this item. Everything said under it stands in the minute.
> Crewe: I hadn't finished the list.
> Kerrow: It goes in whole. There is nothing further to be done with it today.

- **The count is eight to three; notice was served on the 6th of Dust; the instrument's clause is
  article nine.** All three are new and all three are quoted by number in the scene.
- **Crewe is not defiant.** His only objection is that he had not finished reading, and it is
  answered procedurally and correctly. He agrees with the ruling because it is correct.
- The board's next words are **"Next item. Tier One ventilation, and then the returns for Dust."**
  That is the cruellest thing in the chapter and it is spoken by a man with no name.

### Kerrow's unrefutable half — `1285_every_large_bore_inverts`

She is unpressed throughout. Nobody in the party answers any of it.

> Kerrow: I took the message at six on the morning of the tenth of Fallow, ninety-three. I was twenty-one. I woke the Director.
> Kerrow: In ninety-nine I finished a study. Extraction, ninety-nine, twenty-six. I put it in a drawer and left it there.
> **Kerrow: Drawing thins a branch. A thin branch inverts. Every large bore inverts in the end. There's no engineering in it.**
> Kerrow: The safe way is small bores. Several hundred of them. It costs about four times what this company is worth.
> Kerrow: Two. I wrote it; a draughtsman set the tables and he died in eleven oh two. I'll have it copied for you.
> **Kerrow: Publish it if you like. Then count. Forty-one thousand roofs under Ossun; nine wells standing; two running.**
> Kerrow: The branch is under the hills. That's the whole of the geography. There is nowhere else to take them.
> **Kerrow: Fifty-one heads under the Rim. Take them out and the Ossun head is cold inside nineteen months. I costed that too.**
> **Kerrow: You may still be right to publish. I'm not telling you you're wrong. I'm giving you the number.**

- Figures fixed here, all new: **two people had read the study; fifty-one bore heads under the Rim;
  the Ossun head goes cold inside nineteen months without them; forty-one thousand roofs.** The
  nine-standing-two-running count is the bible's and Ket confirms it on the page: **"Two running.
  Correct."**
- Bron's only question is **"How many people have read it."** Hesk's only objection is **"Then take
  your bores out of my hills,"** and it is answered with geography. Zeph says nothing at all. The
  scene ends on Bron's "...".
- Her fear of cold is staged and never named (back to the window, shaking slightly, leaves early).
- She never mentions Lyra or the Tellwater decommission, and nobody raises them with her.

### Ket's third light — `1255_the_ossun_head`

Per `canon.md` section 3 she is at **three** from here to `1390`.

> **Ket: I am at three lights. One on this side, two on the other. Plan around it.**
> Ket: — Off the rail. All of you, off the rail, now.

The governor lets go on the second line. She is not sentimental and nobody remarks on it.
**Panel 6 shows one temple with three dark and one lit**, which with two lit on the other side is
the ledger's three — the outline's "two of them dark" would have read as four of eight.

### The Ossun head's pitch — `1235` and `1255`

The chapter's machine clue is one number moving.

> Ket, at the school wall: **Two hundred and eight in the second, at this wall, tonight. Not two hundred and nine. Two hundred and eight.**
> Ket, at the head, three hours later: **Two hundred and thirty-four in the second.** / **Nine over eight. A full tone, in three hours.**

208 → 234 is exactly 9:8, which is why she can call it a full tone without rounding. Both figures
are invented and nothing outside this chapter depends on them. **The party never learns what the
extra draw is** — only that the number does not close. Ket's diagnosis: *"By being driven past its
rating. Something underneath is taking, and it is on no paper of mine."*

### Crewe and Bron in the garden — `1270_what_is_your_name`

Eight boxes, six of them Crewe's. No reveal; the player has known since `1065` and `1080`.

> Crewe: And is Anneke keeping well? Not Hesta. Anneke.
> Bron: ...She's at the laundry.
> Crewe: You'll have known that a long while. — ...You haven't. Have you.
> Crewe: I'm sorry. That was a thing to do carefully and I've done it on a garden path.
> Bron: How long have you had her on a page.

Bron's question is not answered here. It is answered by the confession's last box, which is the
join between the two scenes. Crewe apologises for the wrong thing and it is not his fault.

## Departures from the outline, and why

- **`1295`: the two orders are not posted yet.** The outline says they are "already posted" at the
  chapter end; `canon.md` dates them the **20th of Dust**, four days after the chapter closes. Canon
  wins. Zeph reads them out of the minutes as **two resolutions carried, to go out as orders dated
  the twentieth**, which keeps the outline's beat exactly and is colder besides. Ch.13's `1300`
  prints them in full as orders and nothing here contradicts that.
- **`1255` panel 6 shows three dark and one lit**, not the outline's "two of them dark". See above:
  the outline's count reads as four of eight and Ket leaves this scene at three.
- **`1275` reads two names, not one.** The outline says the names must be allowed to be boring, so he
  reads two entries in the format ch.10 established and is interrupted on the third in `1280`.
  Starting at Emberrow before Windrow keeps it a list rather than an aim.
- **`1275` gives a director two boxes, not one.** The outline's speaker list says "a director (once)"
  but its beat says two directors ask procedural questions. I used the beat, with one speaker label
  (**Director**) both times; split it into two labels if the orchestrator prefers.
- **`1270` is 8 boxes**, the outline's stated count, though its note says four to six. Six of the
  eight are Crewe's and two are Bron's; nothing in it is commentary.
- **`1210` is 13 lines, `1225` 17, `1295` 13** against 12, 16 and 12. `1225` carries a badly ordered
  Tally that cannot be compressed without making Bron sound hurried; the other two are one line over
  and I could not cut one without losing a beat the outline names.
- **`1240` lets Ollo speak** though the outline's speaker list omits him; its beat requires his line
  about the view. Same for Hesk in `1255` and `1205`.
- **`1205`, `1255`, `1265`, `1280`, `1290` list only the characters actually named in a panel.**
  Speakers who are present but not drawn individually are not listed, which is ch.11's `1195`
  precedent and avoids R6 warnings.
- **`1285` ends on Bron's "..." rather than on Kerrow leaving.** Her leaving early because of the
  cold is in the Beat; ending on the party having no answer is the point of the scene.

## Lines I am unsure of

- **`1275`'s last box.** Reading Anneke Brae's entry as the second name is a punch where the outline
  asked for boredom. The alternative is to read two strangers and let `1280` interrupt before Windrow,
  which costs the scene its ending and pushes Crewe's list off the page entirely. If the orchestrator
  wants it colder, swap `Brae, A. — Windrow — laundry` for an invented entry and move Brae into the
  first box of `1280`, where Kerrow's interruption would cut across it.
- **`1285`, "Two. I wrote it; a draughtsman set the tables and he died in eleven oh two."** The dead
  draughtsman is invented and exists only to make "two" a real number rather than a boast. It is the
  one sentimental atom in an unsentimental scene; cut the clause and she simply says "Two. And now
  you."
- **`1260`, "Mr Marrick."** Nobody else in the tree uses an honorific. It is meant to sound like a
  company city and it may simply sound like a different world.
- **`1270`, the Tellwater vowel.** I did not spell it phonetically — Crewe says "I've had that off a
  page for fourteen years. It's a different thing in a mouth" and the Beat carries the vowel. If the
  orchestrator wants the vowel audible, it has to be a stage direction the game cannot draw.
