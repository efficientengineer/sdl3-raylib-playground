## 1. Scene ids give exactly twenty slots per chapter

`CCSS` with `SS` in steps of five means 00, 05, … 95 — twenty scenes per chapter, no more. My
assignment asked for 7-10 panel + 10-16 talk + 1-3 narration, which tops out at twenty-nine. Both
Act V chapters are therefore written at exactly **twenty scenes (8 panel / 11 talk / 1 narration)**,
which is the low end of the requested talk count.

**Decide:** either accept twenty-scene chapters, or permit in-between ids (`1412_`, `1417_`) for
inserted talk scenes. DECISIONS D7 says the steps of five exist *so scenes can be inserted*, so the
second option is probably what was meant, in which case chapter 14 would gain scenes at 1412 (the
party's shock reaction on the street, currently compressed into 1420), 1447 (the boss aftermath,
currently folded into panel 1450's dialogue), and 1492 (Bron learning the fourteen questions,
currently folded into panel 1490). Chapter 15 would gain 1572 (the hunters and Cadder's crew on the
stair during the hold) and 1582 (Zeph after the chant). Say the word and I can supply those five.
**This affects every act**, not just mine — all five outliners hit the same ceiling.

## 2. The fifteen questions must be fixed as canon, now

The Tally is the single most load-bearing plant in the game: heard in full in ch.1, cut into the wall
in ch.5, read off a maintenance manual in ch.9, begun and unfinished in ch.11, and used to hold a
woman together in ch.15. Four different outliners' scenes depend on the same fifteen lines. The bible
never writes them out.

**Proposed canonical text** (Order of the Late Hour wording; free to edit, but fix it somewhere):

1. Do you know where you are?
2. Do you know who is holding your hand?
3. What is your name?
4. What name did your mother use?
5. What did you eat last?
6. What was the weather?
7. What happened the day before this one?
8. What did you do that you would do again?
9. Who is owed something?
10. Who owes you?
11. What is unfinished?
12. What should be told, and who to?
13. Is there anything you would rather nobody knew? *(the sister never asks what it is)*
14. Is there anything you want said out loud now, while there are people?
15. What do you want done?

Why this shape: it doubles cleanly as a strand-integrity check (locate, identify, verify continuity,
verify completeness, verify dependencies, declare disposition), **question 7 is the ch.4 edges test**,
which is why the archivists' trick works — they inherited it without knowing — and **question 15 is
not about the past at all.** It is the only one that cannot be answered from a record, which is why
the thousand-year-old maintenance manual leaves it blank with the instruction *ask the holder*, why
Lyra could not finish it on herself in ch.11 ("ask me the fifteenth" / "you will"), and why Bron
asking it of Maren in 1530 is the turn of the act rather than a trick.

If the orchestrator prefers different wording, only three things must survive: fifteen questions,
question seven is "what happened the day before this one", and the fifteenth asks what the person
wants done.

## 3. How the giving works, and why it empties Maren

The bible says Maren gives the record to the Braid chant-lines, that it takes eleven hours, and that
it empties her — but not by what mechanism, and the hard rules make the obvious readings illegal
(nothing is copied; setting needs a frame or skin contact at a minute per hour; a strand cannot be
spoken into existence). My ruling, used in 1530, 1560 and 1580:

**A stored pattern has to be rehearsed to stay coherent** — that is why the Seam sings (bible §5).
Maren has held four hundred and eleven records coherent for fourteen years by continuously rehearsing
them herself; that *is* the crowding and the voices. When a Vess line takes a record and rehearses it
daily, she stops having to, and that record goes quiet in her and stays quiet. Nothing is copied: it
now lives in the lines and not in her. At the end nothing is rehearsing in her at all and she is
empty and quiet for the first time since 1093.

This keeps every hard rule, explains why she cannot say what happened on the 9th of Fallow afterwards
while eleven hours of chant in Braid can, and makes the epilogue's "the record survives because it is
checked, not because it is sacred" land. **If the orchestrator rejects it, 1530/1560/1580 need
rewriting and so does the bible's §10 last paragraph.**

## 4. Why a chant-line refuses Sefa and accepts Zeph

The bible says the difference "is not sentiment, it is procedure" but not what the procedure is. My
ruling, used in 1550 and 1555: **a chant-line does not accept a record, it accepts a chanter.** A
chanter must be checkable, and a Vess's second tone goes flat when they lie (bible §4). Ilven Tessen
has thirty-one years of Sefa Quill's tone going flat on the public record and will not bond a line to
it. Sefa asked them to take a *thing*; Zeph asked to be taken *in*, as the bonded pair who answers the
record back, and a first chanter cannot refuse a checkable Vess who offers to hold.

This is cruel, exact, entirely procedural, and pays off both Sefa's defining trait and Zeph's
expulsion. Recommend keeping it.

## 5. What the Choir is, and what stopping it costs

The bible says Thurn's three hundred and forty have never held anything older than a week but does not
say why. My ruling, used in 1430, 1455 and 1585: **the Choir is the smallest possible draw head, left
running since year 0**, taking a little continuously from every living thing in the city and keeping
them at a level the way a cistern is kept at a level. Nobody in Thurn was ever emptied. It is also why
the Seam sings there: the chord is twenty-eight generations of Thurn, rehearsing. Ket stops it in
1585 using the shutdown procedure out of the recovered specification — which is the fifteen questions
in maintenance wording, and one line of the check comes back *not correct* and cannot be fixed.

**The cost, which I have deliberately made large:** stopping the Choir stops the city. Lamps, ovens,
water. Three hundred and forty people who cannot hold a plan for a week have to be walked up a mile
of stair by hand over months. They recover nothing — no past, no names, no generation — they simply
get to keep tomorrow from tomorrow. This is the only gain in the ending and I have kept it small on
purpose, per hard rule 16. **Do not let a later writer inflate it into a restoration.**

## 6. Where the bible's final image goes

The bible's ch.15 panel list ends with both "a new bill going up on the Sallowgate board in Tovin's
hand" and "Bron in the door in a cold wind he can finally feel." My assignment says the final image is
a new job on the board, and the epilogue is mine. I have therefore **moved both images to the last
scene of the epilogue (1655)** and given chapter 15 a different closer (1595: dawn at the Ninth Door,
Hesk writing down who comes out). If the orchestrator wants the board image in chapter 15 instead, cut
1655 and the epilogue ends on 1650 with Maren, which is quieter and weaker.

Note on the cold: hard rule — **Kell cannot feel cold coming**, they can feel cold that is on them.
"A wind he can finally feel" is not a physiological change; it is a man standing in real weather
instead of a story about weather. No scene may imply Bron's body changed.

## 10. Kerrow is not refuted, and the ending is not a win

Flagging it because it is the easiest thing for a later scene writer to soften. In 1545 the party
loses the argument outright; the best anyone manages is Hesk refusing to move, and Kerrow points out
that is not an argument. She is stopped by Cadder shutting off a bore head, sixty men on a stair,
Teach in a doorway and a guild that sent everyone — **people, and nothing else**. She does not concede,
apologise, or change her mind, and the epilogue vindicates her technically. Anyone who gives the party
a clever counter-argument has broken the act.

feel the hole — but it is a systems decision, not just a narrative one. Flag to the battle designer
that the chapter-15 hold is fought with six characters, one of whom (Sefa) does not fight.
