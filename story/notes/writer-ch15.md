### Maren takes her own plan apart — `1530`

Four sentences, unprompted, nobody argues her out of anything. This is the direct answer to Bron's
unanswered question at `1385` ("How many people can have it?" / "I haven't got that far").

> **Maren: I meant to put it into everyone. All of it, into everybody, so that nobody could file it away.**
> **Maren: Nothing is ever copied. It would divide. A hundred thousand people with nine minutes each.**
> **Maren: Not one of them could put it together, and there'd be no whole one left anywhere after.**
> **Maren: I'd have burned the only record of four hundred and eleven people in order to publish it.**
> **Maren: I'm sorry. Fourteen years and I couldn't get to the end of that until just now.**
> **Maren: Say it once. Whole. Out loud, to people who keep a thing by saying it. Then I'd like to sleep.**
> Bron: Who.
> **Maren: Braid.**

**Nine minutes each** is invented (411 records ÷ a hundred thousand heads, rounded the way a
frightened woman rounds). Change it freely; nothing depends on it. She apologises twice, never
raises her voice, never says "testimony" — `1385` owns that word and she has stopped using it.

### Kerrow, unrefuted — `1540`, `1545`

> **Kerrow: Forty-one shaped charges. Roof beams the length of this flight, and the lintel at the Ninth Door.**
> **Kerrow: Two thousand feet of hill onto both approaches, and onto the face.**

> Kerrow: Six months to walk them out by hand, one at a time. I costed it coming down. I have hours.
> Kerrow: A record is not people. Nothing in it is alive and nothing in it is in pain. You know that better than I do.
> **Sefa: ...I do.**

> **Kerrow: Drawing thins a branch. A thin branch inverts. Every large bore. There's no engineering in it.**
> **Kerrow: Cap this one and in nine years it's Dunnow. Or Lomm. Or your hall, at half past ten at night.**
> **Kerrow: Give me a plan that doesn't end with somebody standing in a street, and I'll stand them down.**

**The coat, entire:**

> Pip: Put this on.

## Departures from the outline, and why

- **Panel layouts.** Six of the outline's page layouts break R3 (neighbouring panels of the same
  scale) or R4 (a three-panel page with only two distinct shapes — nearly always two squares side by
  side). Every scene keeps the outline's shots, its content and its stated last image; the page
  splits and a few shot swaps changed:
  - `1505` is 3+3+2. The `object_insert` of the apparatus moves up between the two wide-scale
    establishing shots; `eyes_slit` (Bron) moves onto page 2; the scene ends on the two bare hands.
  - `1515` is 3+3+2. Bron's `portrait_inset` became a `two_shot` with Pip (shape variety, and it
    puts the party's restraint in frame); `high_angle_down` and `low_angle_menace` end the scene on
    the outline's stated last image, Maren small and overwhelmed.
  - `1525` is 3+3+2. Two of the outline's four squares are replaced: an `eyes_slit` of Maren's
    doubled eyes on page 1, and an `establishing_tall` of the wall on page 2 that carries the
    questions the scene does not print. Ends on the held hands, as required.
  - `1540` is 3+4; Bron's `portrait_inset` and the `over_shoulder` swap so the over-shoulder sits
    next to the `eyes_slit`.
  - `1560` is 3+4; Ilven's `profile_flat` became an `eyes_slit` (eyes closed) for shape variety.
  - `1580` is 3+3+2. The outline's stated last image is split: the unchanged wall is panel 3 and the
    scene ends on the object insert of Maren's empty hands, which is the stronger close. Kerrow's
    stair-head `establishing_tall` became a `full_body_reveal` because the shot has a character in it.
  - `1595` is 3+3 and ends on a `full_body_reveal` of Hesk standing in the open door seen from
    outside, which folds the outline's last two panels into one image that carries both the opening
    and the watching.
- **`1525` and `1530` split the questions differently from the outline.** The outline puts 1, 2, 3
  and 7 in `1525` and only 15 in `1530`. I end `1525` on **question 8 asked** and open `1530` on
  **13 and 14**, so that the seven questions Lyra never got to answer are present on the page
  instead of happening in a Beat. 14 in particular is the one that makes the chant an answer.
- **`1585` does not print the maintenance wording of the questions**, only Ket's responses. Ch.14's
  `1490` owns that wording and the two were being written in parallel.
- **Line counts.** `1505` 8, `1520` 15, `1585` 15 — as outlined. `1570` 15 and `1595` 8 are as
  outlined. Everything else is on the outline's number. No scene is padded.
- **Hesk's bible sample line "Write down who went in. That's the job now. That's all the job ever
  was." is not used**, because ch.14's `1405` reached the beat first with "Names. Trade. Who goes
  in." `1595` uses the `0565` revision instead, which is the same idea aimed at the correct door.
  Flagged for the orchestrator in case the bible line is wanted somewhere.

## Lines I am unsure of

- **`1520`, "She has given me a docket number. It is a real docket. It is one of mine."** It is the
  best failure in the scene, but it needs the chorus to contain somebody who ever touched a Works
  docket, and the four hundred were townspeople. Defensible — the resettlement passed through their
  hands as paper and one of them will have been handed a number — but it is the one line in the
  chapter that could be called out. Replaceable in one box.
- **`1570`, Cadder's "Ten words."** Counting his own sentence is very Kell and slightly too neat.
- **`1585`, "eleven hundred years"** in Ket's mouth. She does not round, and 1,107 is not eleven
  hundred. It reads as her being deliberately loose about a figure everyone in the room already
  knows, but a reader who has been taught she never rounds may flinch. Changing it to "one thousand
  one hundred and seven years" costs the line its rhythm; the orchestrator should pick.
