## 6. Who are these people to each other?

- **Falke.** Seventeen (computed in 0160), a hunter or trying to be one, hot-tempered, fast, climbs
  walls. His father was a hunter who died eleven years ago on this road when Falke was six. I learn
  the anger in 0110, the father in 0110 line 6, and the age in 0160. **I never learn what Falke does
  for a living now, whether he has ever taken a job before, or where he lives.** Nobody mentions his
  mother, ever.
- **Ottilie.** Wears white, patches people, has walked the west road twice, was twelve when the
  father was alive and was in Hart's yard then. I learn all of this in fragments across 0130–0160,
  and every fragment arrives inside a clause about something else. She is the most competent person
  on screen and the least introduced. Her relationship to Falke reads as "childhood friend who is
  waiting for him to notice her" — from 0130 line 6, which is charming and does that job alone.
- **Hart.** Falke's father's partner of twenty years, lame, now some kind of retired hunter who
  posted and privately funded this job, and who has been quietly paying the shrine for eleven years.
  He is the best-drawn person here. The chapter's real emotional spine is Hart, not Falke.
- **Stolz.** A rival hunter. Faster, richer, not actually malicious — he tells the truth and is
  enjoying it. That's all I get, and it's enough for one scene, but I don't know whether he and Falke
  grew up together or merely compete.
- **The clerk.** A function. He has more explanatory lines than any character in the chapter and no
  personality except "unhurried," which is a stage direction I cannot see.
- **The father.** Named nowhere. Referred to as "my father"/"your father" nine times. Cold, this
  feels deliberate at first and then feels like an omission — by 0170 the emotional point is that he
  had a whole secret life, and I cannot hold a person with no name.

**Contradictions I noticed.** (a) 0110 says the father is "the last hunter who walked it alone" and
died; 0170 says a *carter* came instead last year — so somebody else has been doing the run since,
which slightly undercuts "no one walks it alone." (b) 0130 says the woman "takes the jar once a
year"; 0170 says the jar goes on a shelf and last year's is still sealed — so she doesn't take it,
she collects it. Small, but I noticed both on first read. (c) 0110 says "two days out, two days
back"; 0130's line 1 corrects it to four days as if the clerk had said two. The correction reads as
an error unless you remember the clerk's exact phrasing.

## 7. What I understand about the world

**The Stair:** an enormous stone staircase built by "the ancients," descending from cloud and
stopping thirty feet above a grass field, with a small shrine village under it. A sealed jar of water
from Halm's well is delivered there once a year. Why — unknown, and explicitly unknown to the town.
**The jar/water:** a ritual nobody can explain. **The second moon:** *nothing. It is never mentioned
in chapter one at all.* **The lord in the west:** ruler of a city, hiring hunters at eighty coin a
week — ten times the local rate — to walk him to a mountain to demand rain from the ancients, and he
has someone who can compel them. **Hunters/guild:** there is a guild with a hall, a board, a bell, a
ledger, a sign-before-the-bell rule, guarantors, and "guild jobs" vs private ones. Hunters fight
things on roads. **Money:** 30 coin = a four-day job; 6 coin = a sword; 80 coin/week = the west. The
west is draining the valley of labour, which the town NPCs feel more clearly than any cutscene says.

**What this game is about, as far as I can tell:** a seventeen-year-old takes the errand that killed
his father and discovers on the way that the father, his mentor and the world all kept a secret at
the end of the road — while somewhere west, a rich man is going to force something ancient to answer.

**What I'd expect to DO between these scenes.** Walk Halm and talk to people (the field text implies
exactly this, and it's good). Climb the hill to Hart's. Go through the grain shed — the grainwife
line is a quest hook with no quest behind it that I can see. Walk the west road and fight sheets.
Camp. I expect combat because 0160 says nineteen fights happened; I have never seen one.

## 8. Dialogue quality, bluntly

**Author informing the player, not a person talking:**

> "This is not a guild job and it needs no guarantor — Hart is paying the thirty coin out of his own
> pocket." (0110/5, 219 chars)

A clerk explaining guild law to a local hunter who works there. He would say "It's Hart's own money."
Everything else in that line is the author.

> "Did you honestly think he'd sign for you? The lord of the western city is paying hunters — eighty
> coin a week. I sold the coast escort on and signed with him the same morning." (0150/2, 195)

Three facts and a taunt. Stolz would say the taunt and *let Falke ask*.

> "Eleven receipts, one a year, and Hart paid every one of them. Last year's jar is still sealed, so
> she has been gone a year and the carter never noticed." (0170/3, 168)

The climax delivered as a briefing, complete with "so" doing logical work.

**Lines carrying 3+ facts:** 0110/1 (bell, rule, Stolz, the escort, the hour, noon — six), 0110/5,
0110/6 (father reveal + decision + a maxim), 0120/4 (four), 0140/5 (five), 0150/2, 0150/4, 0170/1,
0170/3. That is nine of forty-one lines, and they include every plot-critical line in the chapter.

**Where a shorter exchange would land better:**

- 0110/5 → Clerk: "Not a guild job." / Falke: "Whose money, then?" / Clerk: "His own."
- 0120/4 → Hart: "Twenty years your father and I walked that water." / "Twentieth year it took my
  knee." / "Twenty-first he went alone." / Falke: "...And?" / Hart: "Hold the ladder."
- 0140/5 → Falke: "How?" / Hart: "Sheets. North of the river." / "One drops on you, you're held."
  / "He'd wade in rather than walk round." / Falke: "And you cut him out." / Hart: "Twenty years."
  / "Year he went alone there was no one." — same words, seven beats, seven boxes, one per breath.
- 0170/3 → give Falke the deduction and Ottilie the evidence: she hands him the papers, he counts
  them out loud. The person having the feeling should do the arithmetic.

**Also:** the 41 lines average 98 characters and fourteen of them exceed 110. On a Phantasy Star box
that is a wall of text in a genre whose whole rhythm is short boxes, fast taps.

## 9. Five things that worked and must survive

1. **"What's at the step? I've never been out that far."** (0130/3) — the hero asking the player's
   question, in his own voice, admitting ignorance. Do more of this.
2. **"Paying you to do what, exactly?"** (0150/3) — Ottilie as the flat, unimpressed interrogator.
   It's her whole character in five words and it's the cleanest exposition delivery in the chapter.
3. **"Hart used to stand at the gate and count."** (0160/6) — the only line that shows a feeling
   instead of stating one. Best line in chapter one.
4. **Hart charging six coin for the dead man's sword, with the ledger open.** Character and plot in
   one gesture, and Ottilie's "he will stand there all night at six coin an hour" is genuinely funny.
5. **The shape of the ending:** the father's secret extra day, discovered as arithmetic, in front of
   the staircase, with eleven receipts nailed in a door. The *structure* is excellent even where the
   lines carrying it are overloaded. Also worth keeping: the man-on-the-roof staging of 0120, which
   is the only scene whose picture and dialogue are doing the same job.

## Walking around Halm — what `story/field/text.md` teaches a cold player

**What I learn by walking that the cutscenes don't tell me, or tell me worse:**

- The well is **capped and roped, and the rope is new**, and *the town has never been told what the
  water is for*. This is the single most useful line in the whole chapter — it converts my confusion
  into the intended mystery. It should be a cutscene line, not an optional one.
- The economy, concretely: a woman's sister sends eighty coin a week home to an empty house; an
  ostler had work on eleven carts and now has none. These two NPCs explain the drained valley better
  than 0110/5 does, in half the words, with feeling attached. The clerk's exposition could be cut
  entirely if these were mandatory.
- Hart's knee and his stubbornness, before I meet him — the gate watch tells me both, plus that he'll
  shout at me from the roof. That's a perfect setup for 0120 and it's currently optional.
- That there are **monsters in the grain shed** ("it eats the sacks as well as the grain"), which is
  the only place the world says there are creatures at all, and it points me at the grain-yard wall
  Falke mentions in 0110/2.
- The practice posts, split at shoulder height, sixth one moved — nice, characterful, no exposition.

**What I still cannot learn anywhere in Halm:** what the Stair is or why it takes water (the well
