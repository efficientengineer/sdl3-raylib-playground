## Canon wording established in chapter 3

Later writers: copy these character for character.

### The nickname — `0310_the_welcome`

**"Coppertop."** Never explained, never corrected, never resolved. Bron has never had it in his
life. Used on screen by **three different people** in the square: Ista Fenn ("Coppertop! I said it
was him. I said so at the pump."), an unnamed townsman, and an unnamed townswoman. Nobody in the
party can account for it in `0340` and nothing later may account for it either — the point is that
it came off a file and no file is ever shown.

It is descriptive (a Kell child's copper hair in a town of humans), which is exactly the kind of
nickname a clerk invents off a written description. **No later scene may explain where it came
from**, and Ollo in ch.11 does not know it any better than he knows Denner.

### The repeated sentence — `0310`, `0340`

**"You've got her hands."** Said by Ollo and then, four minutes later and word for word, by Ista.
This is the "same sentence said twice by different mouths" the outline asks for, and it is what
Zeph counts in `0340`. It is also a physical description of a woman who was Neve Ardo playing a
part, so it is true of nobody. Nobody ever remarks on it again.

### Ollo's first words to Bron — `0310`

> Ollo: There now. There he is.
> Ollo: Hesta's boy. Hesta's boy, back in the square.

Ch.11's `1115` ("I had my hand on your shoulder in the square. They'd given me your mother's name
that morning") collects on exactly this, so the grip, the flour and the phrase are all here and
nothing else is. Bron answers with **"...You had the bakery. I remember the smell of it."** —
deliberately soft and sensory, because the inversion of the chapter is that the town remembers him
precisely and he remembers the town approximately.

### The dog

An unnamed townsman asks **"Did you ever get another dog, after?"** Bron says "...No. Not yet." In
`0340` he says, flatly, **"I don't remember a dog."** and assumes he has forgotten. There was no
dog; stock template 4 has a mother, a kitchen, a well and a fair in it. **No later scene may
mention the dog again.**

### Sem-Dree — `0335_the_keeper_of_the_dark_well`

- The flood date, said once: **"The low ground was flooded on the twelfth of Fallow, ten
  ninety-three."** Three days after the ninth; nobody underlines it.
- The correction, which avoids fixing a calendar date for ch.3: Zeph says "About fourteen years,
  then." / Sem: **"Thirteen. Fourteen is in Fallow."**
- Her own state: **"I am at four lights. Two a side. You should plan around that."** Four of eight,
  per canon section 3. `1145` shows two lit on the drawn temple and must stay that way.
- The near miss is Lyra's: "Were you up here when it flooded?" / "Correct. I was on this platform."
  and then the conversation moves on. **Nobody asks what she saw on the ninth.** `1145` is where
  they ask, and this scene must not be replayed as though somebody nearly did.
- Small detail ch.11 or ch.15 may reuse: she is given two loaves a week and puts them on the wall
  for the birds.

### The plate — `0355`, `0360`, `0370`

Cast brass, screwed into a stone alcove above the waterline at the bottom of the shaft, carrying a
maker's mark and the year **1093**. Bron's canon line lands on it verbatim:

> Bron: I've seen the plate. It's dated. That's not an argument, that's a date.

He takes it (`0360`: "It's four pounds of company brass and I'm on nobody's bill"), and Sergeant
Kesk takes it off him on the ridge road with a receipt. **The receipt has a number on it and Pip
kept it** (`0370`, `0395`) — ch.7's Registry work can use the number.

> Hanna: Then I write down that you said no, and somebody else comes, and it isn't me.
> Hanna: Take the number off it as well. The number is the only way anything ever comes back.

Hanna reads all four full names off a list "made at Windrow, the day before yesterday", including
**"Zeph Quill"**. Zeph's only answer is "...". He still never says the name himself before ch.7.

### Teach's three seconds — `0375`

His entire dialogue in the chapter is **"Let them go."** Three words. Hanna's reply is "...Open the
road." Bron's last line is **"...What was that?"** and no character answers it. `0380` has the party
argue about it and get nowhere: Pip has it as being let go, Zeph as being *released*, and Lyra's
version is the frightening one and is still wrong — "He came down to look at something else. He
looked at it, and then he went back up." **Nothing in Acts II or III may improve on that.**

### The fair, and its form — `0320`, `0325`, `0385`

- The fair is **nine days out** and **three weeks earlier than last year, which was a fortnight
  earlier than the year before**. Nobody in Tellwater finds this remarkable.
- Ollo's seal on the guild bill is his **bread stamp** — a wheat ear with a chip out of the top of
  it. (Distinct from `1120`, where his seal is the brass badge pressed into wax.)
- The fair schedule is a printed company form that comes up "in the packet with the postings". It
  fixes frames, bunting spans and the order of the games — the weight, the rope, the children's
  run, the dancing — and it is identical to the last two years'. It does **not** name the town's
  status, the postings or any person. Zeph's objection is professional: "Nobody is keeping this.
  They are re-issuing it."
- Ollo's canon line closes the chapter's warm half, said as a cheerful complaint about being bad at
  his own job: **"You don't act for eleven years, though, do you. After about four you're just
  there."** He has just said "Denner never got the frames wrong."
- The party leaves with **eleven loaves**, which is what they are eating in `0395`.

### Bron's request — `0395`

Underplayed and placed in the middle of other business, immediately after Lyra tells somebody to
bank the fire:

> Bron: ...Lyra.
> Lyra: Mm.
> Bron: If I'm ever wrong about something I remember. Tell me.
> Lyra: All right.
> Bron: Straight away. Not kindly.
> Lyra: All right.

No oath, no music, nobody at the fire reacts. Ch.6's "describe your mother's face" collects it.

## Departures from the outline, and why

- **`0310_the_welcome` is 8 panels over 2 pages, not 7.** The outline's page 1
  (`establishing_wide` / `two_shot` / `object_insert`) is two shapes for three panels and breaks R4,
  and its page 2 is four panels of two shapes. Ollo's entrance became a `full_body_reveal` (the
  ch.1 joke of pointing the villain shot at a company employee, aimed this time at a beaming
  baker), Bron's "working hard to look pleased" became an `eyes_slit` so the smile is cropped out of
  the panel that carries it, and the outline's `high_angle_down` of the whole square turned up and
  inward is now the **last** image rather than the fourth, which is where the outline says the last
  image should be.
- **`0330_the_window` opens on `establishing_tall` of the dark tower**, not `establishing_wide` of
  the square. Four panels of `wide`/`square`/`square`/`wide` break R4; the outline's wide of the
  empty square with Bron small in it is still there, as the closing `high_angle_down` from the inn
  door. Pip still does not speak; her box is "...".
- **`0355` is 8 panels, not 7**, for R4 on page 1 (`wide`/`square`/`square`). Added an
  `establishing_tall` of the winch housing and the canted gimbal ring, which is also where the
  alcove is introduced so panel 5 has somewhere to be.
- **The maker's plate is in a stone alcove above the waterline, not on the winch housing.**
  `locations.md` has `drowned_bore_plate_niche` ("a brass maker's plate screwed to the cut stone")
  and I would rather use the location designer's object than invent a second one. `0355` uses
  `drowned_bore_sump` as its location line and puts the alcove in panels 4 and 5.
- **Panel 6 does not ask for the date to be drawn.** STYLE rule 4 bans readable text in images, so
  the insert is "raised cast marks across its lower edge" and the player gets the year from Bron's
  box. R9 would have passed either wording; this is the safer one for the generator.
- **`0375` page 2 ends on an empty road, not on Teach's back.** `establishing_tall` takes no
  characters; his back is in panel 5's `high_angle_down` instead, walking up through the gap in the
  line. Three panels of `square`/`wide`/`wide` broke R4 as outlined.
- **Lyra's canon line "I'd like to stop arguing and go in" is in `0340`, not `0395`.** The outline
  allows either. It only means something where there is something to go into, and at the salt camp
  there is not; in `0340` it is the last thing said before Bron names the flooded ground.
- **Line counts.** `0340` is 17 against 14-16 and `0395` 18 against 14-16. Both carry two jobs:
  `0340` is the party's first real fight *and* the place Pip's three observations are put on the
  record, and `0395` is the inventory gag *and* Bron's request *and* the act's last joke. I cut
  every line that was not doing work and stopped there. `0380` is 16 against 12-14 for the same
  reason (the Teach argument and the closing of both roads).

## Cast I needed and did not have

- **A third named townsperson of Tellwater.** The outline wants the nickname used three times by
  three different people in `0310`. Ista Fenn is the only named townsperson besides Ollo, and Ollo
  owns "Hesta's boy". I used two one-off speakers, **Townsman** and **Townswoman**, which `check`
  accepts with a warning ("fine for one-off NPCs") and which the game will draw with no portrait.
  They are covered visually by the "Resettled townsperson of Tellwater" extra. **Orchestrator
  decision: is a portrait-only Tellwater entry worth it?** One would also serve ch.11 and ch.15.
- Nothing else. Ollo, Ista, Sem, Ivo and Hanna all have entries, and the thirty coats on the ridge
  road, the constable looking at the wall, and the crowd in the square are all covered by "Uniforms
  and recurring extras".

## Things I deliberately did not do

- **Nothing in the chapter is sinister and nobody in it lies.** There is no lingering look, no odd
  glance at Bron, no line delivered "strangely". The wrongness is only ever *precision*: a
  half-degree too accurate and a half-beat too quick. Pip never says what she noticed until `0340`.
- `0330` has no unease in it at all. Bron does not doubt anything, does not touch his temple, and
  is simply pleased. All of the dread in that scene belongs to the player on a second run.
- Nobody in the party says the word bore, or memory, or anything about what the sleeves are for.
  Pip names them as a shop product and the scene lets all four of them fail to make it mean
  anything.
- The spent sleeves are dull flat grey and described as rubbish, twice, so nothing lootable can be
  read out of that room.
- Nobody theorises convincingly about Teach, and no acting line of his carries recognition — only
  stillness.
- Sem-Dree never volunteers and no party member gets a feeling that she is holding something.

## Lines I am unsure of

- **"Coppertop."** It has to be warm, slightly embarrassing, obviously coined by somebody who had
  only a description to work from, and never explained. It is also comic, which I think is right
  for ladder A's fourth rung — the player should smile and then feel odd, not suspect anything.
  If the orchestrator wants it colder, "Tup" or "Pebble" both fit the same slot and only `0310` and
  `0340` change.
- **`0370`, Hanna naming "Zeph Quill" off a list.** It is free and cold, and it sets up ch.7 by
  establishing that everyone has the name on paper and he still will not say it. If it is felt to
  soften ch.7's price, cut the two words; the line survives as three names and a pause.
- **`0335`, Pip's "Does the town bother you?"** It is the closest anybody comes to asking Sem the
  real question, and it is deliberately the wrong question. It may read as one nudge too many.
- **`0395`, Pip's closing joke.** It is meant to be the worst joke in the act, and Zeph's
  correction of it is the punchline rather than the joke itself. If it lands as merely flat rather
  than as terrible, that is the failure mode.
