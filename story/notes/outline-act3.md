# Notes — Act III outline (chapters 7-10)

From the Act III outline writer. `story/outline/act3.md` is written and complete. These are the
things the orchestrator has to decide, or at least has to know I decided. Items 1, 2 and 3 are real
contradictions in the bible, not preferences, and they land inside the two most important scenes in
the game.

---

## 1. The bible contradicts itself about whether Maren holds Bron — HIGH PRIORITY

**The conflict.**

- Section 10 says Maren is saturated with "four hundred and eleven lives, including her own and the
  two hours of Bron's that were later drawn out of her and sold back into him." Section 8's timeline
  says 407 residents and 4 of 5 crew went blank, which is 411, and Bron was one of the 407.
- Section 12 chapter 10, and decision 4 in section 16, say the opposite and say it emphatically:
  "Bron was in the radius, not at the node. Nothing of his was ever stored anywhere... Maren can give
  four hundred people their lives back and she cannot give him one hour," and "*Reverse:* if he can
  be restored, Act V has no argument."

Both cannot be true. If Maren holds Bron, chapter 10's reveal collapses and so does the ending.

**What I did.** I treated decision 4 as governing and wrote chapter 10 accordingly. Bron is the one
person in the radius the node did not receive.

**Recommended reconciliation, and the bible edit that makes it clean.** The timeline already
supplies the mechanism without saying so: Teach "carries a nine-year-old four hundred yards **from
the bore shed doorway**." Bron was not in the town. He was standing at the head of the shaft, on top
of the open column, with the draw head 340 feet directly below him. Everything the field took from
the town came inward to the bell and was received by the woman standing at it; what was over the open
shaft went down the column past her and dispersed into the branch. Nobody has ever worked this out
and nobody in the game says it in these words — the file just records a blank child and a cheap
disposal. It is bad luck plus paperwork, which is this game's whole theory of harm.

Suggested bible edits, both one-liners:
- Section 10: change "four hundred and eleven lives, including her own and the two hours of Bron's"
  to "four hundred and ten lives, and the two hours that are now Bron's, which were her own."
- Section 8, the 22:31 entry: note that the boy in the bore shed doorway is the one the node did not
  take. One clause.

If you would rather keep section 10 as written, then chapter 10 has no reveal and I need to rewrite
1065, 1070 and 1085. Tell me which and I will.

## 2. Whose winter is it — the mechanism of scene 0985 — HIGH PRIORITY

Hard rule 3 says nothing is ever copied. So the warm childhood in Bron's head cannot be a duplicate
of anything. The bible's own ladder A, chapter 9 rung, resolves it correctly: "the specific warm
parts of his childhood are **hers**."

**What I wrote.** The winter Bron remembers is **Maren Ostry's own childhood**: she got lost above
Ostry Bar at nine. In 1093 a clerk drew two hours of it out of her — she was the only saturated
source on site and the cheapest available warmth — and set them into a nine-year-old alongside stock
template 4, to give an issued life some specific detail. She recites it in the first person in 0985
because it *is* the first person, and she works out in the scene why he has it.

This pays off three separate plants for free:
- Chapter 5: Kell cannot feel cold coming, and Bron remembers shivering. He remembers shivering
  because the child in that winter was a human child from the Flats.
- Chapter 4: the edges test. Bron has no day before. Maren has it, and gives it to him.
- Chapter 2: Pip's joke about Kell sleeping in snowdrifts.

**One consequence worth your eye.** Because the two hours were *drawn* from her and not copied, she
no longer has them and hard rule 6 says she cannot feel that. A scene writer may use this as one line
("and then somebody carried me — I don't have the carrying") or ignore it entirely. I have marked it
optional in 0985's notes. Do not let a writer build the scene on it; the load-bearing proof is the
day before.

## 3. Anneke Brae's people — MEDIUM, affects the laundry scene and the cast designer

Section 11 lists Anneke Brae as human and as Bron's mother. Bron is Kell. The bible never says the
peoples interbreed and the four peoples are written as distinct. Either she is Kell and section 11 has
a typo, or Bron was not born to her, or the peoples interbreed and that needs saying somewhere.

**Recommendation: she is Kell.** Four foot eleven, iron-grey hair that will not lie flat, amber eyes,
bare arms in a hot room because Kell run hot, and she complains about the steam constantly, which is
both funny and exactly what a Kell does. She is the same height as her son and neither of them knows.
"Brae" is a word off a survey map either way, which is the joke Hesk already made in chapter 5.

Scene 1085 plays identically whichever way you go, but the cast designer needs an answer before
drawing her, and she is needed again in the epilogue.

## 4. Chapter 7's side contract is on a tier that does not open until chapter 10

Section 7 puts "Quiet the school" in chapter 7 on Vantage Tier Four. Section 3 opens Tier Four in
chapter 10. **What I did:** Idda Renn walks *down* to the Even Hand's founding hall on Tier Two to
post the bill in person, because the boards above her are company boards (0725), and the party does
the listening while crossing Tier Four at night on the way to the Registry stacks (0760). Tier Four
still does not open as explorable map until chapter 10. Renn walking down two tiers twice, at
forty-four, after six years of nobody believing her, is better than the version where the party
strolls in, so I would keep this even if you unblock the tier.

Side effect: it gives the chapter 7 boss a mechanical payoff — clear the bill and Renn leaves the
school's back stair unbarred, which turns the fighting retreat into a winnable fight, which is what
the bible's chapter 7 brief asks for ("a clean win if the party has done side work").

## 5. Chapter 10 has no guild side contract, on purpose

The bible's eight side contracts skip chapter 10 (the Windrow bill is Teach's, in chapter 12, and
putting a second Windrow bill in chapter 10 would blunt it). I did not invent a ninth. Instead
chapter 10 has one optional **non-guild errand**: Arro-Vintry, at one light, wants a ring seat
cleared at a skywell that has been dead for a hundred and sixty-three years, and refuses to call it a
job or to pay for it, because there is no bill and no money and nobody is anybody's employer (1025).
It sets up his epilogue and Ket's. If you want parity across chapters, tell me and I will write a
second Arro scene, but it costs a scene slot: the id scheme caps a chapter at 19 scenes (05 to 95 in
fives), and all four of my chapters are at 19.

## 6. Bron's given name — a small plant I added, easy to remove

The intake form in 1065 records that the **surname** was issued off the survey map. That is already
canon (Hesk, chapter 5: "Sallow. That's not a name. That's a word off a map"). I have written the
form so that it is silent about the given name, and Bron does not ask, and nobody remarks on it. It
sits one inch away from a much darker question that chapter 12 brushes against when Crewe gets his
name wrong "in a way that is not his fault."

**Decide:** leave it silent (what I wrote), or let the form show the given name as issued too. I
recommend silence. "Bron" being real is the last thing he has, and the game does not need to take it.

She joins at five and must leave at two, and no writer may light one back up. **I spent one:** she

## 8. Recovery Chief Ovey needs a character entry

The bible names him in the chapter 8 brief and gives him nothing else. He is the boss of chapter 8
and he appears in a panel scene (0885), so he needs a `look` line and a portrait. My read: brisk,
professional, genuinely believes the frame is a kindness, travels with a handcart and two bearers,
and is the fourth reasonable person in a row to offer the party a reasonable thing. He should not
look like a villain. The cast designer has not been told he exists.

## 9. Economy: two expensive things in consecutive chapters

Sefa Quill's fee for the resettlement map (0770) and the debt settlement in "Collection at Emberrow"
(0840) are back to back, and the bible's own open question 6 already worries about the second. If
both are punishing, the player cannot take the Emberrow choice, and that choice is one of the four
complicity moments the bible explicitly wants. **Recommendation:** make Sefa's money fee modest — her
real price is her son saying her name, and she says so — and let Emberrow be the one that hurts. The
systems writer needs to know before pricing anything.

## 10. Two things a scene writer will get wrong unless stopped

- **The wards in 1035 are not a horror hospital.** No restraints, no filth, no bars, no screaming.
  Clean, warm, well-fed, forty people who look up with mild friendly interest. Hard rule 13 calls
  this the most important visual in the game and a generated sheet will drift toward zombies unless
  the panel notes fight it. Same for the day-room faces in 0930.
- **The restoration in 0875 is hands, not faces, and has no visual effect at all.** No light, no
  glow, no aura. Hard rule 8 plus composition rule R8. A minute of two people holding hands, and then
  a man walks out into a street.

## 11. Things I deliberately did not do

- No party joins or leaves in Act III. Six available, four in a fight, the whole act.
- No new town and no new skywell (bible decision 14). The only new places are a survey depot yard, a
  squatter camp, a crawler, and rooms inside existing settlements.
- Maren is never fought and the party never arrests her. In 0995 they simply let her walk, and no
  character says why. If Act IV needs her caught, it does not happen here.
- Nothing about Thurn, the people living in it, or why the Seam sings appears anywhere in Act III,
  per "Things the player must NOT learn early." The Ilder stump is a skywell with no branch under it
  and nothing in it is awake.
- Teach's reason for stepping aside is never explained in Act III, in three separate scenes. That is
  chapter 12's, and it will be a much better scene for the wait.

## 12. Tool status

I wrote no scene files, only the outline and this note, so there was nothing to run
`./story_prompt.py check` against. Every PANEL entry in the outline is specified within the
composition rules — 1-3 pages, 2-4 panels a page, 8 panels maximum, shot ids from the STYLE.md menu
only, alternating scale, at least one anchor and one punch per page, and a staging direction implied
by the "who stands where" in each panel list. Scene writers still have to write the `- staging:` line
and the per-character acting lines themselves; the outline gives them the eyelines.
