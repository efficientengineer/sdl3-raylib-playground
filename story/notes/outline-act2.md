# Notes — Act II outline (chapters 4, 5, 6)

Written alongside `story/outline/act2.md`. Everything here needs an orchestrator decision or is a
warning to another writer. Numbered so they can be answered by number.

## Decisions I need

**1. Chapter 6 has no side contract and the bible does not give it one.**
The eight canonical contracts are ch. 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12, 14. Chapter 6's shape is disaster and
aftermath, and I think that is deliberate — a bill would sit badly beside forty-one blanks. I have
put optional non-bill work in its place: `0635_the_sump_in_the_bunk_rows`, in which the party helps
Tibb Orsk and Sela Marrin on *their* bill, purely so the player likes Sela before scene 0670 takes
her. **Approve, or tell me to write a ninth contract for Saltmouth.**

**2. Two optional scenes are PANEL scenes (art cost for content some players skip).**
- `0510_domma_sath` (Braid, 4 panels) — the spent grey sleeve in the grandmother's hands. It is the
  chapter's best gut-punch and the payoff of the side contract the bible wrote.
- `0555_company_mortar` (Ninefold Terrace, 4 panels) — Hesk's hand on fresh company mortar.
Both are small (one page, four panels). **Approve, or downgrade either to a talk scene with a
backdrop** — `0555` survives the downgrade; `0510` mostly does not.

**3. A real plot fact sits behind an optional contract.**
"Count the doors" is the only place the player learns that three of the nine door-mouths have been
opened and re-sealed within the year. The bible puts it there, so I kept it there, and I wrote
chapter 5 so that it works without it (`0570_the_fifth_door` has an alternate argument using the
Sixth Door lease Cadder wins at `0520`). **Act IV writers must not assume the player knows about the
three doors.** If the orchestrator would rather it were mandatory, promote `0550`/`0555`/`0560` to
required scenes and drop the alternate line in `0570`.

**4. The ferry-rail pass mechanism.**
The party cannot be signed onto the rail as an unsealed party, so **Ket-Ossun signs them onto the
Meddra well's maintenance manifest as her hands** — which the Works cannot refuse without conceding
in writing that Meddra runs, which would cost them the "Basin's only working skywell" slogan. It
gives Ket agency, ties her joining to the map opening, and uses bible decision 6. **Confirm**, since
it is a small piece of new world mechanics.

**5. Sefa Quill's name on the Braid annex lease (bible section 6, ch.4).**
The 1067 Concession would make her eighteen at signing. I put her name on the **current schedule to
the lease, as countersigning Registrar** (narration `0430_the_lease_schedule`), which keeps the beat,
keeps the arithmetic, and lets Zeph refuse to say the name. **Confirm the workaround.**

**6. Three invented minor characters**, all built from the bible's own sample-name lists in section 4,
none of them a new town or a new well (bible decision 14):
- **Mir Lirr** — Vess, hall-keeper of the Even Hand's Braid chapter hall. Needed because the bible
  gives Braid a chapter hall and an unsealed bill on its floor but no one to refuse to pin it.
- **Domma Sath** — Vess, 80s, the grandmother of side contract 2.
- **Ol-Semmet** — Onn, keeper of the dead Semmet well at Saltmouth, at one light. Optional scene
  only. Written so he does **not** steal Arro-Vintry's ch.10 material.
**Approve, or supply names the cast designer prefers.**

**7. Teach appears in chapter 6.**
Bible section 6 puts him in the north gallery at Saltmouth, so I gave him a two-hander with Bron at
the relief tent (`0695_the_warden_asks`). He reveals nothing; the whole performance is one beat where
he looks at his cup. It is the second time he steps aside, which makes ch.9's ten minutes and ch.12's
discovery land harder. **Flag if Act III would rather he stayed off-screen until ch.8.**

**8. The fugitive order lands at Saltmouth**, in `0705_carry_that_box`, delivered by Emmet Sark as a
mild administrative fact on top of a box of files. Bible section 6 puts it in ch.6 over Crewe's seal,
unread by him. **Act III should treat the party as formally wanted from the start of ch.7.**
Note the corollary: at Braid in ch.4 there is **no warrant yet** — Tovin reports two constables, a
list of four names, and no paper. Act I writers, please do not end ch.3 with a warrant already out.

**9. A systems hook.** `0520_the_company_shop_at_lomm` is where the player can first buy a retail
strand (*Comfort*, one evening's wage). If the systems writer wants strands introduced earlier or
later, say so and I will move the scene. Hard rules 3 and 11 mean strands are single-use, never
craftable, never resold, and permanently consumed — spending one should hurt.

## Warnings to other writers

**10. Scene `0575_the_sealed_stair` is the most fragile thing in Act II.**
Two of the Order's fifteen questions are printed for the player, cut into a thousand-year-old wall,
and **no character reacts**. Zeph finds the numbering inconsistent and is bored; Lyra touches the
wall and does not speak in the scene at all. Ladder H says the party does not learn this until ch.9.
Every writer working chapters 5 through 8 must preserve **Lyra's four-chapter silence**. One line of
"wait, that sounds like—" destroys the ch.9 payoff and Lyra's whole arc.

**11. Chapter 6 must not foreshadow the inversion.**
Nobody in the party knows what a bore inversion is, the word is not used until Ottoline Kerrow uses
it after the fact, and no character predicts it. The only prefiguring is a bunk-row woman saying the
bore has nine years left, delivered as reassurance, and a density figure on a public board that
nobody reads properly. Hard rule 12: no clever character solves it.

**12. Blanks are not zombies.** Scene `0670_the_ring` and `0700_tibb_and_sela_after` are the game's
first sight of what an inversion leaves. Upright, breathing, warm, mildly friendly, good company. No
bodies, no screaming, no vacant stares, no repetition loops. Sela Marrin is **well**; the grief is
entirely Tibb's, and he never names it.

**13. Ket-Ossun is at five lights for all of Act II** and no writer may light one back up. Her lights
are small, flat and steady — not glowing. Her pauses are the iris ring clicking, written as a short
beat, never an ellipsis.

**14. What Act II does not reveal**, in case a later writer wants to borrow one early:
- Nothing suggests anyone is alive in Thurn. The Kell telling in `0565` ends on "nobody came out" and
  stops (bible: no earlier foreshadowing than ch.14).
- Nobody suggests Bron himself was set. The ch.6 payroll gets the player exactly as far as *his
  mother was an employee playing a role*; the sixty-one Hestas are ch.7.
- Teach never hints that he was at Tellwater.
- The Seam is never described as storing people. Ladder D's ch.4 rung is rule sets only; what Thurn
  did with it is ch.5, told by the Kell, without mechanism; why it sings is ch.14.
- Nobody says the word "memory" anywhere in the act if a writer can help it.

**15. Cast handles used.** I used the bible's handles throughout: `Ket-Ossun`, `Hesk`, `Cassa
Tessen`, `Tovin-Caleth`, `Orrin Cadder`, `Ottoline Kerrow`, `Ivo Teach`, `Emmet Sark`, `Tibb Orsk`,
`Sela Marrin`, `Dov Ninefold`, `Ol-Semmet`, `Mir Lirr`, `Domma Sath`, plus an unnamed hall-guard. If
the cast designer lands on different `characters.md` handles, the outline's `who:` lines need a pass.

**16. The hall-guard in `0590_the_standing_post` must match scene 003's approved design exactly** —
segmented armour over nothing, tattered red cloak, two small steady blue points in the helm,
perfectly still, silent but for the armour. It never strikes first and never pursues, and Hesk's one
mangled line of the relief formula **fails**, which is the point of the scene.
