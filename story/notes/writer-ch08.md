## Canon wording established in chapter 8

Later writers: copy these character for character.

### Ladder A's chapter-8 rung — `0825_she_gives_it_back`

Said by a tanner's wife in a yard, as a fact everybody knows, in the middle of an argument about
which day the lime came. No party member repeats it and nobody underlines it.

> **Tanner's wife: She gives it back. She doesn't make it up. There's a difference and everybody down here knows it.**
> Lyra: Who told you the difference?
> Tanner's wife: Nobody told me. You watch a man come out of his own kitchen knowing his sister's name. You'd know.

Nobody in the chapter says "miracle". The town's register is curiosity and mild approval with a
running factual dispute attached, which is how the player learns she is ordinary news here.

### Maren's first spoken lines — `0860`, `0865`, `0870`, `0875`, `0895`

She is crowded, not mad; courteous; never gloats; never raises her voice; apologises constantly. Her
first three lines in the game are an apology, an explanation of why her hands are visible, and an
unasked-for kindness to the smallest armed person in the room.

> Maren: Sorry. Sorry, there's nowhere to sit but the crate and I'm on the crate.
> Maren: I'm not moving. Hands where you can see them. They're always where you can see them, it saves this.
> Maren: Sit down. You've been on a split seam in that left boot since Windrow and it's taking your foot sideways.

**The look at Bron (`0860`, panel 7) and its one box.** She recognises something he is carrying, not
a person, and she says nothing about it in this chapter or the next:

> **Maren: ...Sorry. Somebody in here just sat up.**

**Her own account of the doing** — `0865`. No "pattern", no Seam, no explanation of herself, and the
hard rules stated as a working woman's shop talk:

> Maren: I don't know. I know what I can do. Nobody ever gave me the other half of it and I stopped waiting for it.
> Maren: I take somebody's hands — sorry, you said exactly. Skin on skin, both hands, and I give them their own back.
> **Maren: It runs about a minute for an hour of them. Some of them are long.**
> **Maren: No. Afterwards it's theirs and it isn't mine, and I couldn't make another one if you paid me. I wouldn't know how.**

**The Vess second tone, paid off** (`0420`'s plant). Zeph says it out loud, in front of her, and it
settles nothing:

> Hesk: Have you ever done it to somebody who didn't ask?
> Maren: No.
> **Zeph: Hesk. There's nothing under her voice. She's human. There is no tone in her to go flat.**
> Hesk: Then I'll make my own mind up. I generally have to.

**Her warning before a restoration** — `0875`. Quote this if any later scene needs her consent
procedure. She says the hard part twice and makes the person say it back:

> **Maren: The years don't come back. You don't get to have lived them. It comes whole, tonight, all at one strength.**
> Maren: Then say it back to me. ...Good. Hands. Both, and don't hold on.

**Her figures** — `0895`, and the only place any number of broken restorations is spoken in the
game so far. Taken from bible section 8 ("eighty-one found and restored in the last two years, of
whom twenty-seven are in care or worse"):

> Hesk: You've no idea how many of them come apart. You hand a man fourteen years and you're gone north by morning.
> **Maren: Eighty-one, in two years. Twenty-seven of those are in a care house or worse. Two of them I sat with until they died.**
> Maren: I count all of it. It's the only sum I've ever been any good at.

Her route out of the chapter, which sets ch.9: **north, Ostry Bar, then out on the salt.** "I walk.
They've crawlers and a telegraph." The party does not arrest her, does not travel with her, and
decides nothing; Bron is asked directly whether they mean to stop her and does not answer.

### Lyra and Maren, the only conversation they ever have — `0870`

Six boxes. Neither offers comfort, neither is impressed, nothing is said in the abstract, and the
Sealed Stair is not touched.

> Lyra: Do you sleep?
> Maren: Not in the way you mean it. I lie down. It's crowded.
> Maren: You've sat with people. You've got the face for it. How many?
> Lyra: **Two hundred and six.** I stay the hour, I ask what has to be asked, and I carry it out with me.
> Maren: And where do you put it down?
> **Lyra: I don't. That's the arrangement.**

Lyra's count of hours sat is **two hundred and six**; invented, and chosen so it does not rhyme with
Merrit Tack's 212 cleared bills. She never says "fifteen" and never quotes a question — canon's
standing rule on the Tally holds. "I ask what has to be asked" is the closest she comes.

### Dalla Kettle — `0880`, `0895`

Her two sentences in `0880` are the chapter, and the second is the one nobody answers:

> Dalla: Ross. Your tea's on the side.
> Ross: Not now, love. I'm looking for my wife.
> Dalla: I'll leave the door. He comes in when he's cold. He always has.
> **Dalla: What am I meant to do now? I'm not being clever about it. I'd like somebody to tell me.**

Ch.12 writer: she does not cry, shout or blame anybody out loud in ch.8, and she makes tea for the
party the following night and tells them not to be polite about it. The last line of the chapter is
hers — **"Anybody want more?"**

### Ross Kettle before and after

`0830` is the warm scene the destruction is measured against: nine yards a week down to two, one
apprentice named **Teb**, bad knees, forty-one steps up from the yard that he counts out loud coming
home. He came down "in ninety-three with the rest of the flood lot, some town up north", cannot
remember much from before, never could, and his wife finds it restful. Nobody applies the edges test
to him and nobody reacts to the year.

After (`0875`, `0880`) he does not scream. He asks, politely, where his house is:

> Ross: Where's my house?
> Ross: The one by the water. Well outside the door, ash behind it. Who's in my house?
> **Ross: Sorry — sorry, I'm sorry. Do you know where my house is? It's the one by the water.**

His first wife is named **Wenna**, once, in `0880`, and never again. The daughter is never named and
never appears.

### Recovery Chief Ovey — `0885`

Brisk to the point of rude (editor's temperature note), right that it would work, and honest about
exactly what it does. His opening is the rudest thing in Act III and it is not meant unkindly:

> **Ovey: Recovery. Chief Ovey. Which of you is the wife?**
> Ovey: Not you. You. Right. Four minutes standing up and he's comfortable before the cart's turned round.
> **Ovey: It doesn't take anything out. It puts something over. It'll want doing again in a month and I'll come back and do it.**
> Ovey: Still there. I've not lied to anybody in this street tonight and I'm not going to start with a sister.
> **Ovey: Missus. It's your say and nobody else's in the Basin. I need it out loud.**

Dalla's answer is a silent box. The ambiguity the outline protects is preserved: she never asks for
it out loud in either branch.

### Teach's second step-aside — `0890`

What Maren says is small, physical, unexplained, and does not name Tellwater, Bron, or a boy:

> Ivo: Maren Ostry.
> Maren: Warden Ivo Teach.
> **Maren: You came out of a house with a kettle in your hand.**
> **Maren: It had boiled dry. You'd taken it off the fire and there was nowhere to set it down, so you carried it about till it got light.**
> Maren: Nobody wrote that down anywhere. Nobody ever does.
> Maren: I'm sorry. It comes up. You're the first thing in a long while that's come up like that.

Teach does not reply. Two silent boxes and a gateway.

**Ch.12 writer, please note the mechanism, because it is load-bearing and easy to break.** Maren
cannot be holding this off one of the four hundred: they were all blank at 22:31 and Teach did not
arrive until 01:20. She is holding it because **she was there herself** — brought up the shaft alive
at 03:10, on a board in the street, speaking in voices, while he was still carrying people. It is
one of the very few things in her head that is genuinely hers, which is why it surfaces when she
sees him. Nothing on screen says any of this, in ch.8 or ch.9.

## Departures from the outline, and why

**All seven panel departures are R3 or R4.** The outline's panel lists for `0810`, `0860`, `0875`,
`0880` and `0890` each put two same-scale panels side by side or gave a page of three or four panels
only two distinct shapes.

- **`0810` is 7 panels (3 + 4), not 6.** `establishing_tall` and `establishing_wide` are both scale
  *wide* and cannot be adjacent, so the outline's panels 1 and 2 are separated by the stone block
  insert, and the outline's *last image* — the care-house roof, the only clean one in the cut — is
  now a seventh panel (`establishing_tall`, no characters) rather than a line of description. Pip's
  profile keeps its place before it. Hesk's last box lands on the roof, which gives "there's nothing
  to put back" a second target for free.
- **`0840` panel 3 has no acting line** because the mother is not in `characters.md`; her eyeline and
  her face doing nothing are written into the panel description instead, which reaches the sheet the
  same way.
- **`0860` panel 6 is `eyes_slit`, not `profile_flat`.** Two square close panels next to the boot
  insert broke R4. Lyra's eyes cut sideways in a letterbox is the same beat — professional
  recognition, unsurprised — and colder.
- **`0875` is 7 panels (2 + 3 + 2), not 8.** The outline's page 1 (`two_shot` / `object_insert` /
  `profile_flat`) has only two shapes, and its page 3 (`impact` / `establishing_wide`) has only one:
  every "street from a long way back" shot in the menu is wide-shaped. So:
  - **page 1 is the two of them and then the hands, and nothing else.** That is the canon ruling on
    `0875` (hands, not faces) built into the page structure: the screen clears on a wide two-shot of
    two people about to touch, and the whole transfer is one insert of four hands.
  - **Maren's `profile_flat` during the transfer is cut** — deliberately, for the same reason. There
    is no face in the minute it takes.
  - **the outline's `impact` of Ross on the steps became `full_body_reveal`**, head to boots at a low
    angle, a man coming up out of the ground. The `establishing_wide` of the street is unchanged and
    is still the last image.
- **`0880` panel 3 is `eyes_slit`, not `profile_flat`** (two adjacent close panels). Ross's mouth
  mid-word moved into panel 1's description so the beat survives.
- **`0890` panel 3 is `eyes_slit`, not `portrait_inset`** (two adjacent close panels). Maren's
  gentleness is in the eyes and in what she says.
- **`0885` is exactly as outlined**, six panels, no change.

**Content departures.**

- **`0850` does not use "Sixteen years and one pair of boots. They were very good boots."** The line
  is already spent **twice** — `0597_pip_and_the_key` (to Hesk) and `0694_pip_cannot_joke` (to Bron,
  as the thing left when the jokes run out), and the ch.6 writer already flagged the collision. A
  third use, again to Bron, two chapters later, would be a literal repeat between the same two
  people. `0850` keeps the whole substance and changes the words, and gains something by being said
  at the actual gate: *"They gave me these at that door. I was six. A man measured my foot with a
  stick and wrote it in a book."* / *"They're the oldest thing I own. By a long way."* She still
  never says she is sad and never says what was done to her. **Orchestrator: if `0597` gets rewritten
  and `0694` keeps the line, this stays as it is. If you would rather ch.8 own it, `0694` is the one
  to change, not `0597`.**
- **`0820` has no Pip in it.** The outline's `who:` lists her and its note says she is not in the
  room and the writer should place her deliberately. She took the bottom of the street, the yards and
  the rope-walks in `0815`, and she has not come up to the hall, and nobody remarks on it.
- **The collector is never named.** The outline offers "Collector Aldo Venn" and says an unnamed
  alternative is fine. **Aldo** is the `characters.md` handle for Aldo Fessel, so the name would
  false-match as a whole word in any panel description; the speaker label is `Collector`. He is
  likeable, unembarrassed, and not a villain in either scene.
- **`0845` is written as three labelled variants in one file.** The scene format has no branch
  selector, so the three blocks sit under `## Dialogue` separated by parenthetical labels that the
  parser ignores (it only reads lines beginning `- `). `check` passes and reports 19 lines, which is
  the sum of the three branches — **only one block should ship** until the game can pick. See "for
  the orchestrator" below.
- **`0830`: Lyra's one question too many is not the edges test.** The outline's note forbids running
  the test on Ross, and "what did you do the day before" is the test in `0465`'s own words. She asks
  whether he misses the town instead, and gets the cheerful nothing the outline wants: *"Miss it? I'd
  have to have had it first."*
- **`0895` is 14 boxes but the party's silence is one of them.** Bron's answer to "do you mean to
  stop me?" is a silent box and stays a silent box; Hesk answers instead. Nobody thanks Dalla in a
  way that would let the player off.

## Lines I am unsure of

- `0880`, **"Wenna."** Ross saying a name the player has never heard is the fastest way to land what
  arrived, but it is also the only proper noun out of Tellwater that ch.8 introduces. Cutting it
  costs one box: "There's somebody put in the house" carries the beat alone.
- `0890`, **the kettle.** It is meant to be opaque now and to detonate in ch.12. If ch.12's writer
  cannot use it, any small physical thing he did between 01:20 and dawn works in its place, as long
  as Maren could have seen it with her own eyes after 03:10 and it is not an accusation.
- `0865`, **"There's nothing under her voice. She's human."** Zeph saying it out loud in front of her
  is rude and exactly him, but it does spell the mechanism out for the player one beat earlier than
  it needs to be spelled. Cutting it leaves Hesk's decision unexplained, which is also good.
- `0870`, **"I don't. That's the arrangement."** Possibly one degree too close to stating the theme.
  It is about her own job and not about the game, which I think saves it.
- `0885`, **Ovey's "Not you. You."** The editor asked for brisk to the point of rude and this is the
  rudest thing in the act. If it tips him into villain, cut the two words and he is merely quick.
