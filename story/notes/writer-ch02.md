## Canon wording established in chapter 2

Later writers: copy these character for character.

### The hall-guard's oath, off the wax cylinder — `0265_the_wax_cylinder`

Sixty-one words, three boxes, played three times, identical to the syllable. It contains chapter 5's
terms (`0585_the_oath`) verbatim and adds a front and a back so it reads as a duty roster, not
poetry. **Chapter 14 still owns the relief formula's front half; nothing here supplies it.**

> Voice: The post is taken. The watch is set at the sixth hour. The way below is barred, at this door and at every door.
> Voice: Bar the way. Give warning. Do not pursue. Do not strike first.
> Voice: And stand aside for a voice that knows the words. The post is held until the relief is spoken, and not one hour past it.

Speaker label is **`Voice`** (no portrait). The count is Zeph's: *"Three times. Sixty-one words.
Every pause in the same place. Every breath."* Lyra's one short line is the ladder C rung: *"Then it
isn't saying it. It's being played."* **Nobody notices the last clause** — the party does not learn a
guard can be talked past until ch.5. Zeph quotes this cylinder back at `0585` and that line already
exists.

### The bench man — `0220`, never named

His only name is the number a conductor greets him by: **four-one-nine**. He says "Good morning"
twice, does not know where he is staying, is asked where he slept last week, thinks about it
agreeably, and then asks Lyra whether she is warm enough. **He is never distressed and nothing is
wrong with him.** Nobody in the party guesses, names it, or runs any test on him.

### The Windrow rumour — `0225`

Garbled on purpose and never corrected: two unrelated men fused into one, and the direction of the
harm exactly backwards. Maren is **a grey coat, walking, on her own** and nothing else. Orla's
closer, said with approval and meant kindly: *"Company sends a man once a month to see he's fed.
Every month. Never missed one."*

## Departures from the outline, and why

- **`0210` is 4 panels on page 1 and 2 on page 2**, not 3 and 3. The outline's page 2
  (`establishing_wide`, `portrait_inset`, `profile_flat`) had two close-scale panels adjacent (R3)
  and only two shapes (R4). Pip's `portrait_inset` moved to page 1; page 2 is the town and Bron's
  profile. Panel count is unchanged at six.
- **`0220` is 2 panels on page 1 and 4 on page 2.** The outline's page 1 was
  `establishing_wide`/`two_shot`/`object_insert` — two wide-shaped panels, so R4 failed. Lyra's
  `profile_flat` became `eyes_slit` on page 2 for the same reason; her acting line still says the
  face gives nothing away, which is the beat.
- **`0265` is 7 panels, not 6**, for R4 on page 1, and the stores ledger insert moved to page 1 so
  page 2 can end on the horn still turning, which is the outline's stated last image. The ledger is
  described as ruled columns of handwriting rather than legible dates, per STYLE rule 4.
- **`0270` is 7 panels, not 6-7 with the cutting-head insert.** Panel 3 is now **the company order
  pinned to the rig's board with two inked signatures at its foot** — canon section 5 requires
  Crewe's and Kerrow's countersignatures on screen at `0275`, and `0275` is a talk scene with no
  art. Nobody reads them, nobody says them, and the signatures are scrawls. The rig is carried by
  panels 1 and 7 instead.
- **`0280` is 7 panels, not 6.** Page 2 needed a third shape; the handshake `over_shoulder` is kept
  as the last panel and `eyes_slit` of Bron (level, not narrowed — he has nothing left to be angry
  with) sits before it.
- **`0285` is 21 lines, not 15-16.** It is the chapter's centre, five speakers, and every beat the
  outline names is load-bearing: the ordering, the admission, the money, the hold question, the
  unanswerable question, the pudding. No line in it is longer than about 110 characters. Cut the
  ordering exchange (first three boxes) if it must come down.
- **`0250` and `0255` do not repeat "a hundred and sixty-three years."** Chapter 10's `1025` (being
  written in parallel) uses it, and chapter 4's `0450` quotes Ket on it, so Arro's joke here lands on
  the year instead: *"I have had since the year nine hundred and forty-four to get it right."*

## Flags for the orchestrator

1. **Arro's one request is a flat file, second cut, eight inches** (`0255`). Chapter 10's `1025` is
   already written and does not collect it. It costs one box; the ch.10 or epilogue writer should be
   told, or the beat quietly dies.
2. **`0255` and `1025` both ask Arro why he keeps a dead machine.** Both were required by their own
   outlines and the answers are different (ch.2: the post did not end, and he has the date he was not
   relieved; ch.10: so that the day somebody wants it is a day's work). They read as complementary to
   me, but they are the same question twice and somebody should decide that deliberately.
3. **`0285` runs to 21 lines** against an outline budget of 15-16. See above.
4. **`0250` gives Arro the joke; `1025` has him "aware of the joke".** No collision, but the two
   scenes should not both be cut down to the same beat.

  words and to make it sound like a roster. Change them freely — ch.5, 13 and 14 only need the terms.

  mouth, and it is the closest this chapter comes to mechanism language.

  whether the hall paid out. This is the only reading where all three scenes are true.
- `0235`, Pip's "I hate that he was nice about it." It may be one beat too explicit about what the
  chapter is doing.
