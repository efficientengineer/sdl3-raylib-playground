## Canon wording established in chapter 7

Later writers: copy these character for character.

### Sefa's price — `0770_the_registrar`

The money half is a **weight charge on superseded stock**, which is the only reading I found that is
funny, legal, in her register, and "modest" as canon D8 requires. It is the whole of the money and
there is no bribe anywhere in the chapter.

> **Sefa: Superseded in ninety-six. Superseded stock goes out by weight — four marks the pound, and that one is three pounds and a little.**
> **Bron: Twelve marks and four.**
> **Sefa: And one other thing, which is that I should like to be asked for it by name. Out loud. In front of your friends.**
> Zeph: No.
> Pip: ...How long can you sit like that?
> **Zeph: Registrar Sefa Quill.**
> **Sefa: Thank you. That is the correct form of it.**

**The fee is twelve marks and four; the rate is four marks the pound; the roll weighs three pounds
and a little.** Bron asks the figure again at `0780` and Lyra gives it, because a fact is the thing
he can hold. Chapters 13 and 15 may quote the rate as the joke it is.

**She never once says Zeph's name in the scene.** That is deliberate and it is the whole of `0775`:

> **Zeph: She didn't say mine.**

Four words, the most sincere thing he has said in seven chapters, and it only works if no later
writer puts "Zeph" in her mouth retroactively in this scene. She does not gloat, does not apologise,
does not lie, and does not raise her voice. Her physical business is a lit lamp at three in the
morning, her coat still on, and **a visitor's chair already pulled out square and empty** (the key
`registry_office` supplies the chair) — she has been waiting up because a flagged docket is a
flagged docket.

Zeph's refusal at `0755`, one scene earlier, does not spend the name:

> **Zeph: The Registrar is my mother. I have not said her name out loud since I was nineteen and I am not going to start in this room.**

### The map, and the sixty-one — `0780_sixty_one`

The resettlement schedule as the party reads it. Zeph reads too fast, admiringly, and then stops:

> Zeph: Eleven towns. Four hundred and seven names, a trade against each, a date, and a cost per head.
> Zeph: Lomm forty-one. Emberrow sixty-three. Windrow a hundred and nine. It is beautiful work and I want that said out loud.
> Zeph: And a cross-index at the back. Issued templates, numbered one to nine, and what is put into each of them.
> **Zeph: Four. Widow. One son. Name used — Hesta. A kitchen. A well on a square. An autumn fair.**
> Zeph: ...
> **Bron: Sixty-one. / I've counted it twice. Sixty-one.**

**Four hundred and seven names** is the bible's resident figure and matches `canon.md`. **Lomm 41,
Emberrow 63, Windrow 109** are mine and are invented; they are three of eleven towns and do not have
to sum to 407. Change freely. **The contents of template 4 are the bible's** (a mother called Hesta,
a kitchen, a well, an autumn fair) with "widow, one son" lifted off the ch.6 payroll line so the two
documents visibly rhyme.

The rung is exactly Ladder A's: **the mother is stock, and no more.** Nobody says "you were set",
nobody says "template" *at* Bron, nobody deduces anything out loud, and **Bron says the number
himself** after counting the column twice — the outline's note asks that the player not have to take
Zeph's word for it.

> **Bron: Day before? Shutters down and the stove out. She'd not leave a fire in an empty house.**

The tell is **"every year of it"**: he is handed a probe for a particular day and returns a thing

## Departures from the outline, and why

### Content

- **`0785` does not use Bron's winter story.** The outline says his answer to "the day before" must
  be "the same story he tells in chapter 1"; DECISIONS D9.1 and `canon.md` say the winter is told in
  ch.1 and retold once only, in ch.5, and my assignment repeats the prohibition. I applied the edges
  test to **the autumn fair** instead — which is template 4's own content, read off the page four
  lines earlier, so the probe lands on the stock material rather than on the two live sleeves. It is
  the better version anyway: the fair is what the document just said was issued to him.
- **`0755` does not say "Quill".** The outline's beat says he "tells them the Registrar's name" and
  its own note forbids him saying it before `0770`. He gives them the **relationship**, not the
  name, which satisfies both and costs nothing.
- **`0760` has sixteen children, not thirty**, per `tier_four_dormitory`. It also plays inside the
  cable house rather than out on the terrace — see the location note below.
- **`0730` is staged with the stage at screen right**, not the outline's left, so that the chapter
  obeys `locations.md`'s Vantage staging default (up-tier right, the drop left, company staff
  up-tier of the party). Every other Vantage scene in the chapter follows it, and ch.10 and ch.12
  should too. The shots and the beats are otherwise the outline's exactly.
- **`0705` is 6 lines, not 5.** The extra box is the join to ch.6: *"Three mornings, the sister asks
  him one question before they start walking. Three mornings, he answers it well."* It collects
  `0696`'s "Ask me again tomorrow. And the day after that. Don't stop asking." without naming it,
  and Bron still realises nothing.
- **Idda Renn's bill closes for one mark and two.** The outline says only "the fee is small".

### Composition rules

Every one of these is R3 (no two neighbouring panels at the same scale) or R4 (a page of three or
more panels needs three distinct shapes). The outline's panel lists break one or the other in five
of the seven panel scenes.

- **`0710`**: page 1 reordered to `establishing_tall` / `portrait_inset` / `establishing_wide` — the
  outline had the two wide-scale establishing shots adjacent. Page 2's closing `profile_flat` became
  `eyes_slit` (Bron's eyes down at the capped hall-mouth), because `object_insert` / `two_shot` /
  `profile_flat` is only two shapes.
- **`0765`**: page 2 reordered and the closing `profile_flat` became `eyes_slit`, so the page reads
  `establishing_wide` / `eyes_slit` / `object_insert` — which also restores the outline's stated
  last image, the open drawer, which its own panel list had put second to last.
- **`0770` is 3 + 3 + 2, not 3 + 3 + 2 as listed.** Same panel count (8), different distribution.
  Page 1 is `establishing_wide` / `full_body_reveal` / `object_insert`: the outline's
  `portrait_inset` + `over_shoulder` gave two wide shapes and two close scales. **Sefa's
  introduction is now a `full_body_reveal`** — she stands up from behind the desk for six armed
  people — which is the right shot for a major character's only Act III appearance. The
  `object_insert` is the empty visitor's chair, already pulled out. The outline's `two_shot` of Bron
  and Pip not intervening is gone; they are named in the final `high_angle_down` instead, which does
  the same job and keeps the outline's last image.
- **`0780` is 2 + 3, not 2 + 3 as listed** — again the same five panels, page break moved so that
  `portrait_inset` / `eyes_slit` / `two_shot` sit together legally. The outline's beat says the last
  image is the column insert while its panel list ends on the two-shot; I kept the panel list.
- **`0790`**: the outline's `low_angle_menace` for Teach is geometrically wrong — he is two landings
  *below* looking up, so the camera is above him. It is a `high_angle_down`. The outline's
  `two_shot` of Bron and Hesk at the rail is replaced by an `eyes_slit` of Teach's eyes coming up
  the shaft, which fixes page 2's shapes and is the more frightening last beat before the `impact`.
- **`0795`**: page break moved to 2 + 3. `high_angle_down` of Idda asleep became a `portrait_inset`
  so that page 1's two panels differ in shape and scale.
