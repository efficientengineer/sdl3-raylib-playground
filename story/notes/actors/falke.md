## 1. The voice — as it is on the page vs how it should be

- **The two-clause double is my tic, and it is the author breathing, not me.** Seven of my
  thirty lines are one thought said twice: "That's it? That's all of it?" · "Get away from her!
  Get back!" · "My arms are gone. I can't swing at it again." · "I'm sorry. I didn't want to." ·
  "I beat two of your machines! I can't be far off!" · "Then we walk. Which way is up your
  mountain?" · "Job sheets go up at dawn. I'll be standing at that board when they do." In every
  one the **first** clause is mine and the second is the writer making sure the player got it.
  Cut the second and I sound seventeen. `characters.md` says *the thing first and the reason
  after* — on the page I get the thing, then the thing again.
- **I ask grown men to restate themselves, twice, and that is a writer's hand in my mouth.**
  "Hart — say it straight" (0110) and "Say that plainly" (0130) are the same move two scenes
  apart, and both are me politely requesting exposition. Seventeen does not say *plainly*. It
  says *what?* — or it says the fact that is stopping it ("I don't have a sword").
  "Say it!" in 0150 is also on the SMELLS banned list (C1).
- **My good lines are the ones with no second thought in them.** "That's how you hit things!" ·
  "What are you?" · "That's a sheep!" · "He forgot you." · "My wrist's fine." Blurt, one clause,
  no irony, no idea how it lands. That is the whole part. Every one of them is short.
- **I am given nothing to feel at the two moments the chapter is built on.** Hart puts the sword
  in my hands, tells me he made it the winter before last and it has been on the hook since — the
  hook the player examined on day one — and my line is about *paperwork*: "I can sign. Tonight, I
  can sign." I never look at the sword and I never say a word to Hart. Then I kill Klee, and I get
  one box — "I'm sorry. I didn't want to." — and I am fine for the rest of the scene, take five
  coin and crack on. The apology is too complete. What I actually have is nothing to say, twice.
  **That is what expression tags and textless lines are for**; it does not cost a word.
- **Irony is not mine and it keeps getting lent to me.** "Stolz. Of course you're standing there."
  is a wry adult noticing the shape of his own bad luck. I don't notice shapes. I notice *Stolz*.

## 4. The three moments where I am most alive — do not touch

1. **0140, "That's how you hit things!"** — Hart names the exact flaw, and I agree with him
   proudly, in five words, having understood nothing. The whole boy is in that line, and the
   two days of play after it are earned by it.
2. **0160, "What are you?" → "That's a sheep!"** — I meet a person no one in Halm has ever seen
   and I am rude, frightened and completely literal, and then I lose the argument to a sheep with
   a name. This is the chapter's laugh and it is mine.
3. **0180, "He forgot you."** — the cruellest possible thing to say to her, said an hour after I
   killed him, because I am not clever enough to hold it in. It is also the line that turns the
   scene into the rest of the story. Leave every word.

Also protect: I get the chapter's **last line**. SMELLS C42 is the older man closing every scene;
the boy closing this one is right. Keep the last line mine, just shorter.

## 5. The three worst lines of mine

1. **0150 — "I can sign. Tonight, I can sign."** The sword arrives, Hart says he made it the
   winter before last, and I answer about the guild book. I never look at it and I never speak to
   him. → a textless `Falke (shock):` on the sword panel, then **"I can sign tonight."**
2. **0110 — "Hart — say it straight. Am I ready or am I not?"** A courtroom construction in a
   seventeen-year-old's mouth, and I make the same request of the clerk two scenes later.
   → **"Hart. Am I ready or not?"** (and the clerk's becomes "I don't have a sword.")
3. **0180 — "I'm sorry. I didn't want to."** First kill, and I tidy it into an apology plus a
   defence in one box. → **"I'm sorry."** and let the silence at the coins carry the rest.

**Lines said to me I cannot answer the way the script has me answer:** Distel's "You killed him,
and he'd have killed me. Both of those are true." is an adult's formulation handed to a boy who
then has exactly one box; and Hart's "It's been on its hook since" pays off the empty hook the
player examined on day one, and I am given no reaction to it whatever.
