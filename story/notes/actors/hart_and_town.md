# Actor's notes — Hart, the clerk, Stolz, Linde, Garbe, and the town

Read as performance, not as prose. Sources: `story/v3/chapter01_script.md` (draft three),
`story/field/text.md`, `story/characters.md`, `story/v3/NAMES.md` §voices, `STYLE.md` r0/r6/r14,

## HART

### Voice — page vs should-be

- **On the page he is an oracle.** Three of the eight sentences he says at supper are shaped to be
  quoted: "The swing is the one that is out there", "You hit everything as hard as you can. Every
  single time", "You've eaten alone enough." That is a 38% aphorism rate in his biggest scene. His
  spec says *four words where others use twelve* and *shows care by making a thing*; it does not say
  *speaks in maxims*. `STYLE.md` bans "aphorisms and epigrams" and "any line audibly written to be
  quoted". **Should be:** mostly dull. Nails, catches, bread, eat. A terse man is not a wise man
  rationing wisdom; he is a busy man who finds talking a waste of a hand.
- **His field lines are already right and his script lines are not.** `hart_yard.errand` — "Take the
  catch down to the guild hall. Bread on the way back, enough for three." — is the best Hart in the
  chapter: subject dropped, two jobs, and *three* says he feeds Ottilie without one word about it.
  The script never reaches that. Write every script line the way the errand line is written.
- **He never asks a question, and he never should explain.** Checked: no question anywhere, good.
  But `characters.md` says *he will not explain the machine he built*, and the designer's flow says
  of day two *"Nobody tells you."* At supper he explains the machine. That is the one real
  contradiction in the part.

**Two notes the orchestrator has to rule on.**

1. **The empty hook.** `hart_yard.workshop_door` (day one) reads "a hook on the back wall with
   nothing hanging on it" and is flagged as the chapter's best setup. Hart then says the sword "has
   been on its hook since". Both cannot be true. Either the sword hangs there on day one (wrapped,
   and the examine says so) or Hart's clause goes. Cutting the clause is cheaper and keeps the
   mystery.
2. **His disbelief has nowhere to live.** `characters.md`: *he does not believe a word of the
   shepherd's job sheet — tracks do not stop, so somebody miscounted sheep.* It is in no line in the
   game. One optional day-two examine buys it and sets up `high_pasture.tracks_stop`, the line the
   designer says the player will quote:
   `hart_yard.hart_day2` — "Tracks do not stop. Somebody miscounted sheep." — name: Hart, 45 chars.

### Does the sword scene make the guild meaning plain without a speech?

**Yes, and it is the best-built thing in the chapter — one fix away.** The chain is: clerk recites
"No sword, no signature" on day one → the player walks past two empty pegs in his own room at night
→ Hart hands over a sword and says *take it to the clerk* → the watch at the gate says "He gave you
that himself, then." Nobody makes a speech; the meaning arrives as an errand. The one fix is "the
book" → "the guild book", so the sentence lands on the exact noun the clerk used.

does not participate, so that the one time it does, it is the sign.

**Best:** `hart_yard.errand` (two jobs and a family, no adjectives) · "Eat." · "Again. From the gate."
**Worst:** "You hit everything as hard as you can. Every single time." (hands the player the
solution) · "The swing is the one that is out there." (a maxim about the road, from a man who is not
in the road plot) · "You've eaten alone enough." (says out loud the one thing canon says he has never
said).

## THE CLERK

- **Page:** unhurried and exact — correct. **But** he recites the sword rule **four times in four
  boxes** (0130), which `STYLE.md` r14 forbids by name: *"No character recites a set of rules as a
  list."*
- **He and Hart sound alike.** Both speak flat declaratives, neither uses a contraction, neither
  jokes. With the names hidden they are not tellable apart, which r14 requires. The separator exists
  and is unused: `NAMES.md` gives him *"says 'the rule is' the way other people say 'well'"* and
  *starts the sentence again when interrupted*. Neither tic appears anywhere in the chapter.
- He never gets flustered, though Falke cuts him off twice. He is currently furniture that is
  correct.

## THE TOWNSPEOPLE

**They are all wry in the same way.** Three of the four day-one townsfolk speak the identical shape:
*a fact about the past, then a dry turn.* Watch/"four years and he still has you on those machines"
· ostler/"Ask me what I do now" · carter/the grandfather's saying. Only the woman by the square
("You'll get one") and the grain-yard woman ("Go in loud") are plainly something else, and they are
the two best. **Fix: one dry turn in the town, not four.** Keep the watch's; make the others want
something.

**Before/after pairs — is the change felt?**
- watch → **yes**, and it is the chapter's proof that the sword reads as the guild sign.
- woman by the square → **yes**: prediction becomes cheering.
- ostler → **no**, and he is the flag. A man bitter about lost work on day one is uncomplicatedly
  delighted on day two with nothing having happened to him. The proposed after keeps him the same
  man who now knows you.

**Numbers.** `text.md`'s own header reserves **eleven** for the flock and **nine** for the ring of
holes. The ostler currently spends eleven on carts and Garbe spends it again on sheep in the same
town on the same afternoon. Take it off the carts.
