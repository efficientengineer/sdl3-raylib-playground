# Notes from the Act I outliner — decisions the orchestrator must make

Outline is in `story/outline/act1.md`. Everything below either needs a ruling or needs passing on to
another writer. Ordered by how expensive it is to get wrong.

---

## 1. The fifteen questions must be written once and frozen before anything else is drafted

Scene `0115_the_last_hour` (chapter 1) is where the player hears the Order of the Late Hour's Tally in
full. That text then recurs, verbatim or in fragments, in **chapter 5** (cut into the wall of the
Sealed Stair, where the player reads it and the party does not), **chapter 9** (Ket reads it off a
thousand-year-old maintenance manual and stops halfway), **chapter 11** (Lyra begins it on herself and
cannot finish; "Ask me the fifteenth"), and **chapter 15** (Bron recites it to hold Maren together).
Five writers across four acts need the same fifteen lines.

It also has to work as two things at once: a deathbed liturgy, and a strand-integrity check written by
the people who built the Seam — a read-back procedure for confirming a stored pattern is whole.

**Proposed set, for the orchestrator to approve, amend or replace.** Each is short enough for one
half of a text box.

1. What is your name?
2. Who gave it to you?
3. What is the first thing you have?
4. What is before it?
5. Whose house were you in?
6. What did you carry?
7. What did you carry it in?
8. Who is owed?
9. What is owed?
10. Who owes you?
11. What will you not have back?
12. Who should be told?
13. What should they be told?
14. What have you not said?
15. Say your name again. I will say it with you.

Notes on the set: **4** is quietly the "a set memory has no edges" test that Zeph teaches the player in
chapter 4 — it must pass unremarked in chapter 1 and nobody may ever point at the resonance.
**7** is the kind of pointlessly specific question that only makes sense as a checksum, which is what
makes chapter 9 land on Ket and Lyra. **15** is a read-back, which is why it holds Maren together in
chapter 15, and it is the one Bron does not know in chapter 11 — so **Bron must not be in the room for
0115**, and my outline keeps him on the landing outside the door. If you change 15, chapter 11's "I
don't know it / You will" and chapter 15's whole climax move with it.

## 2. "Bad water at Kettle" cannot be completed in Act I

The bible lists it as chapter 2's side contract, but Kettle is on the Flats and the drove roads do not
open until chapter 6. My outline has Nessa Tull walk three days and pay tram fare to post it in person
in Windrow (scene `0240`), the party take it, and the scene say out loud that it will keep. **Act II
must close it in chapter 6 or 9** — please tell whoever has those chapters. Alternative if you prefer:
delete `0240` and move the whole contract to Act II, and chapter 2 keeps only the Vintry side job.

## 3. Chapters 1 and 3 have no side contract in the bible, so I invented two small ones

Neither touches the canonical eight. Say if you want them cut.

- **Ch.1, "The roof tank, third terrace"** (Bex Caleth, Kell roofkeeper). A salt-crawler hatchling in a
  cistern; five minutes of work; she cannot pay the fee because her water is metered now and pays in
  beans; Tovin logs the fee at nought and takes a tenth of nothing. It teaches the bill loop, plants
  Sallowgate's water-rights problem, and plants the Kell "complains about heat, never about cold" rule.
- **Ch.3, "The fair, brought forward"** (Ollo Marrick, baker). Fetch the fair poles. The fair has a
  printed company schedule, renewed with the postings, that specifies where the bunting goes, and it
  is identical to last year's, and the town is proud of that. It is an optional second route to the
  chapter's discovery and it makes the player love Ollo eight chapters before he joins.

## 4. The existing scenes: what I did with them

- **`p01_prologue` — REVISE.** Cut the last clause, "Two of them reached the first door ahead of the
  others." **The party is together for the whole of Act I.** The guild's fourth Line is *bring them
  back*, and splitting them in the first thirty seconds of the game argues against the thing the game
  is about. Also change "frontier quest board" to the board in the water-house at Sallowgate.
- **`001_sealed_door` — REPLACE, same file name.** The door is not sealed and Pip has no lock to pick;
  it stands open with pry marks on the *inner* edge. Cast goes from two to four. Keep both existing
  lines; "Then something came out" is the best line in the file.
- **`002_the_guard` — REVISE, same file name, same two-hander framing.** **Cut "Oh, that is old
  magic"** — the word is on the bible's banned list and the concept is wrong; the guards are a
  procedure running in armour. Replace with Zeph's own register: a count, a correction, delight. Lyra
  does not brandish the medallion as a ward; it is her Order's hour-token and using it as a weapon
  misreads her for the rest of the game.
- **`003_the_warning` — KEEP, dialogue unchanged.** Art is approved and frozen and the dialogue is
  already on model and is quoted in the bible.
- **`003b_after_the_warning` — KEEP.** One standing caution recorded in the outline: Lyra's "It sealed
  itself in with the thing it was afraid of" is a **wrong guess**. The doors were sealed to stop the
  people inside coming out. No later scene may treat it as established.

**On 003's frozen art holding only Bron and Lyra:** the cleanest reading is that the party is together
and the camera simply is not on Zeph and Pip, who are twenty feet up the corridor. `003b` already ends
with "we tell the others before we take another step", so I added `0175_we_tell_the_others`
immediately after it and the seam closes itself.

## 5. Structural plant: the party goes *around* the hall-guard

They cannot pass it (hard rule 14) so Pip finds a side passage with fresh chisel marks in it — which
is also how the company crew got in. That sets up a deliberate three-step: chapter 1 you go around a
guard, chapter 5 the Standing Post bars a stair you cannot go around and you have to exhaust it,
chapter 14 you get past thirty of them by saying the words. Please make sure the chapter 5 writer
knows the first rung exists.

## 6. A hard-rule collision in the bible I had to resolve

The bible's chapter 1 boss, the Broken Post, "strikes anything that moves", but hard rule 14 says the
guards never strike first. My resolution, written into scene `0160`: their corridor collapsed, so the
doorway they bar leads into forty feet of rubble, and **stepping into the gap is taking the way**,
which the oath permits them to stop. They stand motionless while the party talks at them and move the
instant Pip steps into the doorway. If you prefer a different fix, chapter 1 is the only place it
matters.

## 7. Facts I had to settle that the bible leaves open

Flagging these because later writers will lean on them.

- **Bron believes his mother was human.** Neve Ardo, who played Hesta Sallow, was human, so the
  childhood Bron was issued has a Kell boy raised by a human widow in a human town. It explains why he
  has no hold and no doorward job, it is unusual-but-unremarkable to other Kell, and it makes Hesk's
  chapter 5 line land harder. Cadder hears it in chapter 2, says "Ah", and lets it go. Confirm.
- **Bron's guild papers read "Bron Sallow"** and are read aloud at the Windrow gate in chapter 2 with
  nobody commenting, per ladder A. That means his 1101 ledger entry at Sallowgate reads Sallow too.
- **The under-impression under the burned seal names a survey office, not Orrin Cadder.** The name
  arrives from the man himself, over dinner, per ladder B. Chapter 1 ends on the office; chapter 2
  spends its first half walking to it.
- **Kell do not float.** Used as a joke before the drowned bore. Harmless, consistent with dense build
  and running hot, and please tell me if it collides with anything in Acts II-V.
- **The maker's plate is confiscated** by Sergeant Kesk on the ridge road in chapter 3, with a receipt,
  and Bron hands it over because she is right about the law. Recommended, not mandatory — but losing
  it is what makes the Registry matter in chapter 7. Say if you would rather they keep it.

## 9. Offers I did not take, in case you want them

- **Anneke Brae in chapter 2.** Bron's mother is a laundry presser in Windrow and Act I spends a
  chapter in Windrow's laundry district. A silent cameo in chapter 2 would make the chapter 10 cup of
  tea devastating. I used a different laundress (Orla Winch) because the risk of a writer
  over-signalling it is real and the call is not mine. If you want it, the instruction has to be: she
  appears once, has two lines about wash, is not named, and nobody looks at her twice.
- **Ottoline Kerrow's and Ansel Crewe's signatures** are on the season's survey order per the bible's
  chapter 2 entry. I did not put either name on screen in Act I — the act is deliberately one valley
  wide and two antagonists is already the right number. If you want the signature seen, the survey
  cut's paperwork in `0275` is where it fits, unread.

## 10. Scene id format — a limit you will hit in Act II

`CCSS` with the order in steps of five caps a chapter at nineteen new scenes (05 to 95). Chapter 1
comes to exactly nineteen new ids only because its five existing scenes keep their legacy names
(`p01`, `001`, `002`, `003`, `003b`). At the scene counts the brief asks for, some chapter is going to
need a twentieth. Suggested rule before it bites someone: allow a three-digit order (`01100_slug`), or
switch later chapters to steps of four. Worth deciding now rather than during a merge.
