# writer-ch13 — Chapter 13, "Evacuate"

20 scenes: 7 panel, 11 talk, 2 narration, 2 optional (`1335`, `1340`). Matches `canon.md` section 8.
`./story_prompt.py check story/scenes/13*.md` passes on all twenty; `sheet` succeeds on all seven
panel scenes (warnings only: missing character reference images, three talk backdrops whose panels
are not generated yet, and three one-off NPC speakers with no portrait).

The editor flagged this as the thinnest chapter in the game. What I did about it: the evacuation is
worked as business with named hands on it (Cassa's running total, Ilven's inventory, Dov's kit on
the salt, Cadder's fourteen streets), the bore shutdown is costed in wheels, minutes and burned
hands before it happens and paid for on screen afterwards, and `1350` is written as the centre of
the chapter rather than as a rest beat.

## Canon wording established in chapter 13

Later writers — chapters 14 and 15 above all — copy these character for character.

### The relief formula is NOT printed — `1345`, `1380`

Per `canon.md` ("Chapter 14 owns it. It is not printed to the player before `1440`") and my
assignment, **no word of the formula appears anywhere in chapter 13.**

- `1345`: Zeph reads it silently. Ket puts a finger on one word of the transcription and says the
  word is wrong, and **which word is never said on the page.** Zeph states only what it *is*:
  > **Zeph: Three recensions. The oath, a duty roster, and the relief — the words a watch says when it takes a post over.**
  > **Zeph: That is why they stand aside. It was never a password. It is a handover.**
  Chapter 14's `1445` restates "a handover, not a password"; that repetition is the payoff and is
  deliberate. If ch.14 would rather own the phrase outright, cut my second box; the scene survives.
- `1380`: Maren speaks and **the box is a `Narrator` line, not her words**:
  > **Narrator: Thirty words. Her accent moves twice inside them, and once her voice arrives doubled.**
  **Reconciled with chapter 14, which landed while I was writing.** The act-4 outline's chapter
  summary says "eleven words"; that is impossible, because ch.5 froze Hesk's mangled back half at
  fifteen words, and ch.14's `1440` prints the complete formula at **thirty words, fifteen a side**.
  I have taken ch.14's figure. **This closes item 1 on ch.14's list for the orchestrator** — no
  further action needed, and their files were not touched.

### The hall-guards' warning — `1380`

The two guards at the inner doors speak twice, in ch.5's register (`0590`: "The way is barred." /
"Turn. Go up."), adjusted because this is a level corridor and not a stair:

> **Guard: The way is barred.**
> **Guard: Turn. Go back.**

Their only other line is a silence box when the halberds lift. Maren's one line in her own voice,
after the doors part, is four words: **"I'm sorry. I know."**

### Ket-Ossun's duty roster — `1345`

The post counts on the thousand-year-old roster, which is chapter 14's boss stated as a number a
chapter early:

> **Ket: The roster is complete. Thirty at the council door. Four on the stair. Two at the inner doors.**
> **Ket: They are all still being kept. That is not a guess. That is what a roster is for.**

Thirty is the Standing Muster (act5 `1440`); two is `1380` and `1385`; **four on the stair** is my
invention and matches the Deep Stair's "four dead guard posts on the way down" in `locations.md`.

### Ket's third and last light — `1390`

Per `canon.md` section 3 she is at **two** from here to the end of the game. It goes out mid-answer
and she corrects the figure without a change of tone:

> Ket: Four on the Flats. Three, thirty-four days ago, on a gallery above the Ossun head. So I am at three—
> **Ket: Two. Correct. Two, as of now. One a side, which is tidy, and I have never had that before.**

Her whole specification, fixed here and quotable: **four a side, eight in all; eight at muster, six
by the year 600, five in 1082 when they metered Ossun, four on the Flats (ch.9), three on the Ossun
gallery (ch.12, thirty-four days before the 5th of Fallow), two now.** And:
**"At none I stop. I do not fall down and there is no noise."** and **"Six hundred and eleven of us
are left. There will not be a six hundred and twelfth."** The cradle is not mentioned and she does
not raise it. Her closer: **"There is a joke in it. I am aware of the joke. So is Arro-Vintry, and
his is a better one."**

### Maren's plan, in her own words — `1385`

Ladder F's ch.13 rung, and the thing `1530` pays off. She never gloats, never monologues, apologises
twice, and is not refuted:

> **Maren: Everybody in the Basin. All of it, into all of them, at once. The whole of that night and everyone in it.**
> **Maren: Then it isn't a file. It's testimony. Nobody puts testimony in a drawer and nobody says it didn't happen.**
> **Maren: It costs me all of it and I'm glad. I've carried a town about for two years and I'd like to set it down.**
> **Bron: How many people can have it?**
> Maren: ...
> **Maren: I don't know. I'm sorry. I haven't got that far. I've had two years and I can't.**

Bron's question is the arithmetic of hard rule 3 and she has no answer to it. Hesk's line is the
nearest thing to a counter-argument and it is about doors, not ethics, and it reveals nothing about
Thurn's history (which ch.14 owns):

> **Hesk: Every door in that hill was shut from the inside. Somebody had a reason and you've not asked them.**
> **Maren: No. I haven't.**

### The two orders — `1300`

Printed in full, in the Works register, with no commentary. Figures fixed here, all new and all free
to change: **fourteen days from posting; two hundred and six sledges; a hardship payment of three
marks the head, paid at the rail head before loading and not against wages; officer responsible

### Sefa joins — `1320`

She is not sorry and is not redeemed. Her opening is the whole character:

> **Sefa: I have been passed over. I am therefore available. Where are you going, and what needs signing?**

Hesk's four flat words and Sefa's precise agreement, which is the scene:

> **Hesk: You wrote Tellwater down.**
> **Sefa: ...Out. I wrote it out. Down implies there was something there to copy from.**
> Sefa: Say it again the way you said it. — No. I have it.
> **Hesk: Write it small.**

The "say it again / I have it" tic is deliberately the same tic as ch.12's `1250`; the outline asks
for the four-words-written-down beat twice on purpose. Zeph speaks to her twice and both times by
title (**"Registrar."**). Nobody welcomes her. Ollo's back pay is a real fault in real paper:
**notice posted the 2nd, postings closed the 11th, nine days, and the instrument requires thirty —
four months at the Rim rate.** Her line about it: *"Do not thank me; it is a fault in their paper."*

### Crewe's last scene — `1375`

> **Crewe: It will be turning again by morning. Not because anybody decides to. Because nobody has to.**
> **Bron: Who do we stop, then.**
> **Crewe: ...That is the right question and I have never had an answer for it.**

He hands over **forty-three pages** (ch.10's figure) with **the constables' roster for the night,
eleven names, folded in at the back.** Bron's whole reaction is one "..." and the pages go in his
coat. **Nobody says Ivo Teach was in that street and Bron never mentions it, here or later.**
Sefa's cruellest true thing, which he agrees with:

> **Sefa: You wrote it beautifully and you filed it nowhere, which is burning it and takes less nerve.**
> **Sefa: A record nobody can find is a record nobody has to answer for. You knew that when you chose the drawer.**
> **Crewe: Yes.**

And his one question, answered honestly and made no better by it: *"Is Anneke Brae keeping well?"* /
*"She's at the laundry. She's well. She doesn't know me."* / *"...Thank you. I had hoped that would
be easier to hear than it is."*

### The Long Bore's numbers — `1360`, `1365`, `1375`

Ch.7's `0745` figures are reused unchanged: **nineteen feet across the bell, eleven hundred and
forty feet of shaft to the working face.** New here, and the only figures ch.15 must agree with:

- **The gimbal is rated at eleven degrees and is running at sixteen. That is "full".** (Kerrow and
  Ket both use it, which is the point: they are reading the same machine.)
- **At sixteen it takes the branch under the Rim to a level inside fourteen months**, after which
  Kerrow shuts the other **fifty-one heads** (ch.12's figure) inside a year and caps the Seam.
- **Shutting the draw gear by hand: eleven wheels on the pedestal, in order, against the governor,
  two people on each of the last four, about forty minutes, and it burns hands.** This is the
  chapter's physical cost and `1375` opens on a basin of water and Ollo's wrapped hand.
- **The stoppage lasts about four hours.** Four relief crews on a rota and a maintenance contract
  put it back at full **by six** the next morning, which is ch.14's "eleven hours since the Long
  Bore went to full".
- The service entrance Kerrow gives them unprompted: **the foot of Tier One, north of the bunk rows,
  gate not manned at night.**

### Teach's arrest — `1355`

Quiet, slow, and obviously not going to hold. **He never says why**, to her or to anybody. Kerrow's
compliment is sincere and is the driest joke in the chapter:

> **Kerrow: You have named yourself swearing officer and superintending officer both. That will not survive a reading.**
> **Kerrow: The second clause is well drafted. It puts the review at the seat, which is two days' post. Somebody sat up with this.**
> **Ivo: Yes.**
> **Ivo: Sergeant. Log the hour.**
> **Hanna: ...Sir.**

The well-drafted clause is what buys the time: review at the seat is two days' post, and the warrant
is discharged at Vantage at noon on the 5th (`canon.md`). See "the chapter's clock" below.

## Departures from the outline, and why

- **`1345` does not carry "the immediate realisation that somebody else already has it another
  way."** The same entry's note forbids anyone deducing that Maren can say the formula, and I could
  not find a version of the realisation that was not that deduction. The dread on the page is Ket's
  post count instead, which sets up ch.14's thirty and `1380`'s two, and the player learns the other
  half by watching `1380` — which is what the outline says should happen.
- **`1380`'s panel order is 1, 2, profile / halberd, doors**, not the outline's 1, 2, halberd,
  profile, doors. She has to speak before the halberds lift, and the outline's order plays it
  backwards. The five shots are otherwise exactly as listed, and the reordering also fixes R3/R4 on
  both pages.
- **`1380` prints no words of the formula at all**, where the outline permits printing them "if
  coordinated". Canon and my assignment both say chapter 14 owns the wording, so the box is a
  `Narrator` line describing eleven words. Precedent for `Narrator` inside a panel scene: `0575`
  (ch.5) and `1065` (ch.10).
- **`1350` says Lyra's name once, in Ollo's first box.** The Act IV design gives ch.13 one
  silence-breaking and this is it: the man who never met her is the only one who can say it. Nobody
  else in the chapter says the name — `1310` uses "the sister" and a date, `1320` and `1375` do not
  touch it at all.
- **`1325` gives the Pale officer a first name in Orrin's mouth ("Marl")**, dialogue only, no
  `characters.md` entry. Orrin first-names everybody including people he is about to ruin, and ch.5
  set the precedent with three councillors.
- **`1340` is written as three labelled blocks under `## Dialogue`** — wait, move them, and the
  common last beat — because the outline requires both outcomes to be written. The labels are plain
  markdown and the parser skips them, so `check` passes, but **the exporter will play all three**; the
  orchestrator may want this split into `1340a`/`1340b` or gated in the game. Flagged below.
- **Line counts over the outline:** `1350` 19/16, `1320` 17/16, `1340` 17/12 (two outcomes in one
  file), `1360` 16/14, `1375` 16/14, `1315` 15/14, `1330` 15/14, `1335` 15/14, `1385` 12/10,
  `1300` and `1395` 6/5, `1365` 10/9. `1350` is the one I deliberately let run: the assignment names
  it as the chapter's real centre and the argument has to be allowed to become an argument. Every
  other overage is one or two boxes and each can lose its last non-load-bearing line.
- **Panel scenes list only the characters actually named in a panel** (ch.11's `1195` and all of
  ch.12's precedent), which is why `1305` lists Bron and Zeph but not Ket, `1325` lists Orrin and
  Hesk but not Dov or Bron, and `1385` lists Maren and Bron but not Hesk.
- **`1325` does not name Dov in a panel** though the outline's `who:` line has him in the crowd; he
  has no line and naming him would need an acting line for a man standing still in a crowd. He is
  a face in the crews and `1330` is his scene.
