# Notes — Act IV outline (chapters 11-13)

Things the orchestrator must decide or arbitrate. Everything else is in `story/outline/act4.md`.

## 1. Scene ids cap each chapter at 20 scenes

Ids are `CCSS` with `SS` in steps of five, so a chapter has exactly twenty slots (`00` to `95`).
All three of my chapters use all twenty. If a scene needs inserting later there is no room, so
either a scene gets cut or the scheme needs a third digit (`110525`) or odd steps. Worth deciding
before five writers hit the ceiling independently.

## 2. Two side contracts invented

The bible lists eight side contracts and assigns only one to my act: **#7 "Sit with him"**
(ch.12, Windrow, posted by Ivo Teach). Chapters 11 and 13 have none. Since every chapter is
supposed to carry one, I wrote two new ones. Both use existing towns and existing people, add no
settlement and no skywell, and can be deleted without touching anything else.

- **Ch.11 — "Set the trestles"**, posted by Ollo Marrick on the Tellwater square, sealed with his
  brass posting badge, paying out of the bakery till. Get the fair-ground trestles out of the loft
  and set them up by dusk, so the autumn fair can be held five weeks early. Scenes `1120`, `1140`,
  and it feeds the non-optional fair at `1155`. **This is load-bearing tonally** — it is the last
  unguarded happiness in the game and the reason the player is not braced the next morning. If the
  contract is cut, `1155` must survive.
- **Ch.13 — "Walk the line"**, posted by Cassa Tessen at the Braid rail head. Walk the laid rail
  ahead of the night's sledges. They find a chant-line of eleven who will not move mid-rehearsal
  and are right not to. Scenes `1310` (tail), `1335`, `1340`. It previews the mechanism of ch.15's
  eleven-hour chant and gives the player a complicity choice that is never punished.

## 3. Dates

I assumed ch.11 = 6-11 Dust 1107, ch.12 = 13-16 Dust, ch.13 = 19-22 Dust, from the bible's "2nd of
Turn to 9th of Fallow" across fifteen chapters. Ch.13's closing narration states the 22nd of Dust
and says it is eighteen days before the 9th of Fallow. If Acts III or V have fixed different
dates, mine are cheap to move — they appear in `1100`, `1180`, `1395` and one line of `1120`.

**One deliberate rhyme:** Lyra dies on the 9th of Dust, one month to the day before the
anniversary of Tellwater. No character notices and no scene mentions it. Cut the coincidence if it
feels like authorial winking.

## 4. Ket-Ossun's lights — needs arbitration

She joins at five and leaves at two, so exactly three go out across chapters 4-15, and no writer
may light one back up. **I have spent one in ch.12 `1255`**, at the Ossun head, the post she was
removed from in 1082. If another act has already claimed that light, cut panel 6 and one line of
`1255`; the scene works without it. Suggested full allocation if you want to fix it centrally:
one in ch.9, one in ch.12, one in ch.14 at the cradle.

## 5. The fifteen questions must be written once, and early

Ch.11 `1150` and `1175` and ch.15 all quote the Order of the Late Hour's Tally, and ch.5 cuts the
same words into the Sealed Stair wall for the player to see. **Whoever writes chapter 1 writes the
fifteen questions and everyone else quotes them.** My scenes need only two properties: Bron can
reach the fourteenth from habit and stalls on the fifteenth, and the fifteenth is the one Lyra
tells him in the road. If ch.1 does not exist when a ch.11 writer starts, they should write around
the content and quote numbers only.

## 6. The relief formula — same problem, smaller

`1345` gives the party the transcript and deliberately never prints the words. `1380` has Maren
say them aloud, which is the first time the player could hear them, and ch.14 has the party say
them. Either ch.14's writer owns the wording and `1380` quotes it, or `1380` leaves a placeholder.
My preference: **ch.14 owns it, `1380` shows Maren speaking and prints nothing**, which is also
the more frightening staging.

## 7. "Taking the hour" is not a device

Flagging this because it is the single easiest thing for a scene writer to get catastrophically
wrong. In `1175` Bron takes Lyra's last hour by **sitting it** — being present, asking the Tally
in order, and carrying it afterwards, which is the whole job of an Attendance sister. There is no
frame, no strand, no glass, no transfer, no light. Hard rules 9 and 10 forbid anything else (only
a saturated person can write without a frame, and Maren is the only one). His certainty that the
hour is real comes from having lived it. I have put this in the scene's notes in bold; please keep
it there through any edit.

## 8. Lyra's death: things I have deliberately denied the player

- No revenge option, no attack prompt, and no dialogue choice about Wen Arden, ever. He is never
  redeemed and never punished, per the bible.
- The gunshot is off-panel, between two panels, in `1170`.
- Nothing before `1165` foreshadows anything. The chapter's first two thirds are warm on purpose,
  and `1155` (the fair) must be the prettiest art sheet in the act.
- No party member mentions her by name from `1195` until `1350`, two chapters later, when Ollo
  asks what she was like — per the bible's tone guide. Act V writers should know the silence is
  broken exactly once, in `1350`, and that grief after that is still handled by tasks and argument.

## 9. Ollo joining in the same chapter

Per the bible's open question 2. My solution: he is introduced as a person in `1115` on day one,
runs the fair in `1155`, asks to come along at the midnight meal in `1160` as comedy, and is
already an accepted party member when the road happens the next morning. He is then the only
person who does a physical act of kindness in `1175` (his apron round the boy's shoulders,
wordless) and the only person allowed to ask about Lyra later, because he did not know her. He
never replaces her mechanically — the party has no healer for two chapters and the systems writer
should let that bite.

## 10. Scenes with no party member present

`1290` (Teach and Kesk in a post room) and `1380` (Maren at the inner doors) are played by the
player as a watcher. If the cutscene system cannot show a scene with no party member, both can be
reframed — `1290` as a talk scene overheard, `1380` as the party arriving to find the doors shut —
but both lose a lot. Worth confirming the tool is fine with it.

## 11. Crewe's confession exceeds the panel-scene line count

The bible fixes it at eleven boxes with reactions between them, which is about sixteen lines;
the outline's own guidance says 5-10 for a panel scene. I have marked `1275` as a deliberate
exception. If a hard limit exists in the tool, the scene splits into `1275` and a talk scene, but
that costs a slot (see item 1).

## 12. Things I placed that other acts may also want

- **Tovin-Caleth refusing to close the Sallowgate hall** (bible ch.11) happens off-screen and is
  reported by Tibb Orsk in `1130`. If an Act V writer wants it on screen in ch.14, take it; my
  scene survives as a rumour.
- **The bar drawn through Lyra's line on the ledger wall** is deliberately not shown in Act IV.
  I have handed it to ch.14's "Nine hundred tallies" as its best first sighting.
- **Ross and Dalla Kettle** (bible ch.12) are an optional Emberrow stop at `1230`. If Act III's
  writer has already given them a ch.12 beat elsewhere, drop mine.
- **Emmet Sark's ch.12 appearance** (`1245`) is written with no plot function at all, so that his
  ch.15 document does not feel planted. Do not let anyone add a hint to it.

## 13. New minor names to approve

`Ondra Pell` (Works decommission clerk, Tellwater), `Jory Tass` (salvage foreman), `Sergeant Bel
Rask` (dying Pale sergeant, Quiet House), and the Vess bonded pair `Threnn Novven` and `Domma
Sath` (names lifted from the bible's own Vess sample list). All follow the bible's naming
conventions and none collide with an existing character. All are replaceable.

## 14. One canon detail I chose not to resolve

Ollo's line "Denner would've had that bread out an hour ago." I have made Denner the name in the
bakery's recipe book, in a hand that is not Ollo's, and **nobody ever finds out whether Denner was
a posted employee or one of the four hundred.** Ollo does not know. No later scene should answer
it. If the orchestrator would rather it were answered, the natural place is Crewe's list in
`1375`, and it costs nothing to add one line — but it is better unanswered.
