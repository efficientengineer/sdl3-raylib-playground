## The story in one page

**Spoilers, deliberately. Working title: *The Fair Copy*.**

**The pitch.** Four hunters take a board contract with the seal burned off it: clear the entrance of
the Ancient Halls, ask no questions, triple rate. Behind the door they were paid to open there is no
monster — there is a canvas company tent, a folding table, and a duty clerk who asks whether they
have an entry chit. They were the fuse. The Clement Works mines *records* out of the rock under the
hills — the stored patterns of people — and fourteen years ago a bore under the town of Tellwater
inverted and emptied four hundred people in nine seconds. The company rebuilt the town with hired
employees on four-year postings and filed the night correctly. Bron, who leads the party, grew up in
Tellwater. The warm childhood he remembers was issued to him out of a drawer.

**Act I — The Contract (ch. 1-3).** Sallowgate, Windrow, Tellwater, on foot. They find who posted the
bill: Field Superintendent Orrin Cadder, who is delightful, admits everything over dinner, pays in
full, and lets them walk. Tellwater's welcome is one degree too warm. A maker's plate in a drowned
bore is dated the year of the flood that was supposed to explain it. Warden Teach looks at Bron for
three seconds and steps aside, and they leave as fugitives with the roads shut.

**Act II — Off the Roads (ch. 4-6).** Ferry-rail across the dry seabed to Braid, then the terraces and
the salt. Ket-Ossun the skywell keeper joins, then Hesk Ninefold the doorward. They learn what the
Seam is (a pattern-store, never a power source, never a villain), the edges test — *a set memory has
no edges; ask what happened the day before* — and that a standing company notice is out for something
called *the Fair Copy*. At Saltmouth a bore inverts forty feet away, forty-one people go blank, and
Junior Director Kerrow says the radius before anyone has counted it.

**Act III — The Fair Copy (ch. 7-10).** Vantage's six tiers, then a stolen crawler. Registrar Sefa
Quill sells them the resettlement map and makes her son say her name in front of people. In Emberrow
they watch Maren Ostry give a man his life back and destroy him doing it. On the Flats she sits on a
fallen ring and recites Bron's winter in the first person, with the timing right. Then the file room:
his childhood was not stored, it was **erased** — he was over the open shaft, not at the bell — and
there is no copy anywhere and never was. He is the one victim who can never be restored. His real
mother, Anneke Brae, presses laundry in Windrow, and he stands in her doorway and says nothing.

**Act IV — What the Paperwork Says (ch. 11-13).** Tellwater is decommissioned; sixty postings close.
Because Ivo Teach made the one conscientious refusal of his career, a nineteen-year-old constable is
standing in the road instead, and he fires once. Lyra dies with her circlet knocked sideways. She
begins her Order's fifteen questions on herself out of habit, cannot finish, and Bron takes the hour —
the first memory in his head he is sure is real, and it is not his. Ollo Marrick the baker joins.
Chairman Crewe confesses everything to his own board; Kerrow takes the chair and orders Braid
evacuated and the Long Bore run at full. Maren reaches the inner doors and the thousand-year-old
guards stand aside for her.

**Act V — The Doors (ch. 14-15).** Down the Ninth Door and a mile of swept stair into Thurn, a lit,
warm, thousand-year-old city with three hundred and forty calm, healthy people in it who cannot tell
you their names. They are the builders' descendants. The last council did not die: they wrote
themselves into the Seam for safekeeping, sealed the doors, and expected to be read back out by
people who never came. Ket finds the Onn cradle deliberately broken — the specification survives, the
facility does not — and reads the four thousand names of her own muster aloud, and Bron sits through
all six hours.

**The ending.** At the Seam face Maren loses coherence and speaks in four hundred voices. The fight is
reaching the woman inside the chorus. Bron holds her together by asking Lyra's fifteen questions in
order — which were always a strand-integrity check, and are also just asking a frightened woman who
she is. Held together, she chooses: she gives the record away whole, once, into Vess chant at Braid,
where it cannot be audited, seized, filed or burned. Ilven Tessen refuses Sefa to her face; Zeph asks
twenty minutes later and is accepted, and sings for eleven hours and cannot stand afterward. Kerrow
arrives to cap the Seam with everyone inside, with the only costed plan anyone has produced, and she
is never refuted — she is stopped by people: Cadder comes down the Long Bore with sixty of his own
crew, Teach holds the door, Emmet Sark files the 22:02 message log because it belongs in the file.
Nothing is undone. No town is restored, no dead come back, the company does not fall. Thurn's people
walk up a mile of stair by hand over seven months and recover nothing except tomorrow. The doors stay
open and watched, and Hesk writes down who goes in. The last image is a new bill going up on the
Sallowgate board, and Bron in the doorway in a cold wind he can finally feel.

## Known risks

Honestly, from the editors' own notes.

1. **A healer dying two-thirds through is the single most recognisable beat in both source games.**
   Chapter 11 opens warm enough that a genre-literate player will brace at scene one. Saved only by
   execution: the cause is paperwork, not a villain.
2. **A sealed underground city of descendants who cannot remember is well-worn ground.** Saved only
   by Thurn being lit, swept, warm and in better repair than the surface.
3. **Chapter 13 is the thinnest chapter** and nothing since the story editor's pass has thickened it.
   It is the first place to cut or merge.
4. **Chapters 7-10 each end on a document.** Four consecutive chapters of filing. Chapters 8 and 9
   lead with physical set-pieces to compensate, but it is still a lot of paper.
5. **Six courteous, partly-right company people** — Cadder, Kerrow, Teach, Ovey, Crewe, Sark — start
   to blur. They are written to distinct temperatures; watch that the art keeps them apart.
6. **The two coldest reveals have zero foreshadowing by design** (the Onn cradle; that Thurn is
   inhabited). That is a lot of weight on chapter 14 doing both in one go.
7. **111 panel sheets** is at the top of a one-person art budget.
8. **The story is not internally dated between chapters 2 and 10.** Elapsed-time figures are
   reconciled to each other and to both ends, but not anchored. Fix a date there and they need
   re-checking as a set.
9. **`Clerk` (34 lines, six scenes) and `Guard` (17 lines, four scenes)** are each several different
   people wearing one label. A player will see "Clerk" six times and may think it is one person.
10. **Five new cast entries need five more reference sheets**, or five speaker boxes with no portrait.
11. **`story/outline/act4.md` and `act5.md` are stale** on two details ("eleven words", "eleven
    months"). The outlines are history; `canon.md` wins. Anyone reading them will trip.
