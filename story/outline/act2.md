# ACT II — Off the Roads (chapters 4, 5, 6)

Scene-by-scene outline. Act II is where the party becomes six, the map opens twice, and the player
learns the halls are not haunted but running. It ends with Bron unable to describe his mother's face.

Read `story/bible.md` sections 5 (hard rules), 13 (ladders) and 14 (tone) before writing any scene
below. Scene ids are `CCSS_slug`. Chapter tallies are at the foot of the file.

---

## Chapter 4 — "Annex"

- **Hub:** Braid (Vess, 7,000; terrace lip and down onto the dry seabed).
- **Dungeon:** the drowned lower stacks beneath the Braid archive annex.
- **Boss:** the Sleeve Vault — a Vess-built automated retrieval gantry the company still runs, plus
  two annex guards who are on overtime and would rather not.
- **Party:** Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip. **Ket-Ossun joins** at scene 0484.
- **Opens:** the ferry-rail. Braid, Wick, Lomm, Brand's Ferry.
- **Side contract:** "Recover one paper sleeve" (0440 offer, 0486 hunt, 0487 delivery).

**The chapter as the player plays it.** Act I ends with the trade roads shut, so the party walks down
the terrace stair onto the old sea floor with no map and no bill. They pick up laid rail, flag a Vess
sledge, and Cassa Tessen carries them to Braid: a town of chant-halls built down a cliff onto white
salt, with rails running out to the horizon and one skywell humming at a fifth of its strength. The
player explores the terrace lip — rail sheds, chant-halls, the Even Hand's Braid hall — and finds the
town's wound in plain sight: seven thousand Vess queue in the cold outside their own archive for two
hours of reading a day, because the halls are a company annex on a lease that runs eighty years past
everyone alive. Zeph will not stand in that queue. He knows another way in: the pre-Concession stacks
on the lowest floor, which the Works never catalogued because they are under water. Between scenes
the player runs the town — buy from the rail sheds, take the unsealed bill off the Braid hall floor,
watch Zeph win a bet demonstrating the edges test on a stranger — then swims and climbs the drowned
stacks, where the Vess archivists' own index gives the thing under the hills a name. Coming up
through the company office above, the party passes a board of standing notices and one of them is a
recovery notice for something called the Fair Copy. The retrieval gantry wakes on the way out. After
it, they come back into daylight owing a skywell keeper a favour, and she can put four hunters on the
rail where the Works cannot touch them.

### 0420_cassa_tessen — TALK — optional: no
- when: on the sledge, on the run in to Braid
- where: the ferry-rail sledge, mid-salt

## Chapter 5 — "Nine Doors"

- **Hub:** Ninefold Terrace (Kell, 900; nine sealed door-mouths in one cliff face, terraced barley
  dying in steps, the Caleth stump on the ridge).
- **Dungeon:** the Sealed Stair, behind the Fifth Door.
- **Boss:** the Standing Post — one complete hall-guard barring the stair correctly. It cannot be
  killed and cannot be talked past. You exhaust it, you go on, and when you come back up it has
  resumed its post.
- **Party:** Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip, Ket-Ossun. **Hesk Ninefold joins** at 0570.
- **Opens:** the hill paths off the company roads, and the crossing to Saltmouth.
- **Side contract:** "Count the doors" (0550 take, 0555 discovery, 0560 aftermath).

**The chapter as the player plays it.** The sledge puts them off at a terrace landing and the party
climbs eleven hundred feet of cut steps into a village that does not want them. Ninefold Terrace is
nine doors, nine doorward households, and barley fields failing in steps down a slope; half the young
have gone to company bore crews for wages, and the village is in the middle of a negotiation it is
losing when the party arrives. Field Superintendent Orrin Cadder is here in person, cheerful, sleeves
cut off, whistle on a cord, and he is winning — not because he is cruel but because he has the only
offer on the table. The player explores the terraces, takes an insultingly small bill from the
doorward council to walk the cliff path and count nine doors, and finds company mortar in three of
them, less than a year old. Hesk Ninefold — doorward of the Fifth, forty-four, furious — is the one
who has to look at it. She will not open her door for the party and cannot stop them, and comes down
the stair behind them because watching them beats losing them. The Sealed Stair is the chapter's
dungeon: a thousand feet of cut stair with a band of inscription running down the wall beside it that
the player can read and the party cannot. At the bottom, one hall-guard bars the way correctly, and
the party exhausts it and gets past and finds that the stair does not go through — the halls beyond
are filled to the roof. The only ways under the Rim Hills are the company's holes, and the nearest
one is Extraction Bore Four at Saltmouth, which is where Hesk's nephew draws his wages.

## Chapter 6 — "Bore Four"

- **Hub:** Saltmouth (Flats, human and Kell, 5,800; a labour town at the foot of the dead Semmet
  well — bore head, counting house, bunk rows, crawler depot, a company care-house).
- **Dungeon:** Extraction Bore Four.
- **Boss:** the Draw Head — a runaway winch and gimbal that must be shut down and cannot be. You do
  not beat it; you survive it and get people out.
- **Party:** Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip, Ket-Ossun, Hesk.
- **Opens:** the salt-crawler freight routes, Kettle, and the drove roads.
- **Side contracts:** "Bad water at Kettle" — the bible's chapter-2 contract, posted at `0240` in ch.2
  and cleared here at `0607` (DECISIONS D8.6). Plus optional non-bill work: "the sump in the bunk
  rows," with Tibb Orsk and Sela Marrin (0635). See `story/notes/outline-act2.md`.
- **Standing rule (ch.5-9):** Lyra says nothing about the Sealed Stair inscription, in this chapter or
  any chapter before 9. One line of "that sounds like—" destroys `0960` and her whole arc.

**The chapter as the player plays it.** Saltmouth is five thousand eight hundred people and one hole.
Everybody's wage comes out of the same bore; the counting house on the square holds the whole Rim
district's payroll, which is what the party has come ninety miles for, and Emmet Sark — the same
mildly annoyed duty clerk from behind the ancient door in chapter one — will not release a page of it
without an entry chit. The player works the town: the bunk rows, the crawler depot, the care-house at
the end of the street that nobody thinks about, Tibb Orsk and Sela Marrin two bunks over on a bill of
their own. The way in is Ket: Bore Four was sunk through the foundation of her dead colleague's
skywell, and a keeper's right of inspection is a thousand years older than the company and has never
been withdrawn, so she walks them down the shaft on paper. Extraction Bore Four is the dungeon —
galleries, gantries, dust that does not fall, and a Seam branch face at the bottom that hums in the
teeth. The draw head runs away while they are down there. They cannot stop it, Cadder clears the
north gallery by hand, the shift comes up, and at 04:40 the stillness goes out across the salt in a
ring that stops forty feet short of where the party is standing. Forty-one people stop where they
are. Nobody dies. Ottoline Kerrow stays in the dust for eleven hours running the count herself, and
Emmet Sark, doing his job in a relief tent, hands Bron a box of counting-house files to carry
because someone has to carry it — and the Rim district payroll is in it. Act II ends at first light
on the salt edge, with a question about a face.
