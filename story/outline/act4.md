# Act IV — What the Paperwork Says (chapters 11-13)

Outline for scene writers. Every entry is meant to be writable without rereading the bible.
**Dates (fixed by the story editor; `story/canon.md` "Calendar and clock" governs).** The year has
eight months of forty-five days. Ch.11 runs 6-11 Dust 1107 and **Lyra dies on the 9th of Dust, one
month to the day before the anniversary of Tellwater**. Ch.12 runs 13-16 Dust. **Ch.13 runs 1-5
Fallow** — Kerrow's two orders are dated the 20th of Dust and the evacuation of a town of seven
thousand takes a fortnight; the party arrives for its last days. Ch.14 is the 6th of Fallow and ch.15
the 7th-9th, which is what Act V's clock and the bible's "the game runs to the 9th of Fallow" require.
The original draft said ch.13 ended on the 22nd of Dust *and* eighteen days before the 9th of Fallow,
which cannot both be true on a 45-day month.

Act IV is the act where the party stops asking what is true and starts asking what they do
tomorrow. No further reveals about Bron are placed here, per bible ladder A: "ch.11-15, no
further reveals about Bron." Anything that looks like one is a mistake.

## Chapter 11 — "Decommission"

- **Hub:** Tellwater.
- **Dungeon:** the town itself, worked house by house in three stages — the south row of houses,
  the fair ground and its loft, and the posting office with the roster room behind it.
- **Boss:** the Pale decommission detail under Constable Wen Arden, nineteen, on a two-day field
  commission. The encounter opens and ends on turn zero. There is a real mid-chapter fight
  earlier — Jory Tass's contracted salvage gang in the fair-ground loft, non-lethal, comic, and
  the last cheerful fight in the game.
- **Party:** Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip, Ket-Ossun, Hesk. Lyra leaves at `1175`. Ollo Marrick joins at
  `1160`.
- **Opens on the map:** nothing. This chapter takes.
- **Side contract:** "Set the trestles", posted by Ollo Marrick on the Tellwater square, sealed
  with his brass posting badge. Fee: whatever is in the bakery till, which is company money he is
  not entitled to keep. (New — flagged in the notes file.)

**The chapter as the player plays it.** Tellwater is being decommissioned: sixty postings close
on the 11th of Dust, the roster is sealed, the buildings pass to a caretaker contract, and the
last physical evidence of the night of 1093 becomes a paragraph. The party arrives to stop that
and finds nothing to fight — just carts, clerks, and sixty people packing eleven years of a life
that was a job. Play is house-by-house: helping employees crate up, running errands between the
square and the loft, picking a company strongbox in the roster room, and brawling a salvage gang
over the fair-ground trestles. Between those the player does the warmest hour of the whole game:
Ollo Marrick holds the autumn fair five weeks early because the town will not exist next week,
and the party dances badly and eats too much, and Ollo joins. Next morning they walk the roster
book out past a cordon and a frightened nineteen-year-old fires once. The rest of the chapter is
two days of ordinary tasks done with too much care, and it ends in an empty square with a
signature on a decommission order pointing at Vantage. The player should not be braced. Nothing
before `1165` may foreshadow a death.

### 1110_what_a_decommission_is — TALK — optional: no
- **when:** immediately after `1105`, at the trestle desk set up outside the posting office.
- **where:** Tellwater, the square, the posting office door.
- **who:** Ondra Pell (Clement Works decommission clerk, human, 38), Bron, Zeph, Hesk.
- **beat:** Pell explains the decommission the way you explain a bus timetable: postings close on
  the 11th, the roster is sealed and goes to the Registry, the buildings pass to a caretaker
  contract, personnel are redistributed against vacancies, and yes the bakery oven stays because
  ovens are fixtures. Zeph gets her to say out loud that the roster is the only paper in the Basin
  that lists all sixty people and what they were hired to be. She is not hiding it. She offers
  them tea. Hesk asks what happens to a town that is not a town, and Pell says, honestly, that
  she has never done one before and had to ask.
- **purpose:** plot (the stakes: the roster is the last physical evidence; it must not be sealed)
  / world (the Works is orderly, not cruel).
- **mood:** tense
- **lines:** 12
- **notes:** Pell must be likeable and must never be defensive. She is the reason the player wants
  the roster and the reason they cannot simply take it — she signs for it. Do not let anyone
  deduce the plan out loud; they argue and one of them turns out right.

### 1120_whatever_is_in_the_till — TALK — optional: yes

- **where:** Tellwater, the square, the notice board by the well.
- **who:** Ollo Marrick, Pip, Bron, Lyra.
- **beat:** Ollo has pinned up a bill, stamped with his brass posting badge because he has no

- **mood:** hope
- **lines:** 10
- **notes:** The Four Lines require a seal; a scene writer should let Pip notice the badge is a
  seal and rule it valid, which is a hunter's joke and also correct. If the player refuses the
  bill, Ollo holds the fair anyway with fewer trestles — `1155` still plays. Keep this scene light.
  This is the last unguarded happiness in the game and the player must not be warned.

### 1150_the_fifteenth_question — TALK — optional: no
- **when:** dusk, the fair ground, while the trestles are going up.
- **where:** Tellwater, the fair ground.
- **who:** Bron, Lyra.
- **beat:** They argue, properly, the way they do — about whether taking the roster is theft, and
  whether it matters that it is. Lyra loses the argument and does not mind. Then, sideways, she
  tells him what she worked out at the Ilder stump: the fifteen questions she has asked at two
  hundred deathbeds were written as a procedure, by people who wanted a thing to survive being
  moved, and she has decided this makes the prayer better and not worse. Bron does not follow and
  says so. She quizzes him on the questions to make a point and he gets to fourteen and stops.
  "Fifteenth question." "I don't know it." "You will." Then somebody calls her to hold a trestle
  and she goes.
- **purpose:** character / ladder H, rung ch.9-11 (Lyra decides the checklist is a better prayer)
  / setup for `1175` and for ch.15's recitation.
- **mood:** tense -> hope
- **lines:** 14
- **notes:** **This must not read as a goodbye.** It is an argument she wins on a technicality,
  in her driest register, and it ends with her being called away mid-thought. She is not wistful,
  does not touch him, and does not look at the sky. The writer **must not invent the fifteen
  questions** — they are frozen in `story/canon.md` section 1 and quoted verbatim here. The
  fifteenth is *"What do you want done?"*; Bron reaching fourteen and
  stopping is the whole plant.

## Chapter 12 — "The Cap"

- **Hub:** Vantage, Tiers Five and Six, reached by lifting car.
- **Dungeon:** the Ossun skywell head — the company gallery bolted round a thousand-year-old
  machine — and then the board floor on Tier Six.
- **Boss:** the Ossun head governor, a machine being run past its rating, fought as a sequence of
  brake and vane work that Ket calls out; then the Cap's house guard on the garden stair, four of
  them, who stand off when Crewe tells them to.
- **Party:** Bron, Zeph, Pip, Ket-Ossun, Hesk, Ollo Marrick. Four in a fight. There is no healer
  and the systems writer should let the player feel that for two chapters.
- **Opens on the map:** Vantage Tiers Five and Six (a lifting-car key, obtained from Idda Renn).
- **Side contract:** "Sit with him" — posted by Ivo Teach in his own name, with his own money,
  through the Quiet House at Windrow.

**The chapter as the player plays it.** They come up into a city having a perfectly ordinary week.
Vantage is warm, lit, busy, cheerful and metered, and the party walks through it with a gap in
the walking order that nobody mentions for the whole chapter. The player's goal is simple and
mercenary: get to the chairman and make him say it out loud with the names. That means a tier
pass, which means the school on Tier Four, which means a schoolmistress the party did a favour
for in chapter 7. Play is city work — the Long Office on Tier Five, the Registry corridors, an
optional trip to Windrow to sit an hour with a dying man, an optional stop at Emberrow — and then
a climb up through the skywell machinery above the Cap, where Ket reads the governor and finds
the Ossun head is being run past its rating to cover a draw that is not on any Ossun paperwork.
They shut the overspeed down by hand, get arrested in the garden, and the chairman tells the
guards to stand off and invites them in. Then Ansel Crewe confesses, fully, calmly, to a room,
with the names — and Ottoline Kerrow relieves him of the chair mid-sentence with the votes
already counted, and none of it changes anything that week. The chapter ends with the new
chairman's first two orders going out, and with Ivo Teach learning in a post room at Windrow what
his one act of conscience did.
