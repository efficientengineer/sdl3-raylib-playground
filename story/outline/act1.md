# ACT I — The Contract. Scene outline, chapters 1-3.

Source of truth: `story/bible.md`. Where this file and the bible disagree, the bible wins; where the
bible and scene `003_the_warning` disagree, 003 wins.

Act I is the player's first three hours. On foot, one valley wide: Sallowgate to Windrow to
Tellwater and no further. Party is fixed at four for the whole act: **Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip**. No
member joins, none leaves. Maren is offstage: one garbled rumour in chapter 2 and nothing else.

Three things Act I has to do, in this order of importance: make the player like these four people,
teach the bill loop and the Basin lightly, and end chapter 3 with a complete small story — four
hunters took a bad job, found out who hired them, walked into the town one of them came from, and
were run out of the front door of the Basin for it.

**Standing rules for every scene writer in this act.**

- Lines under 120 characters. One thought per box. Nobody makes a speech. Nobody deduces out loud.
- Banned words in dialogue: magic, undead, soul, spirit, essence, curse, ancient evil, destiny.
  Scene 002's current "old magic" line is a defect, not a precedent.
- Nobody says "memory". Say: the hour, that winter, an afternoon, a sleeve, a strand.
- Kell run hot. Bron never complains about cold and never notices it coming — **except** inside the
  story he tells about the winter he got lost, which is the plant. Zeph and Pip are the ones who are
  cold on screen.
- Nobody in Act I knows what the Seam is, what a bore inversion is, what Tellwater was, or that
  anything was done to Bron. The party knows strands exist because they are sold in company shops
  like tobacco. That is all.
- Vess speech: Zeph repeats your key word before answering, says "I have it as", and corrects counts.
  Onn speech: real numbers, "correct" / "not correct", announces their own state, no metaphor.

## Chapter 1 — "Clear the Entrance"

- **Hub:** Sallowgate (Rim Hills, human and Kell, 2,100). Sandstone terraces, water cisterns on every
  roof, the Even Hand hall in a converted water-house on the second terrace.
- **Dungeon:** the Ancient Halls, upper works — three floors of cut stone under the hill-mouth above
  the town.
- **Boss:** the Broken Post. Two hall-guard patterns in ruined armour whose corridor collapsed
  centuries ago. They bar a way that is not there. Tragic, not evil.
- **Party:** Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip.
- **Opens:** Sallowgate's three terraces and the guild hall; the hill path to the door-mouth; the
  Ancient Halls upper works; and, at the chapter's end, the Rim road north to Windrow.
- **Side contract:** "The roof tank, third terrace" (2 scenes, optional). Posted by Bex Caleth, a Kell
  roofkeeper. Something is in her cistern. It is a salt-crawler hatchling; the job is five minutes of
  work. The sting is that she cannot pay the fee, because her water is metered now, and she pays in
  beans, and Tovin logs it at nought and does not blink.

**The chapter as the player plays it.** You come up the rim road at dusk with three people you
already sound like you have known for years, carrying proof of a small job, and you walk into a
sandstone hall with a stove that is never let out and a wall covered floor to ceiling in four
thousand handwritten lines. Upstairs, a hunter named Merrit Tack is dying, and Lyra is sitting the
last hour with him, and the player hears the Order's fifteen questions in full, once, through a door
Bron is standing outside of. Then Tovin-Caleth chalks a bar through his line, and puts up a bill with
its seal burned off: clear the entrance, ask no questions, triple rate. She tells Bron to his face
that it stinks and lets him take it, because the hall needs the tenth. You explore three terraces,
argue about it over supper, optionally clear a cistern for a woman who cannot pay, and walk up to the
door-mouth in the morning. The upper works are three floors of cold cut stone: torches, rubble, a
collapsed corridor where two ruined guards strike anything that enters a doorway that no longer has a
door, and a great warded door at the bottom that grinds open by itself with a hall-guard standing in
it. It warns you instead of killing you. You go around it through a side passage with fresh chisel
marks in it. And behind the door you were paid to clear there is a canvas tent, a folding table, a
lamp, and a mildly annoyed man with a clipboard who asks whether you have an entry chit. You were the
fuse. He countersigns your bill, because he is helpful, and that signature is your proof of work. You
walk out at dawn, get paid in full on the spot, and Zeph holds the burned seal up to the stove and
finds the under-impression still legible.

### 0130_the_board_eats_at_six — TALK — optional: no

  payoff target for chapter 9's recitation.
- mood: hope
- lines: 14-16
- notes: The winter story is the single most important plant in Act I and it must land as warmth, not
  as a clue. Rules for it: it is told because someone else complained about cold, it is funny in the

## Chapter 2 — "The Burned Seal"

- **Hub:** Windrow (Rim Hills, human, 3,400). A relay town on the tram spur: laundries and drying
  yards, boarding houses, a tram yard, the dark Vintry skywell standing over it, and the Clement
  Works sanatorium behind a wall with no sign on it.
- **Dungeons:** the hill relay station above the town, then the company survey cut above that.
- **Boss:** the cutting rig, and crew chief Marek Culm, who will not stop work for anybody.
- **Party:** Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip.
- **Opens:** Windrow and its tram yard; the well-head path up to the dark Vintry rings; the relay
  station; the survey cut; and, at the chapter's end, the Tellwater road east.
- **Side contract:** "Birds in the rings" (3 scenes, optional). Posted by Orla Winch, whose drying
  yard sits under the dead Vintry well and whose wash is being fouled. Seems: clear the birds. Is: an
  Onn at one light has been sweeping a dead machine for a hundred and sixty-three years, has counted
  the birds exactly, and has no objection to them. Also available and **not completable in Act I**:
  "Bad water at Kettle", posted in person by Nessa Tull (1 scene here, closes in Act II).
- **Side contract note:** both are marked optional and both are worth doing; the Vintry one is the one
  that pays.

**The chapter as the player plays it.** Two days up the rim road in weather that is arriving earlier
every year, and at the Windrow tram gate a bored constable of the Pale reads their guild papers out
loud, including the name *Bron Sallow*, and nobody comments. Windrow is laundry steam, tram bells and
a dead skywell three hundred feet over the roofs; everyone here works for the tram or for the
sanatorium and nobody has ever asked who the patients are. You take a room with three beds in it for
four people, which is a fight. On a bench by the tram platform there is a man in a company coat with
a brass number at his collar who is warm, healthy, friendly and cannot say where he slept last week,
and the town's story about him involves a woman on the road who touched somebody. You explore, you
optionally climb to the dead well and meet an Onn, you optionally take a bill from a furious old
woman who has walked three days to post it, and then you follow the under-impression up the hill in
the wind — and this is where Pip makes a joke about Bron being cold. The relay station holds a wax
cylinder machine and a recording of a hall-guard's oath, and Zeph plays it three times and the words
are identical to the syllable. Above the relay there is a night shift cutting into the hill, and a
crew chief who will not stop for a bill, a guild or anybody's conscience, and you stop the rig
yourself and it costs eleven people four days' wage. At dawn a short wide Kell in a field coat with
the sleeves cut off walks up the cut singing, and buys you dinner, and tells you everything you came
to ask before you finish asking it, and pays the bill in full plus damages, and leaves. You like him.
That is the chapter.

## Chapter 3 — "The Welcome"

- **Hub:** Tellwater (Rim Hills, human, 60). A neat, well-kept town of four hundred houses with sixty
  people living in it: clean square, working well, a bakery, a fair ground, and the dark Dree skywell
  standing over the whole thing.
- **Dungeon:** the drowned bore — the flooded low ground east of town, the bore shed, and Extraction
  Bore One beneath it, under water since 1093.
- **Boss:** the Sump Head. Sunk flood gear and a churn of spent sleeves in the black water at the
  bottom of the shaft.
- **Party:** Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip. Unchanged, and they leave as fugitives.
- **Opens:** Tellwater, the fair ground, the flooded pasture, the drowned bore. **Then it closes
  everything:** the Pale post the rim road in both directions and the trade roads are shut to the
  party. The only way out is down the terrace stair onto the seabed, which hands the map to Act II.
- **Side contract:** "The fair, brought forward" (3 scenes, optional). Hired directly by Ollo
  Marrick, baker, who has no idea he is not a baker. Seems: fetch the fair poles and bunting from the
  old fair ground. Is: the fair has a schedule, and the schedule is a company form, renewed with the
  postings, and it specifies where the bunting goes and in what order the games are played, and it is
  identical to last year's, and the town is proud of that.

**The chapter as the player plays it.** Two days east and the road drops into good ground: a well-kept
town where every gate is painted and every hedge is cut and there are three hundred and forty empty
houses. The square fills up to meet them. Every face turns to Bron and smiles and gets him very
slightly wrong — a rehearsed grip on the shoulder from a baker with a flour-white hand, "Hesta's boy"
said one degree too well, a childhood nickname he has never had in his life, said warmly, by three
different people. You explore a town that is too clean, optionally take a bakery job that turns up a
schedule form, meet an Onn at the dark well who answers precisely the question she is asked and
nothing else, and stand in the square at night while Bron looks at a lit kitchen window and says
"That's the window" and is happy. Then you go east to the flooded pasture. A bore shed roof stands out
of still water where a farm used to be. You go down into the drowned bore, which is the chapter's
dungeon and the first genuinely frightening place in the game — black water, spent sleeves turning in
it in their hundreds, and flood gear that was installed, not broken. On the winch housing there is a
maker's plate, and the date cast into it is 1093, which is the year of the flood that the flood was
supposed to explain. You come up wet and holding it, and the Pale are on the ridge in force. Warden
Ivo Teach looks at Bron for three seconds, and steps aside, and nobody in the party understands why,
and they are still arguing about it when the roads close behind them.

### Locations Act I scenes require

**Sallowgate** — guild hall main room with the stove, the bill board and the ledger wall; the hall's
upper bunk room (twelve bunks, one lamp); the hall kitchen and stair; a third-terrace roof with a
stone cistern on it; the rim road above the town at dusk; the hill path to the door-mouth; the
**hall-mouth** itself (cut-stone door in a sandstone cliff, Kell doorward marks on the jamb, mortar
out and stacked tidily).
**Ancient Halls, upper works** — cold cut-stone corridors with torch brackets; a gallery over water;
a collapsed junction with a doorway standing in rubble; the great warded door; a side passage with
fresh chisel marks; the **ward chamber** — a high ancient room with a canvas company tent, a folding
table, a hooded lamp and numbered survey stakes in it.
**Windrow** — the tram-spur gate and meter post; the tram platform with a bench; the laundry district
with drying lines and coppers; a boarding-house room with three beds and a window that will not shut;
a chop-house with a long table; a wet street at dawn; the **Vintry well head** (a swept stone platform
under three hundred feet of dark stacked rings, with birds); the sanatorium wall in the background
only, with no sign on it.
**Above Windrow** — the hill road in wind; the **relay station** (a stone hut on the telegraph line
with a bench of instruments and a wax-cylinder recorder); the **survey cut** (new timbering, spoil
heap, naphtha lamps on poles, a cutting rig, a night shift).
**Tellwater** — the square with a working well and painted gates; the bakery (hot, a peel, a rack, a
green apron); the fair ground and its pole store; a lit kitchen window on a night square; the **Dree
well platform**; the ridge road above the town.
**East of Tellwater** — the flooded pasture (still black water, drowned hedge lines, a waterline on
the terrace); the bore shed roof and leaning winch tower; the **drowned bore interior** — a flooded
winch hall with sunk flood gear, a cast maker's plate, and hundreds of spent paper sleeves turning in
black water.
**Below Tellwater** — the terrace stair (four hundred feet of cut steps down the old shoreline); a
camp on the salt pan with two lines of laid rail running west.
