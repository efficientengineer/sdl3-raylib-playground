## Chapter 14 — "Thurn"

- **Hub:** Thurn, the hall-city. No shop, no inn, no money: the city feeds anyone standing in it and
  will not take payment because nobody in it can hold the idea of a debt. Resupply is a ride back up
  the maintenance rail to Sallowgate, which is also where the side contract is.
- **Dungeons:** (1) the Ninth Door approach and the Deep Stair — a mile of cut stair the Works never
  touched, descending past four dead guard posts. (2) Thurn's inner works — the council quarter, the
  cradle vault, and the sealed head of the Choir stair.
- **Boss:** the Standing Muster. Thirty hall-guard patterns holding the council door correctly. You do
  not fight thirty. Sefa has the Registry transcript of the relief formula, Zeph can sing it, Hesk
  knows one mangled line, and rank by rank the Muster grounds its halberds and stands aside. Four
  guards whose helms are crushed or whose armour is split cannot hear the words and come on anyway.
  That is the fight, and it should feel like putting down a horse.
- **Party:** Bron, Zeph, Pip, Ket, Hesk, Ollo, Sefa. Four in a fight; Sefa does not fight.
- **Opens on the map:** Thurn — the long street, the council quarter, the cradle vault, the well-court
  — and the head of the Choir stair, which is barred until chapter 15. Nothing new above ground; the
  maintenance rail from ch.13 already reaches every hall-mouth, Sallowgate included.
- **Side contract:** *Nine hundred tallies* (Sallowgate, posted by Tovin-Caleth, fee: nothing).

**Summary.** The party comes down the maintenance rail to the one hall-mouth the Works never opened,
and finds the Ninth Door standing wide because Maren walked through it a day ahead of them and the
guards stood aside for her. Hesk takes out a notebook at the threshold and writes down every person
who goes in, which is the first thing a doorward has ever done that is not shutting something. A mile
of stair later they walk into a lit, swept, warm street where three hundred and forty healthy people
are going about a day none of them will have tomorrow — the builders' descendants, twenty-eight
generations of a city that kept working, and not one of them has held anything longer than a week.
Gameplay here is a town you cannot help: no shop, no quest-giver who remembers asking, and a
direction-finding problem, because the residents give you good directions to places that have been
walled up for eight hundred years. Zeph and Ket work out why the Seam sings: the Choir has been
drawing a week at a time out of every living thing in Thurn since year 0, so the singing is mostly
them. Past the Standing Muster the council chamber says what the last council actually voted for —
they wrote everything they were into the Seam for safekeeping and sealed the doors on the bodies,
expecting to be read back out by people who never came — which is Maren's plan, run for a thousand
years, and the party has to look at it. Ket finds the cradle broken on purpose by the builders on
their way past, learns the Onn specification survives and the facility does not, and then reads the
four thousand names of her own muster aloud for six hours while Bron sits through every one of them.
The chapter ends at the head of the Choir stair with Maren's satchel on the step and the Long Bore
thinning the branch under the city.

## Chapter 15 — "The Choir"

- **Hub:** none. The maintenance rail platform at the Ninth Door serves as the only safe point, and it
  is a battlefield by the second half.
- **Dungeon:** the Choir stair, the Choir chamber, and the Seam face. Three connected spaces on one
  vertical axis: the stair down from Thurn, the chamber (a bowl of cut stone around a draw apparatus
  a thousand years older than the Works'), and beyond it the exposed Seam face itself.
- **Bosses:** (1) **Maren**, who is not defeated. The encounter is a reaching: the party has no damage
  that helps, the fail state is the party being driven back up the stair, and the win condition is
  getting a voice close enough to her to be heard over four hundred. (2) **the cap**, which is a
  timed hold — the stair must stay occupied while the chant runs, and the player fights Pale
  constables who do not want to be there and are under orders not to fire first.
- **Party:** Bron, Zeph, Pip, Ket, Hesk, Ollo, Sefa. Ket and Ollo leave at the end of the chapter.
  Zeph is unavailable for the last third (he is singing) and the game should make the player feel
  that as a hole in the party.
- **Opens on the map:** nothing. This chapter closes things. After the credits the Ninth Door and
  Thurn stay open and enterable, watched, with Hesk at the threshold.
- **Side contract:** none available — every hunter in the Basin is on the stair. The board at
  Sallowgate is bare except for one unnumbered bill the party posts themselves in 1535, which is the
  only bill in the game the player writes rather than takes.

**Summary.** The party goes down into the Choir chamber and finds Maren at the Seam face, and she has
stopped being one person: four hundred frightened voices arriving through one tired woman's mouth,
none of them able to finish a sentence. Nothing the party is good at works — you cannot hit it, pick
it, file it, count it or out-argue it — and after everyone has tried and failed Bron walks up and asks
her the first of Lyra's fifteen questions, and then the second, and holds her together one answer at
a time. At the fifteenth he has to improvise, because the manual leaves it blank, and she answers it:
not her plan, which would divide the record into a hundred thousand useless pieces, but the one thing
that has never been auditable, seizable or burnable — give it whole, once, out loud, to a Vess
chant-line. Meanwhile Ottoline Kerrow comes down the Long Bore with forty-one charges and the only
costed plan anyone in this game has produced, and she is right, and nobody can answer her, and she is
stopped anyway: Cadder shuts the Long Bore head off on his way past and puts sixty of his own crew on
the stair, Teach stands in the door, Tovin sends the whole board down the maintenance rail, and Emmet
Sark, two hundred miles away, files a message log because it belongs in the file. Gameplay is a long
defensive hold with a countdown that is eleven hours of chant, fought in waves between the talk
scenes, with the party's healer-shaped hole where Zeph used to be. Ilven Tessen refuses Sefa Quill to
her face and accepts Zeph twenty minutes later, on procedure, not sentiment. At the end the record
lives in eleven hours of Vess chant, Maren is empty and quiet for the first time in fourteen years,
Ket runs the fifteen questions on the Choir and shuts it off, the lamps of Thurn go out, and three
hundred and forty people have to be walked up a mile of stair by hand, one at a time, starting now.

## Epilogue — chapter 16

- **Hub:** Sallowgate, the Even Hand hall. No dungeon, no boss, no random encounters. The player walks
  a short overworld with almost everything on it revisitable, and the scenes fire where the people are.
- **Party:** Bron, Pip, Hesk, Zeph, Sefa are in the world; Ket and Ollo have gone where they said they
  would and are visitable, once each.
- **Opens on the map:** everything the player already had, plus Thurn as a walkable, dim, emptying
  town and the Ninth Door as a permanent landmark with Hesk at it.
- **Side contract:** *Nine hundred tallies* is still open and still pays nothing, and the ledger books
  fill in visibly as the epilogue goes on. The final bill in 1655 is the game's last interaction.

**Summary.** Nothing is undone. The epilogue runs from the 12th of Fallow 1107 to the 9th of Fallow
1108 and its job is to let the player walk around a world that did not get fixed and talk to people
who are carrying things on purpose. The inquiry sits at Lomm and produces a document, a striking-off,
an acquittal, a man who cannot finish a sentence, and no convictions at all. Ottoline Kerrow wins the
technical question, is removed by her own board over the cost of the delay, and is better at her next
job than she was at this one. Crewe publishes his list and the Basin argues about whether it is
genuine for a year and mostly decides it does not want to know. Teach tells his wife. Cadder goes
home to a village that still will not look at him and puts his own seal on a bill for the first time
since chapter one. Ket brings Vintry up after a hundred and sixty-three years and it is the only
unambiguously good thing that happens. Zeph goes to Braid twice a year to hear the chant checked and
it is checked properly. Bron tells a laundry presser in Windrow about eleven minutes of her own life
and it is not enough. And then he goes back to the hall, and there is a bill on the board.

- **Doctor Ovel Marsh** — retires. Keeps forty notebooks in Maren Ostry's handwriting that he never
  read and now cannot return to anyone, because she would not know what they were.

**The Basin**

- **Maren Ostry** — lives above the guild hall at Sallowgate and does small survey work. Not crowded
  for the first time in fourteen years. Remembers nothing of Tellwater, the sanatorium, or the last
  two years. Has never been to Braid to hear it.
- **Tovin-Caleth** — two lights a side, four of eight. Finishes copying the ledger wall into books; the hall is leased out
  from under her in 1110 and the books outlast the building, as intended. Chalks the last bill in the
  game.
- **Tibb Orsk** — takes the bills the party turn down, as always. Visits **Sela Marrin**, who is
  perfectly pleasant and does not know who he is, every eleven days.
- **Sem-Dree** — keeper of the dark Dree well; Dree comes back up within two years and she has work
  again. Is now asked something roughly every forty minutes and preferred the previous arrangement.
- **Arro-Vintry** — one light. Keeper of a running well for the first time in a hundred and sixty-three
  years. Puts the broom away.
- **Ilven Tessen** — first chanter of Braid; checks the record twice a year and sends a line back to
  the top of the hour for one wrong word. **Cassa Tessen** counts the repetitions and announces the
  total, which is enormous.
- **Ilsa Tremmel** — still at Ostry Bar, still asking, still writing the answers down. Her eleven-year
  notebook is inquiry evidence and she cannot remember handing it in.
- **Anneke Brae** — laundry presser, Windrow. Puts the kettle on for Bron on the first of every month.
  Neither of them ever gets anything back.
- **Neve Ardo** — company shop clerk at Lomm; was somebody's mother for four years on a three-year
  posting and kept a notebook she no longer has.
- **Aldo Fessel** — retired tram inspector; reads the list in the paper and recognises his own
  signature, and tells nobody, again.
- **Ross Kettle and Dalla Kettle** — still in Emberrow, still married, still ruined, still there.
- **Dov Ninefold** — off the bore crews and onto his aunt's door watch, paid out of a blacklisted
  man's pension, which he finds funny and she does not.
- **Nessa Tull** — still sells water at the metered rate, still hates it, still says so at length. It
  is her well the last bill in the game is about.
- **Idda Renn** — sleeps. **Corrie Fallow** — still cheerful, still at a counter, still never asked.
- **The people of Thurn** — three hundred and forty, walked up a mile of stair by hand over seven
  months. Housed in eleven towns, healthy, calm, employable, and named a different thing most weeks.
  They get no past. From the day the Choir stopped, they get to keep tomorrow.
- **The Clement Works** — not destroyed, not broken up, and still the Basin's warmth utility, bank and
  landlord. Large bores suspended pending the published study. Small bores continue. Somebody's town
  will go blank inside a decade and everyone who read the study knows it.
