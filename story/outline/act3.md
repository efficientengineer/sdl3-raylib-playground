## Chapter 7 — "The Registry"

- **Hub:** Vantage, Tiers One to Three (the Mouth, the Yards, the Market Shelf).
- **Dungeon:** the Registry stacks, Tier Five, entered at night by the staff stairs.
- **Boss:** a Pale recovery detail under Warden Ivo Teach, on the Tier Four/Five stair. A fighting
  retreat down three tiers. If the player cleared "Quiet the school" first, the school's back stair
  is open and the fight is winnable instead of survivable.
- **Party:** Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip, Ket-Ossun, Hesk.
- **Opens on the map:** the company roads — Vantage Tiers One to Three, Emberrow, Dunnow, Kettle,
  and a fast return to Windrow.
- **Side contract:** "Quiet the school" (Idda Renn, Vantage). Scenes 0725, 0760, 0795.

**Summary.** The party comes off the salt with forged road permits and walks up to the company city
for the first time: six tiers of warm windows cut into a quarry face, and the only warm place in the
Basin. The player explores Tier One (bunk rows, the care-house, the roof meters, the one tier where
you can hear the Seam), Tier Two (the Even Hand's founding hall, which the Works is trying to lease
out from under it, and where Idda Renn posts a bill about a school), and Tier Three (shops, strand
counters, the fair, money in the party's pocket for the first time in the game). They have money
because Vantage pays and because they mean to buy something. What they want is on Tier Five: the
Registry, which holds where every resettled person in the Basin was sent. There is no way in without
the Registrar, and the Registrar is Sefa Quill, and Sefa Quill is Zeph's mother. They climb the staff
stairs at night, cross the school terrace (side contract), and take the stacks floor by floor; Sefa
meets them at the top, sells them the map out of spite for a fee, and makes her son say her name out
loud in front of his friends before she hands it over. The map is beautiful work. It lists sixty-one
adults across the Basin who remember a mother named Hesta, and Bron will not have it. On the way down
Teach's recovery detail is coming up — their permits were flagged at five o'clock by Director Kerrow
— and the chapter ends at dawn on Tier Two with a schoolmistress asleep at last and a fresh Pale
recovery notice pasted on the wall beside the guild board: *the Fair Copy. Do not converse.
Emberrow.*

## Chapter 8 — "Give It Back"

- **Hub:** Emberrow, South Ravines — a tannery town built of quarried skywell stone, smoke lying in
  the cut, rope-walks strung wall to wall, a Clement Works care-house at the top of the street.
- **Dungeon:** the tannery undercroft — flooded lime pits and the quarried cellars under them, built
  out of the fallen Kestrin skywell's rings.
- **Boss:** Recovery Chief Ovey and two frame-bearers, in the street, with a setting frame on a
  handcart. They have come to set Ross Kettle quiet again. It would work.
- **Party:** Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip, Ket-Ossun, Hesk.
- **Opens on the map:** nothing new. The chapter closes the ravine roads behind them and points
  north at the Flats.
- **Side contract:** "Collection at Emberrow" (Clement Works accounts office). Scenes 0820, 0840,
  0845.

**Summary.** Emberrow is two days south down the ravine road: nine thousand people, dying tanneries,
smoke that sits in the cut and never leaves, and a company care-house at the top of the street that
is the second largest employer in town. It is also where Pip grew up, and she does not say so, and she
does not walk up that street. The player works the town: rope-walks, the tannery yards, a small Even
Hand hall, and a well-paid bill from the company accounts office that turns out to be a debt
collection. Asking after a woman in a grey coat gets them a straight answer from half a dozen people —
she has been here three days, she goes to houses, *she gives it back, she doesn't make it up* — and
one of the houses is Ross Kettle's, a fifty-eight-year-old tanner with a wife, a business and a
kind manner, whom the party meets and likes before anything happens to him. They go down into the
undercroft after her, through the lime pits and the old ring-stone cellars, and they find her sitting
on a crate eating bread, and she is courteous and crowded and not remotely dangerous. Then Ross
Kettle comes down the steps and asks her for it, and she gives it to him, and fourteen years arrive
at once, and the party watches a man they had tea with two hours ago come apart in the street while
his wife stands in the doorway. Recovery Chief Ovey arrives with a frame on a handcart and offers,
politely and legally, to make it stop, and the player has to decide. Warden Teach has Maren in the
tannery yard afterward and lets her go, and nobody understands why. The chapter ends with Maren
sitting on Ross Kettle's floor for a day and a half, which is more than the Works ever did for her,
and a road north.

## Chapter 9 — "The Recitation"

- **Hub:** none fixed. The party lives in a stolen survey crawler; Ostry Bar and the Kettle
  waystation are the settled stops.
- **Dungeon:** the Ilder stump — a mile of toppled skywell rings lying on the salt, entered at the
  broken cap and climbed along the inside of the fallen column.
- **Boss:** the sweep. Orrin Cadder and Ivo Teach's joint cordon closing on the stump. It is a fight
  you end by getting out, not by winning; the objective is distance, not kills.
- **Party:** Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip, Ket-Ossun, Hesk.
- **Opens on the map:** the Pale Flats, the Ilder stump, Ostry Bar, the drove roads north — and the
  survey crawler, which is the party's own vehicle from here to the end of the game.
- **Side contract:** "The crawler that came back empty" (the salt company, posted at Ostry Bar).
  Scenes 0945, 0950, 0955.

**Summary.** Maren is walking north across four hundred miles of salt and the party cannot follow on
foot, so they steal a Clement Works survey crawler out of a depot yard, which is the most fun the
game has had in three chapters and exactly what the player needs after Emberrow. The Flats are empty,
white and enormous; the crawler becomes a home with sleeping arrangements and arguments; the player
drives it between the Kettle waystation, where Nessa Tull sells water at the metered rate and hates
it aloud, and Ostry Bar, a salt bar around a sunken skywell cap where Onn come and stand for days and
a squatter camp of the resettled has grown against the ring. Everyone in that camp is healthy, content
and unable to say where they were born, and one of them — Ilsa Tremmel, who logged a written objection
at 21:40 on a night she cannot remember — has been asking questions and writing the answers in a book
for eleven years. At the Bar, Ket-Ossun opens a thousand-year-old maintenance manual to find a
diagram, reads a page aloud, and stops halfway, because the page is Lyra's liturgy word for word; the
conversation those two have afterward is the quietest and most important in the act. Then the sweep
starts: Cadder and Teach put a cordon across the Flats, and the party runs the crawler into the only
cover for forty miles, the fallen mile of the Ilder skywell, and climbs into it. Maren is already
inside. She does not fight. She sits down on a fallen ring with her hands open and tells Bron about
the winter he got lost above Tellwater, in the first person, with the timing right, and the detail he
never told anybody because it was too small to tell. They wait out the cordon in the dark with that
between them. At dawn Teach gives them ten minutes he will be asked about later, and they drive out.

## Chapter 10 — "Intake"

- **Hub:** Windrow, Rim Hills — a relay town on the tram spur: laundries, boarding houses, a tram
  yard, the dark Vintry skywell standing over it, and the Clement Works sanatorium behind a wall.
- **Dungeon:** the Clement Works sanatorium — the wards, the holding stock, the room where Maren was
  kept for twelve years, and the file room.
- **Boss:** the holding frame. A setting frame the size of a room, with Doctor Ovel Marsh and his
  ward staff around it. The boss is the room: the fight is not to be put in it.
- **Party:** Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip, Ket-Ossun, Hesk.
- **Opens on the map:** Vantage Tier Four (school, theatre, staff terraces) — the sanatorium's
  transfer paperwork carries the tier pass that gets them there.
- **Side contract:** none. The Even Hand has no bill in Windrow this chapter by design; the Windrow
  bill is Teach's, in chapter 12. An optional non-guild errand, Arro-Vintry's, is scene 1025. See
  `story/notes/outline-act3.md` item 5.

**Summary.** They came through Windrow in chapter 2 and walked past a wall without asking what was
behind it, and now they come back and ask. Windrow is a tram town: everyone works for the tram yard or
the sanatorium, the laundries run on sanatorium linen, and nobody has ever needed to know who the
patients are. The player works the town — a retired tram inspector on a bench who remembers the autumn
of 1093 perfectly and has never been asked about it, an Onn who has swept a dead skywell for a hundred
and sixty-three years, a boarding house, a bad supper — and then goes over the wall. Doctor Ovel
Marsh receives them himself, because he is proud of his wards and delighted to have visitors, and
gives them a tour of calm healthy people who cannot tell you their names; and when asked about the
woman he kept for twelve years he explains, kindly and without any idea what he is saying, that she
was never a patient, she was a record. At night they take the file room, and the file room is where
the game stops being about a mystery. Maren's file is twelve years thick. Bron's is one page: *age
nine, condition blank, disposition set and returned, template 4, two sleeves, source O, four marks* —
and it is already out on the desk under a lit lamp with a Registry docket slip on it in Sefa Quill's
hand, because she pulled it before they arrived and left it there on purpose. He was in the radius,
not at the node. Nothing of his was ever stored; there is no copy; Maren can give four hundred people
their lives back and cannot give him one hour. In the holding stock, a night clerk being nothing but
helpful offers Pip her own drawn days back, and she says no, and the game does not argue with her.
They come out over the wall with a box, and in it is a copy of Chairman Crewe's list in his own hand,
and one line of it says *Brae, A. — Windrow — laundry.* And then Bron walks down into the town in the
morning and stands in the steam outside a laundry door, and a middle-aged woman comes out with a
chipped cup and offers a stranger a cup of tea, and he does not take it and he does not tell her.
