# Pitch C — The Erased Town

## 1. Logline

Fourteen years ago a town went blank in a single night, and the company that did it rebuilt the
place with hired actors and perfect paperwork. One of the two people who walked out of that night
leads your party — and the childhood he remembers belongs to the woman the company is hunting.

## 2. The world

**Ourn** was made livable long ago and is giving up slowly. We stay in the **Sallow Basin**: a dried
inland sea, sandstone towns on the old shore terraces, salt flats, hill country with cut-stone halls
under it. Nine **skywells** — towers that hold the air warm and thick — were built here. Two still
run, and every decade the nights get colder. People still hold the autumn fair; they just hold it
earlier each year.

**Humans** came last and run the trade towns, the tram lines, and most of the payroll. They want
work, and one in four has it from the same employer.

**The Kell**: short and densely built, clay-warm skin, thick hair in one metal color — copper,
brass, iron — amber eyes that catch torchlight like an animal's. They run hot; a Kell in a snowfield
wears no sleeves. They live in terrace villages above the sealed hall-mouths and their inherited job
is stopping anyone digging. They want the company out of the hills. Half their young take company
jobs anyway, because the terrace farms are failing.

**The Vess**: tall and thin, ash-lilac skin, long backswept fluted ears with ribbing you can see
light through, solid-color eyes with no white, hair like drawn wire, a second tone humming under the
speech. They keep records by chanting them between bonded pairs, never on paper, and run the
ferry-rails across the seabed. Forty years ago a desperate Vess council sold their archive halls to
the company, cheap and legally. They want them back and never will.

**The Onn** were grown, not born: matte skin in one flat color, no hair, no nose, big lensed eyes
that click when they refocus, small lights down the temples that dim when they tire. Built to
maintain the skywells, still at it a thousand years after anyone was left to report to. Six hundred
remain and no way to make more. They want the halls opened: the specification for their own bodies
is stored down there.

## 3. The institution

**The Clement Works** is a utility, a bank and a landlord, run from the terraced company city of
**Vantage**, built over a hall-mouth. It owns the Basin's only working skywell and sells warm nights.
What it mines is memory. Under the hills runs **the Seam** (section 4); the Works draws pattern out
of it in glass threads called **strands** and sells them — someone's good afternoon, in a paper
sleeve, for an evening's wage. Writing a strand into a person is **setting**. It cures grief, settles
a difficult workforce, and makes an excellent Founder's Day.

**The lie:** that strands come from paid donors of good feeling, and that the Seam is only a power
source keeping the air alive. The best strands come from children in the Works' care-houses, whose
best days are drawn out and sold, leaving a flat, gapped childhood and a cheerful, incurious adult.

- **Chairman Ansel Crewe**, late sixties, courteous, tired, walks with a cane. Remembers the famine;
  believes the Works is the only thing between the Basin and a dead sky. Signed the Tellwater bore.
  *Holds:* the true list of where Tellwater's people were sent, in his own hand, kept out of the files
  because he couldn't burn it. His sin is that he can live with it.
- **Junior Director Ottoline Vane**, thirties, brilliant, up from a care-house herself and never
  mentions it. Runs Extraction; wants the chair and the skywells restarted, in that order. *Holds:*
  data proving extraction can't be made safe — every large bore inverts eventually. She buried it, and
  she isn't wrong that publishing would kill the Basin.
- **Warden Ivo Teach**, big, soft-spoken, keeps a boarding house for the widows of men who died on his
  jobs. Commands the Pale, the company police, and hunts Maren. *Holds:* he was the sergeant at
  Tellwater that night and carried children out of the street who didn't know what a street was. He has
  never told anyone, including his wife.
- **Registrar Sefa Quill**, Vess, third-generation company, elegant, funny, entirely amoral about
  paperwork; believes truth is whatever survives filing and says so at parties. *Holds:* the map of the
  lie — which towns are staffed, which residents draw a wage. She wrote the resettlement documents
  herself, a Vess whose own people's archive was taken. She knows exactly what she is.

## 4. What is under the Ancient Halls

The Seam is a lattice of grown crystal-metal filament running for hundreds of miles through bedrock:
the pattern-store of the machinery that made Ourn habitable. Weather rules, seed stock, soil
chemistry — and people, because the cheapest way to store a rule set was to store the person who knew
it. It sings because it is still rehearsing. Stand near it long enough and you remember things that
are not yours.

The hill people who dug down to it did not go mad. They got *good at it*. They gave a widow her
husband's last evening back. They gave a child a dead mother's voice. Then they used it to settle
arguments, and within two generations nobody could say which of their memories they had lived. Their
record-keepers worked it out: the city no longer had a past, it had a consensus anyone could edit. They
sealed every door from the inside — not to keep something in, but to stop themselves walking out and
doing it to everyone else.

The dead at the doors aren't sorcery. The Seam recorded the guards' oath more completely than anything
else about them and still runs that pattern inside the armor. The oath outlived the men because the oath
was all there was left to copy. That is why the guard lowers its halberd instead of swinging it.

## 5. The protagonist's lie

It is **Bron**, and it hurts most because he leads, he is blunt, and he says he only believes what he can
put his hands on. The player has taken his word as ground truth for ten hours.

He believes he grew up in **Tellwater**: his mother Hesta's kitchen, the well, the autumn fair, the winter
he got lost and nearly froze. Warm, specific, ordinary. He isn't hiding a trauma. He's hiding a happiness
that was issued to him.

The truth: Tellwater sat over a shallow branch of the Seam, and the first large extraction bore ran
backwards. Nobody died. Four hundred people were *unwritten* in one night and stood in the street at dawn,
breathing, docile, nameless — a disaster with no corpses. So the Works set them with stock lives, scattered
them across the Basin as clerks and drovers, restaffed the town with employees on three-year postings, and
filed it all as a resettlement. Bron was nine, in the bore-shed, and came out with a hole where his family
had been. Setting him and returning him to the rebuilt town was the cheapest cleanup available, and the
strands came from the only saturated source on site: the other survivor. His childhood is hers.

The clue is planted in hour one and pays in hour ten: Bron remembers being cold. Kell run hot. Kell children
don't remember shivering. Pip makes it a joke in chapter two.

Found out in four steps: the town greets him warmly and slightly wrong (ch.3); the payroll ledger says every
adult in Tellwater is an employee (ch.6); Maren recites his childhood in first person (ch.9); his intake form
reads *age nine, condition blank, disposition set and returned* (ch.10).

## 6. The one the institution made and lost

**Maren Ostry**, thirty-six, a survey clerk who was underground in the bore that night and took the opposite
dose. Where the town was emptied, she was filled — saturated with four hundred lives, including her own and
Bron's real one. Nineteen years in a company sanatorium, filed as **the Fair Copy**, because she is the only
complete record of Tellwater in existence. Then she walked out.

She isn't insane, she's crowded. She goes town to town finding the resettled blanks — settled, middle-aged,
mostly content — and gives back what was taken, by touch, because saturated as she is she can write. About a
third of them break. She keeps going, because those people were robbed and the paperwork has made the crime
legally nonexistent. Her escalation, and the endgame threat, is deciding to make the truth unerasable by
writing Tellwater's night into everyone in the Basin at once. She would call it testimony. It would empty a
hundred thousand people.

## 7. Act structure — 15 chapters

**Act I — The Contract (ch.1-3).** Four hunters take a board job to clear a hall entrance and ask no
questions. They find a door already opened from the inside and a dead guard that warns them out instead of
cutting them down. Following the money turns up a survey crew under a false guild seal and a surveyor who came
back blank. It ends in Tellwater, where the whole town knows Bron's name and gets it slightly wrong, and where
the Pale arrives in force and the party leaves as fugitives.

- Ch.1 — Sallowgate / Ancient Halls, upper works / the guard warns instead of attacking.
- Ch.2 — Windrow / the hill relay station / the contract was posted by the Works under a false seal.
- Ch.3 — Tellwater / the drowned bore / the town greets Bron wrongly; the party runs.

**Act II — Fugitives on the Shore (ch.4-6).** Cut off from the trade roads, they work the Vess shoreline for a
rail pass and a name for the thing under the hills, picking up an Onn who wants the doors opened and a Kell
doorward who wants them shut. What they learn makes it worse: the halls aren't haunted, they're *running*, and
the Works has been extracting for sixty years. In a labor town they find the Tellwater payroll. The act ends
with Bron asked to describe his mother's face, and unable to.

- Ch.4 — Braid / the flooded archive-hall / Ket joins; the Vess archive is a company annex.
- Ch.5 — Ninefold Terrace / the Sealed Stair / Hesk joins; the oath explained; the dead are recordings.
- Ch.6 — Saltmouth / Extraction Bore Four / a bore inverts with them inside; forty workers go blank.

**Act III — The Fair Copy (ch.7-10).** Forged papers get them into Vantage and the Registry, where Zeph's
estranged mother sells them the map of the lie for a fee and out of spite. They chase Maren through the
resettled towns, watch her give a man his life back, and watch him break. Cornered, she doesn't fight; she
tells Bron about the winter he got lost, from the inside. The act ends in a sanatorium file room, and then in
a Windrow laundry where a kind woman who doesn't know him offers him tea.

- Ch.7 — Vantage lower terraces / the Registry / Sefa Quill; the resettlement map; company roads open.
- Ch.8 — Ember Row / the tannery undercroft / Maren restores a man and it destroys him.
- Ch.9 — the Pale Flats, by crawler / the skywell stump / Maren recites Bron's childhood.
- Ch.10 — Windrow / the sanatorium / the intake form, Crewe's list, his real mother.

**Act IV — What the Paperwork Says (ch.11-13).** The Works moves to decommission Tellwater — end the postings,
empty the set, close the file — and the party goes back to stop it. A frightened nineteen-year-old officer of
the Pale shoots **Lyra**, then drops his rifle and sits down in the road. She is an Attendance sister carrying
two hundred deathbed hours with nowhere for her own to go, so Bron takes it, knowingly, with her consent: the
first memory in his head he is certain is real, and it isn't his. Vane relieves Crewe of his seat
mid-confession. It ends with Maren inside the Halls and the dead guards standing aside, because she carries the
oath-keepers' own memories and can say the words.

- Ch.11 — Tellwater / the town itself as the dungeon / Lyra dies; Ollo the baker joins.
- Ch.12 — Vantage upper tier, by lifting car / the skywell machinery / Crewe confesses; Vane takes the chair.
- Ch.13 — Braid under evacuation / the Long Bore / Teach arrests his own superior; Maren reaches the inner doors.

**Act V — The Doors (ch.14-15).** Below the seals is the hall-city: lit, swept, fed, and full of several hundred
calm healthy people who cannot tell you their names — a thousand-year-old preview of what Maren's plan makes.
The confrontation isn't a negotiation the party wins; Maren loses coherence and speaks in four hundred
frightened voices at once, and the fight is about reaching the woman inside the chorus. She gives the record to
the Vess, into chant, where it can't be audited or burned, and the giving empties her. Vane arrives to cap the
Seam with everyone still inside and has to be stopped by people, not miracles.

- Ch.14 — the hall-city / the hall-city / the builders' fate, walking around in daylight.
- Ch.15 — the Choir chamber / the Seam / Maren gives the record away; Vane caps the bore; the doors stay open, and watched.

**Ending.** The Works doesn't fall; it restructures, and Vane wins her hearing on the technical merits before her
own board removes her over the cost. Crewe's list is published and the Basin argues about whether it's genuine.
Two skywells restart, using Ket's recovered specification and the same buried thing that caused all of it. Maren
lives in Sallowgate, remembers nothing of Tellwater, and is for the first time in fourteen years not crowded;
Bron visits and neither of them can say what happened that night. Eleven hours of chant in Braid can. Bron keeps
the false childhood — a borrowed memory you know is borrowed is still yours to carry, the way he carries Lyra's
hour — and takes a new contract off the board in a cold wind he can finally feel.

## 8. Party roster

- **Bron** (Kell, axe, leader) — start. Believes he is from Tellwater. Never leaves; ends a different man wearing the same memories.
- **Lyra** (human, Attendance sister) — start. Sits the last hour of the dying and carries it; heals by spending what she holds. Killed ch.11.
- **Zeph** (Vess, resonance) — start. Ex-cataloguer in the archive the Works bought from his people; left with a coatful of files. Stays. The Registrar is his mother.
- **Pip** (human, blades) — start. Raised in a care-house; her good days were drawn and sold, and she has no memory of a single birthday. Offered them back in ch.10, she says no.
- **Ket-Ossun** (Onn, skywell-keeper) — joins ch.4 for passage, stays for the specification in the Seam. Her temple lights dim all game. Leaves ch.15 to run a restarted skywell.
- **Hesk** (Kell doorward) — joins ch.5 furious that they opened a door, stays because watching them beats losing them. Right about everything, overruled every time.
- **Ollo Marrick** (human) — joins ch.11. Has played "Denner the baker" for eleven years on a renewing posting and has no other life left. Leaves ch.15 to hold the town together as himself.
- **Sefa Quill** (Vess, Registrar) — joins ch.13 after Vane passes her over. The woman who wrote the lie walks back through the town she erased, beside her son.

## 9. Five scenes I can already see

**The welcome.** Wide of Tellwater's square, every face turned to Bron and smiling. Insert on the baker's
flour-white hand gripping his shoulder. Portrait of Bron working hard to look pleased while someone off-panel
calls him a nickname he has never had.

**The recitation.** Maren sits on a broken skywell stump, hands open and empty, while Bron stands over her with
his axe up. Eyes-slit on Bron as she describes, in first person, the winter he got lost. His hands stay up. His
face stops.

**The tea.** A laundry doorway in Windrow, steam, a middle-aged woman holding out a chipped cup and smiling at a
stranger. Profile of Bron in the door, not taking it, while behind him Pip looks anywhere else.

**The hour.** High angle down on Lyra in the road, circlet knocked sideways, Bron's forehead against hers and both
hands on her face. Behind them, a young officer of the Pale sitting down in the dirt beside his rifle.

**The hall-city.** Establishing wide of a lit, swept, thousand-year-old street underground, full of well-fed people
in old clothes standing calmly and all facing the same way. Not one of them looking at the party.

## 10. Risks

- **The false past is the most-copied twist in the genre.** This survives only if the lie is *warm* rather than
  traumatic and the mid-game beats land as dread rather than confirmation. If players call it in chapter three,
  acts I-II must be worth playing on their own.
- **Memory-as-industry patches plot holes too easily.** "The Seam did it" can explain anything, which means it
  explains nothing. The rules need fixing early and never bending — and the battle system has to make strands a
  real resource, or theme and gameplay never touch.
- **The spine sits on one chapter-nine scene and on Maren.** Four hundred victims are offscreen; if she doesn't
  carry them the crime stays abstract. An ending where the company survives and nobody is restored is honest but
  may read as flat. And it kills Lyra, one of only two characters with approved art.
