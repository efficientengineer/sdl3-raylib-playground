# Pitch A — The Company Town

## 1. Logline

Four hunters take a job clearing an old door, and find they were hired to open it for the company
that lights the world by mining memory out of the ground and selling it as lamp oil. Chasing the
woman that company built out of other people's lives, they learn the party's most honest man is
telling her childhood as his own.

## 2. The world

**Ossaly.** Salt flats, terraced hill-farms, black pine, a drowned lowland shelf. Sixty years ago
nobody had light after dusk. Now every window burns all night, because of **quire** — a pale
striated mineral in deep seams that holds recorded life. Stone that remembers. Refined it is called
**bright**: lamps, pumps, rail engines, field surgeries, guns. It has halved child mortality. It is
also somebody's mother's wedding, burning in a streetlamp. Tone: warm people and failing systems,
banter in the rain on a freight car, a country quietly eating itself to keep working.

**Humans** — the majority and the labor force, come late from the coast and built on top of
everything. They want what they were promised: wages, a warm winter, a pension.

**The Hurn** — short, compact, broad-shouldered; small low-set ears, skin with a faint mineral
sheen like frost on slate, hair in hard bright colors from trace quire in the water. *Bron is
Hurn.* Terrace farms and tunnel-halls. They feel a seam through forty feet of rock, which made them
the builders of the Ancient Halls and, now, the Consort's best prospectors at double farm wages.
They want to stop being the people who find the thing that kills their towns.

**The Sile** — tall and slim, long tapered ears, hair that lifts in dry air, narrow eyes with a
second iris ring. *Zeph is Sile.* They don't sleep; they **lapse**, four hours still while a memory
that isn't theirs plays through them, drawn from one shared archive. The Consort bought that vault
in a bankruptcy, so the young ones now lapse into static. They want their dead back.

**The Nim** — petite and quick, big dark eyes with a second translucent lid, fine fur on the
forearms, four minutes of held breath. *Pip is Nim.* Stilt-rafts in the lowlands, drainage warrens
under the company city. The charter lists them under fauna, which is how the Consort has never paid
one a wage. They want one line of text changed.

## 3. The institution

**The Ardent Consort for Light and Carriage.** A chartered company, not an empire: eleven seams,
the freight line, and **Ardent**, the city that never goes dark. Field security is **the Reach**;
the department for what the Reach can't do is **the Ledger**.

**What it takes:** quire, and — as the seams thin — memory straight out of the living. It buys
"settlements," partial drainings from the poor and the dying. Fifty marks for a decade you weren't
using.

**The lie:** that the seams are a deposit like coal, laid down by geology, belonging to nobody.
Every hand is told this on day one; every assay-master knows better.

**Chairwoman Isaura Vell** — sixties, plain, tireless, out of a pit town; her mother went blind in
a shaft at thirty-one. She wrote the eight-hour day, the widow's pension and the free clinics, and
isn't lying about any of it. She holds the truth that **the good is real**: closing the Consort
kills more children than running it, and she has done that arithmetic more honestly than the heroes
have.

**Director Ossian Rye** — the Ledger. Soft, apologetic, keeps a notebook of every name he has
erased and visits the graves. He thinks forgetting is mercy: a town that knows what was done to it
can't be lived in, and he watched one try. He holds the truth that **the Consort cannot survive the
truth** — its collapse isn't justice, it's a winter with no fuel.

**Warden-Principal Cabre Sunn** — the Reach. Hurn, funny, adored by his men, draining his own
people's hills. Better a Hurn on the whistle than a human: he got them wages, a school, a seat at
assay. He knows to the mark what he sold, and holds the truth that **no option ever cost nothing**.

**Assay-Master Nivene Oke** — young, Sile, brilliant, not remotely cruel. She runs the Chord
program because it's the last door to her people's stolen archive, and her curiosity causes the
disaster. She alone holds the truth about **what is actually down there**.

## 4. What is under the Ancient Halls

A seam so old and continuous it stopped being a deposit and became coherent: **the Long Chord**. It
doesn't think fast. It hums, and if you sit beside it long enough it hums back what you gave it.

**Oldmeet** was built downward toward that hum and grew rich; the dead stayed audible and no craft
was ever lost. Then a hard generation's council put the whole city's memory into the Chord for
safekeeping, and it came back wrong — children waking with a stranger's drowning as their own, two
men sharing one wife's name for her. Within a season Oldmeet couldn't tell itself apart, so the
council sealed every door from the inside: not to cage a monster, but to stop the traffic. The
guards' oath went in with everything else and the Chord still plays it, which is why armored dead
lower halberds instead of swinging them. *Scene 003 stands as written.*

The danger is what people do with it. Quire cut near the Chord assays four times richer, which is
why the Consort reopened the Halls — and Nivene has proved the Chord answers a direct question if
you feed it. Not a god. A patient archive with a write head.

## 5. The protagonist's lie

**Bron.** He remembers **Ambry**: a hill town of four hundred above the richest seam in Ossaly. The
bakehouse. The bell in the square. His mother's hands cracked from the cold. These stories are the
warmest thing in the party.

Ambry was drained. Not massacred — *emptied*. Over one winter the people forgot the town and
wandered downhill, and the Ledger rebuilt it thirty miles away as **New Ambry**, with a monument to
a collapse that never happened. Nobody could contradict it, because the thing taken was the part
that would have.

Bron was the second intake of the Index program: his Hurn ear made him a candidate, the same ear
made him leak, and he was discharged as a failure. His own childhood isn't stored anywhere — it was
drained, assayed, sold, burned in a lamp years ago. Since a subject with no past goes catatonic,
the Ledger issued him a stabilizer: somebody else's childhood, clean and warm and complete. Then a
name, a kit, eleven marks, and a bench outside a hunters' hall.

The childhood is **Saire's**. Planted in Chapter 3, when he describes a bell tower and the survey
plate shows none ever stood there. Confirmed in Chapter 9, when Saire recites his best story ahead
of him, word for word, with the timing right. The worst lands four minutes later: **Lyra knew.**
Her order does the Consort's intake work; she read his file three years ago and said nothing,
because he was happy.

It has to be Bron. He's the blunt one who can't lie, the party's floor. If Zeph were hollow it's a
twist; if Bron is hollow, everything they trusted about each other is a question, and he has
nothing to stand on while he asks it.

## 6. The one the institution made and lost

**Saire Orn**, designation *the Index*. A Hurn girl from Ambry, eleven when the Ledger took her,
chosen because she could hear the seam. For nineteen years they poured the Chord's returns into her
and read her out like a meter. She holds thousands of lives and sorts hers from theirs by
elimination each morning; it takes about an hour.

She wants restitution: open the Chord all the way, give everyone back everything, at once, for
free. She's right that it was stolen, right that no court will return it, right that the Consort's
plan is worse than hers — and she is also the proof of what total restitution does to a person,
which she can't see, having not been one for nineteen years. She never gloats. She's the most tired
person in the game.

## 7. Act structure — 14 chapters

**Act I — The Contract (Ch 1-3).** The frontier town of **Weft**, a board, a job: clear the
entrance, ask no questions. What goes wrong is that there's no treasure behind the door — there's a
Consort survey camp that waited three days for somebody expendable to trip the old wards. It ends
in rebuilt New Ambry, with Bron insisting he recognizes a town he's never been to. On foot all act;
the map is one valley wide.

- Ch 1 — Weft | Ancient Halls, upper works | the dead guard warns them off; behind the door is a company tent.
- Ch 2 — Weft under curfew | the Harrow pumping station | Saire takes one crate, hurts nobody; the party is blamed.
- Ch 3 — New Ambry | the drowned cut above the old town | a monument to forty dead from a collapse with no bodies.

**Act II — The Bright City (Ch 4-6).** Forged crew papers put them on the freight line into Ardent,
hunting whoever posted their contract. They find him, and he's delightful: Cabre Sunn buys them
dinner and lets them walk, because a Consort investigated by four hunters is a Consort nobody is
looking at properly. It ends with an audience with Vell — a real job offer, made in good faith —
interrupted by Saire hitting the Assay Tower, the party framed and running for water.

- Ch 4 — Sennow Junction | sorting house, then a moving freight train | rail permits open the salt flats.
- Ch 5 — Ardent, the Low Wards | Sump Nine drain gallery | Pip's family have signed settlement papers; Tuss joins with a barge.
- Ch 6 — Ardent, Consort House | the Assay Tower | Vell's offer is reasonable; Saire's raid makes them fugitives.

**Act III — The Settlements (Ch 7-9).** Down the flooded shelf by barge, through chapel-hulks where
the poor sell decades, racing Saire to the Halls' lower gates. What goes wrong is personal: Zeph
reads a spool he shouldn't and starts leaking a dead stranger into his own speech, and under
Coldstair, Saire tells Bron his own best story before he can. Lyra admits she read his file three
years ago. It ends with Bron alone on a terrace at dawn and Lyra walking down the hill.

- Ch 7 — Lowwater raft towns | the Wake's chapel-hulk | Lyra used to sing for people while they were emptied.
- Ch 8 — Oldmeet outer gates | the halls of the door guards | Keel speaks, and says why the doors were shut.
- Ch 9 — Coldstair | the Ledger's field archive | Saire recites Bron's childhood; Lyra knew; Lyra leaves.

**Act IV — The Fix (Ch 10-12).** The Consort's answer to a destabilizing Chord is to force-feed it,
dumping every drained spool in inventory down the shaft to quiet it. It does the opposite: lamps
speak in dead voices, strangers relive each other's worst hour in the street. Stopping it means a
temporary alliance with Saire, who wants it stopped for the wrong reason. It ends under Ardent with
Cabre holding a door he didn't have to hold, and Zeph going into the channel to keep it open and
not coming out — faintly audible in every lamp for the rest of the game.

- Ch 10 — New Ambry, dark | the Ledger's rewrite station | Rye apologizes; the spool with Bron's designation is blank.
- Ch 11 — Grey Fold uplands | Deep Seam Six | Nivene defects, Saire fights beside them, a sky-lighter opens the north.
- Ch 12 — Ardent burning | the Chord Works | Cabre dies on a door; Zeph stays in the channel; Saire takes the key.

**Act V — The Door (Ch 13-14).** Oldmeet, open at last: a whole city preserved mid-sentence. They
can't simply break the Chord — that erases every settled person's returns, kills Saire, and takes
the last audible trace of everyone's dead. Vell arrives at the final gate with no escort and a
folder of mortality tables, and she is not wrong. The ending is a choice of what to send before the
door shuts: they broadcast one thing, the record of every erased town, back to the people it was
taken from, then seal it from inside, honestly this time. Bright dies — lights out across Ossaly
with a hard winter coming, forty thousand jobs gone, the Nim finally on the charter only because
the ledger went public, and Bron left with nothing before nineteen.

- Ch 13 — Oldmeet, sealed city | the approaches and the choir floor | the council's last minutes; Vell at the gate.
- Ch 14 — the Long Chord | the Chord itself | Saire, the broadcast, the door, the lamps going out street by street.

## 8. Party roster

- **Bron** — Hurn hunter, party leader, from Ch 1. Never leaves; loses everything else instead.
- **Lyra** — human, lapsed Wake sister, conscience and secret-keeper. From Ch 1; walks at Ch 9; returns Ch 10 by her own choice.
- **Zeph** — Sile reader who lapses into stored memory and keeps a piece of everyone he reads. From Ch 1; dies Ch 12 holding a channel open.
- **Pip** — Nim scout from the Ardent warrens; jokes when frightened, touches the thing first. From Ch 1; unplayable in Ch 5 while her family are settled.
- **Tuss** — Nim barge captain, Pip's aunt, sixty and unimpressed. Joins Ch 5 with the *Comber*; leaves Ch 11 to evacuate four raft towns.
- **Keel, Ninth of the Door** — an Oldmeet warden: old armor, a blue light where a face was, the scene 003 design. Joins Ch 8 once they prove they carry no quire out; stiffens away from the Halls.
- **Nivene Oke** — Sile assay-master and Consort executive. Joins Ch 11 when her own program is used for the dump; takes the blame publicly in the epilogue.
- **Saire Orn** — the Index. Playable Ch 11 only; talks like four people, leaves the moment goals diverge.

## 9. Five scenes I can already see

**The tent behind the door.** The great stone door stands open on darkness — and in the darkness, a
canvas company tent, a folding table, a lamp, and a clerk looking up from a clipboard with mild
annoyance. Bron's axe is still raised.

**The bell that was never there.** Bron on an empty hillside in high grass, pointing at nothing,
insisting. Behind him Pip holds up a surveyor's plate of the same slope: no tower, no square. Lyra
is looking at the plate, not at Bron.

**Word for word.** Two-shot in a lamplit archive: Saire speaking quietly, eyes lowered, a hand at
her collar, and Bron opposite her with his mouth open around the sentence he hasn't said yet.

**Lyra at the chapel-hulk.** Flashback insert: a younger Lyra in Wake white, gloved hand over a
stranger's hand on a table, eyes shut, mouth open in song, the lamp beside her coming up bright.

**The lights go out.** High wide of Ardent at night from the air, the whole grid burning — then the
same frame going dark in blocks, street by street, one window still lit near the bottom.

## 10. Risks

- **The false-memory reveal is the most borrowed thing here.** I moved it as far as I could: issued
  administratively as a medical stabilizer, taken from a living antagonist rather than a dead
  friend, a childhood rather than a heroic career. Still adjacent. For more distance, make Bron's
  issued past a *composite* of four donors, none of them Saire — but that costs the Chapter 9
  recitation.
- **Memory-as-fuel sits one metaphor from life-energy-as-fuel.** The differentiator is that quire is
  *specific* — someone's Tuesday, not abstract vitality — and that the extraction is legal, paid for
  and signed. If that slips in the writing, this becomes a very familiar game.
- **Isaura Vell may be too persuasive for her own good.** A chairwoman with the better spreadsheet
  risks reading as a defense of her, or as the party losing an argument the game needed them to win.
  The answer — right about the deaths, wrong about who decides — must be dramatized, not stated,
  which is the kind of line the brief says to cut. Four executives is likely one too many for twenty
  hours; Rye and Vell must land.
