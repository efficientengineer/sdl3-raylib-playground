# Pitch B — Hunters and Peoples

## 1. Logline

A crew of working hunters takes a job clearing an old door, and finds every contract on every tally
board in the basin is the same contract: the buried machines that keep this land livable are dying,
and the company paid to fix them is selling the pieces. To stop it they go down to the thing the
ancients sealed themselves in with, and learn it was never a god — it was a job, and somebody has to
do it.

## 2. The world

**Mereth**, an inland basin: a shrinking salt sea, ravine country south, a white dead interior
creeping outward each year. Under it runs **the Breath**, a thousand-year-old network of conduits
and pumping halls that drags sea-moisture inland and lifts deep water up. Towns sit over
**breath-wells**, where it surfaces. When a well dies the ground crusts over in one season with a
mineral rot called **the White**; you can stand in a dead field and hear your boots crunch. Swords
and spell-work beside brass valve gear, corpses still on duty, survey dirigibles with a company logo
on the envelope. Warm people, dust, cold metal. Melancholy with jokes in it.

**Humans** — newest here, nine hundred years, most numerous. They farm a basin floor that is only
farmable because the Breath was throttled down to dry it for them. They don't know that. They want
what they have to keep working.

**Kelder** — short, compact, heavy-shouldered; clay-brown skin, spiky rust-and-copper hair, amber
eyes that catch light like an animal's and see in near-dark. They live in **step-towns** cut into
ravine shoulders above a breath-well, because they built the Breath and kept it: certified
hereditary **well-keepers**, until the treaty. They want the certificate back. Not compensation. The
work.

**Vessa** — tall, thin, long mobile pointed ears that ache in dry air, hair in cold indigos and
ash-violets, blind in old age. Their cities lie under the old shoreline, drowned when the sea was
high and crusted over since it fell, so they live on **raft-towns** that follow the water down. They
hold the only surviving operating records of the Breath, memorised as songs because paper rots on
the water. They want the sea back, and only they know what that costs.

**Tannik** — petite, quick, huge dark eyes, small high ears, pale down on cheeks and forearms, an
extra joint in every finger so their hands go where tools can't. They live in **under-markets** in
the crawlspaces under human cities: three generations deep, no lease, no vote. They do the repairs
nobody pays for. They want the ground they live under, in writing.

## 3. The institution

**Cistern**, chartered ninety years ago by treaty to steward the Breath for every people in Mereth.
Its city is **Brayce**: twelve tiers in a quarry pit over the largest well, wealth at the top,
metering houses and the Sink at the bottom.

What it takes is pressure. It doesn't repair failing wells — it **caps** them, sells their capacity
to paying districts, and buys the dead land cheap. It took the Kelder certificate by treaty and
hired the same Kelder back as day labour.

The lie: *the Breath is dying of old age and we are slowing the decline.* It is dying because it has
run on a stored pattern for a thousand years with nobody to replace it, and because Cistern's new
shafts crack the conduits faster than decay ever did.

- **Idris Fenn**, Director of Yield. Sixty, kind, tired, does arithmetic out loud. She knows how many
  die under each plan and picked the smallest number she could find. Holds: the ledger.
- **Warden Corbel Strake**, head of the Hooks. Ex-hunter, struck off the Tally rolls. Polite,
  unhurried, hates being thanked. Holds: what was done at Vennock, and where the eleven were dropped.
- **Doctor Mave Sennett**, head of the Deepening. Delighted by everything, cruel by omission; talks
  to test subjects like colleagues and means it. Holds: what is under the Halls, and that she has
  already seated someone beside it.
- **Chair Aurel Vance**, top tier, very old, signs everything. Signed the Kelder treaty himself as a
  young clerk and thought it generous. Holds: the charter dies by one signature — his.

The party's home is **the Free Tally**: a hunters' guild with no patron, open to all four peoples,
jobs on a board, paid on completion. Its frontier chapter sits in the sandstone town of **Sandry**,
above the Ancient Halls.

## 4. What is under the Ancient Halls

Two miles down, a crystalline mass the size of a cathedral that was never built — it grew. It
resonates, and it **copies whatever rhythm is put beside it and repeats that rhythm across the whole
basin.** Sing to it and it sings back a thousandfold, the conduits move, and it rains. The old Kelder
called it **the Low Note**.

The pattern has to come from a living body. So they seated a volunteer beside it — a **Voice** — and
the Low Note took their breath, their pulse, and finally their thinking, and made weather out of them.
It killed the Voice slowly, and kept the pattern afterward, and a dead pattern degrades. Their last
Voice soured over one winter, and the basin got a season of black rain that sickened everyone
downwind.

So they cut the tuning conduits, sealed every door from the inside, and gave the hall-guard corps a
standing order: **nobody goes in, ever, because a person walking in is the danger.** The guards took a
preservative to hold the post past their own deaths. That oath is still running — which is why the
guard in the doorway lowers its halberd instead of swinging it.

The Breath has coasted on the last un-soured pattern for a thousand years, thinning. The world isn't
old; it's an echo running down. Everyone powerful who learns this wants the same thing: to choose the
next Voice. Whoever does owns the weather of a country, and therefore everyone in it.

## 5. The protagonist's lie

**Bron.** He believes he is a well-keeper's grandson from the step-town of **Vennock**, away on a job
the season the well failed, his mother dead in the White with the rest of the town. He remembers her
face, the ravine stairs, the smell of the well house in summer.

The truth: Bron was one of **eleven** early Deepening subjects — Kelder tolerate a shard of the Low
Note better than anyone, because the trait runs in the keeper lines. He didn't take, and the process
burned out everything he was. Disposal was cheap: dose the failures with a donated memory template,
drop them in a distant town, let them live. Bron's childhood is **Ollen Brask's** — the guild
master's own, given to a company technician under duress in exchange for four Kelder children going
home. Brask recognised his mother's kitchen behind Bron's eyes the day the boy walked into the Sandry
hall, said nothing for four years, and taught him everything.

The player learns it in chapter 10: Brask's deposition, then a file with eleven names and Bron's own
name nowhere in it. By then he has buried Brask and stood in Vennock in front of a show-home with an
eight-year-old maker's stamp on the lintel.

Why Bron: he leads, he's blunt, he trusts what he saw with his own eyes. The lie takes his certainty,
his origin, his grief and his mentor in one motion, and his rage at Cistern turns out to be a rage
the company installed — still correct, and not his. He keeps the name Brask gave him. One residue:
the Low Note can't read him, so he can walk into the chamber and out again and do nothing in there
but carry someone else in.

## 6. The one the institution made and lost

**Sohvi Rell.** Vessa, raft-town song-keeper, forty. She sold Cistern her memorised share of the
archive for medicine for a hull of sick children, and Cistern kept her, because whoever knows the
songs knows how a Voice is seated. Sennett cut a shard out of the Low Note and tested it in her. It
half worked. The shard sits under the skin of her throat as a hairline of light; she hums and rain
falls in a column on her and nowhere else, and she leaves wet footprints on white ground.

She wants to seat *her own* pattern deliberately and pull the sea back to its nine-hundred-year line.
Vessa cities surface, Kelder step-towns get rain, and forty human towns and the lower nine tiers of
Brayce go under. She is partly right because the dry basin isn't nature, it's the theft: she isn't
proposing a catastrophe, she's reversing one, late, at an unbearable price. Nobody in the party can
beat her argument. They beat her anyway.

## 7. Act structure — 15 chapters

### Act I — The job (ch. 1-3)

Sandry, the Tally hall, a board of small work; the party wants a contract and a night off. This one
says clear the entrance, ask no questions, and the poster's name is burned off a seal still legible
underneath. A Cistern crew is already down there cutting the conduit, and when the party finds them
the crew seals the door and walks away. It ends with the four of them surfacing through a Tannik
under-market into a city they were never meant to see, holding a serial number.

- Ch 1 — **Sandry** / the Halls' upper corridors. Turn: the dead guard doesn't attack; it points them
  out.
- Ch 2 — **Thurlow Step** (Kelder) / the flooded well sump. Turn: the well didn't fail. It was capped,
  and the plate carries a company serial.
- Ch 3 — **Sandry** / the Ninth Door approach. Turn: Strake takes the shard and seals them in. Boss:
  the cutting rig. The freight road to Brayce opens.

### Act II — The company city (ch. 4-6)

Brayce, on a permit Pip forged. The party wants the serial traced and a name to put on a complaint.
The complaint works: Idris Fenn reads it, agrees with most of it, and hires them — a real, generous
contract to bring in an escaped asset called the Carrier. It ends at a conduit gate in Kettle Reach
with Sohvi getting clear, the Hooks coming through, and Brask holding the gate down and not coming
out.

- Ch 4 — **Brayce, the Sink** / the metering house. Turn: Brask's certificate is on file, with a
  deposition attached he won't discuss.
- Ch 5 — **Brayce, upper tiers** / the Deepening shaft head. Turn: Fenn's offer, and it's a good
  offer. Sennett opens the shard case.
- Ch 6 — **Kettle Reach** / the cracked conduit. Turn: Brask dies, and his last words to Bron are a
  name that isn't Bron's. Boss: Strake — a retreat, not a win.

### Act III — Four peoples (ch. 7-9)

A Vessa skiff opens the sea, then the coast, then the ravines. The party wants Sohvi found before
Cistern finds her, and Brask buried properly. Every town they pass has the same plate on its well,
the Vessa turn out to have sold Sohvi themselves, and Vennock is standing — rebuilt, eight years old,
full of relocated contract workers, none of whom have heard of Bron. It ends underground, with Zeph
singing a phrase from a sealed song and a thousand-year-old hall-guard answering him in Kelder.

- Ch 7 — **Sallow Raft** (Vessa) / a drowned street. Turn: the sealed songs are the Halls' operating
  log; Zeph was expelled for reading them.
- Ch 8 — **Vennock** / the original terraces buried under the fill. Turn: the erased town. Pip leaves
  to move her family out of a condemned market.
- Ch 9 — **the Ninth Door** / the hall-guard barracks. Turn: Ninth speaks and joins; her key opens the
  maintenance rail and the whole interior.

### Act IV — The eleven (ch. 10-12)

The service line runs everywhere now. The party wants the charter revoked and Vance's signature on
it, which means evidence — and the evidence turns out to be about Bron. Then Cistern, panicking,
fires the shard to prove a compliant Voice can be seated, and buys a week of black rain across three
provinces. It ends with Sohvi walking into Brayce through that rain, lifting the shard off Sennett's
bench, and leaving.

- Ch 10 — **Ashgate**, the Tally's founding hall / the company records house. Turn: Brask's deposition,
  the eleven, Bron's file. Ammi Corr defects; Pip rejoins.
- Ch 11 — **Ossen's Rest**, a town living inside the White / the buried well-field. Turn: they see what
  a fresh cap does in one season, and where Sohvi is going. Tibb joins.
- Ch 12 — **Brayce, the Deepening** / the shaft to the shard chamber. Turn: black rain; Sohvi takes the
  shard; the party steals a survey dirigible.

### Act V — Somebody has to do it (ch. 13-15)

Back to Sandry, now a company staging camp, and then down. The party wants Sohvi stopped and the
chamber closed for good, and closing it isn't on the menu: the Low Note can't be repaired. The options
are Sohvi's flood, Fenn's rotation — a paid Voice every nine years, survivor benefits, and she has a
waiting list — or seating one pattern and cutting the chamber's throat so nobody can ever change it
again, with the Voice inside. It ends with the third, and with Lyra, and a basin that will merely
survive.

- Ch 13 — **Sandry under occupation** / the sealed levels. Turn: Ninth holds the inner door against the
  Hooks and completes her oath. She doesn't come back up.
- Ch 14 — **the cut conduits** / the old Voice's chair. Turn: a thousand-year-old body still seated, its
  pattern still faintly running. Boss: Sohvi. She loses, she is right, and nobody pretends otherwise.
- Ch 15 — **the throat.** Turn: Lyra volunteers; Bron carries her in because the chamber won't take him;
  Fenn watches from the stair with her clipboard and her own volunteer, and doesn't stop them.

**Ending.** The White stops spreading; it doesn't retreat. The sea keeps falling, slower. The charter is
revoked and Cistern's assets sold off inside a year, and the Kelder get the certificate back on paper.
Fenn is never charged and advises the successor authority. Bron keeps the name Brask gave him. Last
image: a new job on the Sandry board, and old keepers saying the wells breathe a little slow now, like
someone thinking.

## 8. Party roster

- **Bron** (Kelder, axe, leader) — starts, never leaves, loses everything else instead.
- **Lyra** (human, Order of Standing Water) — starts. Her order's blessings are a valve-check liturgy
  whose meaning was lost: she has done maintenance her whole life and called it faith. Leaves ch. 15 as
  the Voice.
- **Zeph** (Vessa, song-keeper's apprentice, expelled) — starts. He reads the ancients because their
  records are songs. Outlives everyone he'd rather have.
- **Pip** (Tannik, conduit runner) — starts. Leaves ch. 8 to move her family out of a condemned market;
  rejoins ch. 10 angrier, with her whole street behind her.
- **Ollen Brask** (Kelder, Tally master, last certified well-keeper) — playable ch. 1-6, dies ch. 6
  holding a gate. The source of Bron's childhood.
- **Ninth** (Kelder hall-guard, preserved, a thousand years on post) — joins ch. 9 once the party proves
  they mean to close the door. Leaves ch. 13, oath finished.
- **Ammi Corr** (human, Hook sergeant) — joins ch. 10 after refusing an order she has obeyed before.
  Brings company access and a great deal of guilt.
- **Tibb Halloran** (human, metering clerk, terrified, funny) — joins ch. 11. Leaves ch. 15 to testify at
  the hearing instead of going down the shaft. It's the braver choice and the party tells him so.

## 9. Five scenes I can already see

**The cap.** Thurlow Step's well house, ankle-deep black water. Brask kneels with his lamp on a brass
plate bolted across the well mouth, thumb tracing the stamped serial. In the doorway behind him, six
Kelder women wait with empty jugs.

**Brask's last order.** The conduit gate grinding down, orange light one side, dark the other. Brask
beyond it with his spanner braced in the runners. Bron's gauntlets on the descending edge, hauling, not
strong enough. The last word Brask says is a name, and Bron's face doesn't recognise it.

**Vennock.** Bron in the doorway of his mother's house: furniture still wrapped, dust in an even sheet,
a maker's stamp on the lintel dated eight years ago. Pip stands in the street behind him with her mouth
open and nothing to say for the first time in her life.

**Ninth speaks.** Zeph, hoarse, holding one long note with his palm flat on the barracks wall. The
hall-guard's blue eye-lights swivel to him — the first time in a thousand years anything in there has
looked at a living person. The halberd comes down. Not across the door. To the floor.

**The threshold.** The chamber mouth, cold light coming up out of it. Lyra lifts the sun medallion over
her head and presses it into Bron's open gauntlet, closing his fingers on it with both hands. At the top
of the stair, Idris Fenn holds her clipboard to her chest and watches.

## 10. Risks

- **The implanted-past twist is the most recognisable beat in the genre.** Players will name it in Act II
  even though the mechanism differs. Everything rests on Brask being a real, specific old man for six
  chapters, so the reveal plays as grief for him rather than a rug-pull.
- **Sohvi is a well-worn shape** — the wronged radical with a correct diagnosis and a monstrous cure — and
  one person staying behind to hold the machine forever is the default JRPG ending. They work only if her
  scenes stay quiet and Lyra's choice is planted by chapter 4, not offered in the last hour.
- **Four peoples, a company, a guild and a thousand years of backstory is a lot of nouns** for twenty hours
  told in 120-character boxes. The Tannik and the treaty history are the first things that get compressed
  to one town and lose their dignity, which would gut the angle. Ninth is a scene-eater too: a witness who
  can answer any question flattens Acts IV and V unless kept mostly silent.
