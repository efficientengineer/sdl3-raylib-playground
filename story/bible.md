# STORY BIBLE — *The Fair Copy*

Everything later writers need. If this file and a pitch disagree, this file wins. If this file
and scene 003 disagree, scene 003 wins and this file is wrong; report it.

Dates are given in **A.S.** — After Seal, counted from the year the doors of Thurn were shut.
The present is **1107 A.S.** The game runs from the 2nd of Turn to the 9th of Fallow, 1107.
The year has eight months of forty-five days: **Thaw, Turn, Green, High, Dust, Fallow, Rime, Deep.**

---

## 1. Title candidates

1. **The Fair Copy** — the company's file name for Maren Ostry, the only complete record of a town.
   "Fair copy" also means a clean final draft made from a messy original, which is what Bron's
   childhood is. Two meanings, both load-bearing, both cold.
2. **Ask No Questions** — the line on the chapter-one contract, and the Clement Works' whole ethic in
   three words. Blunt, memorable, and it makes the player complicit from the title screen.
3. **Sallow** — the Basin, the colour of a failing light, and the surname a clerk gave to four hundred
   people who could not object. One word. The joke at the centre of Bron's papers.
4. **The Last Warm Night** — the autumn fair that moves earlier every year, the thing the Works
   genuinely sells, and the threat under everything. Warm and elegiac; slightly soft.
5. **Everything But the Names** — what the survivors were left with, what the ledger wall holds, and
   what the chant at the end preserves. Beautiful, but long for a logo.

**Recommendation: *The Fair Copy*.** It puts Maren at the centre where she belongs, it reads as a
filing term until hour ten and then as an accusation, and it collides with nothing.

## 2. Premise and themes

Fourteen years ago the town of Tellwater went blank in a single night, and the company that did it
rebuilt the place with hired employees and perfect paperwork. One of the two people who walked out of
that night leads your party, and the warm childhood he remembers was issued to him out of a drawer.

**What the game is about underneath.** Not memory. *Records.* Who keeps them, who can afford to, and
what happens to a person whose account of themselves is held by somebody else's filing clerk. The
Clement Works does not do evil by being cruel; it does evil by being **orderly**. Nobody in this game
decides to ruin four hundred lives. A season order is signed in the spring, a duty clerk quotes it at
22:02 on an autumn night, and a resettlement instrument is drafted in two days by a woman who is
proud of how good it is. The horror is that the paperwork is correct.

Against that: the small technologies of ordinary people. A guild ledger written by hand since 1053.
A liturgy of fifteen questions that outlived the machine it was written for. Eleven hours of Vess
chant. Telling somebody something out loud. The game's argument — never stated by any character — is
that the only thing that has ever reliably preserved a person is other people agreeing to remember
them, and that this is fragile, slow, unauditable, and the best we have.

**The three things it must make the player feel.**

1. **Warmth first, dread after.** The party must be genuinely good company for ten hours. The banter
   is not decoration; the banter is the thing at risk. If Acts I and II are only setup, the whole
   design fails.
2. **Complicity.** The player takes the contract. The player is offered the map, the wage, the child's
   debt, the frame that would quiet the screaming man. At least four times the game hands them a
   reasonable thing to do and lets them notice afterward what they did.
3. **Grief that does not resolve.** Nothing is undone. No town is restored, no dead return, the
   company does not fall. What the player gets instead is people carrying things on purpose, out
   loud, where others can hear them.

**Evil is people.** Every antagonist has a reason a decent person could hold. Two of them — Ottoline
Kerrow and Orrin Cadder — are partly right in ways the party never refutes and should never be
written as refuting. The Seam is never a villain, never has intentions, and never acts.

## 3. The Sallow Basin

A dried inland sea, four hundred miles across, on the continent of **Ourn**. The old sea floor is a
white salt pan; the old shoreline is a ring of sandstone terraces two and three hundred feet high;
behind the terraces, north and east, is hill country with cut stone under it. The nights get colder
every decade. People still hold the autumn fair; they hold it earlier each year.

Nine **skywells** were raised here long before anyone counted years — towers of stacked stone rings,
three hundred feet, that hold the air warm and thick over the Basin. A running skywell shimmers
faintly at the rings and hums at a pitch you feel in your teeth rather than hear. A dead one is just
cold stone with birds in it.

### The nine skywells

- **Ossun** (Vantage). Running at full. Company-owned and metered since 1082. The one the Works
  means when it advertises "the Basin's only working skywell."
- **Meddra** (Braid). Running at about a fifth, unmetered, kept alive by Onn hand-work and Vess
  scavenged parts. Ket-Ossun calls the Works' advertisement a lie every time she hears it.
- **Caleth** (Ninefold Terrace). Died 1058. The stump is a Kell shrine; the terrace farms have been
  failing since.
- **Ilder** (the Pale Flats). Toppled in a quake around year 300. A mile of fallen rings lying on the
  salt, climbable inside. Chapter 9 happens here.
- **Semmet** (Saltmouth). Died 1061. The Works sank Extraction Bore Four through its foundation.
- **Vintry** (Windrow). Standing, dark since year 944, structurally whole. Its keeper Arro-Vintry has
  swept a dead machine for a hundred and sixty-three years. Restarts in the epilogue.
- **Dree** (Tellwater). Standing, dark since year 1039. Its root shaft reaches a shallow Seam branch,
  which is exactly why the Works put its first large bore at Tellwater. Restarts in the epilogue.
- **Kestrin** (south ravines). Collapsed 1002 and quarried for stone. The town of Emberrow is built
  out of it.
- **Sarn** (Ostry Bar). Sank into the salt in 1006. Only the cap ring shows. Onn go there.

### The regions

**The Rim Hills** (north and east) — sandstone terraces, cut-stone halls beneath, Kell terrace
villages above the sealed door-mouths. Cold, dry, poor, beautiful.
**The Flats** (centre) — the old sea floor. White crust, salt-crawler tracks, drovers, nothing.
**The Old Shoreline** (west) — the drowned Vess cities under the crust, ferry-rail lines laid on the
seabed, the last standing water at Wick.
**The South Ravines** — cut country, wind-dried air, tanneries and rope-walks.

### The sixteen settlements

**Sallowgate** (Rim Hills, human and Kell, 2,100). Sandstone terraces above the Ancient Halls' upper
works; steep streets, water cisterns on every roof, the Even Hand's frontier hall in a converted
water-house. *Problem:* the Works has bought the town's water rights and three of its four streets
are one contract away from being a company camp.

**Windrow** (Rim Hills, human, 3,400). A relay town on the tram spur: laundries, boarding houses, a
tram yard, the dark Vintry well standing over it, and the Clement Works sanatorium behind a wall.
*Problem:* everybody works for the tram or the sanatorium and nobody has ever asked who the patients
are.

**Tellwater** (Rim Hills, human, 60). A neat, well-kept town of four hundred houses with sixty people
living in it. Clean square, working well, a bakery, a fair ground, the dark Dree well. *Problem:*
every adult is an employee on a renewable three-year posting, and the town has no past, only a file.

**Ninefold Terrace** (Rim Hills, Kell, 900). Nine sealed door-mouths in one cliff face, each with a
doorward household living directly above it, terraced barley fields dying in steps down the slope,
the Caleth stump on the ridge. *Problem:* the farms are failing, half the young have taken company
jobs, and three of the nine doors already have company mortar in them.

**Cadder Step** (Rim Hills, Kell, 400). A terrace village turned drill camp: bunkhouses, a company
clinic, a school with glass in the windows, and a door-mouth opened under a leasing agreement.
*Problem:* it is the richest Kell village in the Basin and no other Kell village will trade with it.

**Vantage** (Rim Hills, human, 41,000). The company city, cut into the rim over a hall-mouth, six
tiers on a quarry face. *Problem:* it is warm, and the warmth is metered, and it is the only warm
place. See section 6.

**Saltmouth** (Flats, human and Kell, 5,800). A labour town at the foot of the dead Semmet well: bore
head, counting house, bunk rows, a salt-crawler depot, a company care-house. *Problem:* everyone here
works one bore, and Extraction Bore Four inverts in chapter 6.

**Lomm** (Flats/shoreline, human and Vess, 4,000). The ferry-rail junction. Freight sheds, a company
shop, a rough Even Hand chapter hall, permit offices. *Problem:* nothing moves in the Basin without a
permit and the permits are issued here.

**Ostry Bar** (Flats, mixed, 300). A salt bar around the sunken Sarn cap ring. Onn come here on foot
and stand for days. A squatter camp of the resettled has grown up against the ring. *Problem:* nobody
who lives here can tell you where they were born, and they are all perfectly content.

**Kettle** (Flats, human, 11). Three buildings and a metered cistern on the drove road. *Problem:* the
cistern is metered.

**Braid** (Old Shoreline, Vess, 7,000). Built on the terrace lip and down onto the dry seabed:
chant-halls, rail sheds, the Meddra well, and the archive halls — Vess-built, company-leased since
1067, with Vess queueing outside for reading hours. *Problem:* their own records are a company annex
and the lease runs to 1187.

**Wick** (Old Shoreline, Vess, 600). A raft hamlet on the last standing water, moved eight times in
forty years as the water fell. *Problem:* it is going to have to move again and there is nowhere left.

**Brand's Ferry** (Old Shoreline, human, 1,200). The last human town before the west shore; a
ferry-rail terminus and a Pale checkpoint. *Problem:* the checkpoint.

**Emberrow** (South Ravines, human, 9,000). A tannery town built of quarried skywell stone in a
ravine, smoke lying in the cut, rope-walks strung between the walls, a Clement Works care-house at
the top of the street. *Problem:* the tanneries are dying and the care-house is the second largest
employer.

**Dunnow** (South Ravines, human and Kell, 1,800). Stilted houses over a dry ravine, wind-dried meat,
rope. *Problem:* the ravine road is the only route south and the Pale hold the post at its head.

**Thurn** (under the Rim Hills). The hall-city the Kell built downward, sealed in year 0. Lit, swept,
warm, fed, and inhabited by three hundred and forty calm healthy people who cannot tell you their
names. Act V. See section 8.

### Routes, and the order the map opens

- **Act I (ch. 1-3), on foot.** The Rim road only: Sallowgate ↔ Windrow ↔ Tellwater, with the
  terrace path up to Ninefold Terrace visible and closed. One valley wide, deliberately.
- **End of ch. 3.** The trade roads close to the party. The only way out is down the terrace stair to
  the seabed.
- **Ch. 4, the ferry-rail pass.** A Vess rail sledge — sail and hand-crank on laid rail across the dry
  seabed. Opens Braid, Wick, Lomm, Brand's Ferry.
- **Ch. 5.** Hill paths off the company roads open Ninefold Terrace and the crossing to Saltmouth.
- **Ch. 6, the salt-crawler.** Freight crawlers on the Flats. Opens Saltmouth, Kettle, and the drove
  roads.
- **Ch. 7, forged company road permits.** Opens the company roads: Vantage Tiers One to Three,
  Emberrow, Dunnow, and a faster return to Windrow.
- **Ch. 9, a stolen survey crawler.** Opens the Pale Flats, the Ilder stump, and Ostry Bar. This is
  the party's own vehicle from here on.
- **Ch. 10.** Vantage Tier Four opens (school, theatre, staff terraces).
- **Ch. 12, a lifting-car key.** Opens Vantage Tiers Five and Six.
- **Ch. 13, the maintenance rail.** The Works' rail under the hills, linking every hall-mouth. This is
  the game's fast travel and it opens the whole map at once, including the Ninth Door approach.
- **Ch. 14-15.** Thurn, and the Choir chamber below it.

## 4. The peoples

Four peoples share the Basin. None of them is a fantasy species and none of them should ever be
written with a Western-fantasy word. They are peoples.

### Humans

**Look.** The Basin baseline: brown, tan, and pale skins, hair in ordinary blacks, browns, blondes
and greys, round-topped ears, whites to the eyes. What marks a human in a crowd is *cloth*: humans
layer for cold, so a human on a winter street is three garments deep where a Kell beside them is in
one. Company employees wear a grey coat with a stitched brass number at the collar.

**How they live.** They came from the coast in 812 and took the trade towns, the tram lines, the
freight and the payroll. One human in four in the Basin draws a wage from the Clement Works. They are
the most numerous and the most precarious: no inherited job, no chant-line, no door.

**What they want.** The thing they were promised. A warm winter, a wage that lasts, a pension, a
child who does better. Almost every harm in this game is done by someone protecting one of those.

**How they talk.** The baseline register. Short sentences, contractions, place-names used as
adjectives. Company employees talk in nouns and passives — *a recovery, a decommission, a
contributory, it was determined* — and the game should make the player start to flinch at that.

**Names.** Given name plus a surname taken from a trade, a tool, or a place. Foundlings in the
Works' care-houses are surnamed for the month they arrived.
*Samples:* Ansel Crewe, Ottoline Kerrow, Ivo Teach, Ollo Marrick, Anneke Brae, Neve Ardo, Nessa Tull,
Aldo Fessel, Ilsa Tremmel, Pip Rime.

**How they regard the others.** *Kell:* useful, stubborn, slightly comic, "always cold about
something." *Vess:* clever, unknowable, a bit grand, and reliably late because of the chanting.
*Onn:* not quite people, said kindly, which is worse — humans thank the Onn the way you thank a
lamp-post.

### The Kell

**Look.** Short and densely built — a Kell adult is four foot eight to five foot two and half again
as wide through the shoulders as a human of the same height. Clay-warm skin with a faint reddish
undertone. Thick hair that grows in a single metal colour — copper, brass, iron-grey, and rarely gold
— and stands up on its own; Kell hair does not lie flat and Kell find combs funny. Amber eyes that
catch lamplight like an animal's, with a visible bright ring at the pupil. Broad flat fingernails.
They run hot: skin temperature well above a human's, so a Kell in a snowfield wears no sleeves, and a
Kell in a warm room complains constantly.

**How they live.** Terrace villages cut into the cliff faces directly above the sealed door-mouths,
farming barley and beans in stepped fields watered by hand. Every household above a door is a
**doorward** household, and the inherited job — the only job that has ever mattered — is stopping
anyone digging. Households are large, loud, and hold property in common. The farms have been failing
since Caleth died.

**What they want.** The Works out of the hills. And, unadmitted, a way to feed the terraces without
it. Half their young have taken company jobs.

**How they talk.** Concrete and short. They name tools, distances, and weights instead of describing.
They say the thing, then the reason — never the reverse. They swear by doors (*shut it*, *by the
Fifth*, *door and stone*). They have no small talk and regard human small talk as a form of lying.
They never complain about cold, because they cannot feel it coming; they complain about heat
constantly. *This is a planted clue and no writer may contradict it.*

**Names.** A short hard given name, plus a hold name — the village or the door the family holds.
*Samples:* Bron, Hesk, Orrin, Dov, Bex, Tarn, Ruel, Ordo, Mave, Sesk. Full forms: Hesk Ninefold,
Orrin Cadder, Dov Ninefold, Bex Caleth, Ruel Cadder.

**How they regard the others.** *Humans:* late arrivals who bought the hills with money the hills
made. Individually fine. *Vess:* the only people who understand having sold something you can't buy
back; a wary, real sympathy. *Onn:* respected without warmth. The Onn kept working when everyone else
stopped, and the Kell shut a door on them.

### The Vess

**Look.** Tall and thin — six foot to six foot six, narrow through the shoulder. Ash-lilac skin with
a grey cast. Long backswept ears, fluted, with visible ribbing you can see lamplight through when
they turn. Eyes in one solid colour with no whites at all — violet, slate, amber-brown. Hair like
drawn wire, in cold indigos, ash-violets and silvers, worn long and often pinned with tools. Long
hands. When they speak there is a second tone humming under the voice, a fifth below, which they
cannot switch off and which goes flat when they lie.

**How they live.** On the old shoreline and out on the laid rail across the dry seabed. They keep
records by **chanting** them between bonded pairs and never on paper: a chant-line is a matrilineal
household of eight to twenty who hold one body of record between them and rehearse it daily. Paper
rots, paper burns, paper can be seized — which is what happened in 1067 anyway.

**What they want.** The archive halls back. They will not get them; the lease runs to 1187 and it is
legal. Under that: to be the people who know things again.

**How they talk.** Acknowledge before you answer — a chant habit. They repeat the key word of your
sentence back before replying. They never say "I think"; they say **"I have it as."** They correct
numbers pedantically and cannot let a wrong count stand. Formal Vess speech avoids contractions. They
count things aloud when upset.

**Names.** A given name plus the **line name**, matrilineal, inherited from the chant-line's first
bonded pair. Vess given names are often the short form of a sung name that takes several seconds to
perform properly.
*Samples:* Sefa Quill, Zeph Quill, Ilven Tessen, Cassa Tessen, Assa Lirr, Mir Lirr, Threnn Novven,
Domma Sath, Veya Ander, Oren Quill.

**How they regard the others.** *Humans:* a weather system. You do not resent weather, you plan for
it. *Kell:* the other people with an inherited job, and the only ones who came to the Concession
hearings. *Onn:* the Vess are the only people who treat the Onn as senior, because the Onn remember
the shoreline where it used to be and can be asked.

### The Onn

**Look.** Grown, not born. Matte skin in one flat colour per individual — bone-white, slate, ochre,
dull green — with no variation, no flush, no freckle. No hair anywhere. No nose: two small slits
above the mouth. Large lensed eyes with a visible iris ring that clicks audibly when it refocuses.
Small lights set in a vertical row down each temple, four to a side, which dim one by one as the
individual's reserve runs down and do not come back. Five foot six to five foot ten, narrow, the same
build regardless of age. They do not change appearance across a thousand years except that the lights
go out.

**How they live.** As skywell keepers. They were grown to maintain the array and they have not
stopped, a thousand years after the last person who could give them an order. They sleep four hours
in eleven days. They eat little and share quarters at the well heads. Six hundred and eleven remain.
There is no way to make more: the cradle that grew the last muster is behind Thurn's doors, and the
builders broke it before they sealed them.

**What they want.** The doors opened. Specifically the hall-mouth at Thurn, where the maintenance
specification for the skywells is stored — and, privately, where the cradle is. They have been asking
politely for a thousand years.

**How they talk.** Literal, exact, unrounded. They give real numbers and refuse approximations
("about ten" gets corrected to "eleven"). They say **"correct"** and **"not correct"** where others
say yes and no. They do not use metaphor unless quoting someone. They announce their own state
without embarrassment: *"I am at four lights."* Their pauses are the iris clicking as they refocus,
and a dialogue writer should use a short beat rather than an ellipsis. They are extremely funny by
accident and know it, and lean into it.

**Names.** A personal syllable-name, hyphenated to the well of their first post. The post name never
changes, even after the well dies, even after they leave the service.
*Samples:* Ket-Ossun, Tovin-Caleth, Sem-Dree, Arro-Vintry, Ol-Semmet, Dur-Sarn, Ess-Ilder,
Han-Meddra, Pel-Ossun, Vay-Kestrin.

**How they regard the others.** *Humans:* the current occupants. Polite, patient, and quietly
appalled at the metering. *Kell:* the people who shut the door. Ket is civil to Hesk for nine
chapters and it costs her. *Vess:* colleagues. The only people who ask the Onn questions and write
down the answers.

## 5. The Seam and its rules

### What it is

Two thousand feet under the Rim Hills, running for hundreds of miles, there is a lattice of grown
crystal-metal filament. It is the **pattern-store** of the machinery that made Ourn livable: the rule
sets for weather, seed stock, soil chemistry, water tables, and the skywell array itself. It also
holds people, because the cheapest way to store a rule set was to store the person who knew it.

It sings because it is rehearsing. A stored pattern has to be run to stay coherent, so the Seam runs
everything in it, endlessly, in parallel. Stand near an exposed face for an hour and you remember
things that are not yours: a road you never walked, a hand you never held, the taste of a fruit that
has not grown on Ourn in three thousand years. It is not talking to you. It is not aware of you.

**The Seam is never a villain and never has intentions. This is not negotiable.**

### The vocabulary

**A strand.** A drawn length of pattern, spun into a glass thread the length of a finger and kept in
a paper sleeve. One strand holds roughly one continuous experience of up to about an hour. Sold at
any company shop, in sleeves printed with the season's colour.

**Setting.** Writing a strand into a person, using a **setting frame** — a brass yoke that closes at
the temples. Four minutes. Legal since 1052 as a medical treatment; used on the bereaved, the
sleepless, and difficult workforces. From the inside, a set memory is indistinguishable from a lived
one.

**Drawing.** Pulling pattern out — of the Seam, or (the same operation at shorter reach) out of a
living person. Drawing from a person leaves a **gap** whose edges close behind it. Nobody knows they
have been robbed.

**A bore.** A shaft driven to a Seam branch with a **draw head** at the bottom. Small bores are
tolerable. Large bores are where the money is.

**A bore inversion.** When the local branch's pattern density falls below the draw head's rating, the
field reverses: instead of drawing out of the Seam, it draws out of every living thing inside the
radius and dumps it into the Seam. It takes about nine seconds. Nobody dies. Everybody inside goes
blank where they stand.

**Saturation.** The opposite dose, taken by standing at the draw head itself — the node — while a
field inverts around you. You receive everything it takes. One person in the world is saturated.

**The dead guards.** Not undead, not sorcery, not animate corpses. The hall-guard corps of Thurn
recited a standing oath four times a day for years, and the Seam stores by repetition, so the oath
was recorded more completely than anything else about those men. The pattern still runs, and the only
thing left holding a body shape is the armour, so it runs in the armour. They are a procedure wearing
a suit. They obey the oath's terms exactly and cannot exceed them: **bar the way, warn, do not
pursue, do not strike first, stand aside for a voice that knows the words.** That is why the guard in
scene 003 lowers its halberd instead of swinging it.

### HARD RULES — no later writer may bend these

1. The Seam has no will, no intent, no plan, and never acts. It stores and it repeats. It never
   warns, never spares, never chooses, and never speaks.
2. The Seam stores pattern, not souls. Nothing in it is alive or suffering. A stored person is a
   record. Nobody can be freed, released, or rescued from the Seam.
3. **Nothing is ever copied.** Drawing removes. A strand set into one person is gone from the Seam
   and from everyone else, forever. There is no duplication anywhere in this world, by anyone, at any
   price. Every sold strand is subtracted from somewhere.
4. Setting delivers experience, not capability. It cannot teach a skill, a language, or a way of
   fighting. Nobody ever learns to do anything from a strand.
5. **A set memory has no edges.** It begins and ends cleanly with nothing on either side. Asking what
   happened just before is the only reliable test, and it is a conversation, not a device.
6. Victims of drawing do not feel anything missing. They feel fine. Nobody ever senses a hole, aches
   for what they lost, or dreams of it.
7. Returning a memory does not return the years. It arrives whole, at full strength, with every
   year of consequence attached at once. About a third of the restored break. Restoration is a
   violence, not a cure, and must never be written as a happy ending.
8. Everything is local. Drawing, setting, inversion, and the guards all operate within a few hundred
   paces of a Seam branch or a physical strand. No visions across the Basin, no dreams sent from the
   ground, no long-range memory anything.
9. Nothing about the Seam heals a wound, stops a blade, or wins a fight. Strands are not weapons. A
   strand can flood or distract a person standing next to you. It cannot harm at a distance and
   cannot kill.
10. Only a saturated person can write without a frame, and only skin to skin, roughly a minute per
    hour given. Maren is the only saturated person alive. If a story needs a second one, the answer
    is no.
11. Spent strands go dull grey and cannot be redrawn. No recycling, no second-hand market, no backups.
12. Bore inversion cannot be engineered out. **Every large bore inverts eventually**, because drawing
    thins the local branch and thinning is what causes inversion. No clever character solves this.
13. Inversion kills nobody. There are never bodies. Every atrocity in this game leaves survivors who
    are healthy, calm, and cannot tell you their names.
14. The guards never strike first, never pursue past their post, and never speak except in the oath's
    words. They stand aside only for someone who can say the relief formula correctly. In the whole
    game that is Maren (she carries a guard's own memory), and whoever she teaches. Hesk knows one
    mangled line that will get a guard to unbar a side passage and nothing more.
15. The Onn are people, grown to a specification. They cannot be repaired, reprogrammed, overridden,
    hacked, shut down, or mass-produced. Nobody ever tries and succeeds.
16. Nothing is undone at the end. No town is restored, no dead return, the Works is not destroyed,
    and Bron never gets a real childhood.

### How each of these looks on screen

- **The Seam face:** a wall of packed hair-fine filament, pale gold going white where it is dense,
  lying in strata like grain in wood. It brightens very slightly and slowly, in bands, always
  travelling the same direction. Sound: a chord you feel in the jaw.
- **A strand:** a thread of pale glass in a paper sleeve, with a slow ripple travelling its length.
  Warm in the hand. Dull flat grey when spent.
- **Drawing:** the draw head is a brass bell on a gimbal at the bottom of a shaft. Running, it throws
  no light; the air around it goes visibly *still* — dust stops moving, a flame stands straight.
- **Setting:** a brass yoke at the temples, four minutes, the recipient's pupils stop tracking. They
  come out mildly drunk and very pleased with everyone.
- **Inversion:** no explosion. The stillness inverts outward from the bore in a visible ring across
  dust and grass, and everything it passes stops. Draw this as a wide shot with no violence in it.
- **A blank person:** upright, breathing, warm, looking at you with mild friendly interest. Not slack,
  not staring. This is the most important visual in the game and it must never look like a zombie.
- **Saturation (Maren):** normal, until you are close. Then: her hands make other people's gestures a
  half-second before she speaks, her accent moves, and her voice occasionally arrives doubled.
- **A hall-guard:** old segmented armour over nothing, a tattered red cloak, two small steady blue
  points in the helm. Perfectly still. No sound but the armour.

**Design note for the systems writer.** Strands are a consumable item class: single-use, permanently
consumed, never craftable, obtained mostly by purchase from the Works. Spending one in battle should
cost the player something they can feel.

### The Tally — the fifteen questions

*Added by the story editor per DECISIONS D8.1. These are frozen. `story/canon.md` section 1 is the
copy scene writers quote; this is the same text. Question 7 is the edges test of hard rule 5, and
question 15 is the only one that cannot be answered from a record, which is why the maintenance
manual leaves it blank with the instruction "ask the holder".*

1. Do you know where you are?
2. Do you know who is holding your hand?
3. What is your name?
4. What name did your mother use?
5. What did you eat last?
6. What was the weather?
7. What happened the day before this one?
8. What did you do that you would do again?
9. Who is owed something?
10. Who owes you?
11. What is unfinished?
12. What should be told, and who to?
13. Is there anything you would rather nobody knew?
14. Is there anything you want said out loud now, while there are people?
15. What do you want done?

The sister never asks what the answer to 13 is. She asks whether there is one.

### Two clarifications of the hard rules

*Added by the story editor. Neither bends a hard rule; both close a gap writers were filling by
guesswork.*

**Rehearsal.** A stored pattern has to be rehearsed to stay coherent — that is already why the Seam
sings (above). It follows that a saturated person holds what she holds by continuously rehearsing it
herself, which is what the crowding and the voices are. When a Vess chant-line takes a record and
rehearses it daily, she stops having to, and it goes quiet in her and stays quiet. **Nothing is
copied** (hard rule 3): the record now lives in the line and not in her. Give away all of it and she
is empty. This is the mechanism of chapter 15 and it is the only mechanism permitted for it.

**"Taking the hour" is not a device.** It is what an Attendance sister does: be present, ask the
Tally in order, and carry it afterwards because you were there. There is no frame, no strand, no
glass, no transfer, no light, and nothing changes hands. A memory taken this way is a memory of
having been in the room, which is why it is the only thing in Bron's head he is certain is real —
and why it is not his. Any writer who adds machinery to it breaks hard rules 9 and 10.

## 6. The Clement Works

### History

Chartered in 1041 by **Hallam Clement**, a human engineer who got the Ossun skywell running again at
the end of the Hungry Winters and named the firm after himself. For two years the Works was the most
loved institution in the Basin and it has been trading on that ever since.

In 1047 a shaft cut under Vantage hit a Seam branch. The pattern that came up the shaft was not
understood for five years; by 1052 the Works had a legal framework for setting, a retail sleeve, and
a price. In 1058 Caleth died, in 1061 Semmet died, and the Works bought both valleys cheap. In 1067
the Vess council of Braid sold the archive halls, legally, for medicine and rail iron. In 1070 the
first care-houses opened under a "contributory childhood" clause. In 1093 the first large bore, at
Tellwater, ran backwards.

### How it makes money

1. **Warmth.** Every roof under Ossun pays by the night, metered at the ridge.
2. **Water.** The Ossun condensers. The Basin's only reliable supply.
3. **Strands.** Retail sleeves in every company shop — *Comfort*, one evening's wage for someone
   else's good afternoon — and wholesale supply to sanatoria, mine offices, and three town councils
   with difficult workforces.
4. **Land.** It buys dead ground cheap after a well fails, and it is very good at knowing when.
5. **Carriage.** The tram spur, the ferry-rail permits, and the salt-crawler freight monopoly.

### Vantage, tier by tier

Cut into the rim over a hall-mouth, six tiers on a quarry face, three hundred feet top to bottom.
Stairs for everyone; **lifting cars** on cable for staff with a tier pass.

- **Tier Six — the Cap.** The board floor, the chairman's house, the Ossun head. Gardens that are
  warm at night. Forty people live here.
- **Tier Five — the Long Office.** Extraction, Accounts, and the Registry. Where the paperwork lives.
  Four thousand clerks, and the best-lit rooms in the Basin.
- **Tier Four — the Terraces.** Senior staff housing, the school, the theatre, the hospital.
- **Tier Three — the Market Shelf.** Shops, the strand counters, the fair ground, the good bars.
- **Tier Two — the Yards.** Workshops, the crawler depot, the Pale barracks, and the Even Hand's
  founding hall, which the Works has been trying to lease out from under for six years.
- **Tier One — the Mouth.** The hall-mouth itself, capped with the **Long Bore** head. Bunk rows, the
  care-house, the worst air in the Basin, and the only tier where you can hear the Seam.

### The Pale

Company police, about nine hundred in the Basin, named for the pale grey coats. Wardens, sergeants,
constables. They do evictions, meter enforcement, escort, site security, and **recovery** — the
retrieval of company assets, a category that includes strays, debtors, and blanks. They are not
soldiers and are not written as thugs: they are underpaid men and women doing a job most of them
think is necessary, and about one in six of them is ashamed.

### The care-houses

Five: Vantage Tier One, Emberrow, Windrow, Lomm, Saltmouth. They take orphans and the children of
debtors. They are clean, warm, and well-fed — better than most homes in the Basin — and the children
are schooled and genuinely happy, which is the point, because happiness is the crop. Twice a year,
under the contributory clause on the intake papers, a child's best days are drawn. They leave at
sixteen cheerful, incurious, employable, and unable to tell you about a single birthday.

**Ottoline Kerrow came out of Emberrow. So did Pip.**

### What ordinary employees believe

- Strands come from paid donors: old people selling a good afternoon so their grandchildren eat.
- The Seam is a power source that keeps the air alive.
- Tellwater was a flood and a subsidence and a relocation, and there was a memorial.
- The skywells are dying of old age and the Works is slowing it down.
- The Kell are being unreasonable about the hills.
- Warden Teach is a decent man. (They are right about this, which is the problem.)

## 7. The hunters' guild — the Even Hand

### What it is

A patronless hunters' guild, founded in Sallowgate in 1053 by four hunters who refused a mining
house's retainer and put their own board up in a water-house instead. Open to every people in the
Basin. It takes no standing money from any company, house, or council, and funds itself on a tenth of
every job. It is called the Even Hand because the tenth and the pay are the same for everyone,
whoever you are — which in 1053 was a radical act and is now simply how it is done.

### How it works

Jobs are **bills**, chalked or pinned on the board. A bill carries the work, the fee, and the
poster's seal. You take the bill down, sign the ledger, do the work, bring proof, and are paid in
full on the spot. A hunter working a job is *on the bill*. There are no ranks: there are hunters, and
there is the hall. Seniority is how many bills you have cleared, which is public on the ledger wall.

### The Four Lines

Burned into the frame of every board in the Basin:

1. **Pay on completion.** Never in advance. This is why nobody can buy a hunter.
2. **No patron.**
3. **The seal or no bill.** Every bill names its poster. An unsealed bill is not a bill.
4. **Bring them back.** You go out four, you come back four, or you come back and say why.

And a fifth, unwritten and universally understood: **"ask no questions" is a price, not a favour.**
A bill that says it pays triple, and everyone in the hall knows what they are being paid for.

### The hall at Sallowgate

A converted sandstone water-house on the second terrace. Ground floor: the board, and behind it the
**ledger wall** — every bill cleared since 1053, written out by hand, floor to ceiling, four
thousand-odd entries, with the dead marked by a bar through the line. A stove that is never let out.
Upstairs, twelve bunks that nobody pays for. A kitchen that feeds anyone with a bill out.

Other chapter halls: Lomm (rough), Emberrow (small), Braid (the only one not run by humans), and the
founding hall at Vantage Tier Two, which the Works is trying to lease out from under.

### The guild master — Tovin-Caleth, Onn, 1,248

Keeper of the Caleth skywell until it died in 1058. She came down the hill, took a bill because there
was nothing else to do, and has run the Sallowgate hall since 1073. She is at **two lights on each
side** and going down.

Flat delivery, absolutely literal, keeps a running tally out loud, refuses to round any number, and
will not let anyone call her ma'am. She has an extremely filthy sense of humour which she delivers
without any change of expression whatsoever, and the hall's great sport is watching newcomers work
out what she just said. She calls the hunters collectively "the board" — *"the board eats at six."*
She writes the ledger in her own hand and cannot be lied to about a date.

She and Ket-Ossun have not spoken in forty-nine years, because Tovin left the service. When they
finally do, in chapter 4, it lasts four lines and is one of the best scenes in the game.

**Her arc.** Ch.1: she lets Bron take a bill she knows stinks, because the hall needs the fee, and
she tells him so to his face. Ch.11: refuses to close the hall when the Pale demand it. Ch.14: posts
her own bill (below). Ch.15: the last image of the game is her chalking a new one.

### Reputation

Trusted by everyone and respected by nobody powerful. The Works finds the Even Hand useful and
irritating in equal measure; the Kell approve of it because it has doorwards on its ledger; the Vess
think the ledger wall is a charmingly primitive archive and are not wrong; the Pale regard hunters as
unlicensed and a nuisance. To ordinary people a hunter is who you call when the thing in the cistern
is nobody's job.

### Eight optional side contracts

**1. "Bad water at Kettle."** Ch.2, Kettle. Posted by Nessa Tull, waystation keeper. *Seems:* clear
whatever is fouling the cistern. *Is:* a man has been sleeping in the cistern house for nine years
because it is the only building he recognises; the fouling is him washing. He is pleasant, healthy,
and cannot say where he is from. *Lights up:* the resettled are everywhere and nobody looks.

**2. "Recover one paper sleeve."** Ch.4, Braid. Posted unsealed, so Tovin's Braid counterpart refuses
it and a hunter takes it off the floor anyway. *Seems:* a courier job. *Is:* a Vess grandmother has
spent eleven years' savings buying a retail strand of her own drowned daughter's afternoon, and wants
it before she dies. The sleeve in the party's hands is already spent, dull grey. *Lights up:* the
Works sells people their own dead, and does not always deliver.

**3. "Count the doors."** Ch.5, Ninefold Terrace. Posted by the doorward council for an insultingly
small fee, because it is not real work. *Seems:* a census of the nine sealed door-mouths. *Is:* three
of them have been opened and re-sealed with company mortar within the year. *Lights up:* the Kell's
inherited job has already failed and they are the last to know.

**4. "Quiet the school."** Ch.7, Vantage Tier Four. Posted by Idda Renn, schoolmistress. *Seems:*
something is frightening the children at night. *Is:* the lifting-car cable runs behind the dormitory
wall and resonates at the Ossun head's pitch. The children are fine. Renn has not slept in six years
and needed one person to say the noise was real. *Lights up:* the city's comfort has a sound, and one
woman can hear it.

**5. "Collection at Emberrow."** Ch.8, Emberrow. Posted by the Works' accounts office. Pays extremely
well. *Seems:* escort a debt collector. *Is:* the debt is an unpaid care-house intake fee and the
collection is a nine-year-old girl. The party can settle the debt themselves (a punishing amount of
money), refuse and lose standing with the Works' postings for the rest of the game, or do the job.
*Lights up:* the crime is legal and you are being offered a fair wage to commit it.

**6. "The crawler that came back empty."** Ch.9, Ostry Bar. Posted by the salt company. *Seems:* find
a missing crew. *Is:* they are alive, camped against the Sarn cap ring, and have stopped going home,
because one of them met Maren on the road and got his life back. They are neither grateful nor sorry
and they will not be moved. *Lights up:* restoration is a violence.

**7. "Sit with him."** Ch.12, Windrow. Posted by Ivo Teach in his own name, with his own money,
through the Quiet House. *Seems:* a bodyguard job. *Is:* an old Pale sergeant is dying, nobody will
sit the last hour with him because of what he did at Tellwater, and the Order of the Late Hour has
declined to send a sister. The party can sit it or not. Lyra is dead by now, and whoever sits it has
to do the Tally badly, from memory. *Lights up:* nobody gets to be only their worst night.

**8. "Nine hundred tallies."** Ch.14, Sallowgate. Posted by Tovin-Caleth herself. Fee: nothing.
*Seems:* the guild master wants help with the ledger wall. *Is:* she is copying fifty-four years of
entries by hand, into books, because the Works has moved to lease the hall and she intends the names
to outlast the building. *Lights up:* writing it down is the only thing that has ever worked. This is
the game's thesis, posted as a chore, for no money.

### The night — 9th of Fallow, 1093

Tellwater: 407 residents, 5 company crew. Extraction Bore One, 340 feet, sunk in the sheep pasture
east of the town on the shallow branch that Dree's root shaft reaches.

- **21:40.** The night run starts. Branch density logs high. Shift engineer **Ilsa Tremmel** enters a
  written objection and asks to stop. Chief surveyor **Maren Ostry**, twenty-two, third season, is
  already down the shaft taking readings by hand because the gauge disagrees with itself.
- **22:02.** Vantage answers. *Continue, per season order.* A duty clerk quotes Crewe's spring
  signature. **Nobody decides anything.** This message log survives and is produced in chapter 15.
- **22:31.** The field inverts. Nine seconds. Radius 1,100 feet: the pasture, the bore shed, and the
  whole town.
- **22:31.** 407 residents and 4 of the 5 crew go blank standing up. Nobody falls. Two people are
  walking down the lane; they keep walking until they stop. A kettle boils dry in a kitchen and
  nobody comes.
- **22:31.** Maren, at the draw head, at the node, takes all of it — four hundred and ten lives on top
  of her own. **The one the node does not receive is the nine-year-old standing in the bore shed
  doorway at the head of the open shaft.** What the field takes from directly over the open column
  goes down past the bell and disperses into the branch. Nobody ever works this out; the file records
  a blank child. That is Bron, and it is why he can never be restored.
- **22:34.** Tremmel is found at the winch with her hand still on the brake, calm and friendly.
- **22:40.** The relay hut two miles out loses the telegraph acknowledgment and sends a rider.
- **23:55.** The rider reaches Windrow.
- **01:20.** Sergeant **Ivo Teach**, forty-one, arrives with eleven constables expecting a fire.

  when told, so the constables carry them. Teach carries a nine-year-old four hundred yards from the
  bore shed doorway and puts him in a cart. That is Bron.
- **03:10.** Maren is brought up alive and speaking in voices that are not hers. The winch man logs
  *subject vocal.*
- **06:00.** Vantage is told. Ottoline Kerrow, twenty-one, a junior clerk in Extraction, takes the

- **Day 60.** Tellwater is restaffed: sixty employees on renewable three-year postings, a full roster
  of trades, and a new sign.
- **Bron, the exception.** A nine-year-old cannot be posted to a job, and a blank child in a
  care-house would have to be explained to a care-house. The cheapest disposal available is to set
  him and return him as a local child. He receives **stock template 4** (a mother called Hesta, a
  kitchen, a well, an autumn fair) plus **two live sleeves** drawn from the only saturated source on
  site — Maren — to give the template warmth and specific detail. He is signed for by clerk **A.
  Fessel**. Recorded cost: four marks, two sleeves. A woman named **Neve Ardo** is posted to play
  Hesta Sallow, widow, one son, and plays her for four years.

### Where the four hundred are now, fourteen years on

Nineteen dead of ordinary causes. Three walked into the salt. Eighty-one found and restored by Maren
in the last two years, of whom twenty-seven are in care or worse. The rest are alive, employed,
married in some cases, and content, living lives they did not choose and cannot miss.

## 9. The party

Eight playable characters. Four in a fight. Bron and Lyra's designs are **fixed** and approved; Zeph
and Pip may be adjusted to fit their peoples; the rest are new.

### Bron — Kell, 23, hunter, axe, party leader. Ch.1-15.

**Look (fixed, do not change).** Short, compact, broad-shouldered, early twenties, clean-shaven, big
spiky copper-red hair swept back under a white headband with long trailing ends and a small red cross
at its centre, sharp amber eyes, sleeveless burnt-orange high-collared jacket open over a black
high-necked bodysuit, one large rounded steel shoulder plate on the left, oversized segmented steel
gauntlets, white cuffed boots, a big axe on his back.

**Voice.** Short sentences. Concrete nouns. States the thing, then the reason. No irony, no hedging,
and no idea how funny he is. Distrusts anything he cannot put his hands on, which by chapter ten
includes himself.
- "Then something came out."
- "I've seen the plate. It's dated. That's not an argument, that's a date."
- "I don't need to like her. I need to know where she went."
- "Say the rest of it."
- "It's mine because I carried it. That's what having something is."

**Want.** A clean job, a fair wage, and the four of them coming back. Later: one fact about himself
he can verify.
**Wound.** He is a happy man with no floor under him, and the thing he is proudest of — that he only
believes what he can see — is the one thing that was done to him.
**Arc.** Ch.1-5: the reliable one, the party's ground truth. Ch.6-9: the ground gives. Ch.10: he
learns his own childhood was not taken but **erased** — he was in the radius, not at the node, so
nothing of his was ever stored and there is nothing to give back, ever. Ch.11: he takes Lyra's last
hour knowingly, with her consent, and it is the first memory in his head he is certain is real, and
it is not his. Ch.12-14: he stops asking whose memories are whose and starts asking what he is going
to do today. Ch.15: he keeps the borrowed childhood on purpose, out loud, and recites Lyra's fifteen
questions to Maren.
**What he is for.** He is the player's floor, so that when the floor goes the player falls too.

### Lyra — human, 26, Attendance sister of the Order of the Late Hour. Ch.1-11. Dies ch.11.

**Look (fixed, do not change).** Tall, slender, very long straight pale-gold hair with long centre

**Voice.** Complete, unhurried sentences. Says the frightening thing plainly and quietly. Asks one
question too many. Comic register: extremely dry, and she never signals that she is joking.
- "It isn't guarding the door from us. It's still guarding us from the door."
- "You don't have to be calm. You have to be here."
- "I've sat two hundred of these. Nobody has ever had anything clever to say."
- "I'd like to stop arguing and go in. I'd like that to be because we decided."

**Want.** To be sure the hour she sits with people is worth something.
**Wound.** She carries two hundred deathbed hours and has nowhere for her own to go, and the Order
has no rite for a sister who dies alone.
**Arc.** Ch.1-4: the conscience, and secretly the doubter. Ch.5: the Sealed Stair inscription matches

### Zeph — Vess, 24, resonance reader. Ch.1-15.

### Ket-Ossun — Onn, 1,261, skywell keeper. Joins ch.4. Leaves ch.15.

## 10. Maren Ostry

**Who she was.** A survey clerk out of Ostry Bar, twenty-two, third season with the Clement Works,
good at the work and liked. On the 9th of Fallow, 1093, she was 340 feet down Bore One taking density
readings by hand because the gauge disagreed with itself. She was standing at the draw head when the
field inverted.

**What she is.** Saturated. Where the town was emptied, she was filled: **four hundred and ten other
lives, and her own — four hundred and eleven records in all.** She holds **nothing of Bron's own
past.** He was in the radius but standing at the head of the open shaft with the draw head three
hundred and forty feet directly below him, not at the bell, and what the field took from over the
open column went down past her and dispersed into the branch. The two hours he carries were never
his: they are **her** ninth winter, drawn out of her in 1093 and set into him to give a stock template
warmth. Nobody has ever worked this out and nobody in the game says it in these words; the file
records a blank child and a cheap disposal. *(Editor's ruling, DECISIONS D8.2; section 16 decision 4
governs. If Maren held Bron, chapter 10 has no reveal and Act V has no argument.)* The Works held her for twelve years at the Windrow sanatorium under the file name **the
Fair Copy**, because she is the only complete record of Tellwater in existence and they were not
going to destroy a record. She was not tortured. She was catalogued. In 1105 she walked out through
an unlocked door, which nobody had thought to lock, because in twelve years she had never asked to
leave.

**Look.** Thirty-six, human, middle height, lean from walking. Dark hair going grey early, cut short
by her own hand. A weathered brown face. A long grey travelling coat, a canvas satchel, boots that
have been resoled. Ungloved hands, always — she cannot wear gloves and do her work. She looks like a
surveyor who has been on the road for two years, because she is.

**How she talks.** She is **crowded, not mad.** Write her as a tired, courteous, entirely sane woman
with four hundred people trying to use her mouth. The habits:
- Her accent moves mid-sentence, between Basin towns.
- Her hands make gestures a half-beat before she speaks, and they are not always her gestures.
- She refers to strangers' families correctly and without triumph.
- She occasionally answers a question that was asked an hour ago by someone else.
- Under pressure her voice arrives doubled. At the very end, quadrupled.
- She never gloats, never monologues, and never raises her voice. She apologises a lot.

**Sample lines.**
- "Sit down. You've been standing since Windrow and your left boot's wrong."
- "I'm sorry. Someone in here knew your mother and they're very pleased to see you."
- "It isn't mine to keep. That's the whole argument. That's all of the argument."
- "You got lost in the drifts above the fold. You were nine. You weren't cold, you were *bored*, and
  then you were frightened, and then you were carried."
- "No. I'm going to give it back. All of it. To everyone. And then I'd like to go to sleep."

**What she does, act by act.**
- **Act I.** Offstage. A rumour: a surveyor near Windrow who came back blank talks about "the woman
  who touched him."
- **Act II.** A standing company recovery notice — *the Fair Copy; do not converse* — is found at
  Braid in ch.4. In ch.6 she is glimpsed once at Saltmouth, through the dust after the inversion,
  walking **toward** the bore, because she wanted to know the radius.
- **Act III.** Ch.8: seen and spoken to, in Emberrow, where she restores Ross Kettle and it destroys
  him, and she stays with him afterward for a day and a half, which is more than the Works ever did.
  Ch.9: cornered at the Ilder stump. She does not fight. She sits on a fallen ring with her hands
  open and tells Bron about the winter he got lost, in the first person, with the timing right.
- **Act IV.** She works ahead of the party toward the hills, and in ch.13 she reaches the inner doors
  and the guards stand aside, because she carries an oath-keeper's own memory and can say the relief
  formula. In ch.13 she states her plan plainly and it is not insane: write Tellwater's night into
  every person in the Basin at once, so that the truth cannot be filed away. She calls it testimony.
  Because nothing in this world is ever copied, it would be **divided** — a hundred thousand
  fragments — and it would empty her and blank nobody, but it would also destroy the record forever
  and put four hundred people's worst night into a hundred thousand heads in pieces they cannot
  assemble. She has not thought this part through. She has not been able to think anything through
  for fourteen years.
- **Act V.** At the Choir chamber she loses coherence and speaks in four hundred frightened voices at
  once. The fight is reaching the woman inside the chorus, not defeating her. Bron holds her together
  by reciting the Order's fifteen questions — Lyra's Tally — which is, and always was, a
  strand-integrity check. Held together, she chooses instead to give the record whole, once, to the
  Braid chant-lines, into chant, where it cannot be audited, seized or burned. It takes eleven hours
  and it empties her.

**How she ends.** She lives. She remembers nothing at all of Tellwater, or of the sanatorium, or of
the last two years, and for the first time in fourteen years she is **not crowded**. She takes a room
in Sallowgate, above the guild hall, and does small survey work for the Even Hand. Bron visits. They
like each other and neither of them can say what happened on the 9th of Fallow, 1093. Eleven hours of
chant in Braid can.

- **Arro-Vintry** — Onn, keeper of the dark Vintry well above Windrow, which has been dead for a

## 12. The fifteen chapters

Five acts. **Acts I and II must satisfy on their own**: a complete small story about four hunters who
took a bad job, found out who hired them, discovered the town one of them came from is a stage set,
and were run out of the Basin's front door for it.

### Act I — The Contract (ch. 1-3). On foot, one valley wide.

#### Chapter 1 — "Clear the Entrance"
**Hub:** Sallowgate. **Dungeon:** the Ancient Halls, upper works. **Boss:** the Broken Post — two
hall-guard patterns in ruined armour whose post no longer exists, so they bar a corridor that is not
there and strike anything that moves. Tragic, not evil.
**Wants:** the fee. The hall needs the posting tenth and Bron needs a bill that pays. Tovin-Caleth
tells him to his face that the bill stinks, and lets him take it: *clear the entrance, ask no
questions*, triple rate, seal burned off.
**Goes wrong:** the first great door is already open, and it was opened **from the inside** (scene
001). A hall-guard bars the way beyond it and warns them off instead of attacking (scenes 002, 003,
003b). And when they finally get through the door they were hired to clear, what is behind it is a
canvas company tent, a folding table, a lamp, and a duty clerk who looks up from his clipboard with
mild annoyance and asks whether they have an entry chit. They were the fuse. Somebody paid four
hunters to trip the old wards so a survey crew would not have to.
**Learn:** somebody with money knew exactly what was down here. The dead do not attack. The halls
were sealed from the inside.
**Turn:** the anticlimax. The monster at the end of the dungeon is paperwork, and it is worse.
**Party:** Bron, Lyra, Zeph, Pip.
**Opens:** Sallowgate, the Rim road to Windrow.
**Panels:** the board and the burned seal; scene 001; scene 002; scene 003 (approved art, frozen);
the tent behind the door; the walk out at dawn. **Talk:** 003b; the hall stove; Tovin's tally.

#### Chapter 2 — "The Burned Seal"
**Hub:** Windrow. **Dungeons:** the hill relay station, then the company survey cut above it.
**Boss:** a cutting rig and a crew chief who will not stop work for anybody.
**Wants:** the name under the burned seal. The under-impression is still legible.
**Goes wrong:** they find him, and he is delightful. Field Superintendent Orrin Cadder buys them
dinner, admits everything cheerfully, pays the bill in full plus damages for the door, and lets them
walk — because four hunters looking at the Works is four hunters not looking properly. Also in
Windrow: a surveyor sitting on a bench who is perfectly pleasant and cannot say where he slept last
week, and a rumour about "the woman who touched him."
**Learn:** the Clement Works has been under the hills for sixty years. The relay station holds a wax
cylinder of a hall-guard's oath, and the words are identical every time — it is a recording, not a
ghost.
**Turn:** the villain is funny, pays his debts, and is one of Bron's own people.
**Side contract:** Bad water at Kettle.
**Opens:** the Tellwater road.
**Panels:** Cadder's entrance (he is singing); the blank surveyor on the bench; the wax cylinder.
**Talk:** the dinner — the best banter scene in Act I, and the player should like him.

#### Chapter 3 — "The Welcome"
**Hub:** Tellwater. **Dungeon:** the drowned bore. **Boss:** the Sump Head — flood gear and a
pattern-storm of spent strands churning in the water.
**Wants:** to see the place Bron is from, and to ask the town about the survey crews.
**Goes wrong:** every face in the square turns to him and smiles, and gets him slightly wrong. The
baker grips his shoulder with a flour-white hand and calls him "Hesta's boy" one degree too well.
Somebody calls him a nickname he has never had. In the drowned bore they find a maker's plate dated
1093 — the year of the flood that the flood was supposed to explain. Then the Pale arrive in force.
Warden Teach looks at Bron for three seconds and steps aside, and nobody understands why.
**Learn:** the flood was made.
**Turn:** the welcome. Warm, and wrong, and nobody will say what is wrong about it.
**Party:** unchanged. They leave as fugitives with the trade roads closed.
**Opens:** nothing. It closes things. The only way out is down the terrace stair.
**Panels:** the welcome (wide of the square, every face turned to him; insert on the floury hand;
portrait of Bron working hard to look pleased); the maker's plate; the Pale on the ridge; Teach
stepping aside. **Talk:** the party arguing about whether a town can be wrong; Sem-Dree at the dark
well, who answers a question they do not ask.

### Act II — Off the Roads (ch. 4-6). Ferry-rail, then salt-crawler.

#### Chapter 4 — "Annex"
**Hub:** Braid. **Dungeon:** the flooded archive-hall. **Boss:** the Sleeve Vault — an automated
retrieval gantry, plus annex guards who are only doing overtime.
**Wants:** a ferry-rail pass, and a name for the thing under the hills.
**Goes wrong:** the Vess archive is a leased company annex and the Vess queue outside it for reading
hours. Zeph's line name is on the lease. Ket-Ossun is at the Meddra well head arguing with a clerk
about a part she needs and has been promised for eleven years.
**Learn:** the Seam is a pattern-store, not a power source. **A set memory has no edges** — the
archivists' standard test, and the tool the player will use for six chapters. And a standing company
recovery notice: *the Fair Copy. Do not converse.*
**Party:** Ket-Ossun joins for passage.
**Side contract:** Recover one paper sleeve.
**Opens:** ferry-rail — Braid, Wick, Lomm, Brand's Ferry.
**Panels:** Braid on the dry seabed with the rails running out to the horizon; Ket in the well head;
the recovery notice. **Talk:** Tovin and Ket's four-line reunion after forty-nine years; Zeph
demonstrating the edges test on a stranger, for fun, and it working.

#### Chapter 5 — "Nine Doors"
**Hub:** Ninefold Terrace. **Dungeon:** the Sealed Stair, behind the Fifth Door. **Boss:** the
Standing Post — one complete hall-guard barring the stair correctly. You cannot kill it and cannot
talk past it; you exhaust it, and when you come back up it has resumed its post. That is the point.
**Wants:** somebody who knows what the guards are, and a way under the hills off the company roads.
**Goes wrong:** Cadder is here for a negotiation. The village will not look at him. Hesk spits on his
boots and he takes it and finishes the meeting, and gets most of what he came for.
**Learn:** the oath, the recording, and Thurn — why the doors were sealed from the inside, told the
way the Kell tell it. And: Kell children are taken to the cold on purpose at six, because Kell cannot
feel cold coming. Bron says he remembers shivering, and Hesk goes quiet.
**Party:** Hesk joins, furious that they opened a door.
**Side contract:** Count the doors.
**Opens:** the hill paths, and the crossing to Saltmouth.
**Panels:** the Sealed Stair inscription — the player can read that it is Lyra's liturgy, and Lyra
cannot; Hesk's refusal; Bron and the cold. **Talk:** the doorward council; Dov Ninefold's dinner-table
argument with his aunt.

#### Chapter 6 — "Bore Four"
**Hub:** Saltmouth. **Dungeon:** Extraction Bore Four. **Boss:** the Draw Head — a runaway winch and
gimbal that must be shut down and cannot be.
**Wants:** the Tellwater payroll, which the Saltmouth counting house holds.
**Goes wrong:** Bore Four inverts with them inside the radius. The stillness goes out across the salt
in a visible ring and forty people stop, including a hunter they know. Ottoline Kerrow is on site and
stays in the dust for eleven hours running the count herself. Cadder clears the north gallery with
his own hands and is the reason it was forty and not two hundred. A woman in a grey coat is glimpsed
once, walking **toward** the bore.
**Learn:** the payroll. Every adult in Tellwater is an employee on a renewable three-year posting —
including *Ardo, N. — role: Hesta Sallow, widow, one son — posting closed 1097.*
**Turn:** Lyra asks Bron to describe his mother's face, and what comes out of him is a role
description.
**Opens:** the salt-crawler freight routes, Kettle, the drove roads.
**Panels:** the inversion (wide, no violence in it, dust stopping); Kerrow in the dust with a tally
board; the payroll page; Bron's face in the counting house. **Talk:** Tibb Orsk trying to have a
conversation with Sela afterward. This ends Act II, and it should feel like a complete story that has
just become a much larger one.

### Act III — The Fair Copy (ch. 7-10). Company roads, then a stolen crawler.

#### Chapter 7 — "The Registry"
**Hub:** Vantage, Tiers One to Three. **Dungeon:** the Registry stacks on Tier Five. **Boss:** a Pale
recovery detail under Teach — a fighting retreat, or a clean win if the party has done side work.
**Wants:** to find out where Tellwater's four hundred went.
**Goes wrong:** Registrar Sefa Quill sells them the map for a fee and out of spite, and the fee is
not only money: she wants her son to say her name out loud, in front of people, and she makes him.
**Learn:** the resettlement is a map, and the map is beautiful work. The Registry lists **sixty-one**
adults across the Basin who remember a mother named Hesta. The player now knows Bron was set. Bron
does not accept it.
**Opens:** the company roads — Vantage Tiers One to Three, Emberrow, Dunnow, Kettle, Windrow.
**Side contract:** Quiet the school.
**Panels:** Vantage from below, six tiers of warm windows; Sefa and Zeph across a desk; the sixty-one
names; Crewe opening the fair on Tier Three and being kind to a child. **Talk:** the party in a
Tier Three bar with money for the first time, and nobody enjoying it.

#### Chapter 8 — "Give It Back"
**Hub:** Emberrow. **Dungeon:** the tannery undercroft. **Boss:** Recovery Chief Ovey and two
frame-bearers, who have come to set Ross Kettle quiet again — and setting him *would* stop the
screaming, and the party has to decide.
**Wants:** Maren, before the Works gets her.
**Goes wrong:** they find her, and she does exactly what she says she does. Ross Kettle, fifty-eight,
a tanner with a wife and a business, gets his life back in one piece with fourteen years of
consequence attached, and it destroys him in front of them. Dalla Kettle is standing in the doorway.
**Learn:** she gives it back, she does not make it up. Restoration is a violence, not a cure.
**Side contract:** Collection at Emberrow.
**Panels:** Maren's full reveal in the undercroft; the restoration (hands, not faces); Ross in the
street; Dalla in the doorway. **Talk:** Maren and Lyra, four lines, in which each recognises what the
other does for a living.

#### Chapter 9 — "The Recitation"
**Hub:** the Pale Flats, from a stolen survey crawler. **Dungeon:** the Ilder stump — a mile of
toppled skywell rings lying on the salt, entered and climbed from the inside. **Boss:** the sweep —
Cadder and Teach's joint cordon. A fight you end by getting out, not by winning.
**Wants:** Maren, and answers.
**Goes wrong:** they corner her and she does not fight. She sits down on a fallen ring with her hands
open and empty and tells Bron about the winter he got lost — in the first person, with the timing
right, with the detail he has never told anybody because it was too small to tell.
**Learn:** the warm, specific parts of Bron's childhood are hers. Separately: at Ostry Bar, Ket reads
the Order's fifteen questions aloud off her own thousand-year-old maintenance manual and stops
halfway through, appalled, with Lyra standing next to her. And Ilsa Tremmel, the blank who asks
questions, has been writing answers in a book for eleven years.
**Turn:** the recitation. Bron's hands stay up. His face stops.
**Opens:** the Pale Flats, Ostry Bar, and the crawler, which is the party's own from here.
**Side contract:** The crawler that came back empty.
**Panels:** the recitation (Maren on the stump, hands open; eyes-slit on Bron; his axe still up); the
stump from outside at dawn; Ket with the manual. **Talk:** Lyra and Ket afterward, which is the
quietest and most important conversation in Act III.

#### Chapter 10 — "Intake"
**Hub:** Windrow. **Dungeon:** the Clement Works sanatorium — file room, wards, and the room where
Maren was kept for twelve years. **Boss:** the holding frame, a setting frame the size of a room,
with Doctor Marsh's ward staff around it. The boss is the room.
**Wants:** Maren's file, to understand what she is.
**Goes wrong:** they find Bron's. *Age nine. Condition: blank. Disposition: set and returned.
Template 4, two sleeves, source O. Four marks.* And Sefa Quill had the file pulled before they
arrived and left it on the desk on purpose.
**Learn:** the worst thing in the game. Bron was in the **radius**, not at the node. Nothing of his
was ever stored anywhere. His childhood was not taken — it was erased, and there is no copy, and
there never will be. Maren can give four hundred people their lives back and she cannot give him one
hour. Also here: a copy of Crewe's list, in his own hand, with *Brae, A. — Windrow — laundry.*
**Also:** Pip's drawn days are in the sanatorium's holding stock. She is offered them. She says no,
and the game does not argue with her.
**Turn:** the tea. A laundry doorway in Windrow, steam, a middle-aged woman holding out a chipped cup
and smiling at a stranger. He does not take it and he does not tell her.
**Opens:** Vantage Tier Four.
**Panels:** the file room; the intake form (object insert, and the player reads it before Bron does);
the holding frame; the tea. **Talk:** Aldo Fessel, who thought he was being kind; Pip and Bron on the
tram platform, in which she answers a personal question straight for the first time.

### Act IV — What the Paperwork Says (ch. 11-13). Lifting car, then the maintenance rail.

#### Chapter 11 — "Decommission"
**Hub:** Tellwater. **Dungeon:** the town itself — house by house, as sixty postings close and sixty
employees pack up eleven years of a life that was a job. **Boss:** a Pale decommission detail under a
nineteen-year-old on a two-day field commission. And then, abruptly, no boss at all.
**Wants:** to stop the file being closed. If Tellwater is decommissioned the set is struck, the
roster is sealed, and the last physical evidence of the night becomes a paragraph.
**Goes wrong:** Ivo Teach refused the escort — the one conscientious refusal of his career — so
Constable Wen Arden, nineteen, is standing in the road instead, and his superintendent's last words
to him that morning were *"nobody has to get hurt, just stand in the road."* He fires once. Lyra dies
in the road with her circlet knocked sideways.
**Learn:** that the harm in this story does not come from a villain's decision. It comes from two
decent men each doing one small careful thing.
**Turn:** the hour. Lyra begins the Order's Tally on herself, out of habit, and cannot finish it.
She asks Bron to take the hour, and he does — knowingly, with her consent — and it becomes the first
memory in his head he is certain is real, and it is not his.
**Party:** Lyra leaves. Ollo Marrick joins.
**Also here:** Sem-Dree finally gets asked. Neve Ardo has come back on her own money to stand in the
square (optional, and the best optional scene in the game).
**Opens:** nothing. This chapter takes.
**Panels:** the postings coming down; the road; **the hour** (high angle, Lyra in the road, Bron's
forehead against hers, both hands on her face, and behind them a young officer sitting down in the
dirt beside his rifle); Pip holding the other hand; the square at dusk. **Talk:** Neve Ardo; Ollo
packing a bakery he never owned.

#### Chapter 12 — "The Cap"
**Hub:** Vantage, Tiers Five and Six, by lifting car. **Dungeon:** the Ossun skywell machinery and
the board floor. **Boss:** the Ossun head governor — a thousand-year-old machine being run past its
rating — and the Cap's house guard.
**Wants:** Crewe. His list, his signature, and him saying it out loud.
**Goes wrong:** he gives them all of it. Fully, calmly, on the record, to a room, with the names. And
Ottoline Kerrow relieves him of the chair mid-sentence, legally, with the votes already counted, and
the confession changes nothing that week. Kerrow then tells the party the truth herself, because she
does not lie: every large bore inverts, there is no fix, and publishing it kills the Basin.
**Learn:** that a confession is a document, and documents can be filed.
**Turn:** Teach finds out why a nineteen-year-old was in the road at Tellwater, and what he himself
did to cause it, and he does not say a word about it to anyone for a chapter and a half.
**Side contract:** Sit with him (Windrow).
**Opens:** Vantage Tiers Five and Six.
**Panels:** the lifting car rising past the tiers; Crewe's confession; Kerrow taking the chair (she is
not triumphant, she is relieved); the warm gardens at night. **Talk:** Crewe and Bron alone, four
lines, in which Crewe asks him his name and gets it wrong in a way that is not his fault.

#### Chapter 13 — "Evacuate"
**Hub:** Braid, under evacuation. **Dungeon:** the Long Bore, under Vantage Tier One, running at
full. **Boss:** the Long Bore draw gear, and a Pale company under Sergeant Kesk.
**Wants:** to stop the Long Bore before it drains the branch under Thurn — and to reach Maren.
**Goes wrong:** Kerrow's first act as chairman is to evacuate Braid, which saves four thousand
people, and to run the Long Bore at full, which is why Act V happens. Cadder evacuates his own crews
and then the whole town against a direct order and is relieved of his post in the street. Teach
arrests Chairman Kerrow on a public-safety warrant; it collapses within a day and buys twelve hours.
And Maren reaches the inner doors, and the guards **stand aside**, because she carries an
oath-keeper's own memory and can say the relief formula.
**Learn:** Maren's plan, stated plainly by her, and it is not insane. Write Tellwater's night into
every person in the Basin at once so that it cannot be filed away. She calls it testimony.
**Party:** Sefa Quill joins, having been passed over for the chair.
**Opens:** the maintenance rail under the hills — fast travel between every hall-mouth, and the Ninth
Door approach.
**Panels:** Braid emptying onto the seabed; Cadder relieved in the street, still trying to make the
joke land; Teach arresting his own chairman; the guards standing aside for Maren. **Talk:** Sefa's
first hour in the party, which is comedy, and Hesk's reaction, which is not.

### Act V — The Doors (ch. 14-15).

#### Chapter 14 — "Thurn"
**Hub:** Thurn, the hall-city. **Dungeon:** Thurn. **Boss:** the Standing Muster — thirty hall-guard
patterns holding the council door correctly. You pass it by **saying the words**: Sefa has the
transcript, Zeph can sing it, Hesk knows one mangled line. The fight is only with the ones whose
armour is too broken to hear you, and it should feel like a mercy killing.
**Wants:** the Choir chamber, ahead of Maren and ahead of Kerrow.
**Goes wrong:** Thurn is lit, swept, warm and fed, and there are three hundred and forty calm,
healthy people living in it who cannot tell you their names. They are not the builders. They are the
builders' descendants: twenty-eight generations of people raised by a city that keeps working, none
of whom has ever held a memory older than a week.
**Learn:** what the last council actually voted for. They did not die and they were not taken. They
wrote everything they were into the Seam for safekeeping, sealed the doors on the bodies, and
expected to be read back out by people who never came. The singing in the dark that drew them down
there is now mostly **them**, rehearsing. And this is precisely what Maren's plan makes, at scale.
**Turn:** Ket reaches the cradle and finds it deliberately broken by the builders on their way past.
The Onn specification survives; the facility does not. A recipe with no kitchen. She then reads the
four thousand names of her own muster aloud, which takes six hours, and Bron sits through all of it.
**Side contract:** Nine hundred tallies (Sallowgate, reachable by the maintenance rail).
**Panels:** the hall-city (establishing wide: a lit, swept, thousand-year-old street underground,
well-fed people in old clothes all facing the same way, not one of them looking at the party); the
council chamber; the broken cradle; Ket and the list of names. **Talk:** Ollo talking to the people
of Thurn, because he is the only one with practice; Hesk and Ket resolving nine chapters in two
lines.

#### Chapter 15 — "The Choir"
**Hub:** none. **Dungeon:** the Choir chamber and the Seam face. **Bosses:** Maren, then the cap.
**Wants:** to stop Maren without destroying the record, and to stop Kerrow without being unable to
answer her.
**Goes wrong:** at the Seam face Maren loses coherence and speaks in four hundred frightened voices
at once. The fight is reaching the woman inside the chorus; nobody wins the argument, because there
is no argument left to have with someone who is no longer one person.
**The turn:** Bron recites the Order's fifteen questions. Lyra's Tally — which is a
strand-integrity check written by the people who built the Seam, and which is also just somebody
asking a frightened woman who she is, in order. It holds her together long enough to choose.
**The choice:** she gives the record away whole, once, to the Braid chant-lines, into chant, where it
cannot be audited, seized, filed, or burned. Ilven Tessen refuses Sefa Quill to her face; Zeph asks
twenty minutes later and the line accepts him, on procedure, and it takes eleven hours to sing. The
giving empties Maren completely.
**Meanwhile:** Ottoline Kerrow arrives to cap the Seam with everyone still inside, with the only
costed plan anyone has produced, and she is not bluffing and does not change her mind. She is stopped
by **people**: Cadder comes down the Long Bore with sixty of his own crew and holds the stair; Teach
holds the door; Tovin sends the board down the maintenance rail; Emmet Sark, elsewhere, files the
22:02 message log because it belongs in the file.
**Ending:** the doors stay open, and watched. Hesk writes down who goes in.
**Panels:** the Seam face; Maren in four voices; Bron reciting; the cap charge on the stair; Cadder
on the stair; the chant at Braid; and the last image — a new bill going up on the Sallowgate board in
Tovin's hand, and Bron in the door in a cold wind he can finally feel.

### Epilogue beats (narration and talk, no boss)

The hearing. Crewe publishes the list and the Basin spends a year arguing about whether it is
genuine. Kerrow wins on the technical merits and her own board removes her over the cost of the
delay. Teach is dismissed, charged, acquitted, and tells his wife. Cadder goes home to Cadder Step
and starts a wage fund. Sefa is struck off and starts teaching the Vess to file. Ket brings Vintry
back up; within two years Dree follows; four of nine. Ollo holds Tellwater together as himself and
the sixty of them vote him something. Pip stays. Zeph goes to Braid twice a year to hear the chant
checked. Anneke Brae asks Bron to tell her, and he does, and it takes eleven minutes and is not
enough, and they agree to meet again. Maren lives above the guild hall and is not crowded. Bron keeps
the borrowed childhood, out loud, on purpose, the way he carries Lyra's hour, and takes a new bill
off the board.

## 13. Mystery management

Nobody reveals early. Nobody contradicts a plant. If a scene needs the party to know something that
is not yet on this ladder, the answer is that they do not know it.

### Ladder A — Bron's lie

- **Ch.1.** Bron mentions, in passing, the winter he got lost above Tellwater and nearly froze. It is
  a warm story and he tells it well. *Player believes:* Bron is a hill-town boy who had a hard year.
- **Ch.2.** Pip jokes: *"You got cold? You lot sleep in snowdrifts for fun."* Bron shrugs it off.
  *Player believes:* it was a joke.
- **Ch.2.** A checkpoint clerk reads his guild papers aloud: **Bron Sallow.** Nobody comments.
  *Player believes:* nothing. This is a seed.
- **Ch.3.** The town greets him warmly and slightly wrong: a rehearsed grip, a nickname he never had,
  "Hesta's boy" one degree too well. *Player believes:* the town is faked around a real survivor.
- **Ch.3.** The drowned bore's maker's plate is dated 1093. *Player believes:* the flood was company
  work.
- **Ch.4.** The archivists' test: a set memory has no edges. Zeph demonstrates it on a stranger for
  fun. *Player believes:* remember this. (They will try it on Bron in their head immediately. Let
  them.)
- **Ch.5.** Hesk: *"Sallow. That's not a name. That's a word off a map."*
- **Ch.5.** Kell cannot feel cold coming, which is why Kell children are taken to the cold at six.
  Bron remembers shivering. Hesk goes quiet. *Player believes:* Bron's childhood is wrong. Not yet:
  Bron is wrong.
- **Ch.6.** The Tellwater payroll: every adult an employee on a posting, including *Ardo, N. — role:
  Hesta Sallow.* *Player believes:* his mother was an actress. He was still a real child there.
- **Ch.6.** Bron cannot describe his mother's face; what comes out is a role description.
- **Ch.7.** Sixty-one adults in the Registry remember a mother named Hesta. *Player believes:* Bron
  was set, with a stock template. Bron does not accept it. **This is the point where most players
  will call the twist, and the game should let them, because the real reveal is not that he was set.**
- **Ch.8.** A bystander, watching Maren work: *"She gives it back. She doesn't make it up."*
  Establishing that she is a source, not a forger.
- **Ch.9. Reveal one.** Maren recites the winter he got lost, first person, timing right. The
  specific warm parts of his childhood are hers, drawn out of her and sold back into him.
- **Ch.10. Reveal two, the real one.** The intake form: *age nine, condition blank, disposition set
  and returned.* He was in the radius, not at the node. **Nothing of his was ever stored.** There is
  no copy. He is the only victim in the Basin who cannot be restored, by anyone, ever.
- **Ch.10.** Crewe's list gives him his mother's name and where she was sent.
- **Ch.11-15.** No further reveals about Bron. The question stops being *what is true* and becomes
  *what do you do tomorrow.*

### Ladder B — Who posted the contract

Ch.1: burned seal, triple rate, "ask no questions." Ch.1: the tent behind the door proves a company
crew was waiting. Ch.2: the under-impression is legible and Orrin Cadder admits it over dinner
without being asked twice. **Resolved in chapter 2, deliberately.** A mystery that outlives its
usefulness becomes a chore.

### Ladder C — What the dead guards are

Ch.1: it warns instead of attacking. Ch.2: a wax cylinder at the relay station plays a guard's oath,
and the words are identical every time — it is a recording. Ch.5: Hesk and the Sealed Stair give the
full explanation: the oath outlived the men because the oath was all there was left to copy. Ch.13:
they stand aside for Maren. Ch.14: thirty of them, and you talk your way past.

### Ladder D — What the Seam is

Ch.1: it sings. Ch.4: the Vess archive calls it the pattern-store. Ch.5: what Thurn did with it.
Ch.6: what it does to people when a bore runs backwards. Ch.14: why it sings.

### Ladder E — What happened at Tellwater

Ch.3: the town is wrong and the flood was made. Ch.6: the payroll. Ch.7: the resettlement map. Ch.10:
the intake forms and Crewe's list. Ch.12: Crewe says it out loud with the names. Ch.15: the 22:02
message log, which proves nobody decided.

### Ladder F — Maren

Ch.2: a rumour — a blank surveyor and "the woman who touched him." Ch.4: a company recovery notice
for *the Fair Copy; do not converse.* Ch.6: glimpsed in the dust walking toward the bore. Ch.8: seen,
in full, doing the thing she does. Ch.9: spoken to. Ch.10: her file, twelve years of it. Ch.13: her
plan, in her own words. Ch.15: the chorus.

### Ladder G — Kerrow's inversion data

Ch.6: in the dust she says a radius figure out loud that she should not know in advance. Ch.9: a
buried study number on a survey tag. Ch.12: Crewe's confession names the study. Ch.12-13: she
confirms it herself, without being pressed, and dares them to publish.

### Ladder H — Lyra's order

Ch.1: she sits the Tally at a dying hunter's bedside and the player hears the fifteen questions in
full, once, and they sound like a liturgy. Ch.5: the same words are cut into the wall of the Sealed
Stair — **the player sees this and the party does not.** Ch.9: Ket reads them off a maintenance
manual and stops halfway. Ch.9-11: Lyra decides it is a better prayer than she thought, because
someone wrote it so that a thing would survive being moved. Ch.11: she begins it on herself. Ch.15:
Bron finishes it.

### Things the player must NOT learn early

- That Bron's own childhood is unrecoverable (ch.10, not before).
- That the people of Thurn are alive and living down there (ch.14, not before — no earlier
  foreshadowing beyond "nobody came out").
- That Teach carried Bron out of the street (ch.12 at the earliest, and he never says it; it is on
  the constables' roster in Crewe's list).
- That the Tally is a maintenance procedure (ch.9 for the party; ch.5 for the player, visually only).

## 14. Tone and dialogue guide

### How it sounds

Lines are **under 120 characters**, usually well under. One thought per box. The rhythm is
short-short-long. Characters interrupt each other. Nobody makes a speech; the longest speech in the
game is Crewe's confession and it is broken into eleven boxes with reactions between them.

Humour is constant and comes from character, never from parody. Pip jokes because she is frightened.
Ket is funny because she is literal. Sefa is funny because she is cruel and correct. Cadder is funny
because he wants you to like him and it works. **The villains get the best jokes.** A world this cold
is only bearable if the people in it are good company.

Grief is handled by **not handling it**. Nobody in this game says "I'm sad" or "I miss her." They
change the subject badly, do a task with too much care, correct a small detail that does not matter,
or ask someone else if they have eaten. After chapter 11 the party does not talk about Lyra for two
chapters, and then Ollo asks a question nobody has dared to, because he did not know her.

### What is never said aloud

- The theme. Nobody ever says that memory makes us who we are, or that the truth must be preserved,
  or that the company is like a machine. Cut any line that explains the game.
- "I'm sorry for your loss."
- Any character calling another character's people by a generalisation, except deliberately, by
  someone we are meant to notice doing it.
- Bron never says he feels hollow, empty, fake, or unreal. He asks factual questions instead, and
  that is how we know.
- Maren never says she is suffering.
- The word "memory" should appear far less often than writers will want it to. Prefer: the hour, the
  afternoon, the winter, a sleeve, a strand, what she has, what he was given.

### Ten example exchanges in the target voice

**1. Chapter 1, the hall.**
> Tovin-Caleth: Triple rate. Burned seal. No questions.
> Bron: What's the catch.
> Tovin-Caleth: I just said it three times.

**2. Chapter 1, behind the door.**
> Clerk: Entry chit?
> Pip: A what?
> Clerk: Then you'll have to wait outside. There's a bench.

**3. Chapter 2, the dinner.**
> Cadder: I posted it. Burned the seal myself, made a mess of it.
> Bron: You sent us in to trip the wards.
> Cadder: I sent *someone* in. You lot were cheap and very keen.

**4. Chapter 4, the annex.**
> Zeph: Ask him what he did the day before.
> Pip: That's it? That's the whole trick?
> Zeph: That's the whole trick. Nobody builds the day before. Why would you.

**5. Chapter 5, the terrace.**
> Hesk: You remember being cold.
> Bron: One winter. I got lost.
> Hesk: Mm.
> Bron: Say the rest of it.
> Hesk: I've nothing to say. Eat your food.

**6. Chapter 6, after the count.**
> Kerrow: Eleven hundred feet. Forty-one people.
> Lyra: You had that number before you counted.
> Kerrow: Yes.

**7. Chapter 9, the stump.**
> Maren: You weren't cold. You were bored. Then you were frightened. Then someone carried you.
> Bron: Anyone could know that.
> Maren: Nobody knows that. You never told it. It's too small to tell.

**8. Chapter 11, the road.**
> Lyra: Fifteenth question. Ask me the fifteenth.
> Bron: I don't know it.
> Lyra: You will.

**9. Chapter 13, the party.**
> Sefa: File it, and then it happened.
> Hesk: That's not how anything works.
> Sefa: Registrar of the Clement Works, fourteen years. It is exactly how everything works.

**10. Chapter 15, the board.**
> Tovin-Caleth: Two days' walk. Bad water. Pays badly.
> Bron: Who posted it?
> Tovin-Caleth: A woman with a well and no money. Seal's on it.
> Bron: Fine.

### Avoid

- Exposition in the mouths of people who would not say it. If the party needs a fact, find someone
  whose job it is to be tedious about it.
- "Ah, so the Seam must be…" Nobody deduces out loud. They argue, and one of them turns out right.
- Villain monologues. Cadder and Kerrow both explain themselves in under five boxes.
- Sarcasm as a default register. Only Sefa and Pip live there, and for opposite reasons.
- Any character apologising for the plot ("if only we'd been faster").
- Prophecies, chosen ones, destinies, ancient evils, awakening, darkness, the gods.
- The words: *soul, spirit, essence, life force, magic, curse, undead, ancient evil.*
- Named attacks in dialogue.
- Anyone explaining to Bron what he is feeling.
- Onomatopoeia in dialogue lines. It belongs in art direction, not text.

## 15. Glossary

- **A.S.** — After Seal. The year count, from the sealing of Thurn. Present: 1107.
- **Annex, the** — the Braid archive halls, Vess-built, company-leased since 1067.
- **Attendance sister** — a member of the Order of the Late Hour, who sits the last hour of the dying
  and carries it.
- **Bill** — a job on an Even Hand board. *On the bill* = working a job.
- **Blank** — a person emptied by a bore inversion. Healthy, calm, and nameless. Not a slur, but the
  Works uses it as a file category, which is the problem.
- **Bore** — a shaft driven to a Seam branch with a draw head at the bottom.
- **Bore inversion** — the draw head's field reversing, emptying every living thing in radius.
- **Care-house** — a Clement Works children's home. Five in the Basin. See "contributory childhood."
- **Chant-line** — a Vess matrilineal household of eight to twenty that holds one body of record
  between them and rehearses it daily.
- **Clement Works, the** — the Basin's warmth utility, bank, landlord, and largest employer.
  Chartered 1041.
- **Contributory childhood** — the clause on a care-house intake form under which a child's best days
  are drawn twice a year and sold.
- **Doorward** — a Kell household whose inherited job is to keep a sealed hall-mouth shut.
- **Draw head** — the brass bell on a gimbal at the bottom of a bore.
- **Drawing** — pulling pattern out of the Seam, or out of a living person.
- **Even Hand, the** — the patronless hunters' guild. Founded Sallowgate, 1053.
- **Fair Copy, the** — the Clement Works' file name for Maren Ostry.
- **Ferry-rail** — Vess sail-and-crank rail sledges running on laid track across the dry seabed.
- **Four Lines, the** — the Even Hand's rules: pay on completion, no patron, the seal or no bill,
  bring them back.
- **Gap** — what drawing leaves in a person. The edges close; nobody notices.
- **Hall-guard** — the guard corps of Thurn, whose standing oath still runs inside its armour.
- **Hall-mouth** — a sealed entrance to the halls beneath the Rim Hills.
- **Hungry Winters, the** — 1038-1041, when Ossun was failing and two thousand died.
- **Ledger wall** — the Sallowgate hall's record of every bill cleared since 1053, in hand.
- **Lifting car** — a cable car between Vantage's tiers. Needs a tier pass.
- **Long Bore, the** — the Works' largest draw head, capping the hall-mouth under Vantage Tier One.
- **Muster** — a batch of Onn grown together. The last ran from year -161 to year -141 and produced
  four thousand. Ket-Ossun and Tovin-Caleth are both of it.
- **Onn** — the people grown to maintain the skywells. Six hundred and eleven remain.
- **Order of the Late Hour** — Lyra's order. Its liturgy, the Tally, is a lost maintenance procedure.
- **Ourn** — the world.
- **Pale, the** — the Clement Works' police, named for their grey coats.
- **Pattern-store** — what the Seam is.
- **Posting** — a Clement Works employment placement. At Tellwater, a renewable three-year role.
- **Quiet House, the** — Ivo Teach's boarding house in Windrow for the widows of his dead.
- **Recovery** — the Pale's term for retrieving company assets, including people.
- **Relief formula** — the words a relieving watch says to take a post from a hall-guard. Saying them
  correctly makes a guard stand aside.
- **Saturation** — receiving everything an inverting field takes, by standing at the node.
- **Seam, the** — the grown crystal-metal lattice under the Rim Hills; the pattern-store of the
  machinery that made Ourn livable.
- **Setting** — writing a strand into a person, with a setting frame. Legal since 1052.
- **Skywell** — one of nine ring towers that hold the Basin's air warm and thick. Two run.
- **Sleeve** — the paper envelope a strand is sold in. By extension, a unit: "two sleeves."
- **Strand** — a drawn length of pattern in glass thread. About an hour of someone's life.
- **Tally, the** — the Order of the Late Hour's fifteen questions, asked at a deathbed. Also a
  strand-integrity check written by the people who built the Seam.
- **Template** — a stock issued life, used in resettlement. Bron received template 4.
- **Thurn** — the hall-city the Kell built downward and sealed in year 0. Still inhabited.
- **Tier pass** — Clement Works staff credential for the Vantage lifting cars.
- **Vess Concession, the** — the 1067 sale of Braid's archive halls to the Works. Legal. Runs to 1187.

## 16. Decisions made in this bible

Non-obvious calls, with how to reverse them.

1. **Junior Director Vane is renamed Ottoline Kerrow.** "Vane" is too close to Vayne, a principal
   Final Fantasy antagonist who is also a young ambitious executive seizing a seat. Character is
   unchanged. *Reverse:* global find and replace.
2. **The guild is the Even Hand**, not the Free Tally. Its jobs are "bills," its rules are the Four
   Lines, and its Sallowgate master is **Tovin-Caleth, an Onn**, which makes the guild feel older
   than the company and gives Ket a personal history in chapter 4.
3. **Bron's childhood is a stock template plus two live strands drawn from Maren**, not wholly
   Maren's. This makes the reveal two-staged (his mother is stock in ch.7; the warm specifics are
   Maren's in ch.9), explains why sixty-one other adults remember a mother called Hesta, and lets a
   posted employee, Neve Ardo, have played that mother for four years.
4. **Bron's own past was erased, not stored.** He was in the radius, not at the node, so there is no
   copy of it anywhere, and he is the one victim who can never be restored. This is the chapter 10
   reveal and the reason the ending works. *Reverse:* if he can be restored, Act V has no argument.
5. **Nothing is ever copied, by anyone.** This one rule generates the whole moral economy: every sold
   strand is subtracted from someone, Maren's plan would *divide* rather than duplicate, and the only
   way to make the truth durable is to tell people out loud. It is also why the ending is a chant.
6. **Pitch C's "two skywells run" versus "the Works owns the only working skywell" is resolved** in
   favour of both: Ossun runs at full and is metered; Meddra runs at a fifth on Onn hand-work and is
   not sold. The company slogan is a true-sounding lie.
7. **Maren's timeline is corrected.** The pitch said thirty-six years old, fourteen years since
   Tellwater, and nineteen years in a sanatorium, which does not add up. She was twenty-two in 1093,
   held twelve years, and has been walking for two.
8. **Lyra's order carries Pitch B's lost-liturgy import.** The Tally is a strand-integrity check.
   Lyra learns this in chapter 9, decides it makes the prayer better rather than worse, and dies in
   chapter 11 — and Bron uses her fifteen questions to hold Maren together in chapter 15, which is
   how her death pays off. The Onn are left clean; they are literal maintenance staff already.
9. **Teach's one act of conscience kills Lyra.** He refuses the Tellwater decommission escort, which
   is why a nineteen-year-old is in the road. Cadder signed the boy's field commission and told him
   to stand there. No villain decides her death.
10. **Cadder lives.** The orchestrator asked for a real ending, and survival with no career, no
    employer, and a village that will not look at him is a harder one than a heroic death.
11. **Thurn is inhabited by twenty-eight generations of descendants**, not by preserved builders.
    Three hundred and forty living people who have never held a memory older than a week. It solves
    the thousand-year-old-bodies problem and makes them the working preview of Maren's plan.
12. **The Onn's extinction was caused by the sealing.** The cradle is behind Thurn's doors and the
    builders broke it on the way past. Ket's quest ends in a recipe with no kitchen.
13. **Emmet Sark, the mildly annoyed clerk from chapter one, recurs all game** and produces the
    decisive document at the hearing, having never done anything but his job.
14. **Sixteen settlements and nine named skywells are fixed here.** Later writers may add a farm, a
    camp, or a waystation, but not a town or a well.
15. **Originality sweep done.** Every proper noun in this document was checked against Phantasy Star
    and Final Fantasy. One rename resulted (Vane → Kerrow). "Onn," "Kell," "Vess," "Ourn," "Seam,"
    "Vantage," "the Pale," "Thurn," "Tellwater," and the cast names are clear.

## Open questions for the owner

1. **The title.** *The Fair Copy* is the recommendation; *Ask No Questions* is the loud alternative.
2. **Lyra's death stands** per DECISIONS D2, with Bron taking her hour. The one thing worth a second
   look: chapter 11 asks the player to lose the healer and gain a baker in the same scene.
3. **Party size.** Eight playable, four in a fight, and Sefa Quill does not actually fight. If that is
   a problem for the battle designer, she becomes a support-only slot or a non-playable companion.
4. **Zeph's and Pip's `look` lines need updating** in `story/characters.md` by the cast designer —
   Zeph to full Vess features (ash-lilac skin, fluted ribbed ears, solid violet eyes, wire hair), Pip
   only cosmetically. Bron and Lyra are untouched.
5. **Restoration as a game system.** Maren restores people by touch. If the battle or field systems
   ever let the player do it, hard rules 7 and 10 break. Recommended: it is never a player verb.
6. **Chapter 8's side contract** ("Collection at Emberrow") lets the player buy a child out of a debt
   for a punishing sum. Confirm the economy can support a genuinely expensive optional choice.
